# 12. Voices: in-world documents

An anthology of things people in Calder Bay wrote, printed, pinned, chalked, sang or said out loud. Each piece has a line in
italics saying where it comes from. All the names, numbers and floors follow `00-canon-brief.md` and the files before this
one. Bylaw numbers, Arbiter numbers and ordinance sections here are chosen so they fit around the ones already quoted in
`05-law.md` and `03-water.md`, never on top of them.

Marking, as everywhere in this folder: plain text is the main version; **Alternative:** is another version of the same
thing; **Hook:** is a story seed; **In play:** is how the document turns up in the game. Most documents here could be an
actual item on the field: a slip, a card, a page, a flyer, a receipt. Paper takes squares. That is half the point.

---

## Part one: the Company speaks

Company paper has a look. White card, or thin grey paper for the floors below 30. A small blue mark in the top corner: the
founding-era Halden gear with the tower standing in it. A reference code in the bottom corner that means something to the
Count House and nothing to anyone else. The sentences are short and calm and always end with a thank-you. People in the
Mills say a Company notice is the only thing in the tower that is always polite and never wrong.

### 1.1 "Bylaws under review this Period"

*Printed notice, white card, pinned in clip 1 of the Four Hall shift board, floor 17, the first morning of Period 86/4. The
same notice, with the floor number changed, goes up on every lobby board below 60.*

> **HALDEN COMPANY · LEASE OFFICE · RESIDENT INFORMATION**
>
> **Bylaws under review, Period 86/4**
>
> To help residents enjoy a safe and orderly Structure, the following Bylaws will receive particular attention from
> officers, delegated licensors and floor wardens during this Period:
>
> **Bylaw 39** (Storage of water). Residents below floor 60 are reminded that more than forty litres of water held on a
> lease requires a Storage Notice. Stockpiling is a threat to supply.
>
> **Bylaw 44** (Charging of cells). Cells shall be charged only at a Company socket on the lease where they are used.
>
> **Bylaw 81** (Singing). Singing is noise.
>
> **Bylaw 87** (Rain). Persons below floor 30 shall not gather on the outer face of the Structure for the purpose of
> receiving rain. A rain day is forecast for the second week of this Period.
>
> Residents who are unsure whether their conduct is compliant are invited to consult their floor warden, who will be glad
> to advise.
>
> *These Bylaws are made for the wellbeing of all residents. Thank you for your cooperation.*
>
> LO/BR/86-4/17 · Posted 06:58 · Notice to every person on this floor and on any floor served by the same lift (Bylaw 2).

Underneath, in pencil, small, in the handwriting the whole floor knows is Hester Moyle's: *Tasters walk 14 to 19 on the 9th.
Sing quietly.*

**In play:** the monthly modifier from `05-law.md`, made into a real object. The notice is a card on the lobby board for
thirty days. Each named bylaw raises the weight of a matching inspection check (water stock over 40 L, cells on your field
with no socket in reach, a Static receiver turned up, a catch pan on the window ledge on rain day). The pencil line is the
warden's regard showing itself: at high regard, the notice carries her date, and the inspection hour stops being a surprise.

### 1.2 Rate Review, Period 86/4

*Cover letter clipped to the new Floor Rate Card, delivered by drone to every Commercial lease, floor 17. The card itself is
in `04-economy.md`; this is the letter that comes with it.*

> Dear Leaseholder,
>
> **Your Floor Rate Card for Period 86/4 is enclosed.**
>
> As part of the Company's commitment to transparent and predictable occupancy costs, rates for every floor are reviewed
> each Period in line with the cost of services.
>
> On your floor (17), the base rate per square per rent week will move from **5.00 cr** to **5.10 cr**. For a typical
> Commercial lease of 24 squares with 4 frontage squares, this represents an adjustment of **2.60 cr** per rent week.
>
> **What your rate pays for**
> - Structural integrity of a founding-era building designed to last
> - Lift availability (lift services are provided by the Lift Guild under separate fare)
> - Refuse discharge through a dedicated chute (contents conveyed without warranty)
> - Corridor lighting where fitted
> - Access to metered water and power (metered separately)
> - The protection of the Occupancy Charter and the Office of the Arbiter (filing fees apply)
>
> We know that every credit matters. That is why your rate on floor 17 remains **lower than the rate on floor 18**, and
> significantly lower than the rate in the Middle.
>
> Leaseholders wishing to reduce their occupancy costs may surrender squares at any time. Surrendered squares are
> re-offered on the next card.
>
> With thanks for your continued tenancy,
> **Halden Survey and Allocation**
>
> *Rate Reviews are carried out under Clause 7 of the Halden Standard Tenancy Agreement. The Rate per Square is as
> published in the Floor Rate Card for the Floor and Period, as amended.*

**In play:** this letter is the Creep's monthly tick, seen as paper instead of a number change. Make it a 1x1 slip that
arrives on day 30, 60, 90 in a drone bay with nothing else in it, so unpacking it is a tiny, deflating mystery box. Keeping
old cards (pinned on a wall square) gives the player a visible record of the climb. "Remains lower than the rate on floor
18" is the line people quote back at it.

**In play:** the re-cut event from `04-economy.md`, announced. The chalk lines on the field the evening before (a faint

### 1.9 *Halden Hours: Thoughts for the Upward Tenant*

*Pocket booklet, blue cover, 32 pages, given free with every Middle lease and found by the dozen in the West Chute. Extracts
from the middle pages. In the Mills it is a 1x1 item worth nothing, and the paper is good for lighting a stove.*

> **On Squares.** A square kept tidy is a square kept. The tenant who fills his squares with clutter is renting space for
> things that do not pay rent.
>
> **On Water.** Water is not free anywhere in the world. Where it seems to be free, someone else is paying, and usually
> it is your health.
>
> **On Neighbours.** Be generous with your time and careful with your goods. A gift returned is a sale (Bylaw 71), and a
> sale needs a licence.
>
> **On Standing.** The Ledger remembers kindness. Every rent paid on time, every delivery made early, is a small deposit
> in the account of your future.
>
> **On the Floors Below.** Every tenant above floor 30 was once a tenant below it, or their parents were. Remember where
> you came from, and visit it rarely.
>
> **On Rain.** Rain is weather. Water is a service.
>
> **On Complaining.** A complaint is a request for information. Put it in writing to the Lease Office, and you will
> receive information.
>
> **On Rest.** Sleep is the one thing every tenant does on a residential square. Do it there.

**In play:** a chute item with exactly one honest use (fuel, packing filler, 0 value) and a single line of text when
hovered, one aphorism picked at random. It teaches the bylaws by quoting them. A Middle client's slip can quote it back,
sincerely: "As Halden Hours says, a square kept tidy is a square kept. Please pack neatly."

### 1.10 Meter upgrade notice

*Grey slip, drone-delivered to every lease on floors 15 to 19, Y86.*

> **HALDEN UTILITIES · YOUR METER IS BEING IMPROVED**
>
> Over the coming Period, a Halden fitter will visit your lease to fit a **Fairness Plus** interval meter to your tap and
> socket.
>
> **What's changing?** Fairness Plus records your draw every hour, not every day. This means your bill will reflect exactly
> when you use water and power, so you can choose to use them at quieter times.
>
> **Quieter times?** From Period 86/6, Fairness Plus leases will pay a **Peak Use Adjustment** of +30% on water drawn
> between 06:00 and 09:00 and 18:00 and 21:00, when demand on the Structure is highest.
>
> (Tap hours on your floor are 06:00 to 09:00 and 18:00 to 21:00.)
>
> **Is there a charge?** The meter itself is free. A **fitting fee** of 12 cr will be added to your next bill.
>
> **Do I have to?** Fairness Plus is being fitted to all leases. Leases that decline the visit will be charged the Peak Use
> Adjustment on all water drawn.

**In play:** an upgrade the player is given, not offered. It changes the water tariff from flat to hourly, which only
matters because the game runs in hours: water drawn at 07:00 costs more than water drawn at 07:00 used to. The parenthesis
is the whole document.

**Hook:** the Old Hands say the founding-era meters already recorded hourly. They always did. The Ledger simply never
billed by it, because in Y0 the Works wanted its workers' showers taken whenever their shift ended.

## Part two: the water law, and the paper around it

### 2.1 The Public Wellbeing (Water) Ordinance, Y58: the sections nobody frames

*From the Archivists' copy of the full text, 22 pages, as gazetted by the Halden Company in the Second Month of Y58. The
framed copy in every tap hall shows Sections 2, 3, 4, 9, 11 and 14 (quoted in `03-water.md`). The rest is below, in
order, cut where it repeats itself.*

> **PUBLIC WELLBEING (WATER) ORDINANCE, Y58**
>
> *Made by the Halden Company in exercise of the powers conferred by the Emergency Stewardship Order, in response to the
> unprecedented public health emergency arising from the storm and from the contamination of Calder's groundwater by
> unregulated private wells.*
>
> **Preamble.** Whereas the waters of Calder Bay and of the ground beneath Calder are unfit for human consumption; and
> whereas the Company is the sole operator of a works capable of rendering water safe in sufficient quantity; and whereas
> water of uncertain origin endangers both those who drink it and public confidence in water of certain origin; now the
> Company makes the following Ordinance for public wellbeing.
>
> **Section 1. Short title and duration.** This Ordinance may be cited as the Water Ordinance. It shall remain in force for
> the duration of the emergency. The emergency continues until the Company declares it ended.
>
> **Section 5. Licensed persons.** A Water Licence may be granted to any person who (a) applies in the prescribed form, (b)
> pays the prescribed fee, (c) submits each device to be used to sealing by the Water Wellbeing Office, and (d) is, in the
> opinion of the Office, a fit person. A person who has previously treated water without a licence is not, by reason of
> that fact alone, unfit. Experience is relevant.
>
> **Section 6. Certified Safe Provider.** A holder of a Class D Licence shall display, at every point of sale, a plaque
> bearing the words CERTIFIED SAFE PROVIDER and a number. Water sold from a point of sale displaying a plaque is
> presumed safe. Water sold from a point of sale not displaying a plaque is presumed unsafe. The presumptions are not
> rebuttable by testing.
>
> **Section 7. Inspection.** An inspector may at any reasonable hour enter any lease on which water is held, drawn, stored or
> believed to be treated, and may take samples, examine devices, seal devices, unseal devices, and seize water. Any hour at
> which water is being treated is a reasonable hour.
>
> **Section 8. Testing materials.** No person other than a licensed person or an inspector shall possess reagent strips,
> test kits or other materials for determining the fitness of water. *(Note: a resident who does not know whether their
> water is safe should not drink it.)*
>
> **Section 10. Grey water.** Water which has been used for washing, cooling, flushing or any industrial purpose, and which
> has left the lease on which it was used, is waste. Waste water which is subsequently treated is treated water, and Section 2
> applies. Waste water which is subsequently drunk without treatment is the drinker's affair.
>
> **Section 12. Ice and vapour.** Water in any state is water. Water conveyed in a state other than liquid (as ice, as
> vapour, or within a gel, sachet, fruit, ferment or other vehicle) is water conveyed.
>
> **Section 13. Supply Events.** The Company may declare a Supply Event when, in its opinion, the supply of licensed water
> is or may become insufficient. During a Supply Event, tariffs may be varied, tap hours may be varied, and Section 14
> does not apply. The Company shall give notice of a Supply Event by posting at tap halls. Posting may follow the Event.
>
> **Section 15. Offences.** A person who contravenes Section 2 commits an offence and is liable to (a) a standards charge
> not exceeding 2,000 cr; (b) the seizure of all water, devices, vessels and materials connected with the offence; (c) an
> order for remediation labour under Section 17; (d) any combination. A device seized under this Section is forfeit.
> A vessel seized under this Section is forfeit, together with its contents, together with any vessel in which the vessel
> was found.
>
> **Section 16. Information.** The Company may reward any person who provides information leading to the detection of an
> offence. The identity of an informant shall not be disclosed, save to the Company.
>
> **Section 17. Remediation.** A person liable to a standards charge under Section 15 who cannot pay it may be ordered to
> perform remediation labour at the Waterhouse at a rate to be fixed by the Company, until the charge and the costs of
> supervision are discharged. The costs of supervision accrue daily.
>
> **Section 18. Seized water.** Water seized under this Ordinance shall be disposed of safely. Safe disposal includes
> treatment at the Waterhouse and supply to residents through the licensed framework.
>
> **Section 19. Transitional provision.** A person who, at the commencement of this Ordinance, was supplying water to
> residents by a device of their own may continue to do so for thirty days, and shall within that time apply for a
> licence under Section 5. A person who so applies, and pays, shall be certified. A person who does not shall be a
> person supplying unlicensed water, and shall be described in Company notices accordingly.
>
> **Section 22. Interpretation of "safe".** In this Ordinance, "safe" means certified.
>
> **Schedule 3. Form of plaque.** Enamel on steel, 20 cm by 12 cm, blue on white, bearing the words CERTIFIED SAFE PROVIDER,
> the Licence number, the year, and the words *Public Wellbeing*. The plaque remains the property of the Company.

Section 22 is the one the Old Hands quote. Section 18 is the one the stinkers quote. Section 1's second sentence is the one
nobody quotes, because it is the whole Ordinance.

**In play:** Section 12 is why gel bricks, ice, fruit and kvass are all "water" to an inspector, so an inspection that
finds 41 L of melon on your field is, technically, a Bylaw 39 matter. Section 15's last sentence is the rule that makes the
**closed container** trick (hiding stock in a crate during a sweep, `03-water.md`) a gamble with a real cost: if a hidden
jug is found, the crate it was in goes too. Section 19 is the stinker-to-certified path in one paragraph: the same still,
the same water, a fee.

## Part five: the Arbiter

The Register board on 19 is read like a newspaper. These four determinations were posted in the last few years and are
still talked about. The form is the Office's: number, parties, finding, order, costs, signed **A.** People add their own
notes on the board in pencil; the clerks rub them out on Seventh.

### 5.1 A/80/0144: the meter that was fast

*Posted on the Register boards on 19 and 77, Y80. Copies, 5 cr a page.*

> **A/80/0144.** Petitioner: **D. Kasprzak, lease 12-G.** Respondent: **Halden Company.**
>
> **Finding.** The petitioner disputed the readings of the water meter on lease 12-G for Periods 79/7 to 80/2. On her
> request and at her cost (calibration fee, 35 cr, Bylaw 34), the meter was tested by a Company fitter and found to record
> 118 litres for every 100 litres passed. The petitioner seeks the return of the overcharge for the seven Periods, which
> she calculates at 412 cr.
>
> The Ledger records the readings of the meter. The bill is drawn from the Ledger. The Ledger accurately recorded what the
> meter read. The bills were therefore accurate statements of the Ledger. The meter was inaccurate. A meter is presumed
> accurate (Bylaw 34) until it is found inaccurate, and it was found inaccurate on the date of the test. Before that date
> it was presumed accurate, and the presumption is not affected by the later finding.
>
> **Order.** The meter on lease 12-G is to be recalibrated (recalibration charge, 20 cr, payable by the Leaseholder, the
> meter being on the lease). Readings after the date of recalibration will be accurate. The claim for the overcharge is
> dismissed. The calibration fee is not refunded, the test having been performed.
>
> **Costs** to the respondent.
>
> **A.**

Pencilled on the board under it, before the clerk came: *She got a straight meter for 467 cr. Cheapest on the floor.*

**In play:** meters can drift. An analyser on the meter (the player's own test bench can't legally touch a Company fixture,
Bylaw 93) would show "+18%" as a revealed feature; the honest route is the 35 cr test and this outcome. The dishonest
route is an Old Hand who "looks closely at" the seal (Bylaw 33) and makes it read slow. The determination is the reason
the player will take the second route.

### 5.2 A/84/0287: listening is not watching

*Posted Y84. The bed-watchers of the Mills have it by heart.*

> **A/84/0287.** Petitioner: **Halden Company.** Respondent: **lease 21-C (Mills Growers' and Workers' Co-operative,
> grow hall 3).**
>
> **Finding.** A floor warden found a member of the respondent's Co-operative, **P. Sandoval**, seated with his eyes
> closed for forty minutes on square C-31, which is registered Commercial. Under Bylaw 11, a person who closes their eyes
> for more than twenty minutes on a non-residential square is presumed to be sleeping.
>
> The respondent says that Mr. Sandoval was bed-watching: keeping an overnight watch on eleven grow beds whose shared
> pump fails without warning, and that he closed his eyes in order to hear the pump more clearly, as is the practice of
> bed-watchers. The respondent produces the pump, which is noisy, and a statement from Mr. Sandoval that he was awake.
>
> The presumption in Bylaw 11 arises from the closing of the eyes. It is not displaced by the purpose for which they were
> closed. The Office notes that the practice described is listening. The Bylaw does not distinguish listening from
> sleeping, and the Office does not either.
>
> **Order.** A standards charge of 25 cr. Square C-31 is reclassified as **Residential (Occasional)** at the residential
> rate plus 10%, so that persons may lawfully close their eyes upon it.
>
> **Costs** to the petitioner.
>
> **A.**

**In play:** the reason bed-watching is a job you pay a neighbour for (`08-daily-life.md`), and the reason it carries a
risk. Night is out of scope for play, but the *hour* before 20:00 isn't: a "bed-watcher" hireling, packed with a stool and
a lamp, keeps a grow bed's pump alive through the night block, and has a small chance of a Bylaw 11 charge landing on
your ledger in the morning.

### 5.3 A/86/0351: the crate between

*Posted Y86, the Fifth Month. Two neighbours on floor 12, both now ruined, both still neighbours.*

> **A/86/0351.** Petitioner: **lease 12-A (A. Mbatha).** Respondent: **lease 12-B (S. Rourke).**
>
> **Finding.** Leases 12-A and 12-B hold adjacent catch positions (5 and 6 of 12) on the North Trunk. On the morning of
> day 9, a sealed crate of linen, marked to a Crown household and not retired, fell from the drop door. The parties agree
> it struck the rim of both containers and came to rest on the floor between them. Each party claims the crate as its
> chute contents.
>
> Under Bylaw 51, chute contents are the property of the lease to which the chute discharges, from the moment of
> discharge. Under Bylaw 54, goods falling from a container onto the floor of a lease are discharged onto that lease. The
> crate did not come to rest in either container and did not come to rest on either lease. The squares between the two
> catch positions are **common squares**. Goods resting on common squares are in the possession of the Company.
>
> The crate bore no evidence of retirement. Unretired branded goods discharged by a Crown lease are a matter for the
> Brand Integrity Bureau.
>
> **Order.** The crate and its contents are delivered to the Company. The Company will notify the Bureau. Each party shall
> pay its own costs and one half of the respondent Company's costs, the Company having become a party by possession.
>
> **A.**

Underneath, two pencil notes, one in each hand: *Should've let you have it.* / *Should've let YOU have it.*

**In play:** a rare, rich chute event: a whole crate lands *between* catch positions (an item on a shared common square at
the edge of the player's field) for one hour. The player can take it (theft from the Company, Bylaw 51), leave it for a
neighbour (regard), or both can reach for it, and the one who loses goes to the Arbiter. Teaches common squares in one
morning. **Hook:** the linen was unretired because a Crown disposal valet was bribed by the Voided to send it down whole.
It was meant for a third catch, further down, and fell early. Someone on 9 is still waiting for it.

### 5.4 A/86/0377: the woman who was never relieved

*Posted Y86, the Sixth Month. The most-copied determination in the Mills this year. Static read it in full three nights

>
> **Finding.** The petitioner was employed by the respondent in Works Building Services, in the fan rooms of the floor 30

**In play:** proof that the Arbiter sometimes rules by rules written in Y0 for a company that needed its workers (the

### 6.1 *Summary of Findings for Residents* (the back of the Resilience Day card)

*"In their full extent." The Glasshouse bought the Sounding's Certain tier. Three days' warning. The Lifeline barges were

> complex. The Commission noted the proliferation of unlicensed private wells as a significant and avoidable contributing

*Works abstraction: 60,000 m³ a day from the deep beds. Every licensed well in Calder at its fullest: 400 m³ a day from the

> **The wall (4.11).** The Calder Wall was maintained within the terms of the Calder Development Accord.

*Wall Book, last year: 371 repairs requested, 3 done. "Good and sufficient" was in the contract. The contract was

> **The rescue (6.1).** The Commission commends the Halden Company for Operation Lifeline, which saved many thousands of

*Lifeline took 31,000 off the roofs. The barges were the Works' own, fuelled on the 17th. 22,000 signed on the water, for a bed. The beds are the ones you rent.*

*It considered. Unrecoverable Zone, Y59. Which vested the seabed in the holder of the harbour concession. Which is Halden

> **The dead.** The Inquiry recorded **3,912** deaths.

*The Tally: 21,640, last revision. Every name has a page.*

> *Halden Stands With Calder.*

*Seven: they knew, and they never told.*

**In play:** the card is a 1x1 item that comes free with every Resilience Day and is mostly fuel. The annotated copy is a
different item: a find from the chute (West Chute, the Paper Chute) that an Archivist will buy, Static will read, and HPS
will fine you for under Bylaw 84 (marking a surface: the card is the Company's, the words are not).

**Hook:** the annotation hand matches the pencil corrections on one of the Archivists' three typescripts of the Rusk

**Alternative:** the annotations are in two hands. The first, careful and dry, writes the numbers. The second, written
later and harder, adds only the last line of each note: *Go and look. So does she.* Two people have held this card, years
apart.

### 7.1 "It'll rain": the Dry Mother at the Dry Font, a Wet Sabbath

*Taken down by a Catcher on the landing of floor 30, at the cut tap, on the Wet Sabbath of the Fourth Month, Y86, and copied
around the Fonts. Agnes Moll spoke for about six minutes, sitting on the stair. It had not started raining yet.*

> Sit down, the young ones. The stair's dry. It won't be for long.
>
> I want to tell you about the gutter today. Not the rain. You know about the rain.
>
> When they built this place they put a gutter round the top of it. Steel. Round the whole roof. And from the gutter, they
> ran pipes down the inside walls, all the way down, and on every tenth landing they put a tap, and on the cap of the tap
> they cast one word. You've all seen it. It's under my hand. RAIN. They didn't cast HALDEN on it. They didn't cast WATER.
> Water is what they sell. They cast RAIN, because in Y0 nobody thought anyone would need telling what rain was.
>
> They did it because it was cheap. I want you to remember that, the young ones. They didn't do it because they loved us.
> Rain was cheaper than pumping. Clean workers were cheaper than sick ones. So for fifty-seven years, if you were wet, the
> rain came down the inside of this tower and out of a tap with its name on it, and nobody paid, and nobody thought it was a
> miracle. It was plumbing.
>
> And then it was theirs. In one afternoon. A man with a cutting torch walked down from 89 to 10 and put a weld on every
> tap. I know it was one afternoon because my sister heard him coming down the stair, tap, tap, tap, landing by landing.
>
> Nineteen rings on this one. Count them, Ilse, you're nearest. Nineteen times they've closed it and nineteen times it's
> been opened. Every ring's a person who sat where I'm sitting with a file.
>
> I'm not going to tell you to hate anybody. The people up there have never waited for rain in their lives. You can't hate
> somebody for not knowing a thing. You can pity them. You can take the overflow off their roof on a storm night when it
> would flood their garden anyway. That's not hate. That's drainage.
>
> The Arbiter said this year they cannot restrain the weather. You'll have seen it on the walls. People laugh. I don't
> laugh. It's the truest thing the Company has ever written down. Somebody in that office knows it. That's a start.
>
> Now. There's a cup for everyone. One. If you've had yours at the Font this morning, it's still one; God doesn't keep a
> register, but Wen Liao does.
>
> It'll rain.

(It rained at 14:20. The Catcher who copied the sermon has added the time at the bottom.)

**In play:** a Wet Sabbath is a forecast rain day (from Static or the lobby board). On that day the Church's Fonts open
twice and the Dry Font crowd needs vessels: a contract from the Church for "cups, any, 40" that pays in Rainmarks, and a
better one for "a 20 L carrier, sealed, down the R3 grate to 9" that pays in Font water (real-grade rain, which is the best
water in the tower). "That's not hate. That's drainage" is the Church's line on the Roofman (06a): helping the Church can be
the same act as helping a Crown garden.

### 7.2 *Nineteen Rings*: a hymn

*Sung at the Fonts, slowly, to the tune of an old Works safety chant (the Old Hands recognise it: it was the chant for
closing a valve). Bylaw 81 makes it noise. It is usually hummed.*

> *They cast a word upon the tap*
> *in Y-and-nothing, plain and true,*
> *and then they cast a weld on that,*
> *and we cut through.*
>
> *One ring for her who held the file,*
> *one ring for him who held the light,*
> *one ring for every waiting while*
> *it rained all night.*
>
> *Nineteen rings upon the brass,*
> *nineteen closings, nineteen days,*
> *and all the rain that had to pass*
> *above our ways.*
>
> *It is not made, it is not sold,*
> *it falls on lid and falls on stair.*
> *The roof is high, the roof is old.*
> *The sky's still there.*

The fourth verse is the one children learn first. "Falls on lid" is the Crown (the lid); "falls on stair" is the Church's
hope, not a fact.

**In play:** flavour on Rainmark items and on the Font as a place. A receiver tuned to Static on a Wet Sabbath plays a
hummed line. A Rain Church client signs slips with *the sky's still there*.

### 7.3 The washing of the dead

*The Church's funeral order, as a Fontkeeper says it. Copied on the back of a Halden Sanitary Services rate card (1.12), which
is where most copies are kept, on purpose.*

> **Before the Carry.**
>
> *(The body is laid on the floor of the lease, not on a bed. The floor is what they lived on.)*
>
> **Keeper:** Who brought rain?
> **Family:** We did. *(Or:)* The Font did. *(Or, if there is none:)* We brought what we had.
>
> **Keeper:** Rain is not made.
> *(The hands are washed. Then the face. Then the feet, which walked the stair.)*
>
> **Keeper:** What is not made cannot be sold.
> *(The water left in the cup is not thrown away. It is poured on the floor of the lease, where the person lived. The Lease
> Office calls this a "release of water outside the metering framework". The Church calls it paying the floor back.)*
>
> **Keeper:** What is given is given to all.
> *(A Rainmark is closed in the right hand.)*
>
> **All:** It fell on them once. It will fall on them again.
>
> *(Then the Carry. The stair, not the lift. Down, as far as the family can carry. The Church asks nothing about what
> happens after that. The Tide Folk walk the rest of the way.)*

**In play:** a death on the floor produces a small, unpaid contract chain: rain (or Font water), a Rainmark (Church token),
and candles for the Carry. Fulfilling it raises regard with the Church and the family's floor. The last line of the order
ties the Church to the Tide Folk's ebb burial (06a): the body walks down the tower and out onto the mud.

### 7.4 The catechism of the cup

*Questions and answers taught to children before their first wetting. Said fast, in a sing-song, the way children say
anything they know by heart.*

> *Who made the rain?* Nobody made the rain.
> *Who owns the rain?* Nobody owns the rain.
> *Who sells the rain?* Varne and Daughters, under licence.
> *Is that allowed?* It's allowed. It's not right.
> *What's the difference?* The Arbiter's on 77. Right's on the roof.
> *Where does the rain fall first?* On the lid.
> *Where does it fall next?* In the gutter.
> *Where does it fall after that?* In the bottle.
> *And after that?* Down the chute, with the neck snapped.
> *And after that?* *(the child holds out the cup)* Here.
> *How much do you take?* One cup.
> *Why only one?* Because it's not mine either.
> *What do you say?* It'll rain.

**In play:** children's lines for vent kid clients. "Because it's not mine either" is the Church's rule against hoarding,
which in game terms is why the Fonts never sell or stockpile, and why a player who sells Font water loses the Church for
good.

### 7.5 The Gutter Psalm

*Painted in blue on the wall of the stairwell at the Dry Font, floor 30, after A/86/0204 (the Gutter Case). Painted over by
the Company twice. The third time, the Company left it.*

> THE OFFICE NOTES IT CANNOT RESTRAIN THE WEATHER.
> — A/86/0204
>
> NEITHER CAN WE. THAT'S WHY WE'RE HERE.

**In play:** a background detail on the floor 30 landing, which the player passes on any trip up. Its third, unpainted state
is the sign that the Company has decided the Church is cheaper tolerated than fought.

## Part eight: Static

Transcripts were taken down by listeners (paper is cheaper than recording), traded, and in some cases sold to the
Archivists. Static's sign-off for thirty years has been Lorca Venn's: *That's Static. Turn it down if you can hear boots.*

*Morning broadcast, Seventh, the Fourth Month, Y86. Dial talks fast; this is roughly verbatim.*

**In play:** the receiver item (1x1). Each morning at 05:00 the player gets four lines like these: which chute trunk is

## Part nine: the Tide Folk

The Tide Folk write little down. What they say is short, practical and told with the tide in mind: a saying has to be
finished before the water comes back.

### 9.1 Sayings of the berths

*Collected on the Flats and at the Landing. Some are rules; some are jokes; most are both.*

- **"The moon keeps the book. Halden keeps a copy."** Everything the Company has, it got by writing down what was already true.
- **"Two lows a day, and neither of them is you."** Said to someone feeling sorry for themselves: the water goes lower than
  you ever will, twice daily, and comes back.
- **"Sell on the ebb, buy on the flood."** On the ebb the trader is in a hurry to get out on the mud, so sell to him; on the
  flood he is in a hurry to get off it, so buy from him.
- **"Never dive your own street."** The kin-house rule.
- **"First find's the Bay's."** Throw back the first thing you find each ebb.
- **"A tired magnet's an honest magnet."** If it comes up empty three times, rest it. If a person comes back empty three
  times, believe them.
- **"The Nail doesn't float."** The tower people think they are above the water. The tower is a nail driven into it.
- **"Drys count in squares. Wets count in hours."** Tower life is space; Flats life is time between tides.
- **"Mud remembers."** What you dumped comes back. What you buried comes back. Said by people who bury their dead in it.
- **"Dry man's rope, wet man's knot."** A tower person gives you the materials; you'll have to do the work that makes them
  hold.
- **"The hoist charges by weight. So does grief."** The Landing hoist charges by the kilo; bringing your dead up costs, so
  you don't.
- **"Every barnacle's a reckoner."** Look at the line on the Stack's base; it knows the tide better than any slate.
- **"Neap's for nets."** On slack days, mend. Don't be idle and don't be greedy.
- **"Halden owns the mud. The mud doesn't know."** The vesting clause, said with a shrug.
- **"They sold us the sea twice: once when they let it in, once when they made us buy it clean."**
- **"Count the gulls, not the coins."** Gulls gather where the outfall brings food; where the food is, the salvage is shallow.
- **"Wet hands, clean name."** On the Flats, a person who works the mud is honest by definition; a middleman is not.
- **"The tide's never late and never early. Be like the tide or be like rent."** Be dependable, or be cruel. Those are the
  choices.
- **"Don't sell the Bay's share."** Never make money from what should be given back; also, never sell the dead's things.
- **"Up the hoist, down the price."** Anything you sell up the tower loses value at every hand it passes.

**In play:** these are the voice of Flats slips (cords with shells and coloured threads, `06a-factions.md`). "Sell on the
ebb, buy on the flood" is a real rule the game can use: Flats contracts posted before low tide pay better for speed;
salvage offered as the tide turns is cheaper.

> *They kept the wall with eleven men*
> *(the Bay keeps book)*
> *and wrote "refused" and wrote again*
> *(the Bay keeps book)*

> *and then the lagoon came walking through.*
>
> **The Bay keeps book, the Bay keeps book...**
>
> *My mother's house was on Rope Street*
> *(the Bay keeps book)*
> *I'll never dive it, never eat*
> *(the Bay keeps book)*

**In play:** the song is a background sound of the Flats and the Landing. The "Rope Street" verse is the kin-house rule
taught by singing. The magnet verse is the Bay's share. A Flats client who signs a slip with *the Bay keeps book* has a
grievance with the tower and will pay the player in salvage, never in Ledger credits.

### 9.4 The trader's call at the Exchange steps

*The call the Flats traders use to open the Great Ebb market, shouted from the top step when the water clears the bottom

> *Water's off the steps!*
> *Wet goods for dry money!*

> *nothing from the water in the mouth,*
> *nothing from your kin's house in the sack,*

**In play:** the Great Ebb market is a twice-monthly vendor event (chits and barter only, no Ledger). "No thumb" means no
rounding against you. It is the only market in the game where the player's prices come out exact.

## Part eleven: letters between floors

Letters go by drone (a few cents in a shared bay, or free in your own), by Old Two (the Guild carries post at freight rate,
and reads it, people assume), by chute mail (down only, folded into a cup, landing in a stranger's catch), or by vent kid
(fast, 1 cr, and the kid can't read, or says so).
