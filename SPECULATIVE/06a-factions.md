# Factions, part one

*Speculative. Rocks and soil. Everything here is a proposal unless `00-canon-brief.md` already decided it.*

Ten groups in this file: the Rain Church, the Tide Folk, the Mills Co-op, the Old Hands, the Archivists, the Lift Guild,
the shaft rats, the Stinkers, the seed savers and the insect ranchers. The other ten (sublet barons, hot-bunk houses, the
Foundation, plasma buyers, Static, penthouse fixers, the insurance syndicate, vent kids, the Voided, Flats divers) live in
`06b-factions.md`; they show up here only where they touch these ten.

---

## How a faction reaches a workshop on floor 17

Nobody in the Stack joins a faction the way you join a club. You start buying water from someone, then you start carrying
for them, then one morning there is a slip on your board that only people they trust get to see. Factions are relationships
that have hardened into habits, and the game should feel like that: you drift into them through trade.

### Standing is just a ledger somebody else keeps

The Company already keeps a number on you: the **credit rating**. It rises when you deliver on time and falls when you miss
rent. The cleanest way to run factions is to treat every group as a smaller, sloppier version of the same thing: someone,
somewhere, is keeping a ledger about you.

- The Company's ledger is a number on a screen, and it gates which contracts the board shows you.
- The Mills Co-op's ledger is an actual ledger, kept by a treasurer in pencil, with your dues and your water draws in it.
- The Lift Guild's ledger is the Ride Book: who rode, who paid, who was seen loading a drone at a shaft hatch.
- The Rain Church does not keep a ledger at all, which is the point, but the Fontkeepers remember faces.
- The Tide Folk count in knots on a cord.

**In play:** each faction has a standing from *Hostile* through *Wary*, *Known*, *Trusted* to *Kin*. Standing does three
jobs, the same three the credit rating does: it decides which of that faction's slips reach your board, what price they
give you, and what they send back in the drone's bay. One number per faction, visible on a single ledger page; nothing
hidden. Doing work for one group often costs you with another (carrying Font water down the free shafts warms the Rain
Church and cools the Lift Guild), and those trade-offs are listed in each entry below.

### Tokens: credentials you pack

The tower runs on paper and tin. Most factions hand out a physical token, and tokens are **items**: they take a square,
they sit on your shelf or in your pocket, and some of them do something when packed into a drone's bay next to a slip,
exactly as the Company's permits do. This ties faction standing to the core action (packing) instead of a menu.

| Faction | Token | Footprint | What packing it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rain Church | **Rainmark** (a blue enamel cap off a dead rain tap) | 1x1 | A Font accepts the drone; Church slips pay in water |
| Tide Folk | **tide cord** (knotted twine, one knot per good trade) | 1x2 | The Landing crews unload you first on a short ebb |
| Mills Co-op | **Co-op tag** (stamped tin) | 1x1 | Co-op patrols vouch for the drone on Mills floors: fewer losses |
| Old Hands | **clock badge** (a brass works badge with a number) | 1x1 | An Old Hand accepts a repair job by drone |
| Archivists | **reader's card** (index card, punched per visit) | 1x1 | Archive slips appear; documents ship to you unsealed |
| Lift Guild | **freight chit** (punched card, single use) | 1x1 | The drone rides a lift car: slow, safe, no shaft rolls |
| Shaft rats | **rat-chalk** (a stub of yellow chalk) | 1x1 | The free shaft's trouble roll is softened for that flight |
| Stinkers | **taster** (a stoppered sample vial) | 1x1 | Proves your water is drinkable; Stinker buyers pay full |
| Seed savers | **lending packet** (a paper seed envelope with a family name) | 1x1 | A saver slip appears; returning it raises standing |
| Insect ranchers | **herd ticket** (a carbon-copy delivery ticket) | 1x1 | Orrin depots or wild-stock buyers accept bulk insects |

**Alternative:** tokens never do anything mechanical and are just keepsakes that raise standing when returned. Simpler,
but loses the "a credential is cargo" idea, which is the part that makes standing physical.

**In play:** losing a token matters. A token packed into a drone that is looted in a free shaft is gone, and with it,
perhaps, the good opinion of whoever gave it to you. That gives tokens a reason to ride the licensed lanes, which costs
money, which is the point.

### Slips look like where they come from

The brief already says slips are styled by origin. Factions add a second layer: the same floor can send a slip in very
different hands. A Rain Church slip is written on the back of a Skywater label. A Lift Guild slip is a printed form with a
Guild stamp and three carbon copies. A Tide Folk slip is not paper at all: it is a knotted cord with a shell tied to it,
and a vent kid says the words out loud. A shaft rat slip is chalk on a scrap of hatch plate. If slips have a look, the look
tells you who you are dealing with before you read a word.

## The Rain Church

> "It fell on all of us once. Now it falls on them, and they sell it back to us by the half-litre. We are not asking for
> anything. We are asking for weather."
> (the Dry Mother, at the Dry Font, floor 30)

### Origin: the Capping

When the Stack was built in Y0 it was built well, and one of the things built well was the rain. The founding engineers
put a catchment on the roof (the **Founders' Gutter**: a ring of steel troughs around the crown of the tower) and fed it
through settling tanks into downpipes that ran the whole height of the building. On every tenth landing, from floor 90
down to floor 10, there was a **rain tap**: a brass fitting set into the stairwell wall with the word RAIN cast into its
cap, so that a worker's family could draw washing water without paying for it. The Works did not do this out of kindness.
Rainwater was cheaper than pumping, and clean workers got sick less.

The rain taps ran for fifty-seven years. After the Drowning, when the upper floors were cut into new leases and sold, the
roof went with them. In **Y61** Halden granted **Skywater** the *Roof Catchment Concession*. Skywater re-plumbed the
Founders' Gutter into its own risers down to its bottling hall in the Terraces, and the old downpipes were capped at floor
89. On the landings, the rain taps were welded shut. Most were not removed: removing them meant opening walls, and walls
cost money. So for twenty-five years people have been walking past a brass tap that says RAIN and does not run.

People kept putting cups under them. Nobody can say exactly when it became a church. The usual story is that on a rain
night in **Y63**, a former cistern fitter called **Dorota Kell** went to the dead tap on landing 30, cut through the weld
with a hand file over six hours, and found that the length of downpipe between the floor-89 cap and the floor-30 tap
still held about forty litres of old rain, trapped there since the Capping. She drew it off into whatever people brought
and gave it away until it ran dry. The landing on floor 30 is still called **the Dry Font**, and the cut tap is still
there, re-welded by Skywater, re-cut by the Church, re-welded again. The weld beads on it are layered like tree rings.
Members count them. There are nineteen.

**Alternative:** the Church did not start at a tap but at a funeral: a Mills family washed their dead with rain caught in a
window pan, an inspector fined them for "unauthorised abstraction", and the fine notice (pinned to the coffin as a joke)
became the first relic. Either origin works; both can be told by different members as the "true" one.

### What they believe

The creed is short enough to say in one breath and is said that way, quickly, by children:

> Rain is not made. What is not made cannot be sold. What is given is given to all.

- **Rain is not made.** Water from the tap is made: pumped, treated, metered, licensed. Rain is weather. Nobody did any work
  to make it, so nobody can own it. The Church is very precise about this. Members pay their meter bills without complaint,
  because Halden Pure is a made thing and a made thing has a price.
- **The roof is the Theft.** Not the tower, not the Company: specifically the roof. The Church is not revolutionary about
  anything except the gutter. This is what keeps it alive: HPS can tolerate people who want one pipe back.
- **The Crown is dry-hearted, not wicked.** People at the top are pitied as people who have never had to wait for rain.
- **Heaven is the Open Roof.** Not an afterlife, a plumbing diagram: the day the caps come off and the rain taps run on
  every tenth landing again.
- **What is given is not sold.** Members may trade, but may not take money for water, ever, and may not sell anything that
  "falls from you": blood, hair, tears, milk. This is the source of the Church's war with the plasma buyers (see below).

### What they do

- **The Catching.** On a rain day every member puts out a vessel: on a window ledge, in a light well, under a cracked
  duct. A Mills dormitory on a rain morning is all pans and buckets. Most of what is caught is a few spoonfuls of grey
  water that tastes of the facade, but it is caught, and that is the observance.
- **Tithing the Gutter.** The serious water comes from **tappers**, who drill Skywater's risers where they run down the
  outside of the Terraces, fit a bleed valve, and draw off a few litres an hour into carriers that go down the free shafts
  in drones. A good tap lasts days before Skywater's line-pressure alarms find it. A tapper caught is charged with
  "contamination of a licensed supply", which carries a heavier fine than theft because the bleed valve "could have let
  pathogens in".
- **The Fonts.** Hidden, unmetered taps in the Mills and the Sump, fed by tithed water carried down in jerrycans. A Font is
  usually a tank behind a false wall in a laundry, a stairwell cupboard, a hollow pillar. Anyone who comes gets one cup, no
  questions, once a day. A **Fontkeeper** lives next to each one.
- **The Wet Sabbath.** On a forecast rain day (forecasts are bought from the Sounding, and passed down by Static), the
  Church holds the Wet Sabbath: Fonts open twice, sermons are read at the Dry Font, children are given their first wetting
  (a cup of rain poured over the head; they are called "wet" afterwards, as in "he's been wet since he was four").
- **The Servants' Communion.** The Church's most valuable members are not in the Mills at all. They are maids, gardeners,
  pool cleaners and cooks who work in the Crown on day passes and go home to the Middle and the Mills at night. Up there,
  they are the only people in the tower who touch the Founders' Gutter. They open an overflow valve on a storm night, they
  leave a hose in a planter, they carry two litres down in a laundry bag. They are called the **Wet Servants**, and the
  Church protects their names above everything.
- **Funerals.** The dead are washed in rain if there is rain to be had, and in Font water if not. A Rainmark goes into the
  hand. The body goes down to the Sump like everyone else's.

### Structure

| Rank | Who | Number (rough) |
|---|---|---|
| **The Dry Mother** (or Dry Father) | the leader; takes a vow to drink only given water | 1 |
| **Elders of the Downpipe** | one per surviving downpipe stack (seven of the original twelve still hold water) | 7 |
| **Fontkeepers** | keep a Font; hand out the cup | about 20 |
| **Tappers** | drill risers; skilled, few, often arrested | a dozen |
| **Wet Servants** | Crown staff members; names known only to Elders | unknown, perhaps 40 |
| **Catchers** | ordinary believers | a few thousand, mostly Mills and Sump |

### People

- **Agnes Moll, the Dry Mother.** Seventy-eight. Took the vow at fifty-three and has not drunk a drop of metered water
  since, living on Font cups and gifts. She is thin as wire and coughs from the Sump air, and she still climbs to the Dry
  Font every Wet Sabbath (twenty floors of stairs, because the Lift Guild once searched her bag for water and she has
  never forgiven them). She is not a firebrand. She negotiates with HPS sergeants, she knows which inspector takes what, and
  she will give up a Font to save a tapper. Her line on everything is "it'll rain".
- **Tomas Reyes, called Tap.** Elder of the Downpipe for stack 3. Fifties, a licensed pipefitter in the Middle by day. He
  designed the bleed valve the tappers use (a clamp-on saddle with a needle drill, which Halden inspectors call a "Reyes
  saddle" in their reports without knowing why). He has a credit rating good enough for a Terraces contract and risks it
  every week.
- **Wen Liao, Fontkeeper of floor 12.** Runs a laundry, *Liao's Wash & Press*, with a 200-litre Font behind the dryer
  wall. She is not very religious. She took the Font because the laundry already used a lot of water and nobody would
  notice the meter. She knows every Mills family by what their sheets smell like.
- **Corin Asch, the Roofman.** A gardener in the Crown, employed by a household on floor 97, and the Church's best Wet
  Servant: on storm nights he opens the Founders' Gutter overflow so that the excess runs into an old downpipe instead of
  Skywater's riser. He has done it eleven times. Nobody in the Church below the Elders knows his name; they just say "the
  Roofman's been". **Hook:** someone in the Crown knows exactly what Corin does, and lets him, because the overflow protects
  their terrace from flooding. The Roofman thinks he is a saint; he is also free drainage.
- **Brother Mattias Grell.** Twenty-two, a Catcher from the Sump who wants the Church to stop asking. He talks about "the
  Burst": cutting Skywater's main riser in a storm so that the rain comes down the outside of the tower for everyone. The
  Elders keep him close so they can keep him quiet.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** short term, vessels, carriers and Fonts kept running; long term, the Open Roof.
- **Offer:** water, not for money. Raw rain (untreated, sooty, safe to wash with, a gamble to drink unboiled) and Font water
  (tithed Skywater, which is the best water in the tower, the same water the Crown drinks, given away in a laundry).
  Labour: Wet Servants carry things in and out of the Crown, which makes the Church a quiet back door for Crown leftovers.
- **Buy (by gift):** jerrycans, bottles, hose, clamps, needle drill bits, sealant, carbon filters, any vessel that holds water.
  They pay in water, in Rainmarks, in favours, and in the Wet Servants' laundry bags.
- **Sell:** nothing. The Church's whole economy is barter dressed as liturgy, and everyone involved knows it, and it works.

**In play:**
- **Raw rain** is its own grade of water: free, ungraded, needs boiling or filtering before a drinking contract accepts it,
  but fine for grow beds (it is actually better for plants than Halden Pure, which carries treatment salts). A grow-bed
  player has a reason to care about rain days.
- **Font water** is Skywater-grade water with no label. It passes any purity test. It cannot be sold to a licensed buyer
  without a Skywater label, which is where the Voided come in (relabelling; see 06b).
- **Rain days** are a world event. The Sounding forecasts them a day ahead. On a rain day: Church slips flood the board, the
  Fonts pay double, HPS raids are more likely, Skywater's Middle price dips because the bottling hall is full, and anyone
  with a catch container on a light well gets a few free litres.
- **A Font of your own.** At *Trusted*, the Church offers to put a Font in your workshop: a 2x3 tank that takes squares
  (and rent), fills with tithed water every rain day, and must give one cup an hour to whoever comes. Visitors are
  customers: Font days bring foot traffic past your shelves. An inspector visit with a Font on the field is a fine and a
  standing hit with the Company; hiding it behind shelving (a packing problem) halves the chance.

### Example slips

1. **Vessels for the Catching.** *Written on the back of a Skywater label.* "Eight vessels, two litres or more, to the
   Font at 12 (Liao's) before the fourth bell. Forecast is rain." Pay: 0 cr. Return: one Rainmark, and 10 L raw rain the
   morning after. Destination floor 12; any route.
2. **Fill the Font.** "Twenty litres, any water you can spare, to the Font at 12." Pay: 0 cr. Return: Church standing,
   and the Font remembers you (a Font you filled lets you draw back half on a dry day).
3. **The Laundry Run.** "A laundry bag to the service door on 94. Lane permit inside. Bring the bag back." Pay: 30 cr. The
   bag goes up with a real Crown laundry-service permit (packed in the bay, it clears the lane). It comes back heavier: a
   folded tablecloth around four litres of Founders' rain. Risk: a lane inspection opens the bag.
4. **The Reyes saddle.** "Two needle bits, size 3, and a clamp. Floor 73 by free shaft, drop at the bird spikes." Pay:
   2 Rainmarks. Risk: high. It is a shaft run into the Terraces, and the drop point is a ledge.
5. **Ten for the Sump.** "Ten litres boiled water to the Font at 6. They have had none for two days." Pay: 0 cr. Return:
   a standing jump. The board shows this slip only if the Font at 6 really has run dry, which happens when HPS raids it.

### How they treat the player

They do not care about your credit rating, your floor, or your money. They notice whether you give. At *Wary* you get
asked for vessels. At *Known* you get Font cups for free and the Fontkeepers greet you by name. At *Trusted* you get the
Laundry Run and a Font of your own. At *Kin* you learn which Crown households have a Wet Servant, which is information
worth more than any water, and which the Church will never forgive you for selling.

If you sell water at a profit, they will still talk to you. If you sell water to a plasma buyer's clinic, or sell blood
yourself, the Fontkeepers stop greeting you.

### Relations

- **Halden and Skywater:** a running fight, with rules. Skywater's legal language is "unauthorised abstraction" and
  "contamination of a licensed supply". Skywater pays floor wardens 50 cr per confirmed Font. HPS raids a Font, seals it,
  fines the Fontkeeper, and a month later the Font is back behind a different wall. Neither side wants it to stop being a
  game; a real crackdown would cost Skywater more in patrols than it loses in rain.
- **The Stinkers:** practical allies, theological enemies. The Church buys Stinker stills to boil raw rain and refers the
  thirsty to Stinker sellers, but holds that selling water is the one sin. Stinkers call the Church "the free-cup people",
  not kindly, because every free cup is a sale they didn't make.
- **The Tide Folk:** cousins who don't quite trust each other. The Tide Folk think rain is just weather and the sea is the
  real god. But the Flats are the thirstiest place there is, and the Church runs one Font on the Flats Landing, which the
  Tide Folk use and never thank it for.
- **The Mills Co-op:** the Co-op's water Pool accepts Font water and asks no questions. The Co-op's cautious wing wants
  distance, because Font raids bring HPS onto Co-op floors.
- **The Old Hands:** Hollis Brandt (see Old Hands) fitted half the rain taps as an apprentice in Y38 and knows where the
  downpipes run behind the walls. The Church has been asking him to draw it for ten years.
- **The Archivists:** hold **Drawing W-104, Rainwater Downpipe Schedule (Rev. C)**, the founding drawing that shows a rain
  tap on every tenth landing. The Church wants it as scripture. The Archivists want 800 cr. Neither side moves.
- **The Lift Guild:** hostile. Skywater pays the Guild to search bags for unlabelled water on the cars. The Dry Mother
  walks.
- **Shaft rats:** carriers of choice. The Church pays rats in water, which rats like better than credits.
- **Seed savers:** close. Raw rain grows heirloom seed better than treated water; the savers' hidden beds are often next to
  Fonts.
- **Insect ranchers:** little to do with each other. Ranchers need very little water.
- **Part two:**
  - **The Foundation** launches its *Clean Cup* campaign (a free Halden Pure voucher, redeemable at the company store) on
    every forecast rain day. Nobody believes this is a coincidence.
  - **Plasma buyers:** open contempt. Fontkeepers turn away anyone with a fresh plasma bandage. Members who sell blood
    are "dried" (shunned for a season).
  - **Static** reads the rain forecast and the Wet Sabbath sermon on air.
  - **Penthouse fixers** buy Font water in little stoppered bottles as "folk rain" for Crown dinner parties. The Crown pays
    to drink its own stolen rain back as a curiosity. The Church takes the money and pretends it was a gift.
  - **Sublet barons** rent window ledges by the hour on rain days ("catch squares").
  - **Vent kids** run Font messages and carry the Wet Servants' notes.
  - **The Voided:** the Church will use any vessel, VOID-slashed or not. A patched jerrycan holds rain as well as a new
    one.

### In the endings

- **The Open Roof.** A late-game chain: get Drawing W-104 from the Archivists, get Hollis Brandt to mark the live downpipes,
  get the Roofman to open the overflow on a storm night, and carry cutting gear to seven landings in one day. For three
  days the rain taps on every tenth floor run. Then the welders come. The ending text is the three days: people standing on
  landings with pans. **Alternative:** the welders don't come, because Skywater has calculated that re-welding seven taps
  costs more than the lost rain, and quietly rewrites the concession to call the rain taps "a community wellness service
  provided by Skywater".
- **Licensed.** The bleak version: the Church is offered a Halden water licence for its Fonts, the Dry Mother refuses, a
  younger Elder accepts, and the Fonts become "Certified Community Water Points" that charge half a credit a cup.

**Hook:** the Dry Font on floor 30 still drips. Not on rain days: every day, a few drops an hour, behind nineteen welds. The
downpipe above it should be dry. Something above floor 30 is feeding it.

**Hook:** the Dry Mother takes a cup of Halden Pure from her grandson's flat every night. She knows the vow; she also knows
she is seventy-eight. Whoever finds out has something on the most respected person in the Mills.

## The Tide Folk

> "Tide's on the turn. If you want to sell me something, sell it now. In two hours I'm standing in your price."
> (a Landing trader)

### Origin: the wall gangs

Calder had a seawall long before the Stack. When Halden Works opened in Y0, the Company took over its maintenance as part
of the land deal: Halden would keep the wall, and in return the city waived the Works' water abstraction fees. For forty
years the **Halden Seawall Section** employed around three hundred people, called the **wall gangs**: masons, pump men,
sluice keepers and tide watchers. They lived in **the Strand**, the low harbour district under the wall. They were never
housed in the Stack; outdoor workers didn't get tower leases.

The wall gangs kept a log called the **Wall Book**: every tide, every surge, every crack, every repair requested and every
repair refused. In the last fifteen years before the Drowning, as the ground under Calder sank from the Works' pumping, the
Book filled up with refusals. By Y50 the Section had been cut to forty people. By Y55 it was eleven. In Y57 the storm came,
the wall and the Works' slurry reservoir failed together, and the Strand went first.

The survivors had no lease and nowhere to go. They lived on rooftops in the Strand, then on rafts lashed to the rooftops,
then on the mud itself when they learned that at low water the old streets came up and could be walked, sifted and dived.
They were the only people in Calder who could read a tide, because reading tides had been their job. So the tide became
the clock of the Flats, and the people who could read it became the people who decided things. That is the whole of how the
faith began: a work skill that became the only authority left.

### What they believe

- **The Bay is owed.** The ground under Calder was pumped out from beneath them; the sea came in to fill what was taken.
  The sea is not angry, it is collecting. This is said flatly, as fact, the way tower people say "rent's due".
- **The moon keeps the book.** Tides are the moon's accounts: what goes out comes back, to the minute. Of everything in their
  lives, the tide is the one thing that is never late and never lies, so it is the closest thing they have to a god.
- **The tower is the Nail.** Driven into drowned Calder to hold it down. People in it are **drys**; the Tide Folk call
  themselves **wet** (which overlaps with the Rain Church's "wet", to both groups' irritation).
- **Kin-houses.** You never salvage a house you knew before the Drowning. If you were born on Rope Street, you don't dive
  Rope Street. Other people's streets are fine.
- **The Bay's share.** The first thing you find on each ebb goes back into the water. In practice this is a sound rule for
  avoiding fights about who saw what first: nobody keeps the first find, so nobody rushes for it.

### What they do

- **The Reckoning.** Each berth (family or crew) has a **reckoner**, who keeps the berth's tide slate: a board with the
  day's two lows and two highs chalked on it, worked out from the Wall Book's tables and corrected by eye against the
  barnacle line on the Stack's base. Reckoners argue about minutes. The minutes matter.
- **The Great Ebb.** Twice a month, at new and full moon, spring tides pull the water further out than usual and the deep
  streets of old Calder show: the Corn Exchange steps, the tram depot, the fire station yard. The whole Flats walks out. A
  market sets up on the **Exchange steps** for the four hours of low water, the only market in Calder Bay held on dry
  ground. Tower middlemen come down on the Landing's hoist to buy.
- **Magnet rest.** A magnet that comes up empty three casts running is "tired" and must be laid on a roof for a day. (A
  magnet that comes up empty three times is often fouled with mud; laying it in the sun dries and cleans it. The rule works
  for real reasons and is kept for religious ones.)
- **Ebb burial.** The dead are wrapped, weighted, and walked out on the lowest ebb of the month to a place only the
  reckoners know. The tide brings them in, and nobody says so.
- **Slack days.** At neap tide, when the range is smallest and the deep streets stay covered, nobody dives. Nets are
  mended, salt is boiled, children are taught the tables.

### Structure

The **berth** is the unit: a raft, stilt-house or roof shared by a family or a working crew, ten to thirty people, with one
reckoner. About sixty berths live on the Flats, perhaps a thousand people in all. The berths' reckoners sit as **the Tide
Table** at each Great Ebb, on the stump of the old Calder clock tower (**the Stump**), which stands clear of the water for
those four hours only. The Table settles disputes over salvage, sets the berths' shares of the Landing, and decides who
deals with the tower. Anything not settled before the water comes back waits two weeks.

### People

- **Hesper Quill, Eldest Reckoner.** Seventy-four. Her father was the last foreman of the wall gangs, and she has the
  Wall Book: eleven waterlogged ledgers, the last full page written three days before the Drowning ("Sluice 4
  refused again. Told to log it."), and a final entry in Oyelaran's hand on the 18th about gates TR3 and TR4. She has never let anyone copy it. She is not sentimental about it: she is waiting for
  someone to make her an offer that's worth what it cost.
- **Jory Malloch.** Twenty-three, a diver with lungs that should have given out years ago. He wants the Tide Folk to sell
  directly to the tower and cut out the Landing middlemen, and he talks to Static about it on air, which the Table finds
  undignified. He is the Tide Folk's most likely contact for a player who wants Flats goods without paying the Landing.
- **Ma Senna Duku.** Salt-boiler and midwife. She runs the brine pans on the roof of the old Strand post office, boils
  seawater over cell-heated plates three times over, and produces two salts: grey (cheap, heavy-metal-laced, for brining
  hides and curing nothing you'll eat) and white (re-crystallised, slow, safe, and sold as "Senna's" to anyone who knows to
  ask). She has delivered half the Flats under thirty.
- **Gull.** A boy of eleven, real name Teodor, who can read the next low within five minutes off the barnacle line on the
  Stack's base without the tables. The Table treats him like a reckoner already. **Hook:** Gull says the barnacle line is
  wrong: the high-water mark on the Stack's base is two hand-widths lower than it was in his grandfather's chalk marks. The
  water isn't rising. The tower is going down.
- **Ruben Vass.** Not Tide Folk, though he was born on the Strand. He runs the **Landing** (the steel platform chained to
  the floor-4 loading doors, floating at high water and hanging above the mud at low) and buys everything the Flats bring
  up at the prices he sets. The Tide Folk call him "the Lander" and use him because there is no one else. He pays the Lift
  Guild a share for the freight hoist and the Sump gangs a share for being left alone.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** water above everything. Then lamp cells, rope, sealant, waders, medicine, and protein blocks. And to be dealt
  with directly instead of through Vass.
- **Sell:**
  - **Salvage** from old Calder and the drowned plant (via the Flats divers, most of whom are Tide Folk, in 06b): scrap
    metal, cells, fittings, tableware, tools, the occasional founding-era part.
  - **Grey salt** and **Senna's white salt.**
  - **Dried wrack:** the brown weed that grows thick on the old seawall, rinsed and sun-dried. Not food (it holds the bay's
    metals). It is the best cheap packing filler in the tower: springy, light, takes any shape.
  - **Bay eels**, which nobody should eat and plenty of people do. Sold mostly to insect ranchers as bin feed.
  - **Tide tables**, which tell you when the Landing is reachable.
  - **Calder antiques**: pre-Drowning house goods (a clock, a teapot, a framed photograph of a street that isn't there).
    Penthouse fixers pay well for "pre-Drowning provenance".
- **Buy:** water, cells, rope, sealant, rubber, medicine, Vitabricks, needles and thread.

**In play:**
- **Tide windows.** Flats slips carry a tide window instead of a plain deadline: "Landing, low water, hours 10 to 13". A
  drone that arrives outside the window finds the Landing afloat and crowded or hanging over mud, and comes back with the
  cargo and nothing else. The tide moves about an hour later each day, so the window drifts through the day across a
  fortnight; on Great Ebb days it is long and deep; on slack days there are no Flats slips at all. A small tide strip in the
  corner of the day's clock would make this readable at a glance.
- **Wet salvage.** Salvage comes up wet. A wet item is worth less and cannot be analysed until it has dried (a drying rack:
  a machine with slots and hours). This is one item in two states, exactly the kind of depth the icon set favours.
- **Wrack as packing.** A bundle of dried wrack can be torn into pieces that fill odd gaps in a drone bay. Fragile goods
  packed with wrack around them survive a free-shaft rough landing. A bay is a puzzle; wrack is the putty.
- **The tide cord.** Every good trade adds a knot to your cord. At five knots you get *kin-price* at the Landing, a fifth
  off. A cord with one knot is an introduction; a cord with twenty is respect.

### Example slips

*Tide Folk slips arrive as a cord with a shell tied to it, delivered by a vent kid who says the words.*

1. **Low-water drop.** "Fifteen litres, four bricks, the Landing, on the ebb (hours 10 to 13)." Pay: 25 cr and a wet
   salvage lot (2x3 bundle, contents unknown). Destination: floor 4 (the Landing). Route: free shaft down; the Sump's shafts
   are rough.
2. **Reckoner's light.** "Two charged cells for Quill's berth before dark." Pay: 12 cr, one knot.
3. **Great Ebb stall.** "Bring goods to the Exchange steps for the Great Ebb. Four hours." You don't send a drone; you pack a
   crate, it goes down on Vass's hoist (Guild toll), and you get a market screen at low water: sell to Flats buyers in
   barter, buy salvage before the middlemen do. Late cargo is stranded by the flood.
4. **Senna's salt.** "Two kilos white salt, up to 41, licensed lane, sealed." Pay: 60 cr. It's going to a Middle charcuterie
   that sells it as "artisan sea salt". It is.
5. **The Wall Book.** At *Kin* only: "Quill wants a copy of the Wall Book made. Bring paper, ink and somebody who can copy."
   This is the start of a chain that involves the Archivists (see below).

### How they treat the player

Wary by default: the player is a dry. The Tide Folk test you with small trades and remember to the minute whether you were
on time. They never haggle on a flood tide (it's bad luck; also, they've got somewhere to be). Once trusted, they are the
most loyal trading partners in the game: they'll hold the first pick of a Great Ebb for you. If you ever sell a kin-house's
goods knowingly (a fixer may ask you to), the berth will not deal with you again.

### Relations

- **Halden:** Halden claims salvage rights over old Calder under the **Calder Recovery Licence** and runs a licensed
  salvage barge (the *Recovery 2*, a converted dredger) on the deep streets. Its crew are paid by weight and are not Tide
  Folk. The Company calls Flats salvage "unlicensed recovery"; it rarely enforces this because Flats salvage keeps the
  Landing supplied and the Landing pays Company freight fees.
- **The Rain Church:** the Church runs a Font on the Landing. The Tide Folk drink from it and grumble about the hymns.
- **The Mills Co-op:** almost no contact. The Co-op buys wrack for packing and mulch.
- **The Old Hands:** the wall gangs were Halden employees too. Their pensions stopped with everyone else's in Y62, and the Old Hands' **Pension Book** (see below) lists wall gang clock numbers. Hesper Quill is owed eleven years
  of her father's pension. She does not expect it.
- **The Archivists:** want the Wall Book more than anything. It is the only first-hand record of repairs refused. Quill
  will not give it to them; she might sell it.
- **The Lift Guild:** owns the freight hoist at the Landing. Every Flats crate going up pays the hoist toll. The Guild
  charges by weight; wet salvage is heavy.
- **Shaft rats:** the Landing is the bottom of every free shaft. Rats deliver there on the ebb.
- **The Stinkers:** the Flats are their poorest and most loyal customers. Some Stinkers live on the Flats and run solar
  stills on raft roofs.
- **Seed savers:** **Hook:** a seed jar came up from a drowned Strand kitchen garden last year, sealed with wax, labelled in
  a dead woman's hand. The seed savers want it. Its finder's berth holds it as the Bay's share and won't say where.
- **Insect ranchers:** buy eels and wrack as bin feed.
- **Part two:**
  - **Flats divers** are mostly Tide Folk; "tower divers" who come down from the Sump to dive the plant are not, and the
    berths treat them as poachers.
  - **The Voided:** VOID-stamped goods go down the chute, out the Sump outfall, and wash up on the wrack line. The Tide Folk
    pick them out first. They are the bottom link of the Voided's supply chain and are paid least for it.
  - **Penthouse fixers** buy Calder antiques and pay best for kin-house goods, which is exactly what the Tide Folk won't sell.
  - **Plasma buyers** run a boat, called the **blood boat** on the Flats, that comes alongside berths on slack days.
  - **The Foundation** runs an "Outreach Launch" that hands out company-store vouchers on the Flats. The company store is in
    the tower; spending a voucher means paying Vass's hoist toll. Nobody misses the joke.
  - **Hot-bunk houses:** Tide Folk who go up to work day jobs sleep in hot-bunks in the Sump.
  - **Static** carries Jory's broadcasts and the tide tables.

### In the endings

- **Going wet.** The player gives up the lease and moves down: a raft on the Flats, a stall on the Exchange steps, life by
  the tide table. A real ending, framed neither as failure nor as escape: no rent, no meter, and no water rights.
- **The Wall Book.** The player brokers the Wall Book's sale (to the Archivists, to Static, or to **Vantage**, the rival
  tower's company, which would pay a fortune for proof that Halden's negligence drowned Calder). What it changes depends on
  the buyer; see the Archivists' endings.

**Hook:** the old Calder fire station bell can be heard under the water on certain spring tides. The Tide Folk say it
rings for the drowned. The divers say the fire station's bell is gone (it went to a Crown fixer years ago), and something
else down there is ringing.

## The Mills Co-op

> "Nobody's asking you to love your neighbour. We're asking you to put four litres in the Pool on Tuesdays."
> (Ben Books, treasurer)

### Origin: the Settlement, the Nine, and the clean rooms

When Halden automated the Works in **Y41**, the people laid off were not drifters. They were the members of **Local 9**,
the Cell Hall Local of the Halden Works union, eleven hundred assemblers who had worked the cell lines on floors 11 to 16.
Local 9 did the one thing a union can still do on its way out: it bargained over the corpse. The result was **the
Settlement of Y41**, a severance deal under which every laid-off member kept their Stack lease at frozen rent for fifteen
years, and the union's strike fund was released to the members as a mutual fund, which they called **the Fund**.

The fifteen years ran out in **Y56**. Rents jumped. A year later came the Drowning, and rents jumped again as the upper
floors were re-cut and the whole price ladder shifted down. By then the halls on 10 to 29 had stood empty for sixteen years:
stripped cell lines, clean rooms with their doors propped open, conveyor tracks running nowhere. Halden began leasing them
as workshop space in **Y58**, cheaply, by the square.

The Fund's last money went on one lease: half of the old clean room on floor 15. Clean rooms had sealed walls, their own
air handling (founding-era, still partly running) and floor drains, which made them the best room in the Mills for growing
things. Nine families who had been Local 9 members put the first grow beds in, pooled their water, and split the harvest. In
**Y60** they registered as a tenants' association (legal) under the name **Mills Growers' and Workers' Co-operative**. Everyone calls it the
Co-op. The **Nine** are still named at every meeting: Brask, Sato, Okafor, Adair, Haan, Kowal, Mbeki, Petrosyan, Lund.

### Why "half-legal"

Halden tolerates the Co-op because the Co-op keeps the Mills fed and quiet, and because its members pay rent on time more
reliably than anyone else in the band. But three of its core habits break Company rules:

1. **The Pool** is unmetered water transfer between leases. Every tenant's meter is supposed to be the only way water enters
   a lease.
2. **The Round**, the Co-op's night patrol, is unlicensed security. HPS holds the security concession.
3. **Greens chits**, the Co-op's produce vouchers, are an unlicensed currency.

The Company's position is written in the Tenancy Office's file on the Co-op: *"Association provides stabilising function.
Monitor. Do not escalate."* Every Co-op chair knows a version of that sentence exists, and that "do not escalate" can be
changed by one manager.

### What they do

- **The Pool.** A 4,000-litre steel tank (an old cell-rinse tank) on floor 15. Mills taps only run in tap hours. Members draw their
  spare allowance in tap hours, pour it into the Pool, and draw
  back by share in the day. Nobody makes money on it; it just moves water through time and between neighbours, so that a
  family with a dry week doesn't go thirsty and a grower with a thirsty crop doesn't go broke. The treasurer logs every litre
  in and out in pencil.
- **The Crib.** The old Works tool crib on floor 16: a long caged counter, steel shelves, a sliding hatch. Members borrow
  tools by the day and machines by the hour. The Crib does not make machines (nobody does); it owns about forty, bought or
  donated over twenty-five years, each one tagged with who gave it.
- **The Round.** Three nights a week, pairs of members walk the stairwells of the Mills floors with lamps and whistles. They
  don't fight; they witness. A burglar who knows he has been seen by two neighbours who know his mother usually doesn't
  come back.
- **The Split.** The chute delivers food waste every morning, and in the Mills two machines compete for it: insect bins
  (which turn it into protein) and compost (which turns it into grow-bed soil). The Co-op rule, voted in Y71 after a long
  fight, is that members split chute food waste half and half. Nobody keeps to it exactly; everyone argues about it.
- **The Swap.** Every seventh day, a table in the Assembly Hall on floor 19 where members trade chute finds: you got a
  bag of broken cell casings and your neighbour needs casings; she got a box of glass jars and you need jars.
- **The Floor.** The monthly general meeting, also in the Assembly Hall, the former cell assembly floor: two hundred people on
  folding chairs between the bolt holes where the lines stood. Votes are by raised Co-op tags.

### Structure and its split

The Co-op has a **delegate** for each Mills floor it has members on: floors 10 to 21 and 27 to 29 (22 to 26 are the Dark
Floors, behind the welds; nobody lives there). There is a **chair**, a **treasurer**, a **Crib keeper** and a **Round
captain**. About two thousand member households.

Under the surface there are two wings, named after the two rooms where they started arguing:

- **The Ledger** (named for the treasurer's book): moderates, mostly older, mostly growers. They want a **Mills Charter**: a
  collective lease from Halden on the whole band at a fixed rent, signed and binding. They think the Co-op survives by being
  useful to the Company.
- **The Hall** (named for the Assembly Hall): younger, mostly workshop tenants and insect ranchers. They want a **rent
  strike**: the whole Mills holding rent for a week, which would cost Halden more than it costs them. They think the Co-op
  survives only as long as the Company is afraid of it.

### People

- **Odile Brask, Chair.** Fifty-four. Granddaughter of the first Brask of the Nine; wears his Local 9 pin on her collar every
  day. Ledger wing. She has been negotiating the Charter with a Tenancy Office manager called **Mr. Pell Dorrance** for six
  years and has a folder of drafts thicker than her arm. Every draft is a little worse than the last. She keeps negotiating
  because the alternative is the Hall.
- **Benedek Sato, "Ben Books," Treasurer.** Sixty. Keeps the Pool ledger, the dues ledger, the Crib ledger and the Swap book
  in pencil, in a hand so neat that HPS once mistook a seized ledger page for a printed form. He has never taken anything.
  This makes him the one person every faction in the Mills trusts and every faction in the Mills fears, because he knows
  exactly who owes what.
- **Imani Okafor, "Rook," Round captain.** Thirty-one. Washed out of HPS training in her second year for "attitude" and came
  home to run the Round instead. Hall wing. She knows every stairwell, every crawlspace the vent kids use, and every warden on
  the Mills floors. She wants the strike.
- **Fenwick Adair, "Old Fen," Crib keeper.** Seventy-one, Clock 1880 (see the Old Hands). Keeps the Crib like a museum and
  lends from it like a pawnbroker: he knows what every machine is worth and who will bring it back.
- **Lasse Kowal, delegate for floor 21.** Forty. His floor's back wall is the welded bulkhead of the Dark Floors. He reports
  the hum at every Floor meeting, with dates and hours. Nobody quite listens.
- **Gaspard Mbeki, delegate for floor 13.** Fifty. Descendant of the Nine, a popular speaker, a moderate. **Hook:** he is also
  a paid floor warden, reporting Pool volumes and Round rotas to the Tenancy Office. He has been doing it for nine years. He
  tells himself it's what keeps "do not escalate" in the file.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** the Charter (Ledger) or the strike (Hall); to stop the sublet barons buying Mills leases; never to be hungry
  again.
- **Offer:** membership, the Pool, the Crib, the Round's protection, the Swap, a fair produce price, and the Co-op's name on
  your door (which makes certain inspectors less eager).
- **Buy:** produce (at a fixed "Floor price", lower than the Middle pays but always paid, never haggled), protein blocks for the
  Co-op's own stalls, compost and frass, repairs for the Pool pump and the Crib's machines, lamp cells for the Round.
- **Sell (at Co-op stalls, for credits or chits):** cheap greens, beans, protein blocks, compost, borrowed time on machines.

### Greens chits: the third money

The Company pays some wages in scrip, which spends only at the company store. The Co-op pays some of its prices in **greens
chits**: printed paper slips (the Co-op owns one old platen press), each worth a set weight of produce at a Co-op stall.
Chits only spend at Co-op stalls. So a Mills family can hold three kinds of money, each of which works in one place:
credits (anywhere), scrip (the company store), chits (Co-op stalls). Nobody in the Co-op enjoys how much chits resemble scrip.
Halden calls chits "informal vouchers" and has not banned them because the Foundation's vouchers would have to be banned too.

**In play:**
- **Membership** costs dues: 5 cr a week, or a tenth of your produce by weight, paid into a Co-op crate on your field that a
  runner empties each week (a special container you must leave room for).
- **A Pool tap** is a machine you can put on your field once you're a member: it dispenses up to your share each day, which is
  set by what you've poured in. You're trading tower-hours (pour in tap hours) for daytime water. It costs a square.
- **Borrowing from the Crib:** a Crib slip arrives, you pick a machine, it comes up by drone (or you send for it) and sits on
  your field for N hours, then must go back. A way to try a machine before buying one, or to run a one-off batch.
- **Greens chits** appear as pay on Co-op slips. They're cheap to earn and only good for food and Crib time, which is exactly
  what a struggling early game needs and exactly what a later game outgrows.
- **The Round** lowers the chance of theft events on your floor (a shelf raided, a chute emptied before you sort it) as long
  as you pay dues. Lapsed members' floors are walked less.

### Example slips

*Co-op slips are cyclostyled forms with a hand-inked Co-op stamp: a sheaf of wheat over a cell.*

1. **Pool top-up.** "40 L to the Pool (15) by hour 18." Pay: 20 cr in chits, a Pool credit of 40 L.
2. **Greens to the Middle.** "30 heads of greens to Ambler's Grocers, floor 44. We have no lane permit. You do." Pay: 45 cr,
   half to you, half to the grower. Requires: a licensed lane permit in the bay (the Co-op uses members with good credit
   ratings as its outlet to the Middle).
3. **The pump.** "Pool pump has lost a bearing. It's a Works pump, Type 9. Bring a bearing or bring someone who knows." The
   bearing is a founding-era part: you need salvage (Tide Folk, divers) or an Old Hand. Pay: 80 cr, a Crib loan free for a
   week.
4. **Lamps for the Round.** "Six charged cells to Okafor, 18, by sundown." Pay: 18 cr.
5. **Hold your rent.** *A slip with no pay at all.* "The Floor has voted. Hold your rent from day 3 to day 10." The choice
   slip. Join: Company credit rating drops each day of the strike; Co-op standing jumps; if enough of the Mills holds (a
   world roll weighted by Co-op standing across the band, i.e. partly by how much you've helped), the Company cuts the Mills
   rent a step for a season. Refuse: Co-op standing drops to *Wary*, and the Hall remembers. Report the strike to the Tenancy
   Office: credit rating up, a Company reward, and you are now Mbeki.

### How they treat the player

The player is exactly who the Co-op is for: a small Mills tenant with a few squares and a lot of rent. At *Wary* you're a
neighbour who hasn't joined. At *Known* you're a member and get Pool and Crib access. At *Trusted* the Co-op routes its Middle
sales through you (you're the one with the permit), which is lucrative and makes you the Co-op's face to the Company. At *Kin*
you get a vote that counts at the Floor and a seat in the Charter talks.

The Co-op hates two things: members who sell their lease to a sublet baron, and members who become floor wardens. The first
gets you expelled; the second, if found out, gets your name read at the Floor, which in the Mills is worse.

### Relations

- **Halden:** "Monitor. Do not escalate." Charter talks with the Tenancy Office have been going for six years.
- **The Rain Church:** the Pool accepts Font water. The Ledger wing wants distance because Font raids bring HPS onto
  Co-op floors; the Hall wing thinks the Church is the only other group in the Mills with nerve.
- **The Tide Folk:** little contact. The Co-op buys dried wrack for packing and for mulch.
- **The Old Hands:** overlap heavily. Half the Old Hands are Co-op members; Old Fen runs the Crib; the Pool pump only runs
  because Pavel Lisko keeps it running.
- **The Archivists:** the Co-op has wanted the **original signed text of the Settlement of Y41** for decades. **Hook:** the
  copies the Co-op holds say the frozen rent ran fifteen years. Ben Books once saw a page of the original in the Archive's
  reading room, and he swears the clause said fifteen years *or until the reopening of the Works, whichever is later*. The
  Works never "reopened". It was never declared closed either; it just stopped employing anyone (see the Dark Floors).
- **The Lift Guild:** adversaries. The Co-op used to rent a whole lift car once a week for produce (the "Greens Car") until
  the Guild doubled the rate in Y78. The Co-op switched to drones and shaft rats, and the Guild has never forgiven it.
- **Shaft rats:** the Co-op is the rats' steadiest customer in the Mills.
- **The Stinkers:** official line, disapproval (the Co-op's Pool is Halden water, clean and legal). Unofficial practice,
  most members buy Stinker water in a dry week.
- **Seed savers:** the Hall wing shelters them. Several Co-op grow beds are planted with lent heirloom seed, which is the
  Co-op's single biggest legal risk (patent contamination; see the seed savers).
- **Insect ranchers:** half the wild-stock ranchers are Co-op members and drive the Hall wing. The Split is their fight with
  the growers.
- **Part two:**
  - **Sublet barons** are the Co-op's real enemy. Floors 28 and 29 have already gone: a baron called Mezzanine Living bought
    up the leases one by one and now rents them by the hour. The Co-op tracks every lease sale in the Mills on a chart in
    the Assembly Hall.
  - **Hot-bunk houses:** the Co-op runs one on floor 12 (*the Night Shift*), at cost, because if it didn't, a baron would,
    at a profit. Members are uneasy about it.
  - **The Foundation**'s vouchers undercut Co-op stalls: a free company-store protein voucher is a lost chit sale.
  - **The insurance syndicate** hates **the Box**: a Co-op mutual fund that pays out for lost drones at half value, which
    is half more than the syndicate ever pays.
  - **Plasma buyers:** the Co-op runs a small emergency loan, **the Blood Fund**, so that members never have to sell plasma.
    It's always empty by mid-month.
  - **Vent kids** are the Round's lookouts, paid in chits.
  - **Static** broadcasts the Floor meetings live.
  - **The Voided:** Co-op stalls sell repaired voided goods under the counter. The Ledger wing pretends not to know.
  - **Penthouse fixers** buy the Co-op's best produce and sell it in the Crown as "Mills heritage greens".

### In the endings

- **The Mills Charter.** The player's late-game weight (credit rating, Co-op standing, the Settlement document from the
  Archivists) tips the talks. Halden signs a collective lease on the Mills. Rent is frozen. The ending text reads the
  Charter's clauses, and the last one is a buyout option the Company can exercise in twenty years. Everyone signs anyway.
- **The Strike.** The Hall wins. A week-long rent strike, the Company's response (meters capped, lifts closed to Mills
  floors, HPS on the stairwells), and the outcome, which depends on how many floors hold. A good outcome is a rent cut. A
  bad one is evictions and barons.
- **Co-op of the Mills** (from `LORE.md`): the player's own workshop becomes a Co-op floor: you own nothing alone, you share
  everything, you are never evicted, and you never get rich.

**Hook:** if the Settlement's clause really says "until the reopening of the Works", then whether the Works is closed is
worth a lot of money. The Dark Floors are running. Is that a reopening?

## The Old Hands

> "It's not a relic. It's a Type 4 contactor and it's younger than me."
> (Pavel Lisko, Clock 2210)

### Origin: the last people who were taught

The founding-era machines of the Stack (the lifts, the meters, the air plant, the pumps, the Works' own lines) were never
documented for the people who used them. The Works' rule, printed on every locker door until Y41, was **"Documentation
stays in Document Control"**: manuals did not go home, drawings did not leave the floor. Workers learned machines from
other workers, by hand, over years. That was deliberate. A workforce that can only fix the machines in front of it can't
take its skills to a rival.

So when the Works automated in Y41, the knowledge of how the tower worked was spread across a few thousand heads, and it has
been dying ever since. In Y86 the youngest people who worked at the Works before the layoffs are in their sixties; the
oldest are near ninety. The **Old Hands** is what they call themselves, and what the Mills calls them: the people who were
there, who still have their clock badges, and who can listen to a pump and tell you which bearing is going.

They became a group in **Y62**, when the Works pension (paid in scrip since the layoffs) was "suspended pending recovery" after
the Drowning. They met to complain about the pension in the old Works canteen on floor 14, and they never left.

### The clock numbers

Every Works employee had a **clock number** stamped on a brass badge that clocked them in and out. Numbers were assigned by
department, which means a badge tells you a trade:

| Clock range | Department | What an Old Hand from here knows |
|---|---|---|
| 0000s | Clean rooms and chip fab | Founding-era chips and boards by sight and touch |
| 1000s | Toolroom and machine shop | Tolerances, bearings, how to make a part fit that wasn't made for it |
| 2000s | Building services: lifts, air plant | Lifts, fans, dampers, the air plant |
| 3000s | Utilities: water, power, meters | Pipes, pumps, the ultrapure plant, the meters |
| 4000s | Cell formation | Cells: charging, reconditioning, which ones will burn |
| 7000s | Seawall Section | Tides, sluices, pumps (most went to the Flats; see the Tide Folk) |

Lower within a range means earlier hire. Old Hands introduce themselves by number: "Lisko, twenty-two ten." The badge is
worn on a cord, polished, and taken to the grave.

### What they do

- **The Bench.** The canteen on floor 14, known simply as **the Canteen**: long tables, a tea urn that hasn't been off in
  twenty years, a stew of protein block and whatever greens the Co-op sends, and machines on the tables. Anyone can bring a
  founding-era thing to the Canteen and set it down. Whoever's sitting there looks at it. If it's in their range, they say what
  it is, what's wrong, and what it's worth. That's the Bench.
- **Listening.** An Old Hand puts a screwdriver handle to their ear and the blade on a housing and listens. The tower's
  machines make sounds; the sounds mean things.
- **Clock-signing.** A repair by an Old Hand is signed with their clock number, scratched inside the housing. Machines with
  numbers inside them sell for more; buyers know someone who knew what they were doing has been in there.
- **No writing.** Old habit. The Works forbade taking documentation home; the Old Hands still don't write down what they
  know, partly from habit, partly because knowledge is the only thing they own, and partly from spite. This is the centre of
  their quarrel with the Archivists.
- **The Pension Book.** A ledger kept by the Canteen, listing every Works employee owed pension since Y62, with the amount.
  2,340 names. 412 alive. The Book is taken to the Arbiter once a year and the case is "adjourned for recovery" once a year.

### People

- **Ruth Amadi, Clock 0412.** Eighty-four, clean-room technician from Y20 to Y41. Nearly blind now. She identifies chips by
  running her thumb over the pins and smelling the board. She is the only living person who has stood inside the clean rooms
  that became the Dark Floors while they were running. **Hook:** she says the Dark Floors aren't making cells and chips. "The
  cycle's wrong. A formation cycle is forty minutes. That's nineteen. Whatever's in there, it's not what we made."
- **Pavel Lisko, Clock 2210.** Sixty-eight, lift mechanic. Was a Lift Guild member until Y64, when he refused to sign the
  Guild's petition against drone couriers ("a lift is for people; let the boxes fly"). The Guild expelled him. He now repairs
  drone motors and keeps the Co-op's Pool pump alive. He accepts Halden consultancy work in the Crown, paid in scrip, and
  doesn't apologise for it.
- **Hollis Brandt, Clock 3307.** Seventy-three, meter fitter and plumber. As an apprentice in Y38 he fitted rain taps on the
  landings (see the Rain Church). Later he fitted water and power meters on every floor from 10 to 59. He knows how a meter
  logs and how a meter can be made to forget an hour. He has never done it for anyone. He knows that the day he does it once,
  everyone will want it. He refuses all Company work.
- **Desmond Achterberg, "Dez," Clock 4019.** Seventy-six, cell formation. Drinks Stinker spirit and tells stories at the
  Canteen until he's carried home. He worked the formation halls on floors 22 to 26. His toolroll and his good boots are in
  his locker on floor 23, behind the welds, where he left them on the day of the layoffs, because the foreman said they'd be
  back Monday.
- **Marguerite Osei, Clock 3112.** Seventy, from the Works' ultrapure water plant. Knows reverse-osmosis membranes, resin
  beds and how to tell when a membrane is spent. Teaches Stinkers on the quiet, which is why she won't be named on air and why
  inspectors visit her flat twice a year.
- **Fenwick Adair, "Old Fen," Clock 1880.** Seventy-one, toolroom. Runs the Co-op's Crib (above).

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** the pension (they don't expect it); to be asked, and paid, rather than used; and, more than they admit, someone
  to tell it to before they die.
- **Offer:**
  - **Repairs** to founding-era things nobody else can repair, slowly, by hand, signed.
  - **Identification**: what a relic is, what's wrong, what it's worth. They are a living analyser.
  - **Teaching**: an Old Hand can show you a setting on your own machine you didn't know it had.
  - **Authentication** of founding-era parts, which the penthouse fixers need and pay for.
- **Buy:** Vey heart pills and painkillers (expensive), tea, Stinker spirit, good hand tools, magnifiers, lamp cells,
  hearing-aid cells. They pay little, in credits and in scrip from their consultancy, and a lot in favours.

**In play:**
- **The Bench as an analyser.** Packing a relic into a drone with a clock badge sends it to the Canteen. It comes back after
  some hours with its hidden features revealed, one per Old Hand who had a look, and a chalk note on the housing. An Old Hand
  in the right range reveals more (a 4000s for a cell, a 0000s for a board). Faster and cheaper than owning an analyser,
  slower and less reliable than owning one.
- **Repair by drone.** A broken founding-era machine (too big for a drone) needs an Old Hand to come to you: a slip brings
  them, they occupy a 1x2 stool-and-toolbox spot on your field for a day, they eat from your stock, and the machine is fixed
  or gets a clock-signed note listing the part it needs.
- **Settings unlocked.** Machines in the game have hidden depth in their interfaces (a setting you choose). An Old Hand in
  your workshop can teach you one: "your still has a reflux valve, did you know?" After that, the setting is yours.
- **Clock-signed goods.** A repaired item with a clock number inside sells for more to the Middle and the Terraces. The
  number is visible on the item as a small mark.
- **The apprentice.** At *Kin*, one Old Hand offers to take you on. A week of slips, one per day, each a small job, each
  teaching a thing. At the end, you get your own clock-number-style mark, and the Canteen treats your repairs as theirs.

### Example slips

*Old Hands' slips are handwritten on the back of old Works forms (time sheets, goods-in dockets) with a clock number in
the corner instead of a name.*

1. **Bring it to the Bench.** "Bring it. — 0412." Pay: the reveal. Cost: a tin of tea in the bay.
2. **Pills for Ruth.** "Thirty Vey cardio, monthly, to 14." Pay: 40 cr, which doesn't cover Vey's price (60 cr). A choice:
   lose 20 cr, or source **voided** repacked pills from the Voided for 25 cr (the Old Hands know and don't care; a brand
   inspector would care very much).
3. **A Type 9 bearing.** "Pool pump. Type 9, 32 mm. Don't bring me a new one, there aren't new ones. — 2210." Pay: 30 cr, and
   Lisko comes to look at your machines for a day.
4. **The Pension Book.** "Carry the Book to the Arbiter's office, floor 77, by licensed lane. Wait for the receipt." Pay:
   15 cr. It comes back with a receipt stamped *ADJOURNED PENDING RECOVERY*. Every year.
5. **Dez's locker.** *Not a slip; something Dez says at the Canteen.* "My toolroll's in locker 231, floor 23. Good roll. Toolroom
   steel. Whoever brings it, it's theirs." The Dark Floors are welded shut. Nobody goes in. But the vent kids say there's a
   crawlspace, and the shaft rats say there's a shaft that passes a broken grille on 23. **Hook**, never required: the
   drone-sized way in.

### How they treat the player

Gruffly. The Old Hands assume you are going to waste their time until you prove you won't. They like people who bring
the right thing, who don't call things "relics", and who come back. They don't care about your credit rating; they care
whether your machines are clean. At *Kin*, Ruth Amadi tells you what she heard through the Dark Floors' wall. The Old Hands
never ask you for money they need; you find out from someone else.

### Relations

- **Halden:** complicated. The Company owes them a pension and doesn't pay it; the Company also needs them, because nobody else
  can fix the Crown's founding-era lifts and air handlers, so the Tenancy Office quietly hires Old Hands as "heritage systems
  consultants" at day rates paid in scrip. Lisko takes the work. Brandt won't. Every Old Hand who takes Crown work is another
  year the Company doesn't have to train anyone.
- **The Archivists:** the great quarrel of the Mills. The Archivists have the paper: the drawings and manuals Document
  Control printed. The Old Hands have the understanding: what the drawings get wrong, what was changed on the floor and never
  drawn, which manual page is a lie. Each needs the other, and neither will give. The fight has a name: **the Red Binder**, a
  Works maintenance binder covered in Old Hands' handwritten margin notes, which Hollis Brandt kept against the rules and
  which the Archivists acquired from his late brother's flat. The Old Hands say it was stolen. The Archivists say it was
  bought. Brandt wants it back. The Registrar wants Brandt to annotate the rest.
- **The Rain Church:** Hollis knows where the downpipes run and won't draw it, because drawing it would be writing it down.
- **The Mills Co-op:** overlapping memberships; the Canteen is fed by the Co-op.
- **The Lift Guild:** Pavel Lisko is the Guild's one famous traitor. The Guild's older mechanics still call him when a car
  sticks.
- **Shaft rats:** Lisko fixes their motors. The rats bring the Canteen tea.
- **The Stinkers:** Marguerite Osei teaches them. Some Stinker stills have a 3000s clock number scratched inside.
- **Seed savers:** distant; some Old Hands keep window pots of lent seed.
- **Insect ranchers:** Dez's old formation halls are warm (residual heat from the Dark Floors next door), and the floor-21
  ranchers lease space against that bulkhead for it.
- **Part two:**
  - **The Foundation** runs a **Retiree Dignity Programme** offering Old Hands a monthly company-store voucher in exchange
    for signing a waiver of pension claims. Some have signed.
  - **Penthouse fixers** need Old Hands to authenticate founding-era pieces for Crown buyers; they pay a fee per signature, and
    the Old Hands know their signature is being used to sell things to the people who stopped their pension.
  - **The insurance syndicate** employs a former Old Hand, **Nils Ferreira, Clock 2077**, as its loss assessor. He finds
    "pre-existing fault" in every claim. The Canteen doesn't serve him.
  - **Plasma buyers** won't buy Old Hands' blood: too old, too many pills. Dez finds this funnier than anyone.
  - **Vent kids** fetch things from crawlspaces for the Old Hands, who pay in sweets and stories.
  - **Static** has Dez on air some nights, telling Works stories until he forgets the microphone is on.
  - **The Voided:** voided machines (snapped, stamped) are exactly what Old Hands are good at bringing back.
  - **Sublet barons** take over Old Hands' flats when they die, usually within the week.

### In the endings

- **The Last Apprentice.** Over the game, Old Hands die (scripted, not random, one per season or so). If you've been
  apprenticed, the Canteen's Bench passes to you: when the last one goes, you are where people bring machines. The ending
  text is a list of clock numbers, and yours at the end.
- **The Pension.** A long shot: with the Settlement document (Co-op), the Pension Book (Old Hands) and proof that the Works
  never formally closed (the Dark Floors), the Arbiter can't adjourn. What the Company pays is in scrip.
- **Written down.** The player brokers peace between the Old Hands and the Archivists, and the knowledge gets written. This is
  a quiet ending with a big effect: founding-era repairs stop being a mystery, and the price of founding-era parts drops for
  everyone, including the player's own stock.

**Hook:** the Works' clock-in machine on floor 10 still runs on a founding-era cell and still records every badge swiped
through it. Most nights, at 02:00, badge **0001** clocks in. Clock 0001 was the Works' first plant director, dead forty years.

## The Archivists

> "Paper outlives promises. That's why they hate it."
> (Imelda Vos, Registrar)

### Origin: the Lunch Tin Months

Halden Works kept its paper in **Document Control**, a sealed office on floor 28 with a counter, a hatch and four clerks
who signed every drawing in and out. When the Works automated in Y41, Document Control was ordered to box what head office
wanted (master drawings, contracts, patents) for the records vault in the basement, **the Vault** on floor 2, and to pulp
the rest: working copies, maintenance binders, superseded drawings, the shop-floor paper with sixty years of notes on it.

The pulping took eleven weeks. During those weeks, the clerks went home every night with their lunch tins full. A drawing
folds to the size of a sandwich. A binder comes apart into sections. A clerk called **Hanne Vos** took out, by her own
count, 4,100 sheets in 77 lunch tins, and stored them in her flat on floor 33 in the cavity under her bath. She was not the
only one. The Archivists call those eleven weeks **the Lunch Tin Months** and keep a list of the clerks who did it, most
of them now dead.

For sixteen years the stolen paper was just a few old women's hobby. Then the Drowning flooded the Vault. Every master
drawing, every original contract, every Company copy of how the Stack was built went under the water on floor 2. Overnight,
the lunch-tin copies were the only copies. When Halden needed to repair a founding-era pump, it had to come and ask.

### What they believe

- **Paper outlives promises.** Promises are what people at the top make; paper is what they leave behind by accident.
- **Everything is evidence of something.** A goods-in docket is evidence of what came in. A refused repair is evidence of a
  refusal. An Archivist never throws anything out, and never asks what a thing proves until someone wants to buy it.
- **A copy is worth a tenth of an original; an original is worth a tenth of an annotated one.** Notes in the margin are where
  the truth is: what the drawing says, corrected by the person who had to build it.

The Archivists are not idealists. They do not believe that truth sets anyone free. They believe truth has a market, and the
market is thin, so you hold your stock and wait.

### The Heritage Custodian licence

Under Halden's **Record Retention Rules**, every document generated by Halden Works is Company property, and holding one
is "unlawful retention of Company records". By that rule, the entire Archive is stolen goods.

The Company's solution, in **Y64**, was a licence. The Archive pays Halden a yearly fee for the status of **Heritage
Custodian**, which makes its holdings legal. In return Halden gets free access to the reading room for any Company engineer,
and first refusal on anything new the Archive acquires. So the Archive pays the Company for the right to keep the Company's
own records, which the Company then borrows for nothing. Imelda Vos has a framed copy of the licence in the reading room. She
says it reminds her where she lives.

### What they do

- **The Strongroom.** The Archive lives in the old strongroom of the Works Credit Union on floor 38 in the Middle: a dry,
  fireproof vault with a two-ton door, rented from the Company. It holds roughly 60,000 sheets, 900 binders, 200 ledgers and a
  card index of 31,000 cards in oak drawers. It is the driest room in the Stack below the Crown, and the Archivists are
  obsessive about it: every visitor's coat is hung outside, every sheet is handled with cotton gloves the visitor pays for.
- **The Index.** Every item has an accession number (an A-number). "A-14402" means a goods-in docket from Y29; "A-00001" is
  Hanne Vos's first lunch tin, the tin itself, on a shelf.
- **The reading room.** Readers pay by the hour, 3 cr. Copying is 2 cr a sheet by hand. Companies pay more.
- **Copying by hand.** Printers exist in the Stack, but a founding drawing is too large for anything below the Terraces, and
  photographed copies are not accepted by the Arbiter. A hand copy, signed by an Archive copyist and stamped, is. So the
  copyists sit at slanted desks under daylight lamps and trace.
- **Drying.** Much of the Archive's new stock comes up wet, from divers working the drowned plant and the Vault on floor 2.
  Wet paper is laid between blotters in screw presses for weeks, turned daily, until it can be read. A binder that comes up
  as a brick of pulp can sometimes give back thirty readable pages.
- **Provenance.** For a fee, an Archivist will certify that an object is founding-era, by matching its part number to a drawing
  in the Index. A **provenance certificate** with the Archive's stamp turns a lump of metal into a heritage piece that the
  Crown will buy.
- **Record searches.** For a fee, the Archive will tell you who held a lease before you, what was on your floor in Y20, what
  the drawing says is behind your wall.

### Structure

| Role | Who | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **Registrar** | runs the Archive; decides what's sold, to whom, and what's never shown | 1 |
| **Clerks** | catalogue, index, keep the Strongroom | 6 |
| **Copyists** | trace drawings, copy pages, sign copies | 8 |
| **Runners** | buy paper in the Mills, the Sump and at the Landing | about 12 |
| **Readers** | paying users who hold a reader's card | several hundred |

### People

- **Imelda Vos, Registrar.** Fifty-two. Hanne Vos's daughter, born seven years before the Lunch Tin Months; grew up
  sleeping next to a bath full of drawings. Cold, exact, and
  the best-informed person in the Middle. She knows what every document in the Strongroom is worth to every possible buyer,
  and she has never sold anything for less. She does not lie, which is different from telling you things.
- **Kofi Adebayo, "Ink," head copyist.** Forty. His hands are permanently blue-black to the second knuckle. He can copy a
  founding drawing line for line, including the original draughtsman's wobble. **Hook:** some provenance certificates in the
  Crown carry the Archive stamp but no A-number in the Index. Someone has been making second certificates. Ink has the steadiest
  hand in the tower, and the key to the stamp drawer.
- **Margit Halloran.** Fifty-seven. A Halden records officer in the Tenancy Office until Y79, when she walked out of the Company
  with a box of lease files under her coat and walked into the Strongroom. She catalogues lease histories now. She does not
  leave the Middle; she does not take lifts.
- **Simeon Pratt, runner.** Thirty-five, works the Sump and the Landing. Buys wet paper from divers by the kilo and pays a
  bonus by the legible page once dried. Smells of the Sump. Is the Archivist the player will meet first.
- **The Reader in Grey.** A reader who comes on the first of each month, pays in clean credits for six hours, and reads only
  Seawall Section papers. Grey suit, Middle shoes, no reader's card history before Y83. **Hook:** Vantage.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** everything that was ever written down. Specifically: the **Wall Book** (Tide Folk), Hollis Brandt's annotations
  on the rest of the Red Binder (Old Hands), anything from **the Vault** on floor 2 (underwater), and **Drawing Set 22**, the
  layout of floors 22 to 26, which is the one major gap in the Index.
- **Sell:**
  - **Manuals.** Copied maintenance manuals for founding-era machines (pumps, meters, cells, lift drives, fans).
  - **Drawings.** What's behind a wall, where a pipe runs, where a shaft goes.
  - **Provenance certificates.**
  - **Record searches**, including lease histories.
  - **Information**, priced like everything else.
- **Buy:** any paper with a Halden Works stamp, wet binders, ledgers, photographs, pre-Drowning Calder city records,
  goods-in dockets, time sheets, anything handwritten by a Works employee.

**In play:**
- **Manuals are tools.** Tools work from wherever you carry them. A manual on your shelf or in your pack reveals one hidden
  setting or feature on every machine of its type you own, the way an Old Hand's visit does, but permanently and for every
  copy of that machine. Manuals are 1x2 or 2x2 (a binder takes room).
- **Wet paper is a chain.** Wet binders come up from the Landing in salvage lots. On their own they're worth almost nothing. A
  blotter press (a machine: slots for sheets, takes days) dries them; the Archive pays by legible page. Some dried binders turn
  out to be manuals. One in a hundred turns out to be something someone will pay a lot for.
- **Provenance certificates** stack with founding-era items: an item plus its certificate, packed together in a drone to a
  Terraces or Crown buyer, pays several times what the item alone does. The certificate costs a fee and a reader's card.
- **Lease histories** are the quiet route to the "own your squares" ending: a lease search can turn up an old clause, a missed
  transfer, a prior claim, something that makes your squares cheaper to buy outright.

### Example slips

*Archive slips are typed on index cards, with an A-number in the corner where the request relates to an item.*

1. **Dry sheets.** "Twelve sheets of dry Works paper, any content, to the Strongroom (38)." Pay: 24 cr, plus 5 cr per sheet
   the clerks judge legible.
2. **Wet lot.** "A wet binder came up at the Landing on the last ebb. Pratt has paid for it. Carry it to 38, sealed, licensed
   lane." Pay: 20 cr. A choice: open it first and press it yourself (a few days of a press's slots; Archive standing drops if
   they find out; you might be holding something good).
3. **The search.** "Reader's request: lease history, floor 17, squares C4 to C9." *A slip you post yourself.* Cost: 40 cr.
   Return: a document item listing every holder since Y0 and one line of something interesting.
4. **Courier to the Arbiter.** "Certified copy of A-22870 to the Arbiter's clerk, floor 77, by hour 15, signed for." Pay: 35 cr.
   Requires: licensed lane; the copy must arrive dry and flat (the bay must be sealed, or packed flat between boards).
5. **Drawing Set 22.** At *Trusted*: "Anyone who brings sheets from Drawing Set 22, any sheet, any state, can name a price."
   A standing slip that never expires.

### How they treat the player

Politely and as a source. The Archivists assume everyone is either a buyer or a seller and are faintly disappointed by
people who are neither. At *Wary* you can buy reading time. At *Known* runners bring you wet lots first. At *Trusted* you can
buy manuals at copy price and certificates at a discount. At *Kin* Imelda Vos shows you what she's never sold, and asks what
you'd do with it.

### Relations

- **Halden:** licensed, watched, needed. Halden engineers read in the Strongroom every week. The Company could seize the Archive
  any day and has calculated that it is cheaper to licence it.
- **The Old Hands:** the Red Binder. See above. Each side is right.
- **The Rain Church:** wants Drawing W-104; the Registrar wants 800 cr. She has offered a copy for 40, which the Church refuses:
  a copy is not scripture.
- **The Tide Folk:** the Wall Book. Hesper Quill and Imelda Vos have met twice, on the Landing, at low water. Both meetings
  ended with no price named.
- **The Mills Co-op:** the original Settlement of Y41 is in the Strongroom. Its A-number is not in the public Index. Ben Books
  saw one page.
- **The Lift Guild:** the Archive holds the full **LM series** (lift drive manuals, 14 binders). The Guild pays the Archive a
  yearly retainer to keep the LM series out of the reading room. The retainer is the Archive's largest single income.
- **Shaft rats:** the Archive also holds the **Shaft Schedule** (founding drawings of every service shaft, duct and riser in the
  Stack). The rats would give anything for it. The Guild pays to keep it shelved too. The Archive sells single sheets of the
  Shaft Schedule to rats, quietly, at a high price. Selling the same silence to both sides is the Archive's business model.
- **The Stinkers:** the ultrapure plant manuals (reverse-osmosis trains, resin beds) are on a Halden restriction list. The
  Archive has them. A Stinker with a manual is a Stinker with a licence application.
- **Seed savers:** the Archive holds the **Workers' Gardens planting records**, Y0 to Y40, the founding seed lists of the
  roof allotments (see the seed savers). Agathe Haan reads them every month.
- **Insect ranchers:** almost nothing in the Archive concerns them, which is part of why the ranchers think of the Archive
  as a Middle thing.
- **Part two:**
  - **Penthouse fixers** are the Archive's best customers for certificates. The Archive knows which pieces in the Crown are
    fakes, and doesn't say.
  - **The Foundation** sponsors the reading room. A brass plate on the door says *The Foundation Reading Room*. In return,
    the Foundation's own founding documents (its charter, its first donors) are "awaiting cataloguing". They have been
    awaiting it since Y70.
  - **The insurance syndicate** buys structural reports: drawings of load-bearing walls and shaft linings, to prove "inherent
    structural risk" and void claims.
  - **Sublet barons** buy lease histories to find tenants with weak claims.
  - **Static** gets the occasional leaked page, delivered with no A-number, which the Registrar never confirms.
  - **The Voided** buy brand pattern books (original specs for branded goods) so their repairs match.

### In the endings

- **The Proof.** The Archive holds the Works' seawall correspondence (requests from the Seawall Section and the Company's
  refusals, A-series, Y48 to Y56). With the Wall Book (Tide Folk) it is a complete record of the Drowning. Everyone in the Stack
  already knows the truth; the player can decide who gets the proof:
  - **To Vantage:** the rival company takes it to inter-company arbitration and wins Halden's Calder Recovery Licence. The
    Flats get a new owner, with stricter rules. Nothing else changes.
  - **To Static:** it's read aloud on air, over three nights. Everyone hears what they already knew. The ending text is the
    morning after: the meters still run.
  - **To the Strongroom:** catalogued, numbered, shelved. Imelda Vos tells you it's the safest place for it, and it is.
- **Written down.** (With the Old Hands; see above.)

**Hook:** the Vault on floor 2 was flooded, but the Vault had a door rated to keep out fire, not water, and divers say it
was closed when they found it. Somebody shut it before the water came in. What's inside might be dry.

## The Lift Guild

> "We carry people. We carry freight. We don't carry anything that can't sign the Ride Book."
> (Rudolf Kasza, Master of Cars)

### Origin: the Lift Concession

The founding engineers did not build lifts with ropes. They built **linear lifts**: cars driven by magnetic motors set
into the shaft walls, several cars per shaft, each car moving independently up and down its own track, switching at the top
and bottom. Nothing like them has been made since; nobody in Calder Bay knows how to make the drive chips. The Stack has
**six cores**, lettered A to F, each with its own set of cars.

Until Y41 the lifts were run by Works Building Services, staffed by the 2000s clocks: operators in every car, mechanics in
the motor rooms. When the Works automated, the lift operators bargained separately from Local 9. They didn't want severance.
They wanted the lifts. In **Y44** Halden signed the **Lift Concession**: the operators would form a guild, run the lifts,
maintain them, keep the fares and tolls, and pay Halden a fixed yearly royalty, the **Lift Rent**. Halden was glad to be rid
of a cost.

The operators divided the cars between them. A car became a **car right**: the right to operate that car and keep its
takings, inherited within families and occasionally sold. Forty years on, the Guild is a closed, hereditary company of
about forty car-right families who run the only legal way to move people and heavy freight up and down the Stack.

### The cores today

| Core | Runs | Status | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Glass Bank (30, 60, 90) and the Cradle (90 to 104) | Running, four cars | Halden, directly. Not Guild. |
| **B** | Passenger (the Locals), 30 to 89 | Running, four of twelve cars | Guild |
| **C** | Passenger (the Locals), 30 to 89 | Running, four of twelve cars | Guild |
| **D** | Was passenger, 0 to 104 | **Dead since Y70.** Cars parked at 44, track out below 30 | Nobody. The empty shaft is the Stack's biggest free shaft. |
| **E** | Works freight | Dead; Lift Zero runs in a sealed well behind it | Company |
| **F** | Works freight (Old Two), 4 to 60 | Half-dead: Freight One died Y71 (the Empty); the track above 60 failed in Y81 | Guild |
| **Landing hoist** | Freight, the Landing (4) to floor 9 | Running, a cable hoist added after the Drowning | Guild |

### Why the Guild sides with the Company

Since the Y60s, cheap drones have taken more and more small freight off the cars. The Guild lost its parcels first, then its
produce, then its medicine runs. Halden, meanwhile, set up **licensed drone lanes** in the open sections of Cores B and C
(transponder beacons, fees, permits). In **Y66** the Guild made its peace with drones on one condition: **lane share**, a
tenth of every licensed lane fee in B and C goes to the Guild. Since then, the Guild has had one enemy, which is any drone that
doesn't pay: the free shafts, and the shaft rats who fly them.

The Guild lobbies the Tenancy Office every year for the **Shaft Safety Ordinance**, which would close and seal every
unlicensed shaft "for public safety". The Company has never passed it (closing the shafts would cost more than it brings in,
and the free shafts are where the Company's own HPS drones go when they don't want to be logged). But it's always on the
agenda, and the Guild is always paid back for asking, in small ways.

### What they do

- **The Ride Book.** Every car carries a ledger. Every rider and every crate is entered: floor on, floor off, fare, freight
  weight. The Ride Books are kept at **Car House**, the Guild hall on floor 45. They are the most complete record of who
  moves where in the tower, and HPS reads them.
- **Tolls.** Passenger fares by **band crossed** (riding inside your band is cheap; crossing from the Mills into the Middle
  costs more; the Middle into the Terraces costs much more, plus a pass). Freight by weight and band. The Landing hoist
  charges by weight only, and charges a lot.
- **Calling floors.** Operators announce each floor by its old Works name, not its number. Floor 14 is "Canteen", 19 is
  "Assembly", 28 is "Document Control", 38 is "Credit Union". Half of the Mills still uses those names because the operators do.
- **The uniform.** Maroon jacket, brass buttons, a brass car key on a chain. Jackets are inherited and re-tailored. Buttons
  are counted: a jacket with a full set of founding buttons is worth more than most people's rent.
- **Weddings.** Guild families marry in their own cars, between floors, stopped. A Guild wedding car is decked with real
  flowers, which have to be bought from somewhere.

### Structure

The **Master of Cars**, elected for life by the **Board of Cars** (one vote per car right). Under the Board: **operators**
(car-right holders and their kin), **mechanics** (the Guild's own maintenance, led by a chief mechanic), **toll clerks** (at
the hoist and the Old Two (core F) freight doors), and **apprentices**, who must be kin of a car-right family. Outsiders can't join.
Outsiders can buy a car right when a family sells one, which happens once a decade, at a price nobody below the Middle can pay.

### People

- **Rudolf Kasza, Master of Cars.** Sixty-one, third-generation Guild, holder of car right C-1. Smooth, careful, sincerely
  believes that the lifts are the tower's spine and the Guild is what keeps them in it. He dines with Tenancy Office managers
  twice a month and is the Company's most reliable friend below the Terraces.
- **Nell Pradesh, operator of car C-7.** Twenty-nine. Inherited C-7 from her aunt two years ago and found out it came with the
  aunt's debts. She carries shaft-rat parcels in the car's ceiling hatch between floors, unlogged, for cash. She is terrified of
  Kasza. She is the player's way into the Guild's back door.
- **Augustin Moreau, chief mechanic.** Fifty-eight. The Guild's real problem: the linear drives are failing one by one, and
  Moreau can keep them going only with founding-era contactors he can't buy and manuals the Guild pays the Archive to hide even
  from itself (the retainer forbids anyone reading the LM series, including Guild mechanics; Kasza thinks a manual in a
  mechanic's hands is a manual in a rat's hands). Moreau calls Pavel Lisko in secret when a car sticks.
- **Bettina Kasza, toll clerk, Old Two (core F).** Thirty-three, Rudolf's daughter. Wants the Guild to stop fighting drones and start
  running its own licensed drone lane through dead Core D ("we own the shafts, let's own the air"). Her father thinks she's
  naive. She thinks he'll still be lobbying for the Ordinance when the last car stops.
- **Joachim Lindqvist, "Ticket Joe."** Fifty. Toll clerk at the Landing hoist. Weighs Flats crates with his thumb on the scale
  and takes a cut of everything. The Tide Folk have a song about him.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** freight back; free shafts sealed; the Lift Rent kept where it is; enough parts to keep the cars running another
  ten years.
- **Offer:**
  - **Freight chits**: a punched card that lets a drone (or a crate) ride a Guild car. Slow (the car stops at every call), but
    no free-shaft rolls: no looting, no toll, no jam.
  - **Machine delivery**: anything too big for a drone (a bought machine, a 3x3 tank) comes up or down by Old Two (core F), with a Guild
    freight charge.
  - **Information**: the Ride Books, and what the operators saw at the hatches.
- **Buy:** **LM grease** (the founding-era lubricant for the linear drives, which nobody makes; salvage only), founding-era
  contactors and drive boards, brass polish, uniform cloth, cells, real flowers for weddings.
- **Pay:** in credits, promptly, and in free freight chits, which are worth more.

**In play:**
- **Every bought machine arrives by lift.** When you buy a big machine from a vendor, an Old Two (core F) delivery charge is added and
  the machine arrives a few hours later. At *Trusted* with the Guild, the charge drops. At *Hostile*, delivery is "delayed"
  by a day.
- **Freight chits as a route.** A drone with a chit in its bay rides a car: it takes longer than a lane or a shaft (the car
  stops), but it is the only route with no risk at all, and the only route the insurance syndicate considers "certified
  carriage" (see 06b). A good choice for one irreplaceable item.
- **Spotters.** Guild operators watch the shaft hatches near car stops. Each time you launch into a free shaft, there's a
  small chance a spotter logs it, and Guild standing drops one step. Use rat-chalk hatches (away from car stops) and the
  chance drops.
- **The band-crossing fee** is a visible, physical cost of class: a chit that crosses from the Mills to the Middle costs
  twice what a chit within the Mills does, and Terraces chits require a pass item in the bay.

### Example slips

*Guild slips are printed forms in triplicate, with a Guild stamp (a car between two arrows), a Ride Book reference number,
and carbon smudges.*

1. **Grease for E.** "Two tins LM grease, founding grade, to Car House (45)." Pay: 90 cr and three freight chits. LM grease comes
   only from salvage: the drowned plant's motor rooms (divers) or a Crown fixer's back room.
2. **Last door.** "Freight from car stop 71 (Core B) to a Terraces client. Our cars stop at the core; your drone does the
   door." Pay: 40 cr. You pick up from a car stop and deliver the last stretch. Regular, safe, and it makes you the Guild's
   subcontractor, which shaft rats notice.
3. **The wedding car.** "Car C-3, Saturday. Flowers, real, enough to dress a car. Greens for the table." Pay: 150 cr. Requires
   real-grade produce from grow beds. One of the best-paying produce contracts in the Middle, and a door into Guild standing.
4. **Report a hatch.** "Information wanted: unlicensed hatches in use between floors 14 and 21. Locations only." Pay: 25 cr per
   confirmed hatch. A betrayal slip. Shaft rat standing drops sharply for each hatch reported, and HPS welds the hatch, which
   might be one you use.
5. **Strike notices.** *Appears only during a Co-op rent strike.* "Carry Company notices to every Mills floor delegate." Pay:
   60 cr. Co-op standing drops to *Hostile* if you take it.

### How they treat the player

As a customer or a pirate, and they decide which by watching you. Use chits and lanes, and the Guild is polite, prompt and
cheaper. Use free shafts, and things you buy arrive late. At *Trusted*, you get a monthly freight pass. At *Kin* (only through
Nell, or through the wedding car), you might be offered a stake in a car right, which is property, and inherited, and the only
kind of ownership in the Stack that isn't a Halden lease.

### Relations

- **Halden:** allies. Lane share, dinners, the Ordinance. The Company counts on the Guild to keep the lifts running and has no
  intention of paying to do it itself.
- **Shaft rats:** enemies. Rats call Guild operators "cabbies" and chalk a car symbol with a line through it on hatches the
  Guild watches.
- **The Old Hands:** Pavel Lisko, the traitor, still fixes their cars.
- **The Archivists:** the LM retainer, which keeps the Guild's own mechanics ignorant.
- **The Mills Co-op:** the Greens Car dispute. The Co-op's produce used to ride the Guild; now it flies with rats.
- **The Rain Church:** Skywater pays the Guild a fee per seized litre of unlabelled water found in a rider's bag. The Dry Mother
  walks twenty floors to avoid them.
- **The Tide Folk:** the hoist toll. Ticket Joe.
- **The Stinkers:** the Guild searches for unlabelled water; Stinkers bribe operators to look away. Both sides find this works.
- **Seed savers:** none, except that the Guild weds with real flowers and doesn't ask whose seed they grew from.
- **Insect ranchers:** pay a **smell surcharge** on insect freight in passenger cores, and complain about it every year.
- **Part two:**
  - **The insurance syndicate** only covers cargo carried by Guild car ("certified carriage"), which is the Guild's best
    argument for chits.
  - **Vent kids** "roof" the cars: ride on top between floors. The Guild hates it; operators sometimes bring a kid down by the
    ear and sometimes let them ride.
  - **Plasma buyers** run a cold chain to St. Ober's Spire. **The blood car** leaves the Landing hoist at six every morning, a
    refrigerated crate in Old Two (core F), and nobody else rides it.
  - **Penthouse fixers** pay for "quiet cars" (unlogged in the Ride Book) to move things from the Middle to the Core A
    transfer at 60.
  - **Static** leaks toll changes and the Ride Book's busiest hours.
  - **The Voided:** the Guild reports VOID-stamped goods found in freight to brand protection agents, for a fee.
  - **Sublet barons** move evicted tenants' belongings by Old Two (core F), on contract.

### In the endings

- **A car of your own.** A late-game chain through Nell Pradesh: her aunt's debts come due; she has to sell C-7's right; you
  buy it. You become Guild, by purchase. It's the only property in the Stack that isn't a Halden lease, and it only works as
  long as the car runs.
- **The Last Car.** If the player never helps the Guild find parts, the cores fail one by one over the game (a slow world
  event). Late on, only Core A runs (Halden's, Crown only) and the Guild collapses into drone couriers. Everyone else is a
  shaft rat now.
- **Bettina's lane.** If the player backs Bettina, the Guild opens a licensed lane through dead Core D. Freight gets
  cheaper, the rats lose their biggest shaft, and the Guild survives by becoming what it fought.

**Hook:** Core D has been dead since Y70, its cars parked at floor 44. Shaft rats flying the D shaft at night say one car isn't
parked. It moves, slowly, between 22 and 26, with no operator and no lights.

**Hook:** the Guild has a supplier nobody has met. Every few months a box of founding-pattern drive contactors (new, not
salvaged, which should be impossible) arrives at Car House with no sender, and Moreau fits them and doesn't ask. Ruth Amadi says
the Dark Floors' cycle is nineteen minutes. That's about how long it takes to make a contactor.

## Shaft rats

> "Lanes are for people with somewhere to be by noon. Shafts are for people with somewhere to be."
> (chalked at the Drop, floor 20)

### Origin: cheap drones and old holes

The Stack is full of holes going up. The founding engineers built it to breathe and to carry: ventilation risers from the
air plant, cable chases, light wells, the small-bore branch lines of the Tube (**the Pneu**, a 30 cm network that the main Tube no longer uses
between Works offices), the service voids beside every core. After the Drowning, two things met. The Works had left Calder
Bay drowning in cells and control chips, which made small drones absurdly cheap to build from salvage. And the tower's holes
had nobody watching them.

The first couriers flew the ventilation risers in the Y60s, carrying medicine and parcels for people who couldn't pay lift
fares. The Lift Guild called them rats, because they lived in the walls. By the time Halden set up licensed lanes in Cores B
and C (Y66), there were hundreds of rats and the name had stuck, and they had started using it themselves. When Core D died
in **Y70** and its cars were parked at 44, its empty shaft (the full height of the tower, two cars wide) became the biggest
free shaft there is. Rats call it **the Deep**.

### What a free shaft is

"Free" means nobody charges you to fly it. It does not mean safe.

- **Looters.** Sump gangs and freelance netters stretch nets across shafts below floor 10 and take what falls in. The worst stretch
  is the **Gullet**: the D shaft between floors 4 and 9.
- **Tollers.** Gangs who net a drone, then release it for a fee, deducted (in practice) from whatever you're paid on landing.
- **Jams.** Debris, stuck dampers, a dead drone wedged in a riser.
- **The Turn.** The air plant switches its big fans twice a day, at 06:00 and 18:00, and every ventilation riser reverses draught
  for a few minutes. A light drone caught in the Turn is slammed into a wall. Rats time every flight around it.
- **Drips.** Condensation in the lower risers shorts motors. A wet shaft is chalked with a wave.
- **HPS sweeps.** Sometimes HPS flies a clearance drone down a shaft and confiscates anything without a transponder. Sometimes
  HPS uses the same shafts for its own drones, unlogged, which is why the sweeps are rare.

### Rat-chalk

The shafts are marked. Every hatch a rat uses carries chalk on its inside lip, in yellow, the colour that shows best in a lamp:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ○ | Clear, flown today |
| ○ with a line through | Looters below |
| △ | Tollers; a number inside is the usual price |
| a car with a line through | Guild spotter near this hatch |
| ≈ | Wet shaft; motors drip |
| ✕ | A drone went down here |
| ‥ (two dots) | HPS sweep recently |
| ↻ | Turn draught strong here; don't fly near the hour |
| a number in a box | A ledge, with a downed drone on it, floor number |

Chalk is renewed daily by whoever flies past. Old chalk is worse than none. Looters have been known to chalk a false ○.

### Structure

There isn't much. Rats fly alone or in **nests** of two to six who share a hatch and a repair bench. They meet at **the Drop**,
on floor 20: an old ventilation plenum where four risers and the Deep meet, a big echoing steel room with a chalk wall
(the **Drop board**) where jobs, warnings and debts are written. A **Drop warden**, chosen by whoever turns up, keeps the board
honest. Rats talk on a short-range radio channel everyone calls **Nine**.

The code is short and enforced by reputation: don't open a sealed parcel; don't false-chalk; if you see a drone go down, chalk
it; pay a relay rat their half.

### People

- **Ama Duarte, "Kestrel."** Thirty-four. The best rat in the Mills: flies three drones at once by ear, has the whole Deep in
  her head, has crashed eleven drones in fourteen years and never lost a parcel. She charges more than anyone and is booked
  three days ahead. She lost a brother to the Gullet and won't fly below 10 without being paid double.
- **Sixpence.** Fourteen, real name unknown. Half vent kid, half rat. Flies a palm-sized drone through the Pneu, which is too
  small for anyone else's. Carries keys, notes, seed packets, pills. Hates being called a kid.
- **Conall Brannigan.** Fifty. Flew for twenty years until a Turn draught broke both his hands. Now runs **Brannigan's
  Bench** at the Drop: motor rewinding, prop balancing, cell swaps, in the open, by lamp. Sells used drone parts and buys
  downed drones from ledge recoverers.
- **Yusuf Haddad, Drop warden.** Forty-four. Neutral to the point of rudeness. Settles disputes "by chalk": both parties write
  their side on the board, and everyone who passes for a week can add a mark. The side with more marks wins. It is the closest
  thing to a court below the Arbiter, and some people trust it more.
- **Ilona Petrosyan, "Mags."** Twenty-seven. From a Co-op family of the Nine. Carries only for the Co-op and the Church, cheaply.
  Thinks the other rats are mercenaries; they think she's a mark.
- **"Lantern."** A call sign on Nine, nobody's face. **Hook:** Lantern sells routes and schedules to looters in the Gullet.
  Everyone knows Lantern exists; nobody knows who it is; three rats have been accused at the Drop board, and all three were
  cleared by chalk.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** the shafts kept open (no Ordinance), the Shaft Schedule (the founding drawings of every shaft, in the Archive), and
  enough work to make rent.
- **Offer:**
  - **Subcontracting**: hand a slip to a rat, and they fly it with their drone. They take a cut (a third, usually more for
    risky routes), and you don't tie up your own drone.
  - **Route intelligence**: rat-chalk, Nine, Drop board warnings.
  - **Ledge recovery**: retrieving downed drones (and their cargo) from shaft ledges.
  - **Parts and repairs** at Brannigan's Bench.
- **Buy:** drone parts (props, motors, frames, cells), chalk, lamp cells, food, water. And **transponder spoofers**: illegal boards
  that make a free-shaft drone read as licensed to a lane beacon, which lets a rat cut through a licensed lane without paying.
  Spoofers are founding-era chips reflashed; a working one is worth a month's rent.

**In play:**
- **You are a rat whenever you fly free.** The player's free-shaft launches already roll for trouble on landing: a toll off the
  pay, or one item from the bay lost. Rat standing modifies those rolls. At *Known* the rolls soften; at *Kin* you get the Nine
  warnings before you launch (the shaft's current marks appear on the launch screen).
- **Rat-chalk as an item.** A stub of chalk packed with a slip means the drone uses a chalked hatch, and the trouble roll is
  softened for that flight. Chalk is consumable: a stub lasts a few flights.
- **Subcontract board.** At *Known*, a second board appears: a rat rota. You can hand any slip you've taken to a rat. The rat's
  chance of trouble depends on who they are (Kestrel almost never loses anything; Lantern's friends always do). The pay is cut,
  but your drone stays home for a contract it fits better.
- **Ledge recovery.** A slip names a shaft and a floor: a drone is down on a ledge. Send your drone with a **grab** (a part,
  bought) and it comes back with whatever the downed drone had in its bay. Owner unknown, unless the slip says. Keeping the
  cargo is legal (salvage); returning it raises standing with whoever owned it.
- **The Turn** is a world rule: launches into ventilation risers at hours 6 and 18 risk a crash. The game's day runs 8 to 20, so
  the 18:00 Turn is the one the player meets. A "don't launch into a riser at 18" habit is learnable in a day.

### Example slips

*Rat slips are chalk on a scrap of hatch plate or the back of a cell wrapper, with a call sign and a mark.*

1. **No questions.** "Sealed. 2x2. To 9, the Bilge door. Don't open. — Kestrel." Pay: 70 cr, which is too much. Contents unknown.
   (It might be voided goods, it might be a Static transmitter part, it might be someone's medicine.) Opening it: rat standing
   drops; you learn what it is.
2. **Ledge 13.** "Drone down on the ledge at 13, riser 4. Mine. Bring the bay and keep a third. — Mags." Pay: a third of the
   cargo. Requires: a grab.
3. **Relay.** "Half a run: Landing to the Drop. I'll do the Drop to 52. — Sixpence." Pay: half the job's pay. The Gullet stretch,
   which is the dangerous half.
4. **Chalk the new hatch.** "Fly riser 7 from 14 to 21, chalk what you find." Pay: 10 cr and standing. Your drone returns with a
   report of the riser's current marks, which then appear on your launch screen for that riser.
5. **A spoofer.** "Anyone with a reflashable drive chip, Brannigan pays 300." Pay: 300 cr. The chip is founding-era: from salvage,
   the Old Hands, or the Voided. Selling it makes you complicit in every lane theft it enables.

### How they treat the player

As a colleague or a competitor. If you fly free and chalk what you see, you're one of them. If you take the Guild's
last-door contracts, you're a cabby's helper, and the Drop gets a little cooler. If you report a hatch to the Guild, the Drop
board gets your name, which every rat in the Mills reads. At *Kin*, rats will carry your slips for less than a third, and
Kestrel will tell you the one stretch of the Deep she won't fly.

### Relations

- **Halden:** HPS sweeps, the Ordinance threat, and the open secret that HPS uses the same shafts. Licensed lane fees are
  Halden income; every rat flight is a fee not paid.
- **The Lift Guild:** enemies. See above.
- **The Rain Church:** water runs; rats are paid in Font water, which they prefer.
- **The Tide Folk:** the Landing is the bottom of every shaft. Rats deliver on the ebb.
- **The Mills Co-op:** steady customer, the Box (the Co-op's drone mutual) is the only cover most rats ever get.
- **The Old Hands:** Lisko rewinds motors at a discount for rats who bring tea.
- **The Archivists:** sell rats single sheets of the Shaft Schedule at high prices. Every rat wants the whole set. The Guild pays
  to keep it shelved.
- **The Stinkers:** rats carry Stinker water down and taster vials up.
- **Seed savers:** seed packets are the perfect rat cargo: tiny, light, worth a lot to the right person. Sixpence carries most of
  them.
- **Insect ranchers:** larvae runs. Rats charge extra for smell.
- **Part two:**
  - **The insurance syndicate** won't insure free-shaft cargo but sells **shaft protection** through its own runners: pay a
    weekly fee, and the tollers in the Gullet (who are paid by the syndicate) let you through. Rats call this exactly what it is.
  - **Vent kids** are the rats' younger siblings and rivals: they own the Pneu and the crawlspaces; rats own the risers. They
    trade warnings.
  - **Static** broadcasts shaft warnings at the top of each hour; rats carry Static's transmitter parts to wherever it moves.
  - **The Voided** use rats for most of their deliveries: voided goods can't ride a lane (brand agents check lane cargo).
  - **Flats divers** sell rats salvage cells and motors at the Landing.
  - **Penthouse fixers** hire Kestrel for the jobs that can't be logged.

### In the endings

- **The Free Air.** The Shaft Safety Ordinance comes up for a vote at the Tenancy Office (a late-game event). The player's
  standing with the Company, the Guild and the rats, and what they've done (did you report hatches? did you give the Drop the
  Shaft Schedule?), tips it. If it passes, the free shafts are welded floor by floor over a season, and the only routes are
  lanes and cars. If it fails, the shafts stay free and dangerous, as before.
- **The Open Map.** The player buys or steals the full Shaft Schedule from the Archive and chalks it onto the Drop wall. Free
  shafts become safer for everyone (trouble rolls fall for every rat, including you). The Guild's best argument dies. The
  Archive loses its two biggest customers and won't sell you anything again.
- **A fleet.** The business ending: you become the biggest courier in the Mills, with rats flying on your slips. You're not a
  rat anymore. The Drop has a word for that too.

**Hook:** the Pneu still holds pressure. Its pumps were in the Works offices, which are gone, so something else is keeping it
up. Capsules sent upward never come back. A capsule addressed, as a joke, to "the Director's Office" came back with a reply
card: *Received.*

## Stinkers

> "Smell it? Don't sell it."
> (Halden Pure safety poster, Y59; still on the wall at the Kettle on Six, framed)

### Origin: a Company word that stuck

After the Drowning, the bay was poison and the tap was the only safe water. In **Y58** Halden brought in the **Public Wellbeing
(Water) Ordinance** (in the Stack, just **the Standard**): only water treated under Halden licence may be "supplied for consumption", which covers sold, swapped and given
away. The same year the treated tap water was branded **Halden Pure**. Overnight, everyone who had been boiling bay water on a
cell plate and selling it to the neighbours was a criminal.

The early bootleggers boiled seawater and fractionated the poison out by temperature, roughly: the first fumes off the pot
were solvents and smelled like paint stripper; the residue in the pan stank of sulphur and metal. Bootleg flats smelled, and
the people who drank bootleg water smelled faintly of it too. In **Y59** Halden ran a safety campaign with posters of a
cartoon man holding his nose: *Smell it? Don't sell it.* The name **Stinkers**, already in Mills use and in a Company notice of Y58 (file 01), stuck for good within a month, and within a year
they were calling themselves that, the way rats did.

### The licence trick

The Public Wellbeing (Water) Ordinance has a door. Anyone can become a **Certified Safe Provider**: apply, pay the licence fee
(600 cr, then 150 cr a month), pay for inspections (40 cr each, at least monthly), and buy Halden Pure-approved labels
by the roll. Then you may sell treated water under your own name "as a Halden Certified Supply".

The inspection checks paperwork, labels, a sample bottle, and the fee receipt. It does not require a different still.

So the Stack has the same water at two prices. A Stinker with a rusty cell-plate still and a carbon bed sells *flat* at a third
of the meter price, illegally. The same Stinker, the same still, the same carbon, licensed, sells *Certified Pure* at the meter
price or more, legally, and is listed in the Company's wellness reports as an example of "community water partnership". Every
Stinker in the Stack knows the number of the most famous case: **Certified Safe Provider D-0447**. "Doing a four-four-seven" means
going legit. It is not a compliment.

### Grades and craft

*(Water itself is in `03-water.md`. This is what Stinkers call their product and how they hold their trade together.)*

- **First run:** boiled once, the solvent heads thrown off by nose. Cheap. Smells. Fine for bins, grow beds (some), washing.
  Drunk by the poorest.
- **Flat:** distilled properly, twice. Tastes of nothing, has traces of solvent if the stiller was lazy. The Mills standard.
- **Sweet:** distilled, then through carbon, or (the best) pushed through a founding-era **white tube**: a reverse-osmosis membrane
  element salvaged from the Works' drowned ultrapure plant. Sweet water from a good white tube tests cleaner than Halden Pure.
- **Stinker spirit.** A still that boils water can boil mash. Every Stinker outfit has a side line in spirit made from Mills
  produce waste and chute sugar. It's the Mills drink. It's also illegal (unlicensed spirits), under a different rule, enforced
  by a different inspector, who takes a different bribe.

The craft has a code, short: **don't sell flat as sweet**. A Stinker who does is "a four-four-seven without the licence": a
cheat. The way buyers check is the **taster**: a stoppered vial of a seller's water that a buyer can sniff, sip or test with a
cheap conductivity pen. Serious outfits hire a professional nose.

### Structure

Stinkers are a trade, not an organisation. There are hundreds of home stills in the Mills (a cell plate and a pot on a
windowsill) and a few dozen real outfits, called **kettles**, each named for its owner or location. The big kettles are in the
Sump, where the inspectors don't come without HPS, and HPS doesn't come without a reason. Sump kettles pay a share to whichever
gang holds their floor. Mills kettles pay the inspector.

### People

- **Bronwyn Stoatley, "Mama Stoat."** Fifty-eight. Runs **the Kettle on Six**: the biggest still in the Sump, 600 litres a day,
  heated by a rack of forty reconditioned Works cells she buys from Flats divers and has rebuilt by Old Hands. She employs
  twelve people, pays the Pumpmen's floor-6 crew (**the Bilge**) a tenth, and sells sweet to the Sump and flat to the Mills. She keeps
  the Y59 poster framed by the door. She'd never take the licence: "Then I'd have to sell to the people who can pay."
- **Jonah Pell.** Forty-five. Runs **Pell & Daughters** on floor 11, a home kettle that grew. He is 1,400 cr into the 2,000 cr
  licence fee, saved over six years. When he gets it he'll triple his prices and move to the Middle, and he knows exactly how his
  customers will feel, and he's doing it anyway, for his daughters.
- **Dorn Varga, Certified Safe Provider D-0447.** Fifty-one. Took the licence in Y80 with his cousin's still (**Varga's Coils**, floor
  8). Same still. His labels say **Varga Pure: a Halden Certified Supply**. He sells to the Middle and to the Foundation, which
  buys bottled water for its Clean Cup campaign. He sends his cousin a Halden Pure calendar every year.
- **Rosa Tembe, "the Nose."** Thirty-six. A professional taster: smells solvents at a level the cheap pens can't read. Hired by
  outfits to test each batch, and by buyers to test outfits. Lost her sense of taste to the work years ago; still has the
  nose. **Hook:** Rosa says Halden Pure, some weeks, has the same faint note as flat from Varga's Coils. She's sure of it.
- **Marguerite Osei (Old Hands, Clock 3112)** teaches the serious kettles how to run a white tube, rinse it and tell when it's
  spent. She never takes money; she takes water.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** heat (cells), membranes, carbon, not to be raided, and (for some) the licence; for Mama Stoat, the Standard gone.
- **Sell:** water (first run, flat, sweet), at a third to half the meter price; Stinker spirit; still parts and carbon beds to
  home stillers; tasters.
- **Buy:** **cells** for heat (lots), **white tubes** (any price), **spent carbon** (to reactivate by baking), glass bottles and
  jars, conductivity pens, cell plates, copper coil. They need somewhere to put **brine** (the salty sludge left after distilling):
  it goes down the chute, or out the Sump outfall.

**In play:**
- **Unlicensed water** is a grade: lower-floor slips accept it; licensed slips (Middle and above, and any slip marked *Halden
  Certified only*) reject it. A taster packed with it lets a lower-floor buyer pay full instead of the "unknown water" discount.
- **Your own still** (a bought machine, of course) makes you a Stinker as far as the Company is concerned. Storing more than a
  few dozen litres of unlabelled water on your field raises inspection risk.
- **The licence** is an upgrade you can buy: 2,000 cr and a monthly fee. Same machine, same water; your water now carries a
  *Certified* grade and the licensed slips accept it. This is the single cleanest place in the game for the brief's "same
  bootlegger, same rusty kit" point to be a mechanic rather than a line of text.
- **Brine** is a by-product item from any still: heavy, takes room, worth nothing. Down the chute it goes (the chute fills only
  from above, but your own outgoing chute hatch could be a disposal point, with a fee), or to a Tide Folk salt-boiler who'll take
  it. Waste with a use, per the spoilage rule.
- **Stinker spirit** fits the parked still idea: the same still machine with mash instead of water in, and a setting.

### Example slips

*Stinker slips are written on a water label, usually a peeled Halden Pure one, with the kettle's name in pencil.*

1. **Carbon run.** "Five kilos spent carbon to the Kettle on Six." Pay: 20 L sweet, delivered back in the bay.
2. **White tube.** "Any white tube, any state, Mama pays." Pay: 250 cr for a cracked one, 900 cr for a whole one. They come from
   the drowned ultrapure plant (floors 1 to 3) via divers, or from a fixer's stock.
3. **Thirty litres down.** "30 L flat from Pell's (11) to a family on 9. Cash on delivery." Pay: 18 cr. Water is heavy and takes
   space: 30 L of jerrycans fills most of a small bay.
4. **The Nose.** "Rosa will test any water for 5 cr a taster. Send it with the money." Your own water comes back graded, with a
   taster vial you can sell with it.
5. **Pell's licence.** "Jonah needs 600 for the licence. He'll pay it back in water. A third of the meter price, for life." Pay:
   a lifetime discount on water from Pell & Daughters. Twist: once licensed, Pell's prices triple, and a third of the new price is
   what flat used to cost. The slip is honest. It's just not the same deal.

### How they treat the player

As a customer first, a supplier second (cells, carbon, bottles), and a competitor if you build a still. Mama Stoat respects
competitors who don't sell flat as sweet. Varga sells to anyone. Pell needs friends. If you report a kettle to an inspector
(there's a slip for it, from the Company side), every kettle hears. If you take the licence yourself, the Stinkers call you
"four-four-seven" for the rest of the game, and buy from you anyway.

### Relations

- **Halden and Halden Pure:** the Standard, raids, bribes. **Inspector Leopold Fask**, the WWO inspector for floors 10 to 14 (Saxby walks the rest of the Mills), takes a
  tenth from Mills kettles and raids the ones who don't pay (more in `05-law-and-order.md`).
- **The Rain Church:** practical allies (the Church boils rain in Stinker stills), theological enemies (selling water is the one
  sin). "Free-cup people."
- **The Tide Folk:** the Flats are the most loyal customers; some kettles run solar stills on raft roofs; Ma Senna Duku takes
  brine for salt.
- **The Mills Co-op:** officially disapproves; members buy in a dry week.
- **The Old Hands:** Marguerite Osei; some stills have 3000s clock numbers inside.
- **The Archivists:** hold the restricted ultrapure plant manuals. A Stinker with a manual has a licence application.
- **The Lift Guild:** searches bags for unlabelled water; operators take bribes to stop searching.
- **Shaft rats:** carry water down and tasters up.
- **Seed savers:** buy sweet for seedlings and nothing else.
- **Insect ranchers:** buy first run for bins, cheaply.
- **Part two:**
  - **Flats divers** bring up white tubes from the drowned ultrapure plant, and cells for the Kettle's rack.
  - **The Voided** relabel Stinker sweet into Halden Pure bottles and sell it as Pure. Stinkers think this is beneath them and
    sell to the Voided anyway.
  - **Plasma buyers** sell Stinker water at clinic doors, marked up, to donors who've just given.
  - **The Foundation**'s *Clean Cup* vouchers target Stinker floors first. Varga supplies the bottles.
  - **Sublet barons** put a still in their hourly rooms and advertise "water included".
  - **Hot-bunk houses** serve first run with the bunk.
  - **Static** reads a weekly **Stinker report**: which kettle tested sweet, which sold flat as sweet.
  - **Penthouse fixers** buy "Sump-distilled" spirit for Crown bars, as a curiosity.
  - **Vent kids** carry tasters between buyers and kettles.

- **The Kettle.** You become the biggest Stinker in the Mills, unlicensed, paying the inspector, selling sweet at a third of

- **Water for nothing.** Linked to the Rain Church's Open Roof: water becomes, briefly, free. The Stinkers lose their trade

## Seed savers

> "You borrow one. You give back two. That's all the religion there is."
> (Agathe Haan, the Librarian)

### Origin: the Workers' Gardens and the Clearing

From Y0 until the Drowning, the stepped Crown terraces (90 to 100) were not penthouse gardens. They were the **Workers' Gardens**: open
terraces divided into allotment plots that any Works household could rent for a few credits a year. The founding engineers
put them there because a factory town with gardens kept its workers, and because rain fell there for free. Families grew
beans, greens, onions, tomatoes, squash, mint, from seed they had brought out of Calder's allotments and saved year after
year. Varieties took the names of the families who kept them, or the floors they came from, or a joke nobody remembers.

In **Y58**, as the upper floors were re-cut and sold, the Gardens became part of the Crown. Plot holders had one week's notice.
The terraces were cleared for private gardens (**the Clearing**), and the soil itself was sold. The plot holders took what
they could carry. A widow from floor 12, **Marta Haan**, took down thirty varieties in her apron pockets, in twists of
newspaper, and kept them in a biscuit tin under her bed. She was the first Librarian. Her tin is still the Library's oldest
container.

### Why seed saving is illegal

Fresh food in the Stack comes from grow beds, and grow beds need seed. Licensed seed comes from **Greenhold**, the agricultural
tower across the bay, and is sold in the Stack by **Orrin Provisions** under Halden licence. Greenhold seed (**GH-lines**)
is good: fast, high-yielding, uniform. It is also:

- **Single-season.** GH-lines are hybrids. Seed saved from a GH harvest doesn't come true: the next generation is a scatter of
  weak, uneven plants.
- **Licensed per planting.** Every packet carries a licence tag. Planting without one is infringement.
- **Marked.** Every GH-line carries a genetic tag, the **GH mark**, that Greenhold's inspectors can detect with a leaf-punch
  test in the field.

Saving heirloom seed is not, in itself, illegal. What makes the savers criminals is **patent contamination**: under
Greenhold's licence terms (which Halden enforces in the Stack), any plant that tests positive for the GH mark and was not
grown from licensed seed is infringing. Pollen travels: on fan draughts, on hands, on insects. A Brask's Bitter growing two
beds from a GH-line greens bed can test positive by its second generation. At that point the saver owes Greenhold a licence
fee for every plant, and the seed line is "infringing material" to be destroyed.

And then there's the joke the savers tell, which is true. In **Y79** Greenhold sequenced several of the old Workers' Gardens
varieties from produce sold in the Crown and patented them. **Brask's Bitter** is now **GH-Bitterleaf 4**. Growing Brask's Bitter
in the Stack, from seed the Brask family has saved for eighty years, infringes the patent on Brask's Bitter.

### What they do

- **The Library.** The seed bank. Not one place: hundreds of jars, tins and envelopes, hidden across the Mills and the Middle,
  no more than a tenth of any variety in one place. Only the Librarian knows where all of it is. The Library's catalogue is a
  set of index cards, one per variety, with its history.
- **Lending.** You borrow a packet. You grow it out. You return two packets from the harvest. This is **the Return**. It is
  the whole system: every lending doubles the stock, every grower becomes a keeper, and the Library spreads instead of being
  hidden in one place.
- **Keeping.** Each variety has a **keeper** who grows it out every year or two so the seed stays alive and true. A variety
  without a keeper is "orphaned", and the Library lends it out urgently.
- **Isolation.** Savers grow away from GH beds, in sealed spaces where they can (the Co-op's old clean room on floor 15 is the
  best, because of the sealed air), and hand-pollinate with a brush, flower by flower, so the line stays clean.
- **The Reading.** At the Return, the keeper reads the variety's card aloud: who brought it, what floor it grew on, what
  happened to the family. It takes a minute. Nobody skips it.

### Structure

| Role | Who | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **The Librarian** | knows where everything is | 1 |
| **Keepers** | each responsible for a variety | about 60 |
| **Lenders** | anyone who has borrowed and returned | a few hundred |
| **Carriers** | move packets between hiding places, usually children | a dozen |

### People

- **Agathe Haan, the Librarian.** Sixty-one, Marta Haan's granddaughter. Precise, a little paranoid, kind to children, ruthless
  with anyone who doesn't make the Return. She keeps the locations of the Library in her head and has written them down exactly
  once, in a sealed envelope held by someone she won't name, for when she dies.
- **Bartholomew Kett, "Barty."** Eighty. The last living person who gardened on the roof: he had a plot on the 97 terrace as a boy. Keeper
  of **Kett's Long Bean**. He describes the Workers' Gardens in the rain to anyone who asks, and the description is always the
  same, word for word, which is how you know it's true.
- **Dr. Teodora Ilić.** Forty-four. A Greenhold-trained plant geneticist who worked for Orrin's seed office in the Terraces.
  In **Y82** she was asked to help develop a "contamination-assertive" line: a GH variety whose pollen was bred to spread the GH
  mark into nearby plants as fast as possible. She resigned, which in the Terraces means she lost her lease, and she now lives on
  floor 16 under another name, teaching the savers which crosses to fear.
- **Pim Lund.** Nine. A carrier. Has a coat with a hem that holds thirty packets. Has been stopped by a Line Inspector twice and
  had nothing on him both times, because he'd seen the inspector first.
- **Harriet Soames, Line Inspector.** Not a saver: Greenhold's licensed inspector in the Mills, with a punch kit and a quota.
  She is polite, thorough and paid per positive test.

### The varieties

All heirloom seed follows the design rule for breeds: **fixed, visible traits**, no hidden genetics. Each variety does one
thing better and one thing worse than a GH-line, and is always the same.

| Variety | What it is | Trait (better) | Trait (worse) | Card line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Brask's Bitter** | leaf greens | draws a third less water per hour | bitter: Middle buyers pay less, Crown buyers pay more | "Brask grew it because nobody stole it." |
| **Floor 12 Red** | small tomato | tough skin: no bruising in a rough drone landing | small footprint, small yield | "Widow Haan's own. Travels." |
| **Widow Haan's Bean** | climbing bean | feeds the bed's soil: the next crop in that bed grows faster | slow; takes the tall slot | "Plant it first, plant anything after." |
| **Kett's Long Bean** | long pod bean | one plant, a big harvest | huge footprint in the bed (shoves neighbours hard) | "Barty's. Ask him about the rain." |
| **Canteen Mint** | mint | grows fast, sells to the Crown as a herb | spreads: shoves everything else in the bed every hour | "Got into the Canteen soup in Y31 and never left." |
| **Assembly Squash** | big squash | one fruit, sells very high in the Crown | 3x3 when ripe; ten days | "Grown on the Assembly floor during the Settlement strike." |
| **Strand Onion** | onion | stores for weeks without ageing | lost: only the Tide Folk's jar | "Unknown. Came up in a jar from a drowned kitchen." |

**In play:**
- **Two kinds of seed.** **Licensed seed** (from Orrin or the company store): fast, high yield, sells as real produce at full
  price; harvest gives produce only; each packet carries a licence tag item that must be kept (an inspector asks for it).
  **Lending packets** (heirloom): slower, lower yield, fixed traits; harvest gives produce *and* seed back. One loop costs money
  every planting; the other costs time and runs forever.
- **Heirloom grade.** Heirloom produce is real grade with a named variety on it. The Crown pays a premium for named varieties
  ("Brask's Bitter, Mills heritage") through penthouse fixers. So the most illegal food in the tower is the most expensive food in
  the Crown.
- **Contamination is visible, not hidden.** A bed that grows a GH-line and an heirloom in the same slot is a risk; the heirloom's
  seed from that harvest is marked **"crossed?"** on the item (a visible "?" in the analyser style). An analyser or Dr. Ilić can
  test it. Seed that tests crossed is worthless to the Library and dangerous to keep. No proximity rule: contamination only
  happens inside a bed, between plants sharing a slot, and the player can always see it coming.
- **Leaf-punch inspections.** A Line Inspector visit tests your beds. A positive (an heirloom testing GH-marked, or a GH-line
  without its licence tag) is a fine per plant and the seed confiscated.
- **The Return.** A lending slip arrives with a packet. Return two packets of the same variety within N days and standing rises.
  Fail and the Library stops lending. Make the Return with seed marked "crossed?" and Agathe Haan never speaks to you again.

### Example slips

*Saver slips are paper seed envelopes, the request written inside the flap, a variety name on the front.*

1. **Grow out.** *An envelope marked Widow Haan's Bean.* "Grow it. Give back two." Pay: none; the seed is yours to keep after the
   Return. Standing on Return.
2. **Orphan.** "Assembly Squash has no keeper. Will you?" A long commitment: grow it out every season, keep the seed, keep it
   isolated. Return: *Trusted*, and the Crown's best-paying produce line.
3. **Brushes.** "Twenty fine brushes for hand-pollinating, to 16." Pay: 25 cr in greens chits (via the Co-op).
4. **Before the punch.** "Soames is on 17 tomorrow at hour 10. Move what needs moving." A warning slip, no pay. The choice: pull
   your heirloom beds (lose growth hours), hide them (a packing job: an inspector checks visible beds only), or trust that they'll
   test clean.
5. **The Strand jar.** At *Kin*: "The Tide Folk have a jar from a drowned Strand garden. Bring it to the Library." The Tide Folk
   hold it as the Bay's share. It's a chain through Tide Folk standing, and a choice for the berth.

### How they treat the player

Cautiously, then generously. They never ask where your seed came from and don't want you asking about theirs. At *Wary* you
get one packet and a warning. At *Known*, lending slips arrive regularly. At *Trusted*, you keep a variety. At *Kin*, you know
where a tenth of the Library is, and you're one of the people who could lose it.

### Relations

- **Halden and Orrin:** Orrin sells the GH-lines and takes a share of every licence; Halden enforces Greenhold's terms in the
  Stack. The Company does not much care about seed; it cares about the licence revenue.
- **Greenhold** (see `07-outside.md`): the enemy, at a distance. Greenhold never sees the savers; it sees inspection numbers.
- **The Mills Co-op:** shelters the Library in its clean room. The Ledger wing is frightened of what a contamination finding
  would cost the Co-op; the Hall wing doesn't care.
- **The Rain Church:** raw rain grows heirlooms better than Halden Pure. Hidden saver beds are often next to Fonts. Barty Kett
  goes to the Wet Sabbath because it rains in the sermons.
- **The Tide Folk:** the Strand jar.
- **The Old Hands:** keep window pots of lent seed and make the Return religiously.
- **The Archivists:** hold the **Workers' Gardens planting records**, Y0 to Y40: who grew what, in which plot, which year. Agathe
  Haan reads them every month. They are proof of prior use. Nobody has yet found anyone to show them to.
- **The Lift Guild:** Guild weddings need real flowers; the savers grow the best and never say so.
- **Shaft rats:** Sixpence carries most of the Library's moves.
- **The Stinkers:** sell the savers sweet water for seedlings.
- **Insect ranchers:** ranchers' frass is the savers' best fertiliser. Ranchers' crickets are also the savers' worst fear: an
  escaped cricket in a saver's bed is a pollen carrier.
- **Part two:**
  - **Penthouse fixers** buy heirloom produce for the Crown at high prices, and keep asking for seed. The savers know why: seed in
    a fixer's hands goes to Greenhold, gets sequenced, and gets patented.
  - **The Foundation** runs **Seeds of Hope**: free GH-line seed packets handed out to Mills children for window pots, licence
    included. Every free GH packet in the Mills is a contamination risk. The savers think this is deliberate. Dr. Ilić is sure it is.
  - **The Voided** sell second-generation GH seed saved illegally from licensed crops: cheap, and it doesn't come true.
  - **Static** broadcasts the planting calendar in code, as "weather for bean people".
  - **Vent kids** are most of the carriers.

### In the endings

- **The Gardens come back.** Through the Rain Church's Wet Servants, the player gets heirloom seed into the Crown's private
  gardens: a packet of Canteen Mint in a planter, Kett's Long Bean on a trellis. The ending text is a season later: the Crown's
  terraces are full of Workers' Gardens varieties, mistaken for ornamentals, spreading. Nothing changes for anyone in the Mills.
  The Gardens are back.
- **The Library.** The player's workshop becomes the Library's main bed: an isolation room, the keeper of a dozen varieties, the
  envelope with the locations. You're never rich. You're the reason thirty families' seeds are alive.
- **Prior art.** The Archive's planting records, Barty's testimony and the Library's own seed prove Brask's Bitter predates
  GH-Bitterleaf 4. In a tower with no courts, it goes to the Arbiter, who rules that the matter is "outside Halden jurisdiction".
  Vantage, who would love to break a Greenhold patent, pays for the records instead. **Alternative:** the ruling goes the
  savers' way on one variety, and Greenhold quietly re-patents it with a single altered gene.

**Hook:** Marta Haan's biscuit tin had thirty varieties in Y58. The Library's catalogue lists twenty-nine. Agathe has never
said what the thirtieth was, or where it is.

## Insect ranchers

> "You get used to the chirp. Then one day it stops, and that's when you panic."
> (Big Lou Fennimore)

### Origin: the Protein Programme

Before the Drowning, Calder fed itself through its port: grain ships, cold stores, markets. The Drowning took the port. For two
years the Stack lived on emergency stores and the Workers' Gardens' last harvests (the Gardens were cleared in Y58 anyway). In
**Y59** **Orrin Provisions**, Halden's food licensee, launched the **Protein Programme**: a block of pressed protein made from
farmed insects and the food waste of the upper floors. It was called **Vitabrick**, and the company store sold it for two
credits.

Orrin needed insect farms, and the Mills had empty halls. The best were the old cell-formation annexes on floors 20 and 21, next
to the sealed formation halls of the Dark Floors (22 to 26), because the bulkheads on that side were always warm. Orrin
offered tenants a contract as **Licensed Protein Producers**: Orrin would supply breeding stock and a feed protocol, and buy
back the harvest at a fixed price per kilo. The contract had two clauses everybody signed without reading closely: buy stock
only from Orrin; sell insects only to Orrin.

### The Fade

Orrin's crickets are the **Orrin Strain** (catalogue OC-7): fast-growing, uniform, heavy. They are also inbred, and they crash
after a few generations: smaller clutches, more dead in the bins, then nothing. Ranchers call this **the Fade**. Orrin calls it
"expected line performance" and sells replacement stock at a price that eats a third of a rancher's margin. Whether the Fade
is designed in or just tolerated is the most argued question in the Mills halls.

A rancher who breeds their own stock is in breach of contract. A rancher who sells to anyone but Orrin is in breach of contract
and producing unlicensed food. Both happen constantly.

### What they farm

Three kinds of insect, each with its own niche in the chain from the chute to the brick:

| Insect | Eats | Gives | Rancher's word |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crickets** (Orrin Strain OC-7, or wild **Mills Brown**) | good waste: grain, greens, bran | the best protein; Vitabrick's main ingredient | "the chirp" |
| **Mealworms** | dry waste: crusts, bran, paper, and the white foam packing that comes down the chute from the Crown | protein, slower; dry frass | "worms" |
| **Grubs** (soldier fly larvae) | wet waste: rotting food nobody else wants, in bulk | protein for feed, and the best fertiliser in the tower | "the pits" |

Two by-products matter as much as the insects: **frass** (droppings), which is fertiliser for grow beds, and **chitin** (shed
skins and husks), which ends up as a binder in the brick and as filler in cheap Middle cosmetics.

### What they do

- **Warmth.** Bins need heat. Ranchers buy it as power (cells, sockets) or get it free from a warm wall. Floor 21's bulkhead to
  the Dark Floors is the most valuable wall in the Mills.
- **Weigh day.** Once a week, Orrin's field agent comes with a scale and a clipboard, weighs each licensed rancher's harvest,
  and writes a credit note. Rejected batches (too small, too many dead, "contaminated") are taken anyway, at a third of the
  price, "for disposal".
- **Cull and grind.** Harvested insects are chilled to sleep, then ground. Licensed ranchers sell live or chilled to Orrin.
  Wild-stock ranchers grind, mix and press their own: **grey brick**, unbranded, half the price of Vitabrick.
- **Living with it.** Cricket halls are loud. Ranchers are half-deaf by forty. Grub pits smell. Ranchers marry ranchers.

### Structure

- **The Ranchers' Association**: the licensed producers, Orrin-sponsored (Orrin pays the Association's hall rent on floor 20),
  with a chair who negotiates the per-kilo price every year and has never got it raised.
- **Wild-stock ranchers**: independents, unlicensed, breeding **Mills Brown** crickets (hardy, slower, descended from Orrin
  escapes crossed with whatever was already living in the walls), selling grey brick to the Sump, the hot-bunks and the Co-op.
- **The pits**: a few big grub operations in the Sump that take bulk wet waste. Nobody licenses grubs; nobody wants to.

### People

- **Louisa Fennimore, "Big Lou."** Fifty. Orrin's top producer: forty bins on floor 21 against the Dark Floors bulkhead, heated
  for nothing. Chair of the Association. Owes Orrin 3,800 cr for replacement OC-7 stock, rolled over every Fade. She knows
  exactly how trapped she is and tells everyone it's fine. **Hook:** her free heat comes from the Dark Floors. If whatever runs
  behind that wall stops, her bins go cold in a day. Lou is the one person in the Stack who needs the mystery to keep running.
- **Sabine Okoro.** Thirty-eight. Wild-stock rancher on floor 12, Mills Brown crickets, twelve bins, a hand press for grey brick.
  Hall wing of the Co-op. Lends starter colonies of Mills Brown to anyone who'll pay her back in a colony. If enough ranchers
  switched to Mills Brown, the Fade would end, and so would Orrin's hold. She's working on it, bin by bin.
- **Aurelio Tallis.** Orrin Provisions' field agent for the Mills. Forty-five, clean shirt, nice pen. Weighs, notes, smiles. Never
  raises his voice. Has a way of saying "contaminated" that costs a rancher a week's income. Not cruel: he has a quota too.
- **Dunstan Hale, "Grubber."** Sixty. Runs **the Pits** on floor 7: twenty tonnes of soldier-fly grubs eating the Sump's wet waste.
  Sells grubs as feed, frass by the sack, and the smell for free. Rich by Sump standards. Pays the Bilge, pays nobody else.
- **Nadia Fennimore.** Sixteen, Lou's daughter. Deaf in one ear from the chirp. Wants out of the halls more than anything.
  Talks to Static. **Hook:** she's the one who'd sell the Association's books if anyone offered.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** (licensed) a better price and an end to the Fade; (wild) not to be raided for unlicensed food; (everyone) waste,
  warmth, quiet.
- **Sell:** live insects, larvae, grubs; frass; chitin; grey brick (wild only); starter colonies (Mills Brown, wild only).
- **Buy:** **sorted food waste** (the more sorted, the better the price), **dry waste and foam packing** (for mealworms), heat
  (cells, power), bran and feed, bins, a little water (first run is fine).

### Vitabrick and grey brick

**Orrin Vitabrick: Complete Nutrition\*** (\*when consumed as part of a varied diet). Flavours: *Original*, *Savoury* and
*Sweet Ration*, with a seasonal *Sweet Corn* (no corn). Up to 40% insect protein; the rest is upper-floor food waste, chitin binder, salt and flavouring. It
sells for 3 cr at the company store, or for one Foundation voucher. It is what most of the Mills and all of the Sump eats.

**Grey brick** is Vitabrick without Orrin: more insect, less filler, no flavour, half the price, unlicensed. It tastes better,
which nobody can say out loud because it's illegal.

**In play:**
- **The insect bin** is a decided machine (food waste + warmth + a little water → insects over hours). Ranchers add what goes in
  it and where it goes after:
  - **Stock** is an item: an Orrin Strain colony (fast, heavy yield, a visible **Fade counter** that drops each batch; at zero,
    the bin produces nothing until restocked from Orrin) or a Mills Brown colony (slower, smaller, never fades). Fixed, visible
    traits again.
  - **What you sell to:** Orrin's depot buys only Orrin Strain insects and only with a **herd ticket** packed alongside; wild
    buyers (Sabine, the Sump, hot-bunks) buy anything, cheaper.
- **Three bins, three wastes.** Cricket bins want good waste; mealworm bins take the chute's dry waste and white foam (turning
  junk into feed); grub bins take the rotting wet waste. The garbage chute's morning dump stops being "food waste" and becomes a
  sorting job with three destinations, plus compost for grow beds (the Co-op's Split).
- **Frass** comes out of every bin as a by-product and feeds grow beds: the bin chain and the bed chain touch here.
- **Grade:** Vitabrick (branded, licensed, Orrin's) and grey brick (unbranded) are the same chain's two outputs; the press makes
  grey brick, only Orrin makes Vitabrick. Lower-floor slips accept grey; Foundation and company-store slips want Vitabrick.

### Example slips

*Ranchers' slips are carbon-copy delivery tickets, the top copy torn off: either Orrin's printed herd tickets or a wild
rancher's reused one with the Orrin logo scratched out.*

1. **Sorted wet.** "Ten kilos sorted wet waste to the Pits (7)." Pay: 8 cr and a sack of frass. Grubber's standing job: always on
   the board, always cheap, always taken by someone.
2. **Foam for the worms.** "Any amount white foam packing to Sabine (12)." Pay: 1 cr a kilo. Turns chute junk into money.
3. **Weigh day.** "Lou is short for weigh day. 20 kg OC-7 crickets to 21 by hour 14, with a herd ticket." Pay: 6 cr/kg, half again
   what Orrin pays. You're helping her hide a Fade from Tallis. If Tallis checks the herd ticket against your bin's records, you
   both lose.
4. **Grey to the bunks.** "Forty grey bricks to a hot-bunk house on 9." Pay: 40 cr. Unlicensed food; a Lift Guild car will refuse
   it; a free shaft will take it.
5. **A colony back.** "Sabine lends a Mills Brown starter. Return one healthy colony in ten days." Pay: none; you keep the line.
   This is the slip that ends the Fade for you, and starts Orrin's interest in you.

### How they treat the player

As a buyer of stock, a supplier of waste and a competitor in bricks. Licensed ranchers are polite and frightened; wild ranchers
are rude and generous. At *Trusted* with the wild ranchers, you get Mills Brown stock and their Sump buyers. At *Trusted* with the
licensed side (through Lou), you get Orrin's depot price and Tallis's attention. You can't be *Kin* with both: Tallis keeps lists.

### Relations

- **Orrin Provisions and Halden:** the contract, the Fade, weigh day. Orrin is the Company as far as ranchers are concerned.
- **The Mills Co-op:** the Split (chute waste half to bins, half to beds) is the ranchers' long fight with the growers. Wild
  ranchers drive the Co-op's Hall wing.
- **Seed savers:** frass feeds the savers' beds; escaped crickets terrify them.
- **The Rain Church:** little; ranchers need little water.
- **The Tide Folk:** sell bay eels and dried wrack as bin feed and grub-pit bulk.
- **The Old Hands:** Dez's old formation halls; Dez says the ranchers on 21 are "keeping my seat warm".
- **The Archivists:** almost nothing, which the ranchers take as a comment.
- **The Lift Guild:** the **smell surcharge** on insect freight in passenger cores.
- **Shaft rats:** carry larvae and grey brick, charge extra for smell.
- **The Stinkers:** sell ranchers first run for bins.
- **Part two:**
  - **The Foundation** is Orrin's biggest single customer: it buys Vitabrick at full price with Crown donations and hands it out
    as vouchers. Crown charity money flows, through the Foundation, straight to Orrin.
  - **Hot-bunk houses** and **sublet barons** are grey brick's biggest buyers: "breakfast included".
  - **The Voided:** Orrin destroys unsold Vitabrick past its date by stamping it VOID and soaking it in blue dye. It goes down the
    chute. In the Sump, **blue brick** is rinsed, dried and eaten. It's the cheapest food in the tower.
  - **Plasma buyers** give donors a Vitabrick after each donation, bought from Orrin.
  - **Penthouse fixers** sell roasted Mills Brown crickets in the Crown as **artisan crickets**, salted, in little paper cones, at
    a price per cone that would buy a week of grey brick.
  - **Static** started the rumour that **the brick tastes different this month** (see the hook below).

### In the endings

- **Orrin buys you out.** The ending `LORE.md` proposes as "a business the Company has to buy out": the player runs enough
  Mills Brown bins and presses enough grey brick that Orrin's Mills sales drop. Orrin makes an offer for the business: a lump sum,
  a lease upstairs, and a non-compete. Taking it is a valid ending. The ending text follows the brick: within a month, Mills Brown
  is relabelled "Orrin Heritage Strain".
- **The Brown Brick.** Refuse, and spread Mills Brown stock to every rancher who'll take it. The Fade ends across the Mills. Orrin
  responds with a price cut on Vitabrick that wild ranchers can't match for long. The outcome is a season-long price war, and the
  ending depends on how many bins are Brown when it ends.
- **The cold wall.** Linked to the Dark Floors: if the player ever does something that stops the Dark Floors running, floor 21's
  bulkhead goes cold, and Big Lou's forty bins die in a day. It's not an ending; it's a cost.

**Hook:** the brick does taste different this month. Not worse: different. Orrin's Mills depot has been taking in more crates
than the Mills ranchers deliver. Static says the difference is coming in by sea, from **Bastion**, the prison tower, where the
prisoners farm insects too.

## Pressure points: one choice, many ledgers

These are the moments where the factions stop being separate systems and pull on the player at once. Each is a slip, an event,
or a short chain, and each moves several standings in different directions. They're the places where the world's politics
become a decision about what to pack.

### The strike week

The Co-op's Hall wing wins a vote: **hold your rent, day 3 to day 10.**

- **Join:** Co-op up; Company credit rating falls each day; the Lift Guild offers you strike-notice slips (refuse them and Guild
  standing falls); HPS walks the Mills stairwells (theft events fall, inspection events rise); Static broadcasts the Floor
  live and names the floors that hold.
- **Refuse:** Co-op to *Wary*; the Company notices nothing; the Guild notices you didn't take its slips either.
- **Report:** credit rating up, a Company bonus, and Gaspard Mbeki has competition.
- **During the week:** rats carry Co-op food at cost; the Rain Church opens the Fonts to strikers; Big Lou's licensed bins keep
  delivering to Orrin, and the Hall wing never forgives her; the barons buy two leases from families who couldn't hold.

### The Wall Book auction

Hesper Quill decides to sell. Bidders: the Archive (credits and a promise of safekeeping), Static (nothing but the promise to read
it on air), the Reader in Grey (Vantage: more credits than anyone), and the player, if they've got the cash. The Wall Book only
moves on a Great Ebb, at the Stump, in four hours. Whoever the player helps (or outbids), every other bidder's standing falls.

### The Ordinance vote

The Lift Guild finally gets the Shaft Safety Ordinance onto the Tenancy Office agenda. Standing with the Guild, the rats and the
Company each tips the vote. The Archive's Shaft Schedule tips it further: given to the Guild, it shows HPS where to weld;
given to the Drop, it makes the shafts safe enough that the "safety" argument fails.

### The cold wall

Ruth Amadi works out what the Dark Floors are making, and the Old Hands want to know if it can be stopped. The ranchers on floor
21 need it to run. The Lift Guild's mystery contactors come from it. The Co-op's Settlement clause might depend on whether it
counts as a reopening of the Works. Nobody in this file can open the welds, but the player might find out how (see
`14-hooks-and-mysteries.md`), and every one of these factions will have an opinion.

### The Font in the laundry

The Church offers you a Font. Accept: Church up, foot traffic up, inspection risk up, Skywater bounty on you (the floor warden on
17 will try to collect). The Co-op's Ledger wing wants it moved off a Co-op floor. The Stinkers lose a few sales on your floor.
Penthouse fixers ask if you'll bottle some for the Crown. Every one of those is a slip.

### Weigh day

Lou is short for weigh day and asks you to cover her. Cover: ranchers (licensed) up, Tallis's risk on you. Refuse: Lou's Fade
shows on Orrin's books; she loses her bonus; she rolls her debt again. Tell Sabine: wild ranchers up, Lou gets a Mills Brown
colony she can't legally use.

### The Red Binder

Hollis Brandt wants the Red Binder back. Imelda Vos wants Hollis to annotate the rest. Whoever you carry it for, the other side
turns. Unless you can get Hollis into the Strongroom and Imelda into the Canteen, which is the long chain that leads to the
**Written down** ending.

## Open questions for the designer

Collected from the entries above.

1. **Standing:** one number per faction on a ledger page (proposed), or something more physical (only tokens and remembered
   names, no number)?
2. **Tokens as packable credentials:** keep the "a token packed with a slip changes the route or the buyer" rule, or keep tokens
   as plain keepsakes?
3. **Does the player ever leave floor 17 in person?** The Great Ebb market and the Canteen visits assume the player can send a
   crate to a market screen. If the player never travels, those become drone-only.
4. **Tides as a world clock:** a tide strip next to the hour clock is cheap to show and gives the Flats a rhythm. Worth it, or one
   clock too many?
5. **Scripted deaths** (the Old Hands dying one per season): good pressure, or too heavy?
6. **How many Dark Floors threads** should run through factions? This file ties it to the Old Hands (Ruth's nineteen minutes,
   Dez's locker, badge 0001), the Lift Guild (Core D's moving car, the unsigned contactors), the Co-op (the Settlement clause),
   and the ranchers (the warm wall). That might be too many, or exactly the point.
7. **The licence as a mechanic** (Stinkers): paying 2,000 cr to turn the same water into a better grade. Is "same machine,
   better grade, monthly fee" a pattern to repeat elsewhere (food licences, brand licences)?
8. **Heirloom vs licensed seed:** the loop proposed is "licensed: fast, pays every planting; heirloom: slow, gives seed back,
   fixed traits." Does a third grade on produce (named variety) add value or just noise?
9. **Mills Brown vs Orrin Strain:** is a visible Fade counter on stock a fun pressure or a chore?
10. **How far should the Company lean on factions?** Right now the Company tolerates almost all of them because suppressing
    them costs more. Is there a point in the game where that changes, and the Company cracks down (a late-game event)?
