# 02: The Stack, floor by floor

The building as a place and as a machine: what it is made of, how things move through it (people, water, air, power,
garbage, drones), and what it is like to stand on each floor. Plain text is the main version; **Alternative:** offers
another version of the same thing; **Hook:** is a mystery or story seed; **In play:** says how it surfaces in the game.

This file builds on the founding design in `01-history.md` (the Plinth and the Shaft, the mechanical floors, the Kessin
lifts, the Tube, the risers, the Sorrel meters), the plumbing in `03-water.md` (the Waterhouse, the three lines, the break
tanks) and the prices in `04-economy.md` (floor 17 at 125 cr a week, the rate card, the Creep). Where it adds a
number of its own, the number is a working one chosen to sit beside theirs.

---

## Part one: the building as a machine

### The shape: the Plinth and the Shaft

The Stack was built as two buildings poured into one. The bottom thirty floors (0 to 29) are **the Plinth**: Halden Works,
a broad block roughly 180 metres by 140, its floors six metres from slab to slab because they were halls for machines, not
rooms for people. Most Mills halls have since grown mezzanines that tenants bolted in, so a "floor" in the Mills is often
two levels of life stacked inside one hall: workshops below, bunks above, a ladder between.

From the middle of the Plinth's roof rises **the Shaft**, floors 30 to 104, the Residences. The founding drawings call it
that. Nobody else ever did. Calder called the whole thing the Stack, after the exhaust risers that ran up its spine, and
because from the old waterfront it looked like exactly that: a stack of boxes on a bigger box.

The Shaft steps back as it rises, and each step lands on a **mechanical floor**: 30, 60 and 90. Each mechanical floor is a
full storey of plant (a Lung, cisterns and break tanks, lift machinery, the transformer rooms) wrapped by an open ledge where
the tower narrows. The bands of the Stack break at exactly these floors because the founders built walls of machinery there,
and the Company later found the walls useful.

| Section | Floors | Footprint (approx.) | Slab to slab |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plinth (the Works) | 0–29 | 180 × 140 m | 6 m |
| Lower Shaft | 30–59 | 60 × 60 m | 3.5 m |
| Upper Shaft | 60–89 | 45 × 45 m | 3.5 m |
| Crown | 90–100 | 34 × 34 m, stepping in | 4 m and up |
| The Lantern | 101–104 | glass, 24 × 24 m | 5 m |

The ledges:
- **The Shelf** (floor 30, the Plinth's roof). In the founding years this was **the Common**: trees in planters, a running
  track, a bandstand, open to the sky on three sides. It was leased in Y63. The Middle still says "the Common" when it means
  the paved bit by the Dry Font; everyone below says the Shelf, because it is where things land.
- **The Sixty Walk** (floor 60). A garden promenade four metres wide around the 60 mechanical floor. Glass corridor fronts
  open onto it.
- **The Crown Ring** (floor 90) and the Crown's terraced gardens above it, which step in floor by floor "like a wedding
  cake". Nobody below 90 has stood on them.

The line every Mills tenant knows, because the Co-op prints it on its flyers: **floor 17 alone has more people than the
whole Crown and the Lantern together.** Eleven Crown floors and four Lantern floors hold some forty households. Floor 17 has
three thousand people, and one Glove Wall.

The Stack stands about 450 metres. The Company census says 141,000 residents. The Co-op's count, which includes
people who sleep in shifts, under stairs and inside old process tanks, says nearer 190,000.

**In play:** the field's backdrop should change with band not only in light but in ceiling height. In the Mills, the
player's lease is a pen in a vast hall: a six-metre ceiling with a dead gantry crane on rails, mesh partitions, a mezzanine
edge overhead. In the Middle: a corridor wall, a door, a meter box. In the Terraces: glass and a view.

### What it is made of

#### The lattice and the grey

The frame is the founders' **lattice**, a slow-cast composite that flexes in wind and is meant to (see 01). The floors and
walls between the lattice members are a founding-era concrete the Company sold as **Halden Mendstone**. It was poured with a
mineral culture in it: when a hairline crack let in moisture, the culture woke and grew calcite into the gap. For forty years
the Stack healed its own cracks. You can still see it up top: thin white seams on the walls, like old scars.

Everyone calls the stuff **the grey**. The culture is dying from the bottom up. Salt killed it in the Sump; decades of acid and
solvent seeping through the Plinth weakened it in the Mills. The grey has a gradient every tenant can read:

- **Crown and Terraces:** white seams. The grey still mends. A crack closes in a season.
- **Middle:** tan seams. Some cracks close; the rest are filled with Company structural paste, which cracks again.
- **Mills:** brown seams that weep. Cracks are measured with chalk marks and dates. Water finds them.
- **Sump:** "sick grey". It spalls in plates; the rebar shows ("the ribs"), rusted thin as a finger. Halden Structural posts
  red tags; tenants paint over them so the tags do not frighten customers.

**In play:** **mend scrapings**, an item. Live culture scraped off a healthy wall up top, sold damp in a sealed tin. Packed
into a crack in a lower wall it may still take. Terraces cleaning crews scrape it off as "efflorescence" and some of them
sell it down: janitor on 70, fence on 41, tenant on 17. A Sump client might want six tins for a wall that is coming apart,
and pay in founding-era parts from the drowned floors.

**Hook:** an Old Hand insists the culture was never meant to die. It was fed: a nutrient wash ran through the walls'
drainage channels once a year from a tank on each mechanical floor. The tanks are still there, labelled **M-FEED**. The
recipe is not. The Archivists have a page that might be it, water-damaged at the line that matters.

#### Service tiles and lease studs

Every floor in the Stack is laid in founding-era **service tiles**, each a lift-out lid over the services beneath: water, power,
data, air, drain. Under every leased square sits a tile with a flush brass **lease stud**, stamped with floor, sector and
number (`17-C-04411`), which answers a surveyor's wand with a faint founding-era signal. Since the Square Survey of Y58 the
Company has billed by the stud, and the **square** is what it bills: the unit of rent, and of fines ("obstruction of common
squares, 2 cr per square per hour"). On the player's board it is simply the cell an item sits on. The Company has never said how
big a square is.

When a stud is pried out (brass sells) the square goes **dark** on the Ledger: it cannot be billed, and it cannot be leased.
Dark squares are a small economy of their own; `04-economy.md` has the chalkers and the "pending survey" billing.

Because every tile lifts, every floor is also a lid. In the Mills the space under the tiles (forty centimetres deep, full of
founding-era cable trays and dead pipe) is called **the underfloor**, and it is where people hide stills, cells, savings and
sometimes children during an occupancy count.

**In play:**
- A **dark square** at the edge of the lease: usable for free until a **Survey** event finds it.
- **The underfloor** as a hidden container under a few of the player's squares: small, flat (1-high items only), and not
  seen by inspectors. Lifting a tile costs an hour step. A found underfloor stash during an inspection is a fine, but most
  inspections do not lift tiles unless a warden has reported something.
- **Original service tile (with maker's stamp)** is a first-fit item (see 01); a tile from a dark square is how most of them
  enter the market.

#### Glass, light and cut windows

The Shaft's founding-era glazing dims itself in strong sun and clears at dusk. It works in the Crown and most of the
Terraces. In the Middle many panes are stuck dim (**brownglass**), and replacement glass has been "on order" for twenty years.

The Plinth was built for machines, not views, and has almost no windows. Its halls were lit from above, through skylights in
the Common; when the Common was leased and covered in stalls and planters, the skylights went dark. The Mills are lit by
lamps and by **cut windows**: holes a metre across hacked through the Plinth wall by tenants and glazed with whatever was
to hand. Every cut window is on a Company register and fined yearly as an "unauthorised aperture". People pay, because
daylight is worth it. A lease block with a cut window rents for half as much again, and the rate card has a line for it.

**In play:** a lease upgrade that is not more squares but **a cut window**: the squares under it are tagged **daylit**, and a
grow bed standing on daylit squares draws less lamp power in daylight hours, against a yearly fine. It is a property of the
squares, read by the machine standing there, not proximity between items.

### The core

The Shaft stands on a **core**: a services spine about twenty metres square, running from the drowned foundations to the
roof, with the six lift cores of the Kessin system grouped around it. In the Plinth the core rises through the middle of each
hall like a tree trunk through a barn, and the most valuable squares on any Mills floor are those nearest it, because that is
where the tap posts, the socket boards, the chute hatches and the Tube ports are. Mills tenants measure a lease by "how many
pillars from the core". The player's is four.

Inside or along the core, behind panels the public never opens:
- **Lift wells** (six Kessin cores, A to F; see Lifts).
- **The two founding stairs**, North and South.
- **The sweet-line risers** (blue bands): Halden Pure pumped up from the Waterhouse to the roof cisterns, and the down-feeds
  from the break tanks at 89, 59 and 29.
- **The salt line** (red bands): raw bay water for flushing and sprinklers.
- **The soft-line drain** (green bands): Crown and Terraces grey water, falling to the Outfall.
- **The black drains**, falling to the Outfall culvert under the Sump.
- **The capped downpipes** of the old rain system, dead since Y61, with the welded RAIN taps on every tenth landing.
- **The four chutes** (see below).
- **The Lung trunks**: supply and return air.
- **The Bus**: the founding-era power trunk.
- **The Tube**: the old pneumatic post, now the licensed drone lanes.
- **The risers**: the Works' eight exhaust and process-gas risers, now the free shafts. One of them is warm.

#### Lifts

The founders' **Kessin lifts** were twenty-four linear-motor cars in six cores, ropeless, pushed up their wells by magnetic
rails. Nine run today. The **Lift Guild** operates five of them on a Company franchise (Old Two and the Locals; core A's four cars are Halden's own): they keep them running (they are the only
people trained to), they set the fares, and they do not like drones.

The nine, as people know them:

- **Old Two** (core F, the Plinth freight core). One of the two Works freights, a car five metres by four, built to carry
  plant materials. It runs from floor 4 to floor 60, stops at every Sump and Mills floor on a timetable, and carries people,
  carts, beds, machines and the dead. Its twin, **Freight One**, died in Y71; its doors are welded on every floor and its well
  is a free shaft now, called **the Empty**. Old Two takes forty minutes from 4 to 60 with the stops.
- **The Locals** (four cars, cores B and C), floors 30 to 89, stopping everywhere. Middle and Terraces residents ride them on
  monthly passes. Mills people may ride with a day ticket if they can show business above 30.
- **The Glass Bank** (two cars, core A, the outside of the east face), floors 30, 60, 90 only. Glass-walled. Pass holders.
  The Middle sees it pass its windows full of people in clean clothes, and sets clocks by it.
- **The Cradle** (two cars, core A, beside the Glass Bank; run by Halden itself, not the Guild), the Crown's private lifts, 90 to 104, panelled in real wood.

Fares (Lift Guild, as in `04-economy.md`): 0.2 cr per floor per passenger; freight 0.5 cr per crate per floor, minimum 5;
a machine that needs the whole of Old Two is quoted by the Guild "on inspection", which in practice is 25 to 60 cr from
the Mills to the Shelf.

Old Two's timetable is posted on every Mills landing and is wrong on every one. It stops at each Mills floor about once every
two hours each way, more often between 05:00 and 07:00 when day workers go up to the Middle and the Morning Fall comes down.

Then there is a tenth car, which is not counted because it is not public: **Lift Zero**, the Blind Lift, a sealed freight car
in its own doorless well behind core E: no doors on any public floor. See the Dark Floors.

**In play:**
- **Old Two delivers machines.** A bought machine bigger than a drone bay arrives "by freight" at the next stop on the
  player's floor, with a fare on the bill. The timetable gives deliveries a real hour: "your grinder will be on the 14:00
  car".
- **Lift Guild strike** (event): Old Two stops. Machines cannot arrive and large goods cannot leave for a day; drones are the
  only logistics in the Mills, and drone contract pay rises. Good for the player with drones, bad for the neighbour without.
- **Freight chit** (Lift Guild token, see 06a): packed in a drone's bay, the drone rides a lift car instead of a shaft. Slow,
  safe, no trouble roll.

**Hook:** Old Two sometimes stops at a floor that is not on its timetable, opens for exactly eight seconds and closes. The
Guild calls it a controller fault and charges for the "unscheduled stop". The floors and hours it picks are the old Works
shift changes: 06:00, 14:00, 22:00, on the floors where the lines were.

#### Stairs

- **The Long Stair** (the North Stair) is the only continuous public stair from floor 4 to floor 89. It is gated at 30 by
  **the Turnstile** (a Mills resident going up needs a day ticket, a work pass or a delivery slip) and at 60 by **the Sixty
  Gate** (pass holders and guests). Above 89 there is no public stair; the Crown's stairs are inside the Crown.
- **The South Stair** is broken at 41, where a landing fell in Y64. A family on 41 keeps a plank-and-cable bridge across it
  and charges a token per crossing. The landings either side are sublet as stalls. Everyone calls it **the Gap**.
- **The Corner Stairs**, four Works fire stairs at the corners of the Plinth, serve 4 to 29 only. Their landings are where
  Mills life spills out: every one is a stall, a bed, a grow box or a shrine.

Walking from 17 to the Shelf takes twenty minutes; from 17 to 60, with the passes, more than an hour. Nobody walks to the
Crown.

**In play:** a hand-carried contract (later, if wanted) costs the player hours of the day and needs papers at the Turnstile.
Drones exist because stairs are slow and the Turnstile is a desk with a queue.

### The chutes

The founders built a pneumatic refuse system: inlets on every landing, sucked down to sorting rooms on floor 8 that sent
recyclables back to the Works and the rest to barges. The Drowning flooded the sorting rooms and the pneumatics never came
back. The inlets became **gravity chutes**. There are four refuse trunks, one per quadrant of the core, each a metre and a
half across, lined with a founding-era ceramic that has worn through to the grey in places. Each floor has a **drop door** on
each trunk. Where a chute starts and which floors it passes decides what falls down it, and everyone below knows each chute's
character the way farmers know soil.

- **The North Chute ("the Crown Chute").** The only chute that starts in the Lantern, at 103. It passes the Crown's
  residences, the Postern's reject bench on 93, the Arbiter's office on 77, and the Ledger floors on 71. The best things in
  the Stack come down it, and the most thoroughly ruined: real goods slashed and stamped VOID, Crown Assay rejects, cut
  wine-skins, founding-era fittings replaced for fashion. High value, heavy repair.
- **The East Chute ("the Kitchen").** Starts at 89 and passes Lantern Row's restaurants on 75, Orrin's test kitchen on 80,
  and the densest family floors of the Middle. Food waste in bulk, packaging, peel, bones, spoiled stock. The insect ranchers
  fight for East Chute catches.
- **The South Chute ("the Long Drop").** Starts at 85 and passes the Vey clinics on 64 and 65 and the Middle's family floors.
  Clothing, children's things, cheap broken appliances, and medical waste (sharps go in sealed tubes that should go to an
  incinerator, and sometimes do not). Low value, easy repairs.
- **The West Chute ("the Paper Chute").** Starts at 88 and passes the offices of the Terraces and the Brand Integrity Bureau
  on 66. Paper, screens, office furniture, cells from office kit, and, on Bureau clearance days, the Bureau's own seized and
  voided stock in bulk.

#### The order of picking

What falls is picked over several times before it reaches the bottom, and the order of picking is the order of the Stack:

1. **At source.** Up top nothing usable goes down whole. "Devaluation before disposal" is a brand rule (see 05): staff slash,
   puncture, snap or stamp things VOID before they reach the drop door. A Crown household's staff includes a **disposal
   valet** who does nothing else.
2. **The Reclaim (floor 59).** Halden's own reclaim station straddles all four trunks just above the Middle's busiest floors.
   Its founding-era magnetic and optical sorters pull out metals, cells and anything with a Halden part number. The Company
   takes first pick of everyone's rubbish and sells it back as "certified reclaimed".
3. **Middle catches (30–58).** Licensed, dear, owned mostly by repair firms and Company contractors.
4. **Mills catches (10–29).** Hundreds of small catches. The player's is one of them.
5. **The Bale Hall (floor 8).** The old sorting rooms. What is left falls into three founding-era compactors called **the
   Jaws**, and **bale pickers** get one look, by hand, in the minute before the jaws close.
6. **The Spill Door (floor 4).** Bales nobody buys go out through a door in the Landward wall onto the Flats at low tide.
   This is what "everything is dumped on them" means, physically: a door, a ramp, a tide table, and a Company clerk with a
   clipboard.

#### Catches

A **catch** is a diverter in the chute wall: a hinged steel gate that swings into the fall for a few seconds in every minute,
knocking a share of whatever is passing into a **catch bin** on the lessee's side. Halden leases **catch rights** per floor,
per chute, per gate cycle: a "one-in-twelve catch" opens one minute in twelve. The gate has a weight plate: when the bin is
full, it stays shut, and your share falls on to the catches below.

This is the game's **garbage chute** container, and the reason its table is set by who lives above: your catch takes a cut
of what the floors above threw out, after everyone between them and you has had theirs.

Why it fills **each morning:** the Crown keeps a quiet rule from 22:00 to 06:00 and the Terraces compact at night, so all four
trunks are **held** behind a founding-era shutter at the Reclaim overnight. At 06:00 the shutter opens and the night's
rubbish from everything above 59 falls at once. The Mills call it **the Morning Fall**. The trunks roar with it for about ten
minutes, every catch on every floor clanks open and shut, and children on the Corner Stairs count the bangs. After that the
chutes run thin all day: a trickle from the Middle.

Catch-right prices on floor 17 (weekly, on top of rent; working numbers):

| Catch | East (Kitchen) | North (Crown) | South (Long Drop) | West (Paper) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-in-twelve | 8 cr | 14 cr | 5 cr | 7 cr |
| One-in-six | 15 cr | 26 cr | 9 cr | 13 cr |

The same catch on floor 12 costs a third less and gets less; the same catch on 28 costs about double. A one-in-twelve catch
comes with the player's starting lease (the hatch square in `04-economy.md`).

**In play:**
- The chute container's table is set by **which trunk** (four distinct characters) and **which floor** (higher: more, better,
  and more VOIDed). Moving or upgrading a catch is a purchase at the Lease Office, never a building project.
- **Left full, it stops filling** is literal: the gate stays shut and your share goes to the floor below. The neighbour on 16
  who shares your trunk does well when you are slow, and might send you a small thank-you slip. A dark joke that teaches the
  rule.
- Chute events: **Clear-out Day** (a Terraces tenant evicted: whole wardrobes and furniture fall, voided but plentiful);
  **VOID Week** (a Bureau audit: everything is ruined twice); **Reclaim Fault** (the sorters at 59 are down: cells and metal
  fall through to the Mills for a morning, and everyone is rich until noon); **Chute Jam** (no fall today until a vent kid
  clears your trunk; pay to have yours cleared first).
- Your own rejected goods come back. A Crown delivery that fails the Crown Assay is voided and dropped down the North Chute.
  If your catch is on the North Chute, you may pull your own jar of honey out of your bin next morning, VOID stamped through
  the label.

**Hook:** **chute mail.** People in the Middle write on the inside of packaging and drop it, hoping it lands with someone
below. There is an etiquette: chalk the trunk and the floor on the outside, fold it into a cup. Most of it is love letters and
debts. Some of it is not addressed to anyone living.

**Hook:** something heavy comes down the West Chute every few weeks at about 02:00, while the trunks are held. It should stop
on the shutter at the Reclaim. It does not. The Reclaim's log shows nothing. Floor 29's catch-holder says it sounds "like a
filing cabinet with a person in it". The Bale Hall finds nothing in the morning.

### Water going down

The water itself belongs to `03-water.md`. What matters to the building is direction:

- **Up:** Halden Pure is made at the Waterhouse (floors 5 to 8, in the Sump) and pumped up the sweet-line risers to the roof
  cisterns. It then falls through the break tanks (Terraces Header 89, Middle Header 59, Mills Header 29), filling **from the
  top down**. The Sump makes the water and gets it last.
- **Down:** everything else falls. Basins and baths of the Crown and upper Terraces drain down the soft line, Crown grey,
  cleaner than what the Mills drinks. Toilets flush with salt water. Every drain ends at the **Outfall**, a culvert under
  the Sump, and every drop of it has passed every floor on its way.
- **The weep.** The grey leaks downward. Any crack with water above it weeps. A ceiling that weeps is a nuisance and a supply:
  buckets stand under drips, and **drip rights** under a good weep are traded between neighbours ("I'll give you the
  Tuesday drip for a cell"). Weep water is dirty with whatever the floor above spilled, but it is free and salt-free.

**In play:** a **Weep** event on the player's lease: for a few days a ceiling drip fills whatever vessel sits in a fixed
**drip square** with dirty water each hour, free, until the floor above fixes its pipe or the player pays the Lease Office
to report it (which ends the drip sooner and adds a "maintenance contribution" to the bill). Weep water feeds an insect bin
or goes through a filter. It is a container that fills by itself, like the chute; no proximity.

### The Lungs: air

The founders built three air plants, the **Lungs**, on the mechanical floors 30, 60 and 90 (see 01). Each draws outside air
through intake louvres, cleans it through scrubbers, humidity wheels and founding-era electrostatic **plates**, and pushes it
down through supply trunks to the floors below it. Return air from those floors comes back up to be cleaned and sent round
again, so the further below a Lung you live, the older your air. The **90 Lung** serves the Crown and Lantern; the **60 Lung**
the Terraces; the **30 Lung** the Middle above it and the whole Plinth below it, and it has run on cannibalised parts since Y70.

The 60 Lung's intakes are a band of louvres around the tower's face on floors 60 to 62 called **the Gills**. The 30 Lung
breathes through louvres along the edge of the Shelf, two metres above a market, a dye stall and a fish-paste kitchen, which
is why Mills air smells faintly of the Shelf at noon.

What each band breathes:
- **Crown and Lantern:** the 90 Lung, roof-height air, its own plates replaced from spares the Company keeps for no one else.
- **Terraces:** the 60 Lung, first air from the Gills. Metered by **vent register**, tiers with names like Fresh Plus.
- **Middle:** the 30 Lung's upward feed, metered by vent register at Standard unless you pay for the second filter stage.
- **Mills:** the 30 Lung's downward feed, mixed with return air from twenty floors of workshops. Billed as the flat
  Atmosphere Levy on rent (see 04). Warm, dusty, dry in winter and damp in summer, carrying old Works dust that lifts every
  time a hall is swept. The Sorrel air meters in every lease still record, and the billing line still prints at zero.
- **Sump:** whatever the Mills exhale, plus salt, methane from the drains, and a sweet chemical breath that comes up from the
  drowned floors at low tide.

The 30 Lung has four fan rooms. **Fan Room Three** has been dead since Y79. When a second one trips, the Mills get an **air
day**: return air slows to a crawl, candles gutter, the insect halls get hot, and nobody sleeps.

**In play:**
- Air is a **floor attribute** in the Mills, not a meter: nothing to pay, nothing to buy except filters. An **Air Day** event
  thins the shelf customers (people stay in), slows grow beds and favours insect bins (warm, stale, they like it).
- **Plate cloths** (cloth filters Mills households hang over vents) and **dust masks** are steady sellers down the tower.
- A **spent Lung plate**, a founding-era filter plate the Company discards, is salvage an Old Hand can wash and re-charge.
  It can go back up to the Company as a "reconditioned plate" (scrip), or to the Co-op for its own vent on 13 (standing).
- **Fan Room Three**: a contract chain for the late game. The Old Hands know what is wrong with it. The part is in the drowned
  floors.

### The grid: power and meters

#### Where the power comes from

- **The Race.** The Works' cooling water used to leave the Plinth through a founding-era channel under floor 0 that opens into
  the bay. The founders set tidal turbines in it, to give the Works power that did not depend on the province. Half are silted.
  The rest still turn, which means the Stack's base supply comes in **two surges a day**, at mid-flood and mid-ebb, and sags at
  slack water. Turbines nobody can service, in a floor nobody can stand on.
- **The Crown's skin.** The Crown's and Lantern's glass is founding-era photovoltaic. On a sunny day the top produces more than
  it uses and sells the surplus down to Halden, which sells it on.
- **The Banks.** In the old **Power Hall** on floor 9, rows of founding-era storage banks smooth the tide. Two rows of seven
  still hold charge. The hall is Company ground, inside the Waterhouse perimeter.
- **Cells.** The whole Stack runs anything portable off H-cells from the store or charged in the Sump off a stolen line.

#### The Bus and the Steps

The power trunk is **the Bus**, founding-era, as thick as a man, sheathed in something that is not quite metal. On each
mechanical floor it passes through a transformer room called a **Step**. When supply sags (slack water, cloud, a turbine
stalling, a Dark Floor run), the Steps shed load **by band, from the bottom**: Sump first, then the Mills, then the Middle in
rotation. The Terraces and the Crown are never shed. Halden calls this "load balancing". The Mills call it **slack hour**,
because it comes with slack water.

The tide moves about fifty minutes later every day, so slack hour drifts through the working day: one week it is at dawn, a
week later at noon. Every Mills hall has a tide table chalked by its socket board, and children are paid a few cr to keep it.

**In play:** the hour-step day gets a natural rhythm. The tariff has its **Peak Window** (17:00 to 20:00, see 04); on top of
that, **slack hour** cuts Mills sockets dead for one or two hour steps at a time that shifts every day and is printed on the
tide table. Machines on the socket stop; machines with a cell in their slot run on. A **cell rack** is worth its squares
because of the tide, and a player who reads the table runs the grinder before slack and launches during it.

**Alternative:** the Company passes the tide on as price instead of outage: power at half rate during the two surges, double
at slack. Gentler, and it makes the tide table a price chart rather than a schedule.

#### Meters

Every lease has a **socket board** (four outlets and a power drum) and shares a **tap post**. The meters in them are the
founders' **Sorrel meters**: small brass-faced units with a glass eye. They were built to balance the Lungs and the Fall, not to
bill, and since Y43 they bill. They report through the Bus itself; on many Mills floors the reporting fails, and then a
Company **meter reader** walks the floor once a month, and where a meter cannot be read, bills an **estimated reading**.
Estimated readings are always high. Disputing one means a morning in the queue at the **Meter Hall** on 38.

Each meter carries a lead seal (see 04). Tampering is a trade: magnets on the counter housing (the **slow stone** in 03),
shunts, the **back-feed** (charging from a neighbour's socket through a long cable under the tiles).

**In play:** **estimated billing** as a lease state: when your meter stops reporting, you get a fixed high bill until a reader
comes. It rewards heavy use and punishes thrift, which is the joke, and it makes "get your meter fixed" a small contract with an
Old Hand.

### The facade and the roof

#### The faces

The Stack has a **Seaward** face (west and south, toward the open bay, where the storms come from) and a **Landward** face
(north and east, toward drowned old Calder and the Flats). The Crown's glass and gardens face Seaward for the sunsets. Mills cut
windows are mostly Landward, because the Seaward wall is salt-blasted and a storm comes straight through a cheap pane.

#### The Plinth wall

Blind ribbed concrete, stained in vertical streaks below every crack. Banks of rusted louvres from the Works' exhaust. Hundreds
of cut windows. On the Landward face from about floor 12 to 29, **the Hanging Boxes**: grow boxes bolted under cut windows,
sometimes ten to a window, a vertical patchwork of green that Co-op families tend with ropes and long-handled tools. They get
real daylight and real rain. Halden fines them by the box as "facade encroachments"; every few years a storm takes half of them
anyway, and the Flats pick the soil out of the mud.

Facade work is done by Company **riggers**, licensed and roped. The unlicensed ones, who hang boxes, reglaze cut windows and
sometimes go in through them, are **skinners**.

**In play:** a **Hanging Box** as a lease add-on for a player with a cut window: one outside grow slot that gets daylight and
rain (free water some days, storm loss on others), fined weekly. A storm event can strip it.

#### Ledges and outside air

Every Shaft floor has a forty-centimetre **drip ledge** under its windows. In the Middle, **ledge rights** come with a flat and
the ledges are lined with pots, aerials, laundry and little wind turbines that Halden fines as "unregistered generation".

The air around the Stack is Company property. A drone may fly outside the facade only with **skin clearance** (on top of a lane
permit), and never within twenty metres of the Crown's glass: the **Quiet Glass Rule**, enforced by HPS's Crown Detail.
Outside flight is fast and rough; the Seaward wind throws cheap frames into the wall.

**In play:** outside routes as a later unlock: faster than any shaft, needs a sealed frame and skin clearance, closed in storms.

#### The roof

Floor 104 is the Lantern's roof. Around and below it the Crown's terraced roofs form the Stack's only rain catchment, drained by
the **Founders' Gutter** (a ring of steel troughs) into the roof cisterns and the Skywater line down to the Skywater Hall on 88
(see 03). On the Lantern roof itself: a glass pavilion, **the Mast** (Halden's comms mast and weather station, which the Company
says is better than the Sounding's), and **the Crown Pad**, the only inter-tower landing pad on the roof, used by the Crown's own
craft and by penthouse fixers bringing things from elsewhere.

Beside the Mast stand the eight capped heads of the Works risers, under the Lantern's floor, as Marrow Teague warned (see 01).
On still nights the Lantern smells faintly of solvent. Seven of the caps are cold to the touch. One is warm.

**Hook:** the Crown Pad's log, if anyone below could read it, shows a craft that lands every ninth night at 03:00, takes on cargo
from the Lantern's service lift, and leaves. Nobody on the Crown staff loads it. The cargo is already on the pad.

### The Crown (90–104)

**Standing there.** You don't, unless you live there, work there, or were invited. If you did: daylight first, real white
daylight from three sides through clean glass, so much of it that Mills visitors squint for an hour. Green: actual leaves, wet
soil in long troughs, lemon trees in tubs on the terraces. Quiet: carpets, real wood, thick glass; the loudest sound is rain on
the Founders' Gutter and a fountain somewhere that runs all the time because it can. Cool, dry, the air faintly scented on
purpose. The smell is of nothing, which in the Stack is the most expensive smell there is.

**Who.** Some forty households: Halden board families, shareholders who live in other towers half the year, the Varne family
of Skywater, a few who bought their way up after the Drowning when the top was cut into titles and sold. Around them about
six hundred staff: cooks, valets, gardeners, the disposal valets, nannies, Sovereign-tier HPS officers who live with the
families they guard, and the Crown's own doctors.

**For sale.** Nothing, openly. The Crown has no shops, only suppliers. Everything is ordered: by a household's steward, from a
supplier on the steward's list, delivered to the Postern on 93, assayed, and carried in by staff. Being on a steward's list is
the most valuable thing a supplier in the Stack can have, and every supplier on it got there through someone.

**Squares and utilities.** Not leased: held as **title** (a title on 92 last changed hands for 1.4 million cr). Water is rain
from their own roof, plus Halden Pure on request, never billed. Power from their own glass, and the Steps never shed them. Air
from the 90 Lung, whose plates are replaced from a stock of spares the Company keeps for nobody else.

#### Places in the Crown

**1. The Ninety (floor 90).** The mechanical floor: the 90 Lung, the roof cisterns, the top Step, the Cradle's winding room.
Wrapped around them on the Shaft's outer ring is **the Ninety Gate**, the one way in from below: the Glass Bank's top stop, a
decontamination arch that blows a mist of something floral over you, an HPS Crown Detail desk, and a long bench where visitors
wait to be collected. There is no public stair to 90.

**2. The Pantry (a half-floor inside 90).** Between the cisterns and the Lung's ducts, on a steel mezzanine nobody in the
Crown has seen, about three hundred of the Crown's staff sleep in stacked bunks, ten to a bay, under the drip of the cistern
overflow. They pay rent to their employers out of their wages at a Crown rate, prorated for "shared amenity". The Pantry has
the best water and the worst air in the Crown, and the staff joke that they live inside the Crown's lungs.

**In play:** Pantry staff are the Crown's back door. A Pantry cook might send a slip for something the household would never
order (Mills pickles, a real Vitabrick, Sump-grade gin), paid in cash and in kitchen leftovers that are better than anything the
player has seen. These are small, low-pay, high-reward contracts that need no Crown clearance because the drone lands at the
Postern under "staff consignment".

**3. Floors 91 and 92: the Under-Crown.** The lower Crown residences, the "first floors" of new money: the Director of HPS on
91, the Crown physicians, two families who bought in after Y70. The 92 title sale is the number every Middle family can quote.

**4. The Postern (floor 93).** The Crown's receiving dock. Every licensed drone that reaches the Crown lands here unless it has
private clearance: a long room of numbered landing cradles, inspection benches, and the **Crown Assay**, a small lab whose job
is to tell real from synthetic, rain from tap, honey from syrup, wool from blend. Approved goods go up to the households in
padded trolleys. Rejected goods go to the **reject bench**, where a disposal valet slashes or stamps them VOID and drops them
down the North Chute, with a printed **rejection slip** returned to the supplier's drone so that they know.

**In play:** the Postern is where Crown contracts actually land. A Crown slip says "deliver to Postern cradle 14". The Assay is
a pass/fail check on grade: an item marked synthetic, or real but uncertified, fails, and comes back as a rejection slip and no
pay. The goods do not come back. They fall.

**5. Orchard House (floors 96 and 97).** A two-storey glass orchard inside the Crown, owned by the **Vellacott** household:
lemons, figs, a quince, a grape on a wire, and **the Apple**, said to be the only apple tree in Calder Bay, eighty years old, in
a founding-era planter the size of a Mills lease. The Vellacotts hold a private landing licence, and Orchard House has the only
private drone pad between the Lantern and the Postern: **the Orchard Pad**, a glass shelf on the Seaward face of 96.

**In play:** floor 96 is a named destination because of the Orchard Pad: the one place in the Crown a licensed drone can land
without going through the Postern, if it carries a Vellacott clearance card. Orchard House orders real things for the trees:
compost (from real food only, which the player can make from the right chute), **pollination brushes** (no bees survive in the
Stack; the Apple is hand-pollinated every spring by a gardener with a brush made of real hair), glass cloches, seaweed for mulch
from the Flats (the Tide Folk sell it). It pays in credits and, in a good year, in fruit. A single real apple in the player's
bay is worth more than the contract.

**Hook:** the Apple does not set fruit any more. The Vellacotts have kept this secret for three years; the apples served at
their autumn table come from Greenhold by courier. The gardener knows why: the planter's drainage was plumbed into a line that
now runs warm. Warm from below.

**6. The Long Bath (floor 99).** A heated pool thirty metres long under glass, with a view of the whole drowned bay. The
Archivists' water figures put a good part of the Crown's 36 per cent here.

**7. The Assembly (floor 100).** The members' rooms: dining, a library of real paper books, and **the Founders' Room**, which
keeps Marrow Teague's original architectural model of the Stack under glass, every floor removable, every floor of the Plinth
painted grey and unlabelled.

**8. The Lantern (floors 101–104).** Halden's own: the Company's executive offices on 101 and 102, the family's apartments on
103 and 104. Lantern Reception on 101 keeps the first cell off Line One in a glass case (see 01). The Lantern breathes from the
90 Lung through sealed ducts and its windows have never opened.

**9. The roof.** The Founders' Gutter, the roof cisterns' inlets, the Mast, the Crown Pad, the eight riser caps.

### The Terraces (60–89)

**Standing there.** Carpet tiles over service tiles; frosted glass corridor fronts with names etched on them; real plants in
pots, rented from a plant service that swaps them out before they look tired. Daylight through windows on every corridor,
because the Terraces are narrow enough that no flat is far from glass. The hum of air handling, soft lift chimes, the pager
tone of the clinics. The smell is coffee (real on 75, "coff" elsewhere), disinfectant, and the scent cartridges of Fresh Plus
vents. Above 80, people speak quietly in corridors as if somebody important might be asleep.

**Who.** Halden managers and their families; Orrin, Vey and Skywater staff above shift level; lawyers who argue before the
Arbiter; doctors; Bureau stampers' supervisors; inspectors' managers; senior Lift Guild engineers; the people who run the
Ledger. Many of them grew up in the Middle and keep a small, specific fear of it.

**For sale.** Services more than things: clinics, lawyers, grading, restaurants, tutors, the plant service. Goods come by
order or from **Halden Select** on 70, the store branch that does not take scrip.

**Rent and utilities.** A standard board is 1,500 to 2,750 cr a week on 60–74, 3,000 to 4,750 on 75–89. Water a flat 140 cr a week, "unlimited".
Air metered by vent register in tiers. Power never shed. A Terraces flat runs to something like 6,000 cr a week, which is two
hundred days of a Mills labourer's wage.

#### Places in the Terraces

**10. The Sixty (floor 60).** The mechanical floor: the 60 Lung and its intakes, **the Gills** (louvres around floors 60 to 62),
the middle Step, the Locals' winding room. Around it, **the Sixty Walk**: a four-metre garden promenade on the setback, glass
fronts opening onto it, benches, a coffee kiosk. And **the Sixty Gate** on the Long Stair, where Mills and Middle people with
passes are checked and logged. The line where corridor fronts turn to glass is **the Glass Line**; on the stair wall below
the gate, someone keeps a column of chalk marks with the year beside each floor it has reached (80, 70, 66, 63, 60).

**11. Station Sixty-One (floor 61).** HPS headquarters: dispatch, records, the armoury of wands and cheap cells, and **the
Pen**, where custody is charged by the square at a Terraces rate (see 05). The Pen's windows look out over the Shelf thirty
floors below.

**12. The Calder Rooms (floor 63).** The two rented rooms where the drowned city's government sat after the Drowning (see 05).
The lease ran out. They are a Vey storeroom now, and the door still has the city's seal on it under a sticker.

**13. Vey Sixty-Four (floors 64 and 65).** Vey's private clinic: day surgery, dentistry, the "lung lounge" where Terraces
residents breathe bottled air for their health, and, by the back lift, the **plasma desk** where donors from below are paid by
the bag (the plasma buyers resell to St. Ober's Spire). Its sharps and dressings go down the South Chute in sealed tubes.

**In play:** Vey contracts are clean, exacting and pay well: sterile packing (a sealed bay), exact counts, no grade below
certified. A medical slip arriving at a Mills workshop is a sign the player's Standing has risen.

**14. The Bureau (floor 66).** The Brand Integrity Bureau (see 05): the stampers' offices, evidence rooms full of seized
restored goods, and **the Stamp Room**, where bulk returns and seizures are voided with a pneumatic press before going down
the West Chute on clearance days.

**In play:** a **Bureau clearance day** is a West Chute event: the Bureau dumps a whole evidence room. Everything is voided,
and much of it is restored goods confiscated from people the player knows. Restoring them a second time is a quiet kind of
revenge, and twice as illegal.

**15. The Grading House (floor 68).** Halden's assay office for goods bound up the tower: it certifies **Real** grade for a fee
and issues a certificate slip that rides with the goods. Grow beds, honey, wool, leather, rain: anything that claims to be
real. A certified item carries a small lead tag. The Grading House also certifies the opposite: **Real (uncertified)** goods are
simply goods that have not paid.

**In play:** the player can send produce to the Grading House by drone (a short contract with itself as client): pay the fee,
wait hours, get the goods back tagged Certified, or rejected with a reason ("irrigated with service water", "trace salt"). The
Postern on 93 accepts only certified goods.

**16. Seventy (floor 70).** **Halden Select**, the Terraces store; **Nettlefold Settlement**, the debt firm that writes to
employers; and **Tenancy House**, the Company's Lease Office headquarters, where Floor Rate Cards are drafted and
reclassifications decided.

**17. The Count (floor 71).** The climate-controlled room that holds the **Halden Ledger** (see 04); water
licences are issued downstairs by the Water Wellbeing Office on 46 (see 03). The Count's cooling vents onto a light well; in winter Middle children on 58 warm
their hands at a grille that breathes the Ledger's heat.

**18. Lantern Row (floor 75).** A corridor of restaurants with glass fronts on the Seaward side: Orrin's flagship **Provisions
Room**, a fish place that serves farmed fish from Greenhold, a noodle bar that charges forty times the Mills price for the same
noodle with a real egg. Their kitchens feed the East Chute; Lantern Row's busy nights are the Kitchen's best mornings.

**In play:** Lantern Row kitchens buy **real** herbs, eggs, greens and mushrooms in small, frequent, urgent contracts (deadline
in hours, pay high, real grade, certified). They are the classic "first contract above 60" for a player with a grow bed.

**19. The Arbiter's Office (floor 77).** A room with one desk, one chair on each side, and a window. See 05.

**20. Orrin House (floor 80).** Orrin Provisions' head office and **test kitchen**, where new Vitabrick flavours are tasted by
staff who do not eat Vitabrick. Their rejects go down the East Chute in sealed trays.

**Hook:** Orrin's test kitchen is trialling a "Real-Blend" brick at 15 per cent real. To call it real-blend, the real part must
come from certified growers, and Orrin has none below 60. A Mills grower who could pass the Grading House would be asked to
sign an exclusive supply contract, at a price that seems generous for exactly one year.

**21. The Glasshouses (floors 84 to 87).** Four floors of hydroponic growing under licence from **Greenhold**, run for the Crown
households and Lantern Row: lettuces in trays, tomatoes on strings, herbs in towers, everything patented, every seed counted out
and every plant destroyed after harvest so no seed can walk. The Glasshouses buy from the Mills only what they cannot grow in
volume: specific heirloom herbs, edible flowers, live insects for the fish place's "wild" menu.

**Hook:** seed savers say the Glasshouses' destroyed plants go down the West Chute macerated, and that one in a few hundred
seeds survives the macerator. A North-quadrant Mills grower who sifts every bag of West Chute pulp has, in eleven years, raised
nine plants that Greenhold says do not exist outside its vault.

**22. Skywater Hall (floor 88).** Varne & Daughters' bottling hall: rain filtered, UV-treated, put into heavy green glass and
wax-sealed. Its rinse water is the cleanest grey in the tower and goes down the soft line. Its broken bottles go down the
North Chute; a green Skywater bottle with its neck intact is worth 2 cr to a counterfeiter in the Middle.

**23. The Terraces Header (floor 89).** The top break tank, and the top of the Long Stair. The capped rain downpipes end here:
the cap is a steel plate bolted over a founding-era pipe with RAIN cast in its collar.

### The Middle (30–59)

**Standing there.** Corridors. Numbered doors, a meter box beside each, a vent register above each, a light over each that is
on a timer. Painted floors (each building-quarter a different colour, so children can find home). Laundry strung across light
wells. Brownglass on the outer ring giving everything a tea-coloured afternoon. Inner flats, far from any window, lit by
founding-era **light pipes** that bring daylight in from the facade through a fibre bundle and are failing one by one; a flat
with a working light pipe advertises it. The sound is radios, the Locals' chime, school bells on 50, the tide-like breathing of
the 30 Lung's up-feed, a neighbour's argument through a wall that is thinner than it was built. The smell is cooking (real
onions now and then, which a whole corridor notices), detergent, damp in the inner corridors where the light pipes have failed.

**Who.** Skilled workers and the people who keep the tower running: Lift Guild operators, meter readers, nurses, teachers,
licensed tradespeople, lower Halden staff, Protectives (who live rent-free on 53 to 55 while they serve), Orrin and Vey
counter staff. Families. Everyone in the Middle is about two missed rents from the Mills, and everyone knows it, and it shows
in how carefully they dress.

**For sale.** Licensed things. **The Supply** (Halden General Supply, branches on 33, 44, 55) with its painted queue lines;
Orrin outlets; licensed water kiosks run by certified providers with brass plaques; licensed repair shops; tutors; small
cafés; a lot of things that are the Mills' things, cleaned, relabelled and sold at three times the price.

**Rent and utilities.** A standard board is 300 to 450 cr a week on 30–39, 500 to 700 on 40–49, 750 to 1,100 on 50–59. Everything metered at the
door: tap, socket, vent register. Water always on, at low pressure in the afternoon. Power shed in rotation at slack hour,
an hour at a time by building-quarter, announced on the corridor lights by a blink.

#### Places in the Middle

**24. The Thirty (floor 30).** The busiest floor in the Stack. Inside the Shaft's base: the mechanical floor (the 30 Lung, the
bottom Step, the Middle's cisterns), **the Turnstile** on the Long Stair, and **Thirty Station**, the Tube's main station,
where the Lift Guild's **Lane Desk** issues lane permits and transponders. Outside, on the Plinth's roof: **the Shelf**.

**25. The Shelf (floor 30, open air).** Two hectares of cracked roof membrane, half-buried skylights, the stumps of the Common's
trees in their planters (some of them still alive), and on top of it all **the Shelf Market**: four hundred stalls under tarps,
food carts, a dye stall, a fish-paste kitchen, the **Long Pads** (open-air drone pads for inter-tower couriers; see 07), and the
30 Lung's louvres breathing it all in. The Shelf is where the Mills and the Middle meet on purpose, and it is where things
land: anything dropped, thrown or blown off a balcony in the Shaft falls on the Shelf, and **shelf-walkers** go round at dawn
with sacks.

**In play:** the Shelf is the lowest place a licensed lane drone can land in open air, and the cheapest place to meet a
Middle client. Shelf contracts: market stalls ordering stock in bulk, short deadlines, cash. **Shelf rain** (the dawn pickings)
is a cheap supplier for the player's counter: a shelf-walker comes by with a sack and a price.

**26. The Dry Font (landing 30, North Stair).** The welded RAIN tap where the Rain Church began (see 06a), its weld beads layered
like tree rings, nineteen of them. Cups are always left under it. On rain nights there is a crowd on the stairs.

**27. The Forty Plaque (landing 31, South Stair).** The founders' memorial to the construction dead, moved indoors in Y63, now
behind a vending machine that sells Orrin noodle bricks (see 01).

**28. The Guild's public branch office (floor 34), a counter of Car House on 45:** a copy of the Ride Book (the books themselves stay at Car House), the training room with a cut-away Kessin car, a bar
for members, and the office where freight chits are punched. Drone couriers are not welcome; a poster in the window shows a
drone with a red line through it and the words SHAFTS ARE FOR LIFTS.

**29. Meter Hall (floor 38).** Where you dispute a bill. A hall with a numbered ticket machine, sixty plastic chairs bolted
in rows, and nine windows of which three are open. The founding-era **reading board** on the end wall, a grid of brass
counters for every meter in the Middle, still turns, and nobody at the windows looks at it.

**30. The Gap (floor 41).** The South Stair's fallen landing and its bridge. The **Ambler family** has kept the bridge since
Y64 and charges a token a crossing. Below the bridge you can see straight down the stairwell to 30. Shaft rats use the Gap: a
drone can drop through the hole in the stair and skip ten floors of turnstiles, if it knows the way.

**31. Forty-Four (floor 44).** The Middle's centre: the largest Supply branch, **Post Forty-Four** (HPS, with interview rooms),
the stairs where the **Breath Riots** happened in Y43, and the Company's floor office where the air billing was announced. The
stair landing has a small brass plate on the wall that says only *Y43*, put there by nobody.

**32. Brisk & Lowe (floor 48).** The debt buyers' office: cream cards, navy borders (see 05). A whole corridor of their
collectors' flats around it.

**33. The Halden School (floor 50).** The company school, three floors, where Middle children are trained for Company posts and
Mills children can enrol if they pass an exam and pay a fee the size of a year's rent. The Protectives' quarters fill 53 to 55.

**34. The Reclaim (floor 59).** Halden's sorting station on all four chutes, with the Middle Header beside it. A hall of
founding-era sorters, conveyor stubs, and bins marked by metal type, and the overnight shutter that makes the Morning Fall.
Four Company sorters work it on day shift, and sell what the machines miss through the back door on 58.

**In play:** a contact in the Reclaim is a supplier of a specific kind: cells, copper, Halden-numbered parts, at "back door"
prices. And the Reclaim is the target of the **Reclaim Fault** chute event.

### The Mills (10–29)

**Standing there.** A hall the size of a town square, six metres to a ceiling of founding-era light fittings, most of them
dead, and a gantry crane parked on its rails over everyone's heads for forty-five years. The floor is service tiles with the
Works' yellow safety lines still painted on them and the Company's white lease lines painted over those. The hall is cut into
pens by mesh, sheet, pallet walls, curtains and shelving; aisles run between them along the old line routes, and the aisles
are named after what used to run down them ("Coater Run", "Bake Road"). Mezzanines everywhere: bunks above workshops, reached
by ladders. Purple grow light leaks over partitions; amber work lamps; the green glow of a cut window at the end of an aisle.

The sound is a hall full of small machines: fans, pumps, the whine of a grinder, a press thumping, radios on three stations,
the chirp of crickets from the insect halls (on 11 to 13 you hear it through the floor), the plant's dead conveyors creaking as
the building moves, kids, a hawker. At 06:00 the chutes roar. At 06:00, 14:00 and 22:00 the **Works Whistle** blows from
somewhere in the core: a founding-era shift whistle on a timer nobody has found the controller for, still calling the shifts of
a factory that stopped employing anyone forty-five years ago. The Crown had it muffled above 60 in Y58.

The smell: warm insects (nutty, with ammonia under it), wet soil, solder, hot cells, frying, a still somewhere, people.

**Who.** Workshop keepers, Co-op growers, insect ranchers and the people who work for them, day labourers who go up to the
Middle at dawn, hot-bunk sleepers, refurbishers and restorers, Old Hands, stinkers, vent kids, the families of all of the above.
Sublet barons who hold whole aisles. People who came up from the Sump and people who fell down from the Middle.

**For sale.** Everything second-hand, repaired, restored or made here: protein in bulk, produce that goes up, seeds, parts, cells
(charged anywhere for half the store price), clothes from the chute, tools, services. Prices in the Mills are low and
negotiable, and paid in credits, scrip, Co-op produce chits, cells or favours. **The Hatch**, the store branch behind its
grille, is on 11, 18 and 27.

**Rent and utilities.** A standard board is 75 cr a week on 10–13, 100 to 150 on 14–19 (17: 125), 175 to 225 on 20, 21 and 27–29. The Atmosphere Levy on
top. A shared **tap post** per aisle with a key-meter per lease, tap hours twice a day; a **socket board** per lease, shed at
slack hour. Salt-line outlets in most halls for flushing, free and poisonous.

The Mills floor by floor follows in Part four (floor 17 and its neighbours) and in the Mills gazetteer below it. In short, the
Works lines are still legible in the halls: cell lines in the lower Mills (10–16), chip lines in the middle (17–21), the Clean
Core sealed above them (22–26), the old control and assembly floors at the top (27–29).

### The Sump (4–9)

**Standing there.** Wet. Standing water on 4 at every spring tide and a tidemark on the walls at knee height; damp everywhere
above that. The grey is sick: spalled ceilings, rust stains running down every column, rebar showing. Lamps on long cables,
luminous paint on stair edges, and in the middle of every Sump floor from 5 to 8 the glow and hum of the **Waterhouse**: a
fortified block of founding-era plant behind blast doors, lit all night, with HPS posts at its gates and its own lift. The
Sump lives around it the way a village lives around a castle.

The sound: the tide booming in the drowned floors below, a slow wet thud you feel in your feet twice a day; pumps; the Jaws on
8 when the Morning Fall lands; water dripping in a hundred places; gulls at the Landing. The smell: salt, rot, methane from
the drains, and a sweet chemical breath that rises from the drowned floors at low tide, which old people say smells exactly
like the forming rooms used to.

**Who.** Bale pickers, grate pickers (combing the Outfall's grates), bilge pumpers working off Varga debts, divers and their
families, gangs and fixers, plasma buyers' runners, people evicted from above, Flats folk who come up to trade at the Landing,
and the Waterhouse's own few Company staff, who do not live here and come and go by their lift.

**For sale.** Salvage from the drowned floors and the Flats (first-fit parts, if you know who to ask), chute bales, stinker
water, cells charged off stolen lines at half the store price, plasma money, gin, and labour by the hour.

**Rent and utilities.** A standard board is 25 cr a week on 4–5 (wet), 50 on 6–9. Halden barely collects; sublet barons and the gangs collect
instead, as "floor dues". Water from standpipes, one hour a day, in a queue, or from a stinker. Power shed first; most of it
is drawn from tapped cables that run up from the Race, paid for to whichever gang holds that cable.

#### Places in the Sump

**35. The Brink (floor 4).** The lowest dry floor, and not always dry. The **Landing** on the Landward face, a set of old loading
doors opening onto floating pontoons where Flats boats tie up and the Tide Folk trade (see 06a, 07). The **Spill Door** further
along the same wall, where unsold bales go out onto the mud at low tide with a Company clerk ticking them off. Rent is lowest
here, and every tenant keeps their goods on pallets.

**36. The Moat (floor 5).** The ring of stalls, bunks and workshops that presses up against the Waterhouse's outer wall on 5,
around its south gate. Everyone who lives off the Waterhouse lives here: people who sell to its staff, stinkers who distil its
waste brine, pump fixers who get called in when the Company's own cannot, and the gang that "keeps the gate quiet" for a fee
from the Company that nobody admits to. The Moat's best-known place is **Saint Pump**, a shrine around a dead founding-era
pump with a drip-candle on it, where divers leave a bolt before going down.

**In play:** floor 5 is the classic downward destination: the Moat is where a Sump client lives. Moat contracts want what the
Sump cannot make: protein in bulk, filters, medicine, cells, seed, dry clothes. They pay little, often in salvage instead of
credits, and the salvage is the point: a Moat reward bay is where a first-fit part turns up.

**37. Sixes (floor 6).** The Sump's market, under a vaulted hall that used to store Works chemicals and still has hazard
diamonds stencilled on the columns. Open all day, busiest at low tide when the Flats come up. **The Varga Books**, the Varga family's
debt house, keeps its chalk books on the walls of a dry room here (see 05).

**38. Dive Hall (floor 7).** Where the drowned floors are entered. A founding-era freight well, its car long gone, opened at
floor 7 and flooded below 4: **the Well**. Divers kit up on benches around it (masks, hoses, weighted belts, lights) and go down
on a rope to 3 and below, with their families on the pumps above them. A board on the wall lists who is down, and when.

**39. The Bale Hall (floor 8).** The old sorting rooms where all four chutes end. The **Jaws** (three of four working), the bale
pickers' gallery, the bale stacks waiting for buyers or the Spill Door. Next door, the Sump's only **Supply** branch (open three
days a week), the Waterhouse's bottling line, and **the Old Post**, a bar in a former HPS post whose cage front keeps the drunks
away from the bottles.

**40. The Nine Dock (floor 9).** A cantilevered platform on the Seaward face with a gangway stair down to the water: the Stack's
official dock, where Company boats and inter-tower freight call, and where **the boat** to Bastion leaves every Thursday with its
work-off debtors (see 05). Families stand on the outer stair to watch it go. Behind the dock, inside the Waterhouse perimeter, the
**Power Hall** with its two working rows of storage banks.

### The drowned floors (0–3)

Black water, silt to the knee, the Works as it was on the night of the Drowning. Divers know them as rooms, not floors.

**41. The Gate Floor (floor 3).** The old street floor: the public doors, the lobby where Ansel Halden's portrait still hangs
under three metres of water, the loading quays where trucks backed in from Calder's streets, the **Intake** where the
Waterhouse still draws the bay. Closest to the surface; best light for divers; most picked over.

**42. The Polishing Hall (floors 1 and 2).** The Works' water plant: the founding-era membrane trains, ion beds and tanks that
made ultrapure water, and the **spares stores**, racks of sealed crates that held replacement cartridges, pumps, plates and
lift rail. Divers have found the casings empty (see 03).

**43. The Well Hall (floor 0).** Forty wellheads, capped, that pumped the groundwater out from under Calder for forty years,
which is to say the machines that sank the city. Below it, the **Race**, the cooling channel where the tide turbines turn.
Divers do not go into the Race. The current would take them out to the bay.

**In play:** the drowned floors are not a place the player goes in this game (no night expeditions). They are a **supplier**.
Divers' salvage reaches the player through Moat clients and Sixes middlemen, as reward bays and as a counter seller. A diver
client's contract is for things the dive needs: rope, sealant, cells in sealed cases, food that keeps, a lamp. The reward is
something from below, often unidentified, sometimes first-fit.

**Hook:** divers who go to the Polishing Hall swear the water there is clearer than anywhere else in the bay. Not clean: clear.
Something down there is still filtering it.

## Part three: the Dark Floors (22–26)

### What everyone can see

The Clean Core was Line Nine, opened in Y26: the newest hall of the Works, five floors of clean rooms making high-density
cells and the chips for them (see 01). In Y38 it went lights-out for a ninety-day trial that never ended. In Y40 the welders
sealed its stair doors "for contamination control". On the night after the Last Shift in Y41 its lights came on, and they have
come on every night since. Nobody from the tower has worked there for forty-five years.

From the outside, this is what the Dark Floors are to the people who live around them:

**The Welds.** On the North and South stairs, the landing doors on 22, 23, 24, 25 and 26 are steel plates welded into their
frames. Each plate has the same stencil: CLEAN CORE / CONTAMINATION CONTROL / Y40, and a Works part number. Graffiti does not
stay on them; the plates are cleaned. Nobody in the Mills admits to cleaning them.

**The Blind Flights.** The four Corner Stairs of the Plinth pass through 22 to 26 too, but here the founders' design did
something odd: at those five floors the corner stairwells are lined in steel sheet, floor to ceiling, and there are no landings
at all, just ten flights of stair without a door between 21 and 27. Climbing them, you feel the wall get warm under your hand
around 24. People call them the Blind Flights and do not linger.

**The Sleeve.** Old Two passes 22 to 26 without stopping. Its doors on those floors are plated over. Riders say the car gets
warm for about twenty seconds on the way through. Every core shaft that crosses the Dark Floors (the risers, the Tube, the
chutes, the Lung trunks) passes through in a sealed steel **Sleeve**: you can go through, you cannot get out.

**The yellow.** From about 22:00 to 05:30, a thin amber light shows under the welded plates and through the hairline cracks in
the ceiling of 21. It is the colour of a lithography safelight: the yellow that chip halls used, because it does not expose the
resist. In the day there is nothing. The Old Hands who worked Lines Four to Six recognise it at once and do not like talking
about it.

**The hum.** When the yellow comes on, so does the hum: a low, steady note you feel more than hear, from 21 up to 27, with
occasional ticks and a pneumatic sigh every few minutes. It starts within a minute of the Works Whistle's 22:00 blast, the old
night shift, and stops before the 06:00 one. The Clean Core works the night shift, exactly, and has for forty-five years.

**The heat.** The ceiling of 21 and the floor of 27 are warm through every night and stay warm into the morning. On 21 a hand
on the ceiling in the small hours finds it at blood heat. Insect ranchers will pay almost anything for squares on 21.

**The draw.** On the meters, the Clean Core does not exist. On the Bus, it is the biggest load in the Stack after the Crown. The
Common Load Adjustment on 19, 20 and 21 (see 04) is four times anyone else's and highest at night. On long-run nights the Steps
cannot carry both the Clean Core and the Mills, and the Mills are shed: a **dawn slack**, sockets dead from 05:00 into the
first hours of the working day, which the tide table does not predict.

**The water.** The Waterhouse's published figures are a sixth short of what its working cartridges should make. The missing
sixth runs up **Process Line 3**, a founding-era ultrapure line on the blueprints that ends at floor 22 (see 03). On some
nights Mills taps lose a third of their pressure for no announced reason. The nights match the hum.

**The riser.** Of the Works' eight exhaust risers, seven are cold and serve as free shafts. **R6**, which starts in the Clean
Core, is warm. Its cap under the Lantern is warm. Drones flown up R6 come out with a film of something on their rotors that
smells, very faintly, of the forming rooms.

### What nobody can see

How anything gets in or out. A clean room making cells and chips needs raw materials (cathode powders, foils, wafers,
solvents, gases), maintenance, and a way to ship its product. None of it goes past anyone's eyes. The candidates:

- **Lift Zero**, the Blind Lift, in its own doorless well behind core E. The Lift Guild says it is "Company plant" and does
  not service it. It must be serviced by someone.
- **The ninth riser**, R9, "reserve, capped" in Marrow Teague's first drawings and absent from every later sheet (see 01).
  Shaft rats say there is a sealed duct from the Dark Floors to somewhere above 90.
- **The Crown Pad**, where a craft lands every ninth night at 03:00 and takes on cargo already waiting on the pad (see Part one).
- **The Race**, under floor 0. Flats folk say that on some mornings there is a sheen on the water where the Race comes out, and
  the mussels on that stretch of the old seawall do not open.

**In play:**
- **Warm floors.** Floors 21 and 27 carry a **warm** floor attribute: an insect bin there runs its overnight cycle without its
  heater (no power drawn in the night block). Rent is higher on both (7 to 9 cr), and Halden bills a **thermal amenity** line on
  top. A player who moves to 21 trades rent for power. It is a floor property, read by the bin, not proximity.
- **Dawn slack.** An event the player cannot predict from the tide table: some mornings the sockets are dead until 09:00. A cell
  rack saves the morning. A player who logs the hum (an hour-by-hour slip on the wall, for a client) starts to see the pattern.
- **Logging the Dark Floors** is a contract line for the Archivists, the Old Hands and Static, the pirate radio: pay in
  information, tokens and standing, never much money. Meter reader **Benedek Sarr**'s six-year notebook of Common Load readings
  (see 04) is the prize item of this line.
- **Fresh cells.** Now and then the North or West Chute drops an H-cell whose date code is this year and whose plant code is
  **HW-9**: Halden Works, Line Nine. The Company says all new H-cells are made under contract at Bastion and their plant code is
  BX. A cell marked HW-9 is a curio worth ten times a normal cell to the Archivists, and worth a visit from the Bureau if it is
  seen on a shelf.

### The requisitions

**Hook (main version):** every few weeks, a slip appears on floor 17's lobby board that nobody pinned. It is printed on Works
requisition stock, a form the Works stopped using in Y41, in the old dot-matrix type, and it is addressed to **STORES 17-C**. It
asks for something small and specific: "PLATE, LUNG, ELECTROSTATIC, 1 EA, ANY COND." "GLOVE, CLEANROOM, NITRILE, LINE 4 ISSUE,
12 PR." "CLOCK, WALL, SYNC, FIRST-FIT, 1 EA." The delivery point is a call plate on the wall of Lift Zero's well on floor 21,
behind a sublet baron's bunk partition, a brass plate with a slot that nobody had noticed until the first slip came. Goods
placed in the slot are gone by morning. Payment arrives in the player's drone bay the next time it lands: fresh HW-9 cells, a
founding-era part, once a sealed roll of Works scrip from Y40 that no store will take.

The player's lease is inside what was, in the Works years, the **Line Four stores cage**: the fenced corner of Four Hall where
the line's consumables were kept and issued. The Clean Core's automated procurement still sends its requisitions to Stores.
Stores, as far as the Works' system knows, is still on 17, sector C. It is the player.

**Alternative:** the slips are from a person. Someone from Line Nine never left, or someone has been put in there since, and the
requisition stock is all they have to write on.

**Alternative:** the slips are from the Company. An engineer in the Lantern who runs the Clean Core remotely has found it cheaper
to get small parts from a Mills workshop through an old system than through Halden's own procurement, which would ask questions
about what the Clean Core is doing.

**Alternative:** there are no slips. The Dark Floors are exactly what the Company says they are when it says anything: "a
legacy continuous-process installation operating under contract". The yellow is safelights on a timer, the hum is cooling, the
cells are Bastion's with an old code on them, and the mystery is what the Mills need it to be.

**Hook:** Aldo Moyle's pencil map of the tower's clock **sync nodes** (see 05) has three nodes on 22 to 26. Founding-era clocks
take their time from them. Every wall clock on floor 17 that still syncs is four minutes fast, and so is the Works Whistle,
which means the Clean Core's "22:00" is the Works' 22:00, not the Company's.

**Hook:** the control room on 27 that engineers watched the trial from was "moved somewhere else" in Y38. The room on 27 is now
part of a lease (see Overhum below). Its window onto the Clean Core is bricked. The bricks are newer than the wall, and warm.

## Part four: floor 17 and its neighbours

### Four Hall (floor 17)

Floor 17 was **Line Four**: control chips, wafer lithography and etch, the first of the three chip lines (see 01). It was the
hall where the gloves went on the wall. The Mills call it **Four Hall**, and its people are **Fourers**. It became workshop units
in Y58 and is now the Mills' repair floor, because a chip line leaves behind exactly what a repair trade needs: power at every
pillar, founding-era benches bolted to the floor, good drains, and enough people who once knew how to work under a microscope.

**The shape.** A single hall about 180 by 140 metres, the core rising through its middle, cut into four sectors (A to D) by the
two cross-aisles that meet at the core. Each sector is cut into leases along **Runs**, the aisles that follow the old line
routes:

- **Litho Run** (sector A): the old lithography bays. Narrow leases, low mezzanines, some amber safelight fittings still in the
  ceiling, painted over in blue so they do not fog anything.
- **Wet Run** (sector B): the old wet benches where wafers were etched and rinsed. Every lease has a founding-era drain trough,
  so this is where the hall's growers, washers, dyers and stinkers cluster.
- **Stores Run** (sector C): the old line stores and the issuing counter, the tool cribs, the lift lobby, the lobby board. The
  player is here, in what was the **Line Four stores cage**: a fenced corner of the old stores, now the player's starting
  lease, the 25 x 20 board inside the original mesh, with a column near the back wall (the pillar in `04-economy.md`) and the
  old issuing counter still bolted to the frontage.
- **Glove Run** (sector D): along the west wall, where the **Glove Wall** is. Leases backing onto it are cheaper; the gloves have
  been grey for forty years and still move a little when the Lung's air comes through.

**Alternative:** the stores cage is a neighbour's lease, not the player's, and the requisition slips arrive for a tenant who
left in a hurry. Taking over the cage (a lease extension when it falls vacant) is how the player inherits the requisitions.

**Numbers.** About 430 leases; about three thousand people, of whom some eleven hundred sleep on the floor
and the rest come in to work. Rent 125 cr a week for a standard board. Catch rights on all four trunks; the player's starting
catch is a one-in-twelve on the East Chute (the Kitchen), on the catch apron at the back of the cage.

**What 17 has.**
- **The lobby board** at the lift lobby, where contract slips are pinned at 07:00 and drones' returning slips are posted, with
  the warden's **regard** pip beside it (see 05). The board is a founding-era shift board, a steel grid with numbered clips; the
  shift numbers are still painted along the top.
- **Old Two's stop**, at the lift lobby, every two hours each way.
- **Tap post 17-C3**, shared by the forty leases on Stores Run, with key-meters, tap hours 06:00–09:00 and 18:00–21:00, and a
  queue that forms at 05:45.
- **Socket boards** at every pillar, with tide tables chalked beside them by **Wren**, eleven, who charges 1 cr a week per board
  and is never wrong.
- **The 17-C spur**: Halden's lane spur hatch into the Tube, a transponder gate the size of an oven door set into the core wall.
- **The Three Grate**: a rusted grille in the core wall of sector C into riser R3, cut open by vent kids in Y70 and
  officially "scheduled for repair" ever since. It is the floor's free shaft.
- **The Works Benches**: founding-era ESD benches bolted to the floor in about a hundred leases, black-topped, grounded, with
  built-in magnifier arms. Under Clause 11 they are Company property. Repairers pay a fixture charge for the bench and
  consider it cheap.
- **The Glove Wall.**

**Who is on 17.**

- **Hester Moyle**, the warden, sixty-one, and her brother **Aldo**, in a two-row lease by the lift lobby (see 05). Hester
  takes clocks. Aldo reads them.
- **Gus Tanaka-Breen**, sublet baron, whose hot-bunk house **the Eight Squares** at the far end of Glove Run sleeps twenty in
  three shifts timed to the Works Whistle. (The name is the Company's: its plan shows eight beds.) His sleepers buy from the player's shelves at 06:00, 14:00 and 22:00,
  and only then.
- **Mattias Orme**, ninety, an Old Hand, lithographer on Line Four from Y12 to the Last Shift. Lives on Litho Run with a
  founding-era microscope he will not sell. Repairs chips for the Middle at a tenth of a licensed shop's price and sends half his
  work to whoever he trusts with it. He can identify a first-fit part by its solder.
- **Odile Fenn**, screen refurbisher, two leases from the player on Stores Run. Buys every cracked screen the West Chute drops.
  Competes with the player for the same catch on bad days and lends the player her heat gun on good ones.
- **Yusra Dimitriou**, a Co-op grower on Wet Run, eight grow beds on a drain trough, two Hanging Boxes under the floor's best cut
  window. Sells up to Lantern Row through a Middle broker who takes forty per cent. Wants a grower who can get through the Grading
  House on their own.
- **Mother Ruus**, a stinker on Wet Run whose three-pot lives under the tiles. Hester walks past her door every morning.
- **Wren**, eleven, vent kid. Knows the Three Grate, the underfloor, the drip rights and who is sleeping where. Carries messages,
  clears jams, keeps the tide tables.

**What 17 sounds like at different hours.** 05:45 the tap queue. 06:00 the Whistle and the Morning Fall together, a roar in the
core and four hundred catches banging. 07:00 the board. Through the day: magnifier lamps, heat guns, a press on Wet Run, radios,
the Eight Squares' shift change at 14:00. 17:00 the Peak Window, and machines go off up and down the Runs. 18:00 the tap again.
22:00 the Whistle and, faint through five floors, the hum.

**In play:** floor 17 should feel like a neighbourhood of clients and competitors: Hester's small strange slips, Mattias's
repair work (he sends the jobs he cannot see well enough to do), Odile as a rival for the catch, Yusra as the player's first
route up (and the person whose broker the player can undercut), Gus's sleepers as a shelf rhythm tied to the Whistle, Wren as
the one who clears your jam first if you've been kind.

### Floor 16: the Ovens

Below 17, **Line Three**'s electrode hall: the coating line and the long vacuum drying ovens that baked the coated foil before
it was wound into cells. The ovens are still there, twenty of them, each the size of a shipping container, steel-lined,
insulated, fire-rated. The founders built 16 as the one hall in the Plinth rated for sustained heat, and it is still the only
floor in the Mills where an open flame is tolerated.

So 16 is where the Mills bakes, smokes, dries and fires: bakers in three of the ovens (bread from Co-op grain, flatbread from
anything), a smokehouse for fish from the Flats, a dye works, a glass-blower who melts Skywater bottles, a kiln for clay from
the mud. In the cooling ovens at the end of the hall, people sleep: an oven that held 200 degrees in the Works years holds a
warm night for twelve people now, rented by the berth.

Rent 5.5 cr; the oven leases are dearer. 16 is below 17, so its catches come after the player's on the same trunks: when the
player's catch is full, 16 gets the player's share. Its tenants know exactly which leases on 17 are lazy.

**Hook:** chalker **Dorrit Vance** resurveys the same twelve squares on 16 every quarter (see 04). The squares sit under a sealed
hatch in the ceiling that is not on any floor plan. The hatch is in the floor of 17. It is under the Line Four stores cage. It
was the bottom of the **stores hoist**, a small goods lift that carried requisitioned parts between the lines and the Works'
central stores. Its shaft runs up. Where it goes after 17 is the question.

**In play:** 16 is the player's down-floor neighbour and a production partner: the ovens are a service you can contract (bake
your grain, smoke your fish, dry your herbs, fire your clay) for a fee, with a slip and a drone hop of one floor. A one-floor
contract is the smallest, safest, cheapest launch in the game, and a good tutorial.

### Floor 18: Five Hall

Above 17, **Line Five**: the second chip line, a twin of Four Hall with more of the safelights left working, so that half the
hall still glows amber at night and the plants on 18 grow badly. 18 is workshops, cell chargers and the floor's big landmark:
**the Hatch**, a Supply branch behind a steel grille with a hatch the width of a Vitabrick crate, its catalogue painted on the
wall beside it (see 04).

18 also has a long memory of **the Burst**, the day a Company water main split under Five Hall's tiles and flooded forty leases,
and the Arbiter ruled the tenants owed the water (see 05).

Rent 5.5 to 6 cr. 18's catches are above the player's on every trunk: whatever 18 leaves, the player gets. When 18 is busy, the
player's bin is thin.

**In play:** the Hatch on 18 is the nearest store: scrip spends here, drones and machines are ordered here (and delivered on Old
Two), meter seals are not sold here. A one-floor hop up to the Hatch to collect a parts order is a tiny route, and a lesson in the
spur and the grate.

### The rest of the Mills, floor by floor

**44. Ten (floor 10).** The bottom of the Mills and the bottom of the licensed world. **Ten Station**, the lowest Tube station:
Halden's lanes stop here, and the Company's phrase "service ends at Ten" is used in the Mills for everything from lanes to
ambulances. At the Long Stair, the Co-op's patrol keeps a post at **the Grille**, the iron gate onto the stairs down to the Sump,
and logs who goes down. Rent 3 cr; a floor of storage, porters and people on their way down.

**45. The Chirp (floors 11 to 13).** Line One's formation racks (long heated shelving where new cells took their first charge)
and Line Two's forming hall: warm by design, wired for heat, fitted with drains. The insect ranchers took them over from Y58. On
these floors you hear crickets through the tiles. **The Press** on 11 is Orrin's first press line (Y59): bulk insect intake,
grinders, presses, the Orrin depot that accepts herd tickets, and the Hatch on 11. **One Hall** on 12 keeps a plaque where the
first cell came off Line One. **Two Hall** on 13 is where the Co-op began in Y60, and the Co-op's hall is still there, with its
pencil ledgers, its tool library and its seed shelf.

**46. Three Hall (floor 14).** Line Three's electrolyte-filling floor, whose old dry rooms leak now: noodle stalls, repair shops, the remains of Carrow Lane's still (see
03). Busy, cheap, loud.

**47. The Winding Floor (floor 15).** Line Three's winding hall, now dormitories and hot-bunk houses, partitioned into the narrowest leases in the tower. "Tasters
are on fifteen" means the food and water inspectors are working the dorm kitchens, which they do whenever they are short of a
quota.

**48. Nineteen (floor 19).** Line Six's hall and the Mills' civic floor: **Post Nineteen**, the only HPS post in the band, a
cage-fronted room at the lift lobby; the **Register board**, where Arbiter determinations are posted (see 05); and the **Old
Hands' hall**, whose door carries Marrow Teague's line, "Build it so that nobody needs to understand it to live in it, and so
that somebody always can." The hall has a lathe, a founding-era test bench, a kettle, and the Works' own tool register.

**49. Twenty (floor 20).** Line Seven, assembly: long benches where packs were built, now dormitories and a sublet baron's
empire of curtained bays. **Twenty Station**, the Tube's second station, makes 20 a courier floor: shaft rats and licensed
couriers both keep lockers here. Common Load is high and nobody knows whose it is.

**50. Underhum (floor 21).** Line Eight's floor, directly under the Clean Core. The ceiling is warm at night. Insect ranchers pay
200 cr a week for a board here, with a thermal amenity line on top, and sleep beside their bins in winter. The call plate on Lift Zero's well is here,
behind a bunk partition. The cracks in the ceiling show the yellow.

**51. Overhum (floor 27).** Above the Clean Core. Warm floor. The old **Line Nine control room**, now part of a lease (a
mushroom grower's, because it is dark and warm and the grower asks no questions), its window onto the Core bricked over with
warm bricks. The third Mills **Hatch**. Rent 8 cr.

**52. The Exchange (floor 28).** The old Works' parts and logistics hall, the best market in the Mills: dealers in first-fit parts,
cell graders, seed sellers, a Lift Guild freight office, brokers who buy Mills produce and sell it to the Middle. Many of its
tenants are Middle people priced off the Shelf. The Chalk is one floor above.

**53. The Chalk (floor 29).** "Middle (Transitional)": the Mills Header break tank, glass fronts going up along the Long Stair
side, a Supply counter being fitted, and Middle families in leases that were workshops a year ago. Air meters were installed here
last quarter and are not connected yet. A notice on the lobby wall says "Mills air metering will be introduced for fairness". The
Mills read floor 29 the way farmers read the sky.

**In play:** the Chalk is the Creep made visible. When it moves down a floor (a reclassification event, see 04), the player sees
the new tenants' slips appear on the board first (Middle-styled, asking for things the Mills make, at Mills prices), then the
rate card. A player on 27 or 28 feels it first; a player on 17 can count the floors.

## Part five: how a drone moves through the Stack

### The two kinds of shaft

**The Tube** is the licensed world. It was the founders' pneumatic post: sealed tubes carrying capsules from the Works offices to
stations on every tenth floor. When the pneumatics died after the Drowning, Halden bolted transponder gates where the capsule
stations had been and leased the tubes to drones as **lanes** (see 01). There are four tubes, one per quadrant of the core, called
by their founding letters: **T-North, T-East, T-South, T-West**. They run from Ten Station to the Ninety Gate, with a Crown spur
from 90 to the Postern on 93. Between stations Halden has cut **spur hatches** into the tubes on most floors since Y60, so a
tenant can launch straight into a lane from their own sector; the 17-C spur is one of them.

The Tube's bore is eighty centimetres. That is the single most important number in drone design in the Stack: a lane-licensed
drone must be **Tube gauge**, a frame that fits an 80 cm bore with its rotors folded. Bigger bays cannot fly the lanes at all.

**The risers** are the free world. The Works' eight exhaust and process-gas risers run from the fab halls to the roof, through the
core, each about two metres across, sooty, ribbed with old bracket rings. Seven are cold. Since nobody owns the air inside a
dead exhaust stack, they are **free shafts**: no fee, no transponder, no gate, no insurance and no help.

Along with the risers, the free world includes:
- **The Empty**: Freight One's dead well, 4 to 60. Big enough for an oversize frame. Dark, and the welded doors on every floor
  make it a long, featureless tube where a drone that loses power falls all the way to the water at 4.
- **The Gap**: the hole in the South Stair at 41, which lets a drone in the stairwell skip from 40 to 42 around the Turnstile's
  stair checks.
- **The Lung trunks**: return air flows down them, so flying up a trunk is flying into wind. Slow, and only for small frames.
- **The chutes**: down only. "Chute-diving" is illegal, fast, and hard on cargo.

**The Combs.** In Y58 the Company welded grilles across the risers at 30 and 60, to stop drone traffic bypassing the lanes. Shaft
rats cut **rat holes** through them, the Lift Guild reports the holes, Halden rewelds them, the rats cut new ones; at any moment
each comb has two or three working holes, and the Drop board and the notes on the grates say which. At 89 every riser is closed by **the
Crown Comb**, a solid plate with no hole anyone has ever cut. Nothing reaches the Crown through a free shaft.

### The risers, one by one

| Riser | Runs | Character |
|---|---|---|
| R1, **the Chimney** | 4 to roof | The widest. Sooty. Main free highway of the Mills. Combs at 30 and 60 always have holes. |
| R2 | 6 to roof | Partly collapsed at 47; you leave it at 46 and cross to R1 through a duct. |
| R3, **the Three** | 4 to roof | Serves sector C of every Mills floor. The **Three Grate** on 17 is the player's door. |
| R4, **the Wet One** | 4 to 59 | Condensation drips the whole height; cells short in it. Cheap frames avoid it. |
| R5 | 8 to roof | Vent kids' toll nets on 10 to 14. |
| R6, **the Warm One** | 22 to roof | Starts in the Clean Core. Warm. Fast updraft at night. Nobody likes using it. |
| R7 | 4 to roof | Gang toll at 9 ("the Knot") where it passes the Waterhouse perimeter. |
| R8 | 4 to 89 | Quiet. A Rain Church route: Fontkeepers fly rain down it from the Crown Comb. |

**In play:** the free route is not one roll but a **route choice** at launch: which riser (each with its own trouble table) and
whether to slot a fresh **Drop card** (see 06a) to soften it.

### What trouble means

Trouble in a shaft is never a fight. It is what the brief already proposes: a **toll** off the pay, or **one item lost** from the
bay. In the Stack, it has faces:

- **Toll nets** (vent kids, R5 and others): a net across the shaft; the drone is held until the bay gives up something small,
  usually the item nearest the hatch. Kids take food first.
- **The Knot** (Sump gangs, R7 at 9; R1 and R3 at 6): a fixed toll, deducted on landing as a percentage, recorded in chalk.
- **Rat holes closed** (Company): the drone finds the comb rewelded and turns back. The contract is late.
- **Jams and soot** (the risers themselves): a frame without sealed bays gets cargo soiled; food in an open bay loses grade.
- **Pickers** (the chute): a chute-dived drone lands in the Bale Hall, where the bale pickers "receive" it and keep one item.
- **The Warm One** (R6, nights): no one takes anything. Drones come back with their clocks four minutes fast and a film on their
  rotors.

### From floor 17 to floor 96

**By lane** (the only way):

1. **Paper.** A Crown slip for Orchard House on 96 needs three things packed in the bay with the goods: the slip itself, a
   **Terraces lane permit** (90 cr a week, and Standing of 650 to be sold one; or a single-launch ticket at 3 cr + 0.2 per floor),
   and a **Vellacott clearance card** (issued with the contract, returned with the drone, a 1x1 card on heavy cream stock). Without
   the clearance card the drone can only go to the Postern on 93, like everyone else.
2. **Launch, 17-C spur (hour 0).** The drone spins up on the player's pad (2 hu), crosses the lease to the core wall and enters
   T-East through the 17-C spur. The spur gate reads the transponder and logs the launch.
3. **Twenty Station, Thirty Station (hour 0 to 1).** A short climb, then the first real gate at **the Thirty read**: the Middle's
   station scans the manifest, weighs the bay against the slip, and holds anything that weighs wrong. This is where most Mills
   contraband is caught.
4. **Sixty (hour 1 to 2).** The Sixty gate checks the Terraces permit and the Standing on the transponder's account. Drones with the
   wrong permit are turned back here, with the ticket still charged.
5. **The Ninety Gate (hour 2 to 3).** The Crown Detail's gate. A sniffer arm passes over the bay. Between 22:00 and 06:00 the Crown's
   quiet rule closes the gate entirely; drones that arrive late wait in a holding bay until morning.
6. **The Orchard Pad (hour 3).** With the Vellacott card, the drone leaves the Crown spur through a Seaward hatch on 96 and flies six
   metres outside the glass (the only exception to the Quiet Glass Rule in the Stack) to land on the Orchard Pad. A gardener checks
   the goods by hand. If the order is for the Postern instead, the drone lands in a numbered cradle on 93 and waits for the Assay,
   which takes an hour.
7. **Pay and reward (hour 3 to 4).** On acceptance, payment is credited to the player's Ledger account and the reward is packed into
   the bay by Crown staff: often something the player cannot buy (fruit, a real-wood box, a glass jar with a ground stopper). On
   rejection, a rejection slip goes in the bay, and the goods go down the North Chute.
8. **Home (hour 4 to 5).** Down the tube, quicker than up, through the same gates in reverse, out at the 17-C spur.

About five hours for the round trip in a good morning; six or seven if the gates are busy, a tide surge is late, or the Assay has a
queue. A late afternoon launch to the Crown is a gamble on the quiet rule.

**Without a lane:** a free-shaft drone can reach 89 up R1 or R8 and no further. Getting goods into the Crown from there means a
**grille hand**: one of the Pantry staff on 90 who opens a maintenance hatch on the Terraces Header for a cut and carries the parcel
in as "staff consignment". This is how Mills goods reach Crown kitchens without anyone signing for them, and why Pantry cooks are
the most connected people in the tower.

**In play:** routes up are a stack of requirements that can be **seen in the bay**: permit, card, slip. No menu. A Crown contract
is a packing puzzle with three paper squares already spoken for.

### From floor 17 to floor 5

There is no lane. Service ends at Ten. Every way down to the Sump is unlicensed, and the Company does not insure it.

1. **By riser (most common).** Out through the Three Grate into R3, down past 16 to 10 (twelve floors in about fifteen minutes,
   falling is easy), through the Plinth's lower floors where the risers start, to **the Perch**: a drone shelf the Moat's tenants
   hung on the R3 wall at floor 5, with a bell. **Toll:** the Moat gang at the Knot takes its share on landing; with a Co-op tag or
   Tide cord in the bay, it is less. Risk: a toll net on 12 or 13 (kids) or a soot jam at 7.
2. **By lift.** A **freight chit** in the bay, and the drone rides Old Two from 17 down to 5 with the porters. Safe, slow (wait for
   the car, then a stop at every floor), and the Lift Guild logs it in the Ride Book.
3. **By chute.** Drop the drone into the East Chute's drop door. It reaches the Bale Hall on 8 in under a minute, battered, and the
   bale pickers keep one item as receiving fee before the drone flies on down the Bale Hall stairwell to 5. Only for sealed, padded
   bays. Never between 05:30 and 06:30, when the Morning Fall would bury it.
4. **Outside, down the Landward face** to the Landing on 4 and in through the loading doors. Skin clearance is not enforced below 10
   (nobody would come), but the wind on the Seaward corners, the gulls at the Landing, and the long fall to the water if a cell
   fails make it a route for good frames only.

The return is always a climb: back up R3 against the Mills' return air, about forty minutes. Round trip: about two hours by riser,
three to four by lift, less by chute, which is why chute-diving survives.

**In play:** going down pays little and has **no safe route**, which is the point. The player chooses which kind of risk: toll,
time, or damage. And going down is where the rewards are strange: a Moat client pays in what the drowned floors give up.

## Part six: the building's day

The Stack keeps time by its machines. A day on floor 17, as the building runs it:

| Time | What the building does |
|---|---|
| 05:00 | On a dawn-slack morning, Mills sockets go dead (Clean Core long run). |
| 05:30 | The hum and the yellow stop on 21 to 27. |
| 05:45 | Tap queues form. |
| 06:00 | **The Works Whistle** (four minutes fast). **The Morning Fall**: the Reclaim's shutter opens, every catch bangs. Mills tap hours open. Old Two's rush hour. |
| 07:00 | Contract slips on the lobby board. Sump standpipes open for an hour. |
| 08:00 | The game day begins. |
| 09:00 | Mills taps close until evening. |
| 13:00 | Middle water pressure drops until 17:00. |
| 14:00 | **The Whistle**: the old day shift. Hot-bunk changeover in the Eight Squares. |
| ~twice a day, drifting | **Slack hour** at slack water: Mills sockets shed. The tide table by the socket board says when. |
| 17:00 | **Peak Window** opens: power at 1.20 cr a unit until 20:00. |
| 18:00 | Mills taps reopen until 21:00. |
| 20:00 | The game day ends. The Daily Service Statement prints. |
| 22:00 | **The Whistle**: the old night shift. The Crown's quiet rule; the Ninety Gate closes; chutes are held at the Reclaim. The hum and the yellow begin. |
| 02:00 | Sometimes, something heavy in the West Chute. |
| 03:00 | Every ninth night, a craft on the Crown Pad. |

**In play:** this is a ready-made day clock for the hour steps: what happens at each hour is not invented per event but read off
the building. Players learn it the way tenants do.
