# Canon brief: what every speculative file stands on

This folder is **speculative**: raw material to pan through, not decisions. This brief is the one part every other file
must agree with, so the pieces fit together. Things marked **decided** were settled by the designer (see `../LORE.md`,
`../IDEAS.md`). Things marked **working assumption** were picked here so the writing stays consistent; they can be thrown
out later, and files may offer alternatives to them as long as they say so.

---

## The game, in one paragraph (decided)

An inventory game. You run a small workshop-shop on a low floor of a vertical slum. You make, grow, sort, repair and
refurbish goods, and you fulfil **contracts** by packing goods into **drones** (containers with cargo bays) and launching
them up or down the tower. Drones come back with pay and a reward in the bay. Space on a grid is the constraint of
everything: the bays, your shop floor, the shelves. Rent is charged per square you lease. Time runs in hour steps through
a day. Machines are bought (often old, failing, one-of-a-kind), never crafted from parts. Night trips, hunting and combat
are **out of scope** for now; do not build lore that requires them.

## Tone (decided)

- Gritty and bad, not utopian. Dystopian capitalism, inequality, the divide between top and bottom, shown through how
  things work, not lectures.
- Dark humour by understatement. Corporate language that says the opposite of what it does.
- No heroes, no chosen ones, no saviours. People cope, scheme, share, betray, endure.
- **Practical first.** Most things are things people need or steps in a production chain. Variety comes from use, not from
  junk with different names. Flavour lives in names and one-line descriptions.

## The world (decided unless marked)

- **The Stack.** One tower, **104 floors** (working assumption for the exact number). A vertical slum. Built as a **factory
  town**: housing for the workers of a plant on its lower floors. The plant closed as an employer long ago.
- **The city outside drowned.** The Stack stands in water. Leaving is possible, but there is nothing to go to: ruins, no work,
  no water rights. Surrounded by water nobody can drink.
- **The drowning was caused by the people at the top, who shifted the blame, and everyone knows the truth** (decided in
  spirit). Working assumption for how: for decades the plant pumped groundwater from under the city (it needed vast amounts
  of ultrapure water), the ground subsided below sea level, the Company held the seawall maintenance contract and cut it to
  the bone, and in a storm the plant's own slurry reservoir and the seawall failed together. The official story: "an
  unprecedented storm", "climate", and "illegal wells dug by residents". Files may explore variants, marked as such.
- **The water is poisoned** (decided): seawater plus everything the plant left: solvents, heavy metals, slurry.
- **Cleaning water is monopolised** (decided). Treating water without a Halden licence is illegal "for public wellbeing";
  bootleggers are "dangerous". Pay the licence fee (and the recurring one) and the same bootlegger with the same rusty kit
  becomes a "certified safe provider". Licensed water is good but expensive to produce and impossible to live on, so people
  make, steal, recycle and ferment their way around it.
- **Air** is okay to breathe in the short term, bad over years (decided): damp, mould, old factory dust, worse lower down.
- **Fresh food is only grown, with precious water** (decided). No forests, no foraging. **Protein blocks** are ground insects
  and food waste from the upper floors (decided).
- **The Flats** (the mudflats, decided as a concept): at low tide the old streets show. People live there because they can't
  afford anything else; everything is dumped on them; everyone despises them; they dive, magnet-fish and sift for salvage,
  sometimes dangerously, and that salvage goes up the tower through middlemen.
- **Destroying usable goods to protect prices** (decided as a theme): brands and the rich do not throw things out whole.
  They slash, puncture, snap and stamp things **VOID** before they go down the chute, so they cannot undercut new stock.
  Undoing that (patching, resealing, repairing) is a living, and illegal under "brand protection".
- **Other drowned towers** exist and trade between towers is a late-game unlock (decided): it needs strong batteries and a
  stable frame to survive the crossing and the **poachers**, or a slow wind/solar alternative that does the job painfully slowly.
- **Society is not just the Company and the tenants** (decided): many groups formed under pressure (factions below).
- **Drones** (decided that they exist; the rest are working assumptions): a drone flies itself on a route loaded before launch,
  because radio does not reach through the steel; only the licensed Tube lanes carry a feeder cable that lets the Company talk
  to a drone. People set routes, load cards, launch and land by hand, but never pilot in a free shaft. Drones are everyday kit:
  cheap **hoppers** (one box, about eight floors) are as common as bicycles, and a courier frame with a real bay and range is
  the first serious investment.
- **Tech** (decided): everyday tech is near-future and believable (cells, drones, grow lights, filters, cheap screens). The
  tower itself was built with more advanced founding-era tech that is now failing; nobody can make it anymore, only repair it.
  A working founding-era part is worth more than anything new.
- **The player** (decided): open-ended, with several endings; moving up the floors and owning your lease outright are both
  valid paths. Who the player was before is **open**: files may propose several pasts, never one.

## Names and numbers (working assumptions; use exactly these)

- **The Company:** Halden (in full: the Halden Company; people say "Halden" or "the Company"). Owns the tower, the leases,
  the meters, the lanes, the company store, the water licence.
- **The plant:** **Halden Works**. Working assumption: it made **power cells and control chips** (which needed ultrapure water
  in huge volumes and left solvents and heavy metals behind). This is why cells and founding-era electronics are everywhere,
  and why the poison is what it is.
- **The city:** **Calder**, on **Calder Bay**. The drowned streets are "old Calder".
- **Brands under Halden licence:** Orrin Provisions (food, protein blocks: "Vitabrick"), Vey (medicine), Skywater (rain
  bottled from the roof), Halden Pure (the Company's treated water). Other brands may be invented sparingly.
- **Floor bands:**
  - **The Crown** (floors 90–104): the owners. Rain, daylight, real food, quiet, gardens.
  - **The Terraces** (60–89): managers, professionals, private clinics, offices.
  - **The Middle** (30–59): skilled workers, licensed shops, metered everything, respectable and anxious.
  - **The Mills** (10–29): the old plant halls cut into workshops, grow co-ops, insect farms, dormitories. **The player starts
    here** (working assumption: floor 17).
  - **The Sump** (4–9): the lowest dry floors, the flooded remains of the factory's upper halls; wet, rusted, dark; the
    chutes end here; fixers and gangs.
  - **Floors 0–3** are underwater: the drowned plant. Divers go there.
  - **The Flats**: outside, at low tide, around the base.
- **The Dark Floors:** a sealed section of the old plant (working assumption: floors 22–26, inside the Mills band, behind
  welded doors) that still hums at night. Automated, unlit, nobody from the tower works there. What it makes is a mystery
  (working assumption: it still makes cells and chips, lights-out). Production "never stopped, it just stopped employing
  anyone."
- **Timeline** (working assumption, in years since the tower's founding, written "Y"):
  - Y0: Halden Works and the Stack open together, built with the best tech of the time, built to last.
  - Y0–Y40: the boom. Thousands employed. Groundwater pumping for decades. The ground under Calder begins to sink.
  - ~Y41: automation; mass layoffs; the plant "closes" as an employer. Halden becomes a landlord.
  - ~Y57: the Drowning (the storm, the seawall and the slurry reservoir). Old Calder floods. The lowest floors fill. The upper
    floors are cut into smaller leases and sold; the rich move up.
  - ~Y86: the present. Founding-era tech failing, rent climbing, nobody alive who knows how half of it was built.
- **Money:** credits (written "cr", a plain number) and **Halden scrip** (spends only at the company store). Rent per leased
  square. Metered water and power. A **credit rating** gates which contracts you see. Owning your shop is a Company loan.
- **Law:** **Halden Protective Services (HPS)**, by subscription (response time by tier). **Licensing inspectors** (water,
  food, brands). **Brand protection agents**. **Floor wardens** (tenants paid to report). **Debt collectors** (contracted).
  **The Arbiter** (Company arbitration; no courts). Gangs keep order in the Sump; the Mills Co-op runs unofficial patrols.
- **Factions** (names fixed; details are open for invention):
  - **The Rain Church**: rain is sacred, the roof was stolen; steals from the Crown's gutters, gives water away.
  - **The Tide Folk**: the Flats' folk faith; tides and moon; practical, superstitious; distrusts the tower.
  - **The Mills Co-op**: half-legal growers' and workers' union; shares tools and water; pays partly in produce.
  - **The Old Hands**: retired plant workers who remember how the founding-era tech works.
  - **The Archivists**: keep founding-era documents, blueprints, manuals.
  - **The Lift Guild**: runs the lifts, charges tolls, sides with the Company against drone couriers.
  - **Shaft rats**: independent drone couriers using the free shafts.
  - **Stinkers**: what the Company calls water bootleggers.
  - **Seed savers**: keep heirloom seeds alive against the patents.
  - **Insect ranchers**: run the protein farms.
  - **Sublet barons**: lease squares from Halden and sublet them by the hour.
  - **Hot-bunk houses**: beds rented by the shift.
  - **The Foundation**: the Crown's charity; vouchers that only spend at the company store.
  - **Plasma buyers**: buy blood low, sell it to the hospital tower.
  - **Pirate radio** ("Static" as a working name): broadcasts from somewhere in the shafts.
  - **Penthouse fixers**: find "real" things for the Crown.
  - **The insurance syndicate**: insures drones and cargo; never pays.
  - **Kids' gangs** (vent kids): know every crawlspace.
  - **The Voided**: people who sell undone voided goods.
  - **Flats divers / magnet fishers**: the salvage crews of the Flats and the drowned plant.
- **Other towers** (names fixed, details open): **Vantage** (a rival company's newer, stricter tower), **St. Ober's Spire**
  (hospital tower run by a charity that charges like a bank), **Greenhold** (licensed agricultural tower, patents everything),
  **the Hulk** (a dead, half-collapsed tower; squatters and salvage), **Bastion** (a prison tower whose prisoners now make what
  Halden Works used to), **the Pleasure Pier** (entertainment for the rich of every tower), **the Sounding** (a weather and comms
  station on stilts that sells forecasts).
- **Food chains (decided):** grow beds (seed + water every hour → real produce, sells up); insect bins (food waste + warmth +
  a little water → insects → grinder → paste → press → protein blocks, sells down).
- **Mechanics already proposed** (from `IDEAS.md`; lore should explain and use them): rent per square; rent climbing as the
  upper floors expand downward; the **garbage chute** (a container that fills each morning with what the floors above throw
  out); metering (tap and socket); real vs synthetic grades; destination floor = height = class; licensed lanes vs free
  shafts (tolls, looting); credits, scrip, loans, credit rating; contract slips styled by origin.

## Writing rules for every file in this folder

- Long, concrete, immersive. Explain things, connect them, and **use** them: show how each piece of lore would surface in
  play (an item, a machine, a contract, a client, a price, an event, a choice).
- Mark what you are offering: plain text for the main version; **Alternative:** for other versions of the same thing;
  **Hook:** for mysteries or story seeds; **In play:** for gameplay use.
- Stay inside the canon above. If you need a new name, invent it, but prefer reusing the names here. Don't contradict the
  timeline, the floor bands, or the decided points.
- Practical first: things people need, steps in chains. Flavour in names and one-liners.
- No combat, hunting or night expeditions as required content.
- Write in English prose with headings; avoid filler; avoid lecturing the reader about capitalism; let the systems speak.
