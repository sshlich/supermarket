# Factions, part two

*Speculative. Rocks and soil. Everything here is a proposal unless `00-canon-brief.md` already decided it.*

Part one (`06a-factions.md`) holds the ten groups that formed around a **thing**: rain, tides, tools, memory, paper, lifts,
shafts, stills, seed, insects. The ten canon groups in this file mostly formed around a **gap**: a square nobody was using
for eight hours, a bed that was empty while its sleeper worked, a vein with blood in it, a bad valet's careless cut, a
signal nobody was sending, a claim nobody wanted to pay. They are the businesses of the gaps, and most of them are not
loved.

Then six **new** groups, clearly marked, that would plausibly form under the same pressures: Company middle managers who
sell the future of the rate card, the Crown's own residents' committee, floor wardens trading quotas, squatters in a dead
lift shaft, licensed refurbishers who hunt the Voided, and the people who came back on the Bastion boat.

The machinery from part one carries over unchanged: every faction has a **standing** from *Hostile* through *Wary*,
*Known*, *Trusted* to *Kin*, kept on one ledger page, nothing hidden; and most factions hand out a **token**, an item you can
pack into a drone's bay next to a slip.

| Faction | Token | Footprint | What packing it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sublet barons | **door card** (a baron's printed hour-card, clipped at the corner per use) | 1x1 | The drone may land on a baron's landing square instead of the Company dock; no dock fee, no dock queue |
| Hot-bunk houses | **bunk tag** (numbered tin disc on a loop of wire) | 1x1 | The keeper signs for goods addressed to a sleeper with no lease: you can deliver to nobodies |

| Static | **crystal** (a quartz from a dead receiver, wrapped in tape) | 1x1 | Static slips; the drone's delivery is read out on air: foot traffic next day, and attention |
| Penthouse fixers | **fixer's card** (embossed, a name and no address) | 1x1 | A fixer's commission accepts the drone at the Core A transfer; the fixer's word stands in for a Crown pass |
| The insurance syndicate | **policy slip** (a numbered cover note, single use) | 1x1 | Bay Cover for the flight, under the packing standard below |
| Vent kids | **sucker** (a wrapped Orrin sugar lump, chalked with a crew sign) | 1x1 | A kid run: a 1x1 or 1x2 parcel travels through the Under within a band, in an hour, no drone at all |

**In play:** the tokens of part one and part two are meant to clash in the same bay. A drone carrying a Foundation pin and
red thread is a joke the inspectors would enjoy. A drone carrying a donor card and a Rainmark is an insult the Rain Church
will notice if the drone is looted and the contents are talked about.

## The sublet barons

> "I don't rent rooms. I rent hours. Halden rents squares by the week because Halden is old-fashioned."
> (Perpetua Sallow, Mezzanine Living)

### Origin: the dormitories, the barge leases and the licence to occupy

The first barons were not called barons. From **Y50**, when the Works Units on the Mills floors filled with grow co-ops,
insect farms and repair shops, people came in from the dying town each day to work them and could not afford the bus back
(the Company bought the Calder bus company in Y48 and closed it). A handful of operators leased whole halls and partitioned
them into bunks. They called themselves **dormitory providers**. The Mills called them landlords' landlords.

Then came the Drowning and the Lifeline. Twenty-two thousand people **signed on the water**: a bed and a strip of floor each, rent-free for
ninety days, then "the prevailing rate". When the ninety days ended, thousands fell into arrears at once. Halden assigned the
debts; the debtors faced forfeiture of the lease. The dormitory providers did the arithmetic. A forfeited bunk lease
was worth nothing to Halden except the rent. So they offered Halden the rent, in advance, for blocks of forfeited leases, and
offered the debtors the right to stay in the same bunk for a nightly fee. The debtor kept a bed. Halden kept its
rent. The provider kept the difference. Everybody signed.

Clause 31 of the Halden Standard Tenancy Agreement forbids subletting. In **Y61** a provider called **Ambrose Tallis** paid a
Terraces lawyer for an opinion that what he sold was not a sublet but a **licence to occupy**: the occupant held no
interest in the squares, only permission to be on them, revocable at any hour. A tenant who complained took the point to the
Arbiter. The determination (the Mills still quote its number, **A/61/0007**) held that "a licence to occupy confers no
estate and therefore cannot be an assignment". Halden liked the rent the providers paid. The determination has never been
revisited. From then on, they were barons.

The **Sublet Licence** (200 cr, plus 15% of sublet income, from the Lease Office) came later, in **Y69**, when the Company
noticed how much money was moving past it. A baron with a licence may sublet openly. Most big barons hold one licence for
the part of their business inspectors can see, and run the rest on A/61/0007.

### What they do

- **Hour squares.** The core product. A baron leases squares from Halden by the week and rents them by the hour: a hawker
  takes two frontage squares from 10:00 to 14:00, a cobbler takes one square in a stairwell for the morning, a grower takes
  six back squares for a night to dry a harvest. The baron's ledger is a **tick sheet**: a printed grid, squares down the
  side, hours across, every cell ticked when paid. A good baron's tick sheet is full at noon and half full at night.
- **Halves.** On floor 12, a baron called Lorne Abbiss discovered that nothing in the HSTA defines a square as indivisible.
  He runs a strip of tape down the middle of each frontage square and rents each half by the hour. A **half** is too narrow
  for a shelf, wide enough for a tray. Hawkers selling chillies, matches, single cells and cigarettes work halves.
- **Frontage strips.** Barons buy frontage in long runs along the busiest corridors (frontage is billed x1.5 by Halden, and
  rented on by the baron at about three times that). The Shelf on floor 30 is almost entirely baron-held frontage now.
- **Catch squares.** On rain days, barons rent window ledges and light-well squares by the hour to anyone with a pan.
  The Rain Church hates this and pays it.
- **Dead leases.** When an old tenant dies (the Old Hands notice this most), the lease ends, and the squares go back to the
  Lease Office's list. Barons pay Lease Office clerks a small retainer to see that list first. An Old Hand's flat is usually
  under tape and partitioned into bunks within a week.
- **Arrears buying.** A baron buys the arrears of a struggling lease from Brisk & Lowe (who buy them from Halden at 35 cr per
  100), then offers the tenant a deal: sign the lease over and stay on as a licensee, debt "forgiven". The tenant becomes a
  nightly payer on the squares they used to hold. The Mills call this **being turned**.
- **Chalk men.** The big barons employ their own surveyors, ex-chalkers, who walk a floor and mark which leases are weak
  (arrears flags, old tenants, single occupants), so the baron knows where to buy next. Their marks are small yellow ticks on
  the door frame, below the lease stud, and most tenants don't know what they mean.

### The ladder of a square

How one square of floor 17 is paid for, top to bottom, on a busy day:

| Who | Pays whom | For what | Per week, one square |
|---|---|---|---|
| Halden | (nobody) | owns the stud | — |
| Mezzanine Living | Halden | the lease, frontage, x1.5 | 0.375 cr |
| Gus Tanaka-Breen (under-baron) | Mezzanine Living | a block of frontage on a weekly "service agreement" | 0.70 cr |
| A hawker | Gus | the square from 08:00 to 20:00, by the hour at 0.0125 | 1.05 cr |
| A half-hawker | the hawker | the left half of the square, 12:00 to 16:00 | 0.20 cr of that 1.05 |

Every rung is legal, or is A/61/0007, which is the same thing.

### Structure

There is no baron guild. There are three kinds of baron, and they know each other the way sharks know each other.

- **Houses**: companies with offices in the Middle that hold hundreds of leases. **Mezzanine Living** (floors 28 and 29 in
  full, plus scattered holdings through the Mills; office on 52) is the biggest. **Brightwell Habitation** holds most of the
  Sump's dry squares on 6 and 7 and pays the Pumpmen's tithe in bulk. **Tallis & Daughter**, the oldest, holds the Shelf.
- **Under-barons**: one-floor operators who rent from a house on a "service agreement" and rent on by the hour. Gus
  Tanaka-Breen on 17 is one.
- **Sitters**: the people on the floor who collect the hourly money, watch the tick sheet and wake up anyone who overstays.
  Paid 20 cr a week and a bunk. A sitter is the face of the baron; most tenants never see the baron at all.

### People

- **Perpetua Sallow, managing director of Mezzanine Living.** Fifty-eight. Middle-born, never lived below 40, speaks of
  "occupancy yield" and "activated squares". She bought floor 28 one lease at a time over eleven years and floor 29 in three.
  She sends a Foundation gift every month to the Lease Office's collection tin and her name is on the brass plate of the
  Foundation Reading Room's donor list. She is not cruel and would be hurt to be told she was. She thinks a square left empty
  overnight is a moral failure.
- **Gus Tanaka-Breen, under-baron of floor 17.** Forty-four. Grew up in a hot-bunk on 12, worked as a sitter for Mezzanine at
  nineteen, and now runs a hot-bunk house at the far end of 17 (see below) and a frontage strip along the east corridor as hour
  stalls. Warden Hester Moyle files about four reports a month, nearly all against
  him. He pays the fines, pays the recovered rent, and keeps going, because the fines are smaller than the margin. He sends
  Hester a clock at midwinter. She sends it back.
- **Lorne Abbiss, "the Half-Square King".** Sixty-two, floor 12. Invented halves. Now in a long dispute with the Lease
  Office over whether a half-square needs its own stud reading. Wears a tape measure on a chain like a watch.
- **Ambrose Tallis the Younger.** Seventy. Grandson of the first baron, holder of the Shelf's frontage. He owns a framed carbon
  of A/61/0007. He calls himself a "heritage operator" and pays an Archivist to keep his family's lease files in order, because
  he knows that if anyone ever finds a defect in Y61's paperwork, every baron in the tower falls with him.
- **Odile Prentice, sitter, floor 28.** Twenty-six. Collects the hourly money on a floor that used to be her grandmother's Co-op
  floor. Feeds the Co-op information about Mezzanine's next purchases. Has not been caught. Her grandmother does not speak to
  her.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** more squares, filled more hours; the Chalk to move faster (a floor reclassified "Transitional" lifts the price of
  everything a baron already holds below it); tenants in arrears; old tenants without heirs.
- **Offer:** hour squares, halves, frontage by the day, bunks by the shift, storage by the night, and, to tenants in trouble,
  a way out of a lease that doesn't end on the arrears register.
- **Buy:** leases, arrears, information (a dying tenant, a lease in trouble, a floor about to be resurveyed), partitions,
  tape, padlocks, bunk racks, lamps, chalk.
- **Sell:** time on squares; and, quietly, the right to be forgotten by the Lease Office's arrears register, since a turned
  tenant is no longer a party to anything.

**In play:**
- **Hour leases.** A baron's slip offers squares adjacent to your lease (or in a stairwell nearby) for a run of hours: "6 back
  squares, 14:00 to 08:00, 9 cr." The squares appear on your field as a hatched block for that window, then vanish, and
  anything left on them goes into a **displaced goods crate** at your door with a 2 cr "clearing charge". The perfect tool for
  a big packing day (you need room to lay out a Kestrel's whole cargo), a drying run, or a Great Ebb stall. No Standing check,
  no rent day, a high hourly price. Space as a thing you can rent for an afternoon is the purest form of the game's constraint.
- **The offer.** When your rent falls behind (the arrears ladder in file 05), a baron's slip arrives within a day: "We will
  clear your arrears in full. You remain on your squares under licence at 0.004 cr per square per hour. No further obligation."
  Accepting ends the lease game and turns you into a licensee: no rent day, no eviction ladder, no Standing, but you pay by the
  hour forever and can be moved at any hour. A soft fail state the player chooses, with its own way back (buy the lease back
  at a price the baron sets).
- **Becoming a baron.** If you ever own squares outright on a floor you no longer work (file 04's Held Title), you can rent
  them on by the hour. A passive income line, a Sublet Licence or the A/61/0007 risk, a sitter to hire (a hireling-as-plan:
  their plan is "fill the tick sheet"), and the Co-op's permanent hostility. This is how barons start.
- **Neighbours.** A baron buying a lease next to yours changes your floor: an hour-stall strip brings foot traffic past your
  frontage (good for shelves) and a hot-bunk house brings thieves, smells and buyers of cheap food (good for protein blocks,
  bad for the Walk's theft roll).

### Example slips

*Baron slips are printed on the back of tick sheets: a pre-printed grid with the baron's house name in a box, the unused
hours struck through in pencil.*

1. **Racks for 28.** "Twelve bunk racks, steel, 2x3, any condition, delivered to Mezzanine Living, floor 28." Pay: 140 cr.
   Racks come down the chute as VOIDed office shelving (snapped); the Voided re-splint them. A good use of a pile of snapped
   steel, and a direct contribution to turning another Co-op floor into bunks.
2. **Clear the squares.** "Lease 17-K is forfeit as of today. Contents to be crated and carried to storage, floor 7.
   Licensee will not obstruct." Pay: 35 cr. You are packing a neighbour's life into a drone. Some of it is good. All of it is
   theirs. Co-op standing drops if anyone sees.
3. **Tape and chalk.** "Ten rolls of floor tape, yellow; a box of chalk; a stud wand if you can find one." Pay: 30 cr, plus 20
   for the wand. A stud wand is a Survey and Allocation tool. Owning one is a fine.
4. **Information wanted.** *No printed grid; a plain card.* "Tenants on 17 over seventy, living alone. Names and lease
   numbers. 5 cr a name." A betrayal slip. Every name you sell becomes an hour-stall within the season.
5. **The night square.** "Six squares on 16, 22:00 to 06:00, for drying. 6 cr. Bring your own racks." A cheap offer, posted
   to growers on rain-forecast nights.

### How they treat the player

As a customer, a supplier, and a future acquisition, in that order. At *Wary* you get hour leases at the posted price. At
*Known* you get the night rate and first refusal on squares next to yours. At *Trusted* Gus offers you a stake in his
frontage strip (a weekly cut, and your name on the tick sheet). At *Kin* Perpetua Sallow invites you to "come in on" a floor:
you buy into a block of turned leases, and you are a baron. Barons never threaten. They wait for your rent to be late.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the Company's best tenants. They pay early, in full, on hundreds of leases, and they never complain about the
  Creep, because the Creep raises their own hourly prices faster than their weekly rent. The Lease Office's internal phrase
  for them is "high-yield partners".
- **The Mills Co-op:** the real enemy, both ways. The Co-op's chart of lease sales in the Assembly Hall is a chart of barons.
  The Co-op runs the Night Shift hot-bunk on 11 at cost so a baron doesn't run it at a profit. Expulsion from the Co-op is the
  penalty for selling your lease to a baron.
- **The Rain Church:** pays for catch squares on rain days and calls it the tithe of the dry.
- **The Old Hands:** barons take their flats when they die, usually within the week. The Canteen keeps a list of the flats
  and who took them.
- **The Archivists:** barons buy lease histories to find weak claims; Ambrose Tallis pays the Archive to keep his own
  history strong.
- **The Lift Guild:** moves evicted tenants' belongings by Core E on contract, for barons, at a bulk rate.
- **Shaft rats:** barons rent rats hatch squares (a square next to a free-shaft hatch, by the hour), which the rats need and
  resent.
- **Stinkers:** a still behind a hot-bunk wall is invisible to an inspector who counts sleepers. Barons protect stills in
  their buildings for a cut.
- **Seed savers, insect ranchers:** barons rent ranchers warm squares near bulkheads by the night; savers avoid barons
  because a baron's sitter sells everything he sees.
- **Tide Folk:** none. A berth is not a lease.
- **Part two:** hot-bunk houses are barons' most profitable product. The Foundation thanks barons in its annual report. The
  Period Club (new, below) is the barons' oracle: they buy advance notice of reclassifications. The Green Bands (new) take
  barons' lamp oil and report them anyway, to make quota.

### In the endings

- **The Landlord.** The player ends the game owning squares and renting them by the hour. The ending text is a tick sheet,
  full at noon.
- **Turned.** The player took the offer and never bought back. The ending text is a licensee's receipt for one night on
  squares the player used to hold, with the hour stamped.
- **A/61/0007 overturned.** A late chain through the Archivists: the Y61 opinion was written by a lawyer who was not admitted
  to practise before the Arbiter until Y62. If that paper reaches the Arbiter with a Co-op petition, every licence to occupy in
  the tower is an unlawful sublet overnight. The barons don't fall: they all buy Sublet Licences within the week, and pay 15%
  to the Company. The occupants get nothing. The Co-op calls it a victory anyway.

**Hook:** Perpetua Sallow has been buying leases on floors 20 and 21, the two floors under the Dark Floors' welded slab, at
above the going rate, and leaving them empty. Empty squares are the one thing she says she cannot stand.

**Hook:** a square on 16 that Dorrit Vance the chalker keeps resurveying (file 04) is rented by the hour, every hour, every
night, by the same person, through three different barons. Nobody has seen anything on it.

- "Your hour's up. I don't make the hours."
- "A square that sleeps is a square that loses money. Same as people."

## The hot-bunk houses

> "Your bed's warm. You're welcome."
> (a keeper, at the 06:00 knock)

### Origin: three shifts, one bed

The Works ran three shifts, **06:00, 14:00 and 22:00**, and the founding dormitories on the Mills floors were built for single
men on one shift each. When the dormitory providers of Y50 began packing day workers into the old halls, they took the shift
logic with them: a bed that sleeps one worker for eight hours sleeps three workers in a day. After the Drowning, with
twenty-two thousand new tenants and no new floors, it became the way most of the Mills' poorest slept, and still is.

A **hot-bunk** is a bed rented by the shift. It is called hot because when you get into it, the last sleeper has just got
out. Rates (file 04): **2 cr a shift in the Sump, 3 in the Mills**, with a **quarter-litre cup** of water at the hatch, the
house's own **breath water** from its air-wells (file 03: "buy your own breath"). Three hundred people sleep on floor 12
alone in hot-bunks.

### How a house works

- **The rack.** Bunks are steel racks, **boxes** (a closed coffin bunk, stacked three high, with a sliding panel
  and a hook for boots), or **mats** (a pad on the floor, cheapest, no lid). A Mills house sleeps about **seven per shift in
  one small room**: two columns of three boxes and a mat in the aisle, twenty-one beds a day. That is how Gus Tanaka-Breen
  sleeps twenty on floor 17, in a room the Company's plan shows as eight beds' worth: hence the house's name, **the Eight
  Squares**.
- **The rail.** Above each box, a chalk rail: three names (or three numbers, for people who'd rather not), one per shift.
  Rail-mates share a bed and often never meet. They leave things for each other: a sweet, a note, a warning about a loose
  panel. Some marry. The Mills phrase for a couple who met this way is **rail-wed**.
- **The knock.** Fifteen minutes before each shift change the keeper walks the racks knocking on panels with a brass ring. A
  sleeper who doesn't wake at the second knock is pulled out by the ankles at the third. Overstaying costs a full shift.
- **The turn.** Sheets are not washed between sleepers. They are **turned**: the keeper flips the sheet over at each change.
  After three turns (a day) the sheet goes to the laundry. Houses that wash every shift charge half a credit more and say so on
  the door: **WASHED EVERY SHIFT**. The laundry contracts are a business in themselves (Wen Liao's Wash & Press on 12, the
  Rain Church Font laundry in 06a, does most of floor 12's).
- **Lockers.** A 1x1 locker by the shift, 0.5 cr. The one place a nobody can keep anything. Most people carry their life on
  their back instead.
- **The cup.** Every shift gets a quarter-litre at the hatch on waking. The water comes from air-wells in the sleeping rooms:
  a room of thirty sleepers exhales about a litre an hour. Extra cups cost 0.20 cr.
- **The Bunk Book.** Each house keeps a book of who slept when, under what name. The Bunk Books are the closest thing the Mills
  has to a census of nobodies, and HPS, the Foundation's assessors and the debt firms all want to read them.

### Structure

The houses are owned by barons (most), by the Co-op (the Night Shift on 11), by gangs in the Sump (the **Drum** on 7, in the old
drum store, under the Pumpmen's tithe), or by their keepers (a handful of tiny houses). The faction is not the owners. It is
the **keepers**, who run the houses and form a loose web across the tower that the Mills calls **the Knockers**.

Keepers know each other because they pass people between them: a sleeper whose house is full is sent next door; a sleeper
who has been robbed is given a free shift on another keeper's rack. They run a **lost-and-found** that crosses the tower: an
item left in a box on 12 can be collected from the Drum on 7 a week later, because keepers swap lost property every
seventh day. They hold **mail** for sleepers (chute mail, slips, letters from Bastion). They hold **money**: a keeper's tin
holds a few credits for half the house, and a keeper who steals from the tin doesn't keep a house for long.

### People

- **Mother Ivy Okonkwo, keeper of the Long Shift, floor 12.** Sixty-six. Has kept the Long Shift (a baron's house, Lorne Abbiss
  owns it) for thirty-one years. Knows nine hundred people by their sleeping position. Hears everything said in sleep and has
  never repeated a word of it to HPS. Places the house's orders (water, sheets, Vitabricks, lamp cells) by slip, and is one of the
  most regular small clients in the Mills (file 03's "40 L drinkable, any grade over grey, by 18:00" is hers).
- **Tobin Saar, keeper at the Eight Squares (Gus's house on 17).** Twenty-four. Sleeps in the house's hatch cupboard. Paid 20 cr a
  week and a bed (file 04's rate). Wants to own a house, which on his wage will take him until he is ninety. He is the player's
  nearest hot-bunk neighbour and will trade almost anything for lamp cells, because the house's lights are on his budget.
- **Hannelore "Lore" Brisk, keeper of the Night Shift, floor 12 (the Co-op's house).** Fifty. No relation to Brisk & Lowe, and
  tired of saying so. Runs the only house in the tower where the cup is a half-litre and the sheets are washed every shift, at
  cost, which means the Night Shift is always full and always broke.
- **Peder Gall, keeper of the Drum, floor 7.** Thirty-eight. The Drum is wet at the floor on spring tides, so its lowest boxes
  are raised on pallets. Peder keeps a pump horn by the hatch, and on three blasts he empties the house and sends every sleeper
  to the Pumpmen's lines. Half his sleepers are Tide Folk who work day jobs in the tower.
- **"Rail" Wenna Doyle.** Eighty. Has slept on the same box on the Long Shift (middle tier, column two, the 22:00 shift) for
  forty-four years. Has outlived nine rail-mates. Gets visitors.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** full racks; no occupancy counts; air-wells that work; laundry that comes back; and (the keepers, not the owners) a
  way to keep sleepers who can't pay a shift from sleeping on the stairs.
- **Offer:** a bed by the shift; the cup; a locker; an address for people with none; and the knock.
- **Buy, in bulk and by slip:** drinking water (houses run short whenever the air-wells fall behind the sleepers), sheets,
  laundry, Vitabricks and noodle bricks (sold at the hatch), lamp cells, air-well filters and fan motors, bunk racks and
  panels, padlocks, earplugs, insect powder (bedbugs), lowbeer by the cask (file 03).
- **Sell:** breath water (grey grade, sold at the hatch and, in surplus, by the jerrycan to stinkers who boil it again), and
  information, from the Bunk Book, which the keepers pretend they don't sell.

**In play:**
- **Bulk clients for the bottom of the market.** Hot-bunk slips are the steady, cheap, repeatable contracts that keep an early
  game alive: 40 L drinkable, 60 noodle bricks, 30 sheets laundered, 6 lamp cells. Low pay, low clearance, short distances, and
  they never stop coming.
- **Breath water.** A hot-bunk house sells its surplus breath water by the jerrycan, grey, cheap (about 0.10 cr a litre). It
  needs boiling or a filter before any drinking contract accepts it, and it's fine for grow beds and insect bins as it is. A
  cheap water source with a smell.
- **Delivering to nobodies.** Many lower-band slips come from people with no lease: a diver, a sleeper, a day labourer. With a
  **bunk tag** packed beside the slip, the keeper signs for it and holds it. Without one, a slip addressed to a nobody has no
  landing point, and the drone comes back.
- **Labour by the shift.** Sleepers who've woken and have eight hours to fill are the Mills' day labour. A keeper's slip offers
  a worker for a shift (a hireling for eight hours, paid 10 to 15 cr and a meal), with a plan you write: sort the chute, carry,
  pack. Many have been sleeping in houses for years and know exactly how to sort a Crown sack.
- **A bed of your own.** If you lose your lease (forfeiture, file 05), the game doesn't have to end: you can run a reduced
  business from a hot-bunk locker and hour squares. A hard, possible way back.

### Example slips

*Hot-bunk slips are written on the back of the house's own shift tickets, small pink stubs, in the keeper's hand.*

1. **The cup.** "40 L drinkable, any grade over grey, the Long Shift (12), by 18:00." Pay: 22 cr. Repeats every few days.
2. **The turn.** "30 sheets, laundered, back by the 22:00 knock." Pay: 9 cr. A drone runs to Liao's on 12 and back; or you wash
   them yourself, which uses water and a basin.
3. **Bugs.** "Insect powder, enough for 21 boxes. Not Orrin's, it doesn't work." Pay: 12 cr. A rancher's diatom powder (insect
   farm by-product) works; the store's does not. A chain step that ties ranchers to keepers.
4. **The fan.** "Air-well on rack two has stopped. Fan motor, 40 mm, and someone who can fit it." Pay: 30 cr, plus a free shift
   for a hireling. Thirty people will be breathing into a closed room tonight with no water coming out of it.
5. **For Wenna.** "A clean blanket, real wool if anyone has one, to the Long Shift, middle tier, column two. She's eighty."
   Pay: 4 cr and the keeper's thanks. Real wool comes down the Crown chute slashed; a sewing kit makes it a blanket.

### How they treat the player

Kindly, if you supply on time. Keepers have no power to give you except the thing they have: they know where every nobody in
the Mills sleeps. At *Known*, a keeper will tell you who is looking for work. At *Trusted*, the Knockers carry your goods'
reputation: "the cells from 17 last" goes round the houses, and hawkers come to your frontage. At *Kin*, a keeper hides you, or
your goods, on a rack for a shift when a collector comes with yellow tags (a tagged item "moved" to a locker on 12 is a crime
the collector would have to find first).

### Relations

## The Foundation

> "Until Calder is whole."
> (the Foundation's motto, engraved on every collection tin)

### Origin: a speech at the Glasshouse

On the night of the Drowning, while the Gate Floor drowned behind the flood doors, the chair of Halden spoke to the Founding
Reception in the Glasshouse: "Tomorrow we will begin to help, and we will not stop helping until Calder is whole." (The
Archivists hold her notes; file 01.) A year later, on the first anniversary of the Lifeline in **Y58**, the families who had been at that reception, now holding
the newly sold Crown floors as title, founded the **Calder Restoration Foundation** to keep the promise. Everyone calls it the
Foundation. Its founding chair was Odile Aubrac-Mott; Halden-family chairs began in Y67. Its charter commits it to "the relief of hardship among the people of Calder until such time as Calder is whole".
Calder is under the sea. The Foundation has not closed.

What the Foundation gives is **vouchers**. It never gives credits ("cash invites misuse") and never gives goods ("goods
undercut our partners"). It buys vouchers from Halden and hands them out. A voucher spends only at the company store. The
Foundation buys them at **65% of face value**, which Halden calls a "partnership discount"; the store's margin on what the
voucher buys is about 40%; and about a fifth of vouchers expire unspent, which Halden books
as revenue. Every credit the Crown gives the Foundation comes back to Halden with interest. The Crown knows this. The Crown's
own accountants advise it.

Donors receive a **Benefactor's Statement** each year, which Halden's Lease Office accepts as "civic contribution" when a Crown
title is transferred, renewed or reassessed. A family that does not give does not lose its title. It does find the
paperwork slower.

### The programmes

Every Foundation programme has a name, a launch event in the Crown with photographs, and a voucher. Mills people can recite them
like the stations of a cross.

- **The Hope Voucher (Y64).** The main voucher. Printed in sheets of ten, pale blue, face value 1, 5 or 10 cr at the company
  store, valid 30 days. Given to people the Foundation has **assessed** (below). Street rate about 40 cr per 100 of face (file
  04), falling in winter when more are issued.
- **Clean Start (Y66).** A water voucher: Halden Pure at the company store, at full price. For households in arrears who have
  had their tap moderated to a trickle. Clean Start does not restore the tap.
- **Clean Cup.** On every forecast rain day, a free Halden Pure cup voucher handed out on the Mills landings by volunteers in
  blue tabards, often on the same landing as a Rain Church Font. Nobody believes the timing is a coincidence (06a).
- **Brick a Day (Y71).** At Crown dinners, guests "buy a brick" for a Mills child for 20 cr, at the table, and receive a little
  enamel brick for their lapel. The Foundation buys the brick (a plain Vitabrick, 2.60 cr in the Middle) and gives a voucher for
  it. The difference "funds outreach". A Crown host's lapel can carry a dozen enamel bricks by the end of the season.
- **Warm Floors (winter appeal).** Extra Hope Vouchers in the cold months, valid for heaters and cells at the store.
- **The Outreach Launch (Y67).** A white launch that runs out to the Flats on calm days and hands vouchers to the berths. The
  vouchers spend in the tower. The Landing hoist charges by weight. Nobody misses the joke (06a).
- **The Retiree Dignity Programme (Y80).** A monthly voucher for former Works employees, on condition they sign a **waiver of
  pension claims** against Halden Works (Calder) Ltd. The Old Hands' Pension Book has a column for who has signed. It is
  getting longer.
- **The Foundation Scholars (Y69).** One Mills child a year is chosen by examination for a place at a Terraces school, with
  board. In return the scholar signs a ten-year **service bond** to Halden on graduation. Past Scholars are the Company's best
  middle managers, because they know exactly what the Mills is, and exactly what they escaped. (See the Period Club, below.)
- **The Reading Room (Y70).** The Foundation sponsors the Archivists' reading room; a brass plate on the door; the Foundation's
  own founding papers "awaiting cataloguing" ever since (06a).
- **The Gala.** Once a year on the Pleasure Pier. Every Crown family attends. The Gala's catering budget is larger than the
  Hope Voucher budget for the year. This is not a secret; it is in the annual report, under "donor engagement".

### Assessment

Nobody receives a voucher without being **assessed**. An **assessor** (a paid Foundation officer, usually Middle-born, often a
former Scholar) visits the household with a form of nine pages: income, debts, sleepers, water draw, health, "lifestyle
indicators" (drink, a Static receiver, unlicensed apparatus), and "resilience" (how long the household could last without
help). The assessor scores the household into a **band of need**, from 1 (acute) to 5 (resilient), which sets the voucher
allowance.

The form has a box at the bottom, pre-ticked: *I consent to the sharing of this assessment with Foundation partners for the
purpose of coordinated support.* The partners are Halden's Lease Office, Brisk & Lowe Recoveries, and Vey. A household
assessed band 1 is a household the Lease Office now knows is about to fall behind, which a debt firm now knows will soon be
for sale, and which Vey now knows has a member with a mould-lung inhaler on Continuity pricing. Some barons know too, because
some Lease Office clerks sell the list.

### Foundation gifts

File 05 sets this out: above floor 30, bribes are paid as **donations** into an officer's personal Foundation collection tin,
and the donor gets a **receipt**. The Foundation receives a fixed share (a fifth) of what is in each tin and the officer
keeps the rest. The annual report thanks "the many Protective and licensing officers whose personal collections make our work
possible". The Foundation's treasurer reconciles the tins every quarter. The treasurer therefore holds the most complete list
of bribes in the tower, with dates, amounts and names, written down as charitable income.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** to be seen to help; to never be seen to fail; donor engagement; photographs; Scholars who write good letters.
- **Offer:** vouchers (Hope, Clean Start, Clean Cup, Warm Floors, Retiree Dignity); a Scholar place; volunteering credit (for
  Middle and Terraces people, a Standing bonus); receipts.
- **Buy:** catering for the Gala (real food, at Crown prices); printing; tabards; photographs; vouchers (from Halden).
- **Sell:** nothing, officially. Receipts, unofficially, to anyone who needs a bribe to look like a gift.

**In play:**
- **Vouchers as pay.** Some contracts, especially "community" ones, pay in Hope Vouchers: face value high, spend only at the
  store, expire in 30 days, street rate 40. Worse than scrip. A struggling player takes them anyway, because the store sells
  drone parts.
- **The assessor's visit.** An event: an assessor knocks. The player can refuse (nothing happens; no vouchers ever), or be
  assessed. The form is a short screen of honest questions; the score sets a weekly voucher allowance. A band 1 or 2 score
  gives real help and, within a week, an arrears-buying slip from a baron and a "coordinated support" letter from Brisk &
  Lowe. The player learns what the pre-ticked box means by what arrives next.
- **The Gala contract.** Once a year, the best-paying produce contract in the game: "Real-grade produce for the Foundation Gala:
  60 tomatoes R✓, 40 bunches herbs R✓, 24 hen's eggs, to the Pleasure Pier, by launch." Pay at Crown rates, thousands of
  credits. It needs a season of grow beds, a Grow Certificate per bed, cold liners and a fleet. The money the Gala spends on
  the player's tomatoes is more than the Foundation spends on Hope Vouchers for floor 17 in a year. The player can see both
  numbers on the slip, because the Foundation prints the year's outreach total on its letterhead.
- **A tin on your counter.** At *Known*, the Foundation offers the player a collection tin for the counter. Customers drop coins
  (a trickle the player must remit weekly). Inspectors who see a Foundation tin on a counter are a shade more courteous: the
  chance of an inspection turning into a standards charge drops a little. Remit short, and the Treasurer notices.
- **Receipts.** Every bribe paid as a Foundation gift gives the player a 1x1 receipt (file 05). A stack naming one officer is
  leverage. A complete set of an officer's receipts is a contract for Static.
- **The Scholar.** A Mills child the player knows (a vent kid, a keeper's daughter, a picker) sits the Scholars' exam. The
  player can pay for books and tutoring (slips over a season). If the child wins, they go up, and send letters, and ten years
  later their letters come from the Lease Office.

## The plasma buyers

> "Two cards in one name and I'll know. Two names on one arm and I'll know that too. Sit down, make a fist."
> (Ferro Lusk, the Red Door)

### Origin: a hospital that charges like a bank

**St. Ober's Spire**, the hospital tower out in the bay (file 07), is run by a charity that charges like a bank. It treats the
rich of every tower in the bay, and the rich need blood: for surgery, for the long slow treatments of the old, and for
**fractionation**, the separation of plasma into proteins that St. Ober's sells as medicine to the other towers. Rich people do
not sell their blood. Poor people do.

After the Drowning, the Stack's injured went to St. Ober's on Company barges, and the bills came back as debt. In **Y59** St.
Ober's offered the Company a way to settle some of those bills in kind: a **plasma programme** in the Stack, under licence.
Halden wrote **Bylaw 91** ("No person shall sell blood except to a licensed plasma buyer") and sold three licences. That made
plasma a monopoly at the selling end, and the price a monopoly price: **15 cr for 450 ml in the Mills, 12 in the Sump**, twice
a month at most (file 04). St. Ober's charges **400 cr** for a transfusion. The difference pays for the cold chain, the licence,
the boat, and the licensees' houses in the Terraces.

### How blood moves

1. **The draw room.** A licensed buyer's room in the Mills or the Sump: a row of reclining chairs (old Works canteen chairs with
   the backs cut down), a centrifuge, a cold box, a nurse or someone who passes for one. The seller is weighed, pricked, asked
   three questions, and drawn. Plasma is separated in the centrifuge and the red cells go back into the arm (the slow part: an
   hour in the chair). The seller gets 15 cr, a Vitabrick, a cup of water, and a punch in their **donor card**.
2. **The card.** A punched card, one per donor, two holes a month, fourteen days apart. Draw rooms check cards against a shared
   list. A **double-carder** sells under two names; draw rooms that catch one blacklist both.
3. **The grade.** Every bag is tested. Calder's poison is in Calder's blood: lead, cadmium, solvent traces. Plasma is graded
   **A** (low metals: rare, mostly from Middle sellers and the young), **B** (usable for fractionation after cleaning), or **C**
   (research only). A seller's grade is written on their card. An A-grade seller is paid 20 cr, and is courted.
4. **The cold box.** Bags go into cold boxes (insulated, iced) and down to the Landing.
5. **The blood car.** Every morning at **06:00**, a refrigerated crate leaves the top of the Landing hoist in Core E for the
   floor-4 Landing, and nobody else rides it (06a). Cold boxes from Mills draw rooms ride it down.
6. **The boat.** From the Landing, the **Mercy Tender**, St. Ober's own launch, takes the boxes out to the Spire. On slack
   days the same boat comes alongside the Flats' berths with a chair on deck and draws whoever will sit. The Flats call it
   **the blood boat**.

### What else they buy

The licence covers blood. The buyers buy everything the body makes that someone else wants:
- **Hair.** Long hair, uncut, unbleached, sold for Crown wigs and for "real-fibre" brushes. A long plait fetches 30 to 80 cr. The
  Mills has a generation of girls with short hair.
- **Milk.** Breast milk, for Crown and Terraces nurseries, under a "wet-nursing" contract. Frozen in bags. Well paid, tested
  hard (the metals again), and the most bitter trade in the tower.
- **Urine and samples.** St. Ober's "Calder Kidney Programme" buys stone-belly patients' urine and blood samples for a few
  credits (file 03). Nobody knows what they are studying.
- **Teeth.** Not for anything. The buyers do not buy teeth. The Mills keeps saying they do, which tells you something.

The Rain Church holds that nothing that "falls from you" can be sold: blood, hair, tears, milk. That list is the plasma
buyers' catalogue, and it is no accident; the Church wrote the rule after the buyers came.

- **Kip Marlowe, carter.** Nineteen. Runs cold boxes to the blood car at 05:40 every morning, fourteen boxes on a trolley down

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** sellers, regular ones; A-grade sellers, especially; ice; a cold chain that doesn't break; no trouble from the Rain
  Church at the door.
- **Offer:** 15 cr, a brick, a cup, an hour in a chair. Also short loans "against your next draw".
- **Buy:** blood, plasma, hair, milk, samples. And supplies: needles (licensed, from Vey), tubing, bags, ice, cold packs,
  centrifuge parts, Vitabricks by the case, drinking water by the barrel, sugar.
- **Sell:** to St. Ober's, by the box.

**In play:**
- **Selling plasma.** A counter interaction or a draw room slip: the player (or a hireling) sells 450 ml for 15 cr, once
  every fourteen days, punched on a donor card. It costs nothing else in game terms (no hidden stats), except: the Rain Church
  notices ("dried", shunned for a season: Font cups stop), and the player's card shows a grade, which says something about the
  player's water. A clean-water player sells A-grade for 20. It is the most honest way to show the poison in the system.
- **The cold chain.** Cold-box slips: "four plasma boxes, Red Door (13) to the blood car, Landing (4), by 06:00, cold bay
  required." Pay: 18 cr per box. Needs a cold liner (90 cr). The insurance syndicate pays losses on St. Ober's cargo promptly and
  in full (file 04's hook), so these are the only free-shaft runs worth insuring.
- **Supply contracts.** Draw rooms are steady clients for bricks, water and ice. Ice is a chain: ice falls from above in bags
  (file 03: "ice from above"), melts in hours, and needs a cold bay or an insulated crate to move. A draw room pays 2 cr a kilo
  for ice that arrives as ice.
- **Hair.** A rare, odd, high-value item: a **plait** (1x2, real fibre), bought from a seller, sold to a penthouse fixer or a
  wigmaker in the Middle. The player can broker it, or not.
- **Loans against the draw.** In arrears, a plasma buyer's slip offers 30 cr now against two future draws. It is the cheapest
  loan in the Mills. It is also two fourteen-day lockouts in which you cannot sell blood to cover anything else.

### Example slips

*Plasma slips are printed on the back of St. Ober's donor leaflets: a smiling nurse, "Your gift saves lives", and the order
in grease pencil.*

1. **Ice.** "40 kg ice, as ice, to the Red Door (13), before 05:00." Pay: 80 cr. Ice falls from the Crown's ice baths in the
   evening; you need a cold bay and the timing.
2. **Bricks for the chair.** "Two cases Vitabrick, plain, to the Red Door." Pay: 34 cr. The cheapest steady contract on the
   board.
3. **The boat.** "Cold box from Venner (8) to the Mercy Tender at the Landing, slack water, hours 11 to 13." Pay: 25 cr. A tide
   window slip, like the Tide Folk's. The Tide Folk see you loading the blood boat.
4. **A-grade wanted.** "Sister Morrow will pay 40 cr for a 450 ml draw from anyone graded A. Bring the card." *A slip for the
   player's own body.* A clean-water player qualifies. The Rain Church won't see it as a gift.
5. **Samples.** "Ten sealed sample tubes from divers of the Widows' house (8), floors 1 to 3 only. Do not open." Pay: 60 cr.
   The Widows want to know what for. So does everyone else.

### How they treat the player

Correctly. The buyers are polite, punctual and pay to the credit, which is more than most clients do. At *Known* the Red Door
gives you the cold-chain slips. At *Trusted* Ferro Lusk offers you a carter's contract (every morning at 05:40, fixed pay, a
guaranteed income line that eats an hour of every day). At *Kin*, Sister Morrow asks you to carry something to the Spire in
person (a sealed case of C-grade divers' samples, by launch), which is a door into St. Ober's and into its question.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the licences, Bylaw 91, and the old barge-bill settlement, which still runs. Part of every plasma box settles a
  Drowning-year debt the Company owes St. Ober's.
- **The Rain Church:** open contempt. Fontkeepers turn away anyone with a fresh draw bandage; members who sell are "dried" for
  a season. The Church pickets the Red Door on Wet Sabbaths with cups of rain, offering them to the people in the queue.
  About one in five takes the cup and goes home. Ferro Lusk sends the Church a sack of ice every midsummer, which the Church
  accepts, because it is water.
- **The Mills Co-op:** the **Blood Fund**, the Co-op's emergency loan so members never have to sell, is empty by mid-month.
- **The Old Hands:** too old, too many pills; the buyers won't draw them. Dez finds this funnier than anyone.
- **The Tide Folk:** the blood boat on slack days. Divers sell often and grade C. Hesper Quill has forbidden her berth to

## Static (pirate radio)

> "This is Static, from somewhere in the walls. Rain tomorrow, the Sounding says, and the Sounding should know, it charged
> Halden enough for it. Chute report after the names."
> (Dial, the morning voice)

### Origin: eleven days of dark

At 23:48 on the night of the Drowning, the Switch Hall shorted and every floor from 4 to 29 went black (file 01: **Ten to
Midnight**). It stayed black for **eleven days**. The lights above 30 never flickered. In the dark Mills, nobody knew who
had come up the stairs from the Gate Floor, who had been taken off a roof by the Lifeline barges, who was in the Spire and who
was under the water.

**Lorca Venn** was a radio technician from Works Building Services (Clock 2391), forty-four, laid off in Y41, living on 18.
She had a founding-era intercom amplifier in her kitchen, a bank of cells, and a length of cable. On the second night she
climbed into the cold exhaust riser behind her landing (one of the eight founding risers, cold since the Works shut its lines;
file 01), clipped her cable to the riser's steel lining and transmitted into it. The riser was a steel tube forty storeys high.
It rang like a bell. Every cheap receiver in the Mills, every Works intercom set, every child's crystal radio picked her up.
She read names. A clerk on the Lifeline barges, a cousin, was sending her the carbons of the barge registers by vent kid, and
she read them aloud, all night, every night: who had signed on the water, who had been taken to Ferrybridge, who had been seen.
The signal was so weak and the riser so noisy that the names came through a roar, and people sat with their ears to the
speaker waiting for their own name to come out of the static. That was the name.

When the lights came back, Lorca kept going. Somebody had to read the dead. She read the **Tally** as the Archivists and the
Tide Folk built it, house by house. And then, because people were listening, she read other things.

### What it broadcasts

Static is on the air most nights from 20:00 to 02:00 and most mornings from 05:00 to 07:00. The frequency moves, but always
within a band an old Works intercom set can find by turning the knob slowly. A day on Static:

- **05:00, The Fall.** What's coming down which chute this morning, called in by vent kids at the catch doors and pickers on
  the Bale Hall gantries: "North's heavy: Clear-out on 96, furniture, slashed. East is thin. Paper Chute's got Bureau
  clearance, all VOID, all good." Nobody in the Mills sorts a bin without it.
- **05:30, The Rate.** The street rate for scrip, chits and Hope Vouchers (file 04), the price of a litre at the Fonts and the
  stinkers, the price of a Vitabrick at the store and on the street.
- **06:00, The Tide.** The day's two lows from the Tide Folk's reckoners, and whether the Landing will be reachable.
- **06:15, The Sounding.** The weather. Static does not buy forecasts. It steals them: somebody in Halden's Mast office on the
  roof passes the premium tier to a Wet Servant (06a), who passes it to a vent kid. Static reads the 72-hour storm warning that
  Halden pays for, a day before Halden's own notices go up.
- **20:00, The Names.** Still. Every night. Deaths on the Ninefold's register, births, people looking for people, people who
  took the boat, people coming back on it. Lorca's voice for thirty years; someone else's now, but the format is hers.
- **21:00, Notices.** Paid announcements, read in a flat voice: "Stinker water, the blue door, floor 13, point six a litre." "Bunks
  free at the Drum." "Lost: a Rainmark, blue, on the Long Stair, reward." "Gus on 17 has hour squares at the night rate." **This
  is how Static pays for itself**: 2 cr a reading, 10 cr a week. Static has more advertisers than the Company's lobby boards.
- **22:00, The Floor.** Co-op Floor meetings live, when there is one (06a). Arbiter determinations read out in full, with the
  sums. Inspectors' names and which floors they walked today. Ride Book busiest hours and toll changes leaked from Car House.
- **23:00, Dez.** Or someone like him. Old Hands' stories, Tide Folk songs, a reading from the Wall Book if Hesper Quill ever
  lets them have it. The Old Hands' rule against writing things down doesn't cover saying them on the radio, which is the
  loophole Static was built on.
- **01:00, The Confessional.** Notes dropped down the chutes in folded cups (chute mail, file 02) or passed by vent kids,
  read aloud, unsigned. Love letters. Debts. Accusations. Static reads anything that doesn't name a Wet Servant or a Font.

### How it stays on air

HPS has been trying to find the transmitter for twenty-nine years. In a tower, radio direction-finding is useless: the signal
comes out of every steel riser and duct at once. So HPS hunts the **walkers**, the people who carry the rig. Static never
broadcasts from the same place two nights running. The rig breaks down into four bundles (amplifier, cells, coupler, mic), each
carried by a different walker to a different hatch, assembled for the night, and taken apart before dawn. The coupler clips
to whichever riser or cold duct is chosen. The free shafts are Static's antenna, the same shafts the rats fly.

Sweeps find a rig two or three times a year (file 05: "a bootleg broadcasting rig" is one of the things the Ninefold make sure
an HPS sweep finds). It is always an old rig. Static keeps spares.

### Structure

| Role | Who | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **Voices** | read on air; known only by handle | 4 or 5 |
| **The engineer** | builds and repairs rigs | 1 or 2 |
| **Walkers** | carry the rig's four bundles | about 15, rotating |
| **Listeners** | everyone with a receiver; about one Mills household in three | thousands |
| **Tippers** | vent kids, Wet Servants, clerks, Guild operators, a Mast technician | dozens |
| **The book** | the person who takes the paid notices and the money | 1 |

The voices: **Dial** (mornings, dry, fast, a man; may be two men), **Mother Tongue** (the Names, slow, a woman with a Sump
accent), **the Weatherman** (only on storm warnings; nobody has heard him otherwise), **Saltlick** (the Flats hour, Jory
Malloch's slot when he's on, 06a), and **Ninety** (who reads the Crown's gossip from the Wet Servants in a perfect Glasshouse
accent and is either a Crown servant or a genius).

### People

- **Lorca Venn, founder.** Died in Y80, aged sixty-seven, of the Mills cough. Her last broadcast is the one people quote: she
  read the Names and then her own, and said "that's me, then," and played the tide.
- **Rosalind Venn-Achebe, "Mother Tongue".** Fifty-one. Lorca's daughter, a laundry worker on 9, voice of the Names since Y80.
  Nobody at work knows. Her own daughter doesn't know.
- **Halvard Pike, the engineer.** Thirty-three. Repairs Static's rigs from parts the rats find. A licensed electrician in the
  Middle by day, with a credit rating he guards like a pension. He has never been on air and never will be.
- **Mina Szabo, "the book".** Twenty-eight. Takes the notices and the money from a stall on the Shelf where she sells receivers
  (8 cr in the Sump, 12 in the Mills; file 04). A receiver comes with the frequency chart, hand-drawn, updated weekly.
- **Ninety.** Unknown. HPS believes Ninety is a Crown household servant. The Rain Church believes Ninety is the Roofman. The
  Crown believes Ninety is a hoax. Whoever Ninety is, the gossip is always right.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** to stay on air; cells; parts; tips; paper (anything that proves something); and, deep down, the Proof (06a: the Wall
  Book and the seawall correspondence) read out in full over three nights.
- **Offer:** news, forecasts, prices, names, warnings; airtime for notices; a voice to anyone with something to say.
- **Buy:** founding-era intercom amplifiers, cells, coupler clamps, cable, valves, receivers for repair; documents (Foundation
  receipts, Harrow Memo carbons, Pell Report pages, Ride Book extracts); tips.
- **Sell:** notices (2 cr a reading); receivers (via the book).

**In play:**
- **A receiver on your field.** A Static receiver is a 1x1 item (12 cr in the Mills). On the shelf or the bench, it gives the
  player a short line each morning and evening, in the voice of the broadcast: tomorrow's weather (rain day forecast), the chute
  report (which trunk is rich today, so which catch to upgrade or which Crown sack to buy), the street rate, the tide window, and,
  sometimes, a raid warning ("tasters on fifteen tomorrow"). Information as an item, honest and visible, and a reason the
  morning sort can be planned. Without one, the player learns all of this a day late from slips.
- **Notices.** The player can buy a notice: 2 cr a reading, 10 cr a week. Foot traffic past the player's frontage rises the
  next day (a modifier on shelf sales). A notice that advertises grey goods ("restored coats, 17, the green door") brings more
  customers and a stamper.
- **Tips.** A player with information (a raid seen, a rate card leaked by the Period Club, a Foundation receipt) can sell it to
  Static for a few credits and standing. Tips that are wrong cost standing.
- **Hosting the rig.** At *Trusted*: "We need your back wall for three nights." The rig takes a 2x2 block of your squares near a
  hatch or duct (which most Mills workshops have) from 20:00 to 02:00. Pay: free notices for a month. Risk: a sweep event finds
  it on your squares (a heavy fine, a standing hit with the Company, and Static's eternal gratitude, read out on air).
- **Documents.** The richest Static contracts carry documents: deliver the Harrow Memo carbon, the Wall Book, Kettering's
  ledger, to "a hatch on 21, by a free shaft, at 01:00". The pay is small. The effect is world-sized (the Proof ending, 06a).

### Example slips

*Static slips are not paper. A vent kid recites them at your door, word for word, and leaves a crystal (a token) on your
counter if you agree.*

1. **Cells for the rig.** "Six charged cells, the hatch behind the Gap on 41, by 19:00." Pay: 15 cr and a free notice.
2. **The coupler.** "A founding-era clamp, Works pattern, 60 mm, for a riser lining. Old Fen might have one." Pay: 40 cr. A
   first-fit part: the Crib, the Hooks or the divers.
3. **Read this.** "Bring us the receipts. All of them." At *Trusted*, if the player holds three or more Foundation receipts
   naming one officer. Pay: 30 cr. The officer is named on air. The officer's office raises its prices on the player's floor
   for a month, and the officer is moved. Their replacement wants a gift too.
4. **The Names.** "A name for the Names: Bettany Marwood, floor 8, died last night. Tell her daughter on 12 before we read it."
   Pay: nothing. You are the person who brings the news before the radio does. The daughter becomes a client, or doesn't.
5. **Walk.** "A bundle to the hatch on 19, at 19:30. Don't open it. Don't keep it past 20:00." Pay: 10 cr. You are a walker for
   one night. If a sweep catches the bundle in your bay, you are a walker in the HPS file forever.

### How they treat the player

As a listener first, a source second, an advertiser third, and a host if trusted. Static never names its friends on air and
never stops naming its enemies. At *Known* you hear your floor mentioned in the chute report. At *Trusted* your notices are
read in Dial's voice, which brings twice the customers. At *Kin*, Mother Tongue reads your name in the Names on a night you
did something for somebody, which in the Mills is as close as anyone gets to a medal.

- **The Rain Church:** the Wet Sabbath sermon and the rain forecast on air; Static never names a Font.

- **The Mills Co-op:** the Floor meetings live; the Ledger wing thinks this is reckless.
- **The Old Hands:** Dez on air. The loophole: saying is not writing.
- **The Archivists:** the occasional leaked page, never confirmed. Imelda Vos listens every night and has never once been

- **The Lift Guild:** leaks from Car House: toll changes, busy hours. Rudolf Kasza would pay a great deal to know who.
- **Shaft rats:** the rats carry the rig's bundles and fly the riser the coupler is clipped to. Static calls rat warnings on

- **The Stinkers:** advertise. Every night.
- **Seed savers, insect ranchers:** savers' seed swaps announced in code; ranchers advertise wild-line colonies.

## The penthouse fixers

> "They don't want new. New is for the Middle. They want *real*, and they want it to have been somewhere."
> (Ottoline Wick)

### Origin: the Crown wanted things that couldn't be bought

When the Crown floors were sold as title after the Drowning (Y58), the people who bought them had everything they could
order: Greenhold produce by drone, Vey's best, Skywater by the case, Vantage-made appliances. What they could not order was
anything **real** in the old sense: wood that grew, wool from an animal, an egg from a hen with a name, a thing that had a
history. Calder was under the sea with everything that had a history in it.

The first fixers were Esplanade antiques dealers who had lost their shops in the flood and kept their clients. They found
things: a pre-Drowning dresser from a Wellside roof, a chapel bell, a crate of real linen from a drowned hotel. Their clients
paid fortunes, and paid more for the story than for the thing. Within a decade a **fixer** was a profession: someone with a
Crown keycard, a book of clients, a network that ran to the bottom of the tower and out across the bay, and a talent for
**provenance**.

### What a fixer does

- **The brief.** A Crown household writes what it wants, often vaguely ("something for the long wall in the dining room, old,
  with a story, not sad"), sometimes exactly ("twelve brown eggs, same hen, laid this week, for Sunday"). The fixer prices it,
  takes a third up front, and sends **runners** down the tower.
- **The find.** Runners buy from the Mills, the Sump, the Flats and the drowned plant: Calder antiques from the Tide Folk,
  founding-era fittings from the Hooks and divers, real-grade produce from growers, heirloom fruit from seed savers, folk rain
  from the Rain Church, restored goods from the Voided ("vintage"), and things recovered by stampers (Fair Ashe, file 05).
- **The provenance.** A fixer's real product is the **story card**: a thick card, hand-lettered, that tells where the thing has
  been. "Oak dresser, Wellside, c. Y20. Recovered from the roof of 14 Brine Lane, where the Merrow family kept it above the
  flood for three days." Some of it is true. A story card with an Archive provenance certificate and an Old Hand's clock
  signature is worth more than the object. A story card the fixer made up entirely is worth almost as much, if the client never
  checks.
- **The Assay.** Everything delivered into the Crown passes the **Crown Assay**: a residents' standard for "real" goods, set by
  the Amenity (new, below), checked at the Postern on 93 and again at the household by staff with spectro wands. Goods
  that fail are voided and dropped down the North Chute (file 02). A fixer's reputation is their Assay rate.
- **The season.** Crown fashion moves in seasons, and fixers drive them. **Works brass** (Y79: door handles, valve wheels,
  founding-era fittings polished and mounted). **Salt line** (Y82, and still running: objects with a visible flood mark, the
  water's height on them, worth double with the line intact; called "Drowning chic" by people who don't buy it). **Mills
  heritage greens** (the Co-op's produce, sold with a photograph of the grower). **Folk rain** (the Rain Church's Font water in
  stoppered bottles, for dinner parties). **Tide cord** (Tide Folk knotted cords as wall hangings: the knots are trades, and the
  Crown doesn't know that).
- **Quiet cars.** Fixers pay the Lift Guild for unlogged freight from the Middle to the Core A transfer (06a), and use the
  **Crown Pad** on the roof for things that come from other towers (file 02).

### Structure

About a dozen fixers work the Crown, each with a **client book** (a list of households they serve; books are sold, inherited,
stolen). Each fixer runs three to ten **runners** (Middle and Mills people who know the lower tower), keeps a **showroom** on
floor 89, the staff floor, and holds a Crown keycard on a household's account. Runners use **pickers** in the Mills: people who
go through chute bins and shelves for a fee, looking for the fixer's list. Fixers do not cooperate, except to agree, every
season, what the season is.

### People

- **Ottoline Wick.** Sixty-three. The senior fixer; client book of fourteen households, including the Strand-Eskers. Esplanade
  family, lost everything in Y57, has spent thirty years selling the drowned town back to the people who drowned it, and enjoys
  every sale. Smiles with her whole face. Carries a spectro wand on a silver chain and uses it on everything, including food at
  dinner.
- **Ji-ho Carrow.** Twenty-nine. Mills-born on 16, a picker at eleven, a runner at seventeen, a fixer at twenty-five with a book of
  three households bought from a retiring fixer with eleven years' savings. Knows the Mills from the inside, pays fairly, never
  lies on a story card, and is the fixer the player is most likely to work for. Wants Ottoline's book.
- **Lucan Ferrabee, "the Novelist".** Fifty. Writes the best story cards in the tower, and many are fiction. His Assay rate is
  perfect, because the objects are real: it's the stories that aren't. He buys Archive provenance certificates by the dozen
  (06a: some carry a stamp and no A-number; Lucan is one of the reasons Ink the copyist has a nicer flat than a copyist should).
- **Adaeze Thorne.** Sixty-eight. Fixer to the Halden household alone, for thirty years. Says nothing, buys only founding-era
  pieces, and only ones with a Works part number. **Hook:** the Halden household is not decorating. It is buying back the
  founding-era kit, piece by piece, and storing it.
- **Benedikt Rusk, runner for Ottoline.** Forty. Works the Landing and the Exchange steps on Great Ebb days. The Tide Folk know
  him as the man who asks for kin-house goods and doubles the price when told no.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** real things with stories; first-fit parts with numbers; a high Assay rate; exclusives (one hen, one bed of
  strawberries, one diver); the next season before the other fixers.
- **Offer:** prices nobody else in the tower pays; access to the Crown as a market; commissions; a story card that turns your
  goods into an heirloom.
- **Buy:** real-grade produce (R✓ and especially heirloom H), hen's eggs, real wood, real wool, real linen, Calder antiques
  (especially salt-line pieces and kin-house goods), founding-era parts and fittings, folk rain, hair (from the plasma buyers),
  restored luxury goods ("vintage"), Old Hands' signatures, Archive certificates.
- **Sell:** into the Crown, at multiples: a founding-era cell bought for 400 from an Old Hand sells at 1,200 (file 04); a
  re-legged real-wood chair bought for 40 in the Mills sells in the Crown, with a story card, for 600.

**In play:**
- **Commissions.** The fixers' slips are the most specific and best-paying contracts in the game, the ones that ask for exactly
  one thing with exactly one property: "a first-fit Sorrel meter, glass eye intact, polished, for a bathroom wall"; "a
  real-wood chair, pre-Drowning, with the salt line still on it"; "twelve brown hen's eggs, laid by the same hen"; "a bottle of
  rain from the Dry Font, stoppered, with the date". Each one pulls on a different chain, and many need a property the analyser
  or an Old Hand reveals (era, maker's mark, salt line).
- **Story cards as items.** A **story card** (1x1) packed with its object multiplies the price. The player can buy one from a
  fixer, earn one (an Archive certificate plus an Old Hand's signature), or, at *Trusted* with Lucan Ferrabee, write one: a
  choice between true (lower price, safe) and embellished (higher price, a chance per Assay that the story is checked).
- **The Assay.** Crown deliveries are checked: a grade and an origin test. Failing (a synthetic in an R✓ contract, a restored
  item sold as new) voids the goods and drops them down the North Chute, and your Assay rate falls. If your catch is on the North
  Chute, your own voided goods might come back to your bin next morning (file 02).
- **The salt line.** An item property: a visible tide mark. Cleaning salvage removes it (and raises its Middle price); keeping it
  raises its Crown price. A small, meaningful choice about the same object, depending on who you sell to.
- **Kin-house goods.** A Calder antique can carry a hidden property: which street it came from. A fixer pays most for kin-house
  goods (things from a Tide Folk family's own house). Selling one costs the player the berth forever (06a). The analyser or a
  Tide Folk contact reveals it; selling blind is a gamble with someone else's grief.

### Example slips

*Fixer slips are embossed cards (the brief's style for slips from above), with the fixer's name and no address, and the
brief in fountain pen.*

1. **For the long wall.** "Something old, with a story, not sad, at least 2x3. Wood, if possible. Salt line preferred." Pay:
   400 to 1,500 cr, by Assay and story. Deliver to the showroom on 89.
2. **Sunday eggs.** "Twelve brown eggs, one hen, laid this week. Ji-ho C." Pay: 300 cr. There are about two hundred hens in the
   tower (file 04), and the Crown wants eggs from one of them, which means a coop and a hen with a visible trait.
3. **Works brass.** "Eight valve wheels, founding-era, Works pattern, matched. Polished." Pay: 240 cr. They come from the North
   Chute (Crown renovations threw them out last season; the season has turned; the Crown wants them back).
4. **Folk rain.** "Six stoppered bottles, rain from a Font, labelled by hand with the Font and the date." Pay: 90 cr. The Church
   will give you the rain. Selling it is the one sin.
5. **The Novelist.** "Story cards wanted, Mills provenance, any object, by a writer with a Mills hand. 15 cr a card." Pay: per
   card. You are writing false histories for real objects. Your handwriting is the authenticity.

### How they treat the player

As a source, charmingly. Fixers pay well, pay on the day, remember your name, and never ask you to their showroom. At *Known*
you get commissions. At *Trusted* you get exclusives (Ji-ho will take every egg your best hen lays). At *Kin*, a fixer offers
you a runner's place or a share of a client book, which is a way up: the first step to the Terraces that doesn't go through
the Lease Office. A fixer's warmth goes as fast as your Assay rate.

### Relations

- **Halden:** none officially; fixers are private contractors to title holders. Adaeze Thorne's work for the Halden household is
  the closest the Company comes.
- **The Rain Church:** folk rain; the Church takes the money and calls it a gift.
- **The Tide Folk:** Calder antiques; kin-house goods, which the berths won't sell and runners keep asking for.
- **The Mills Co-op:** "Mills heritage greens". The Co-op sells its best produce to Ji-ho Carrow, and buys its pump bearings with
  the money.
- **The Old Hands:** fixers pay a fee per authentication signature. The Old Hands know their names are being used to sell
  founding-era pieces to the people who stopped their pension.
- **The Archivists:** best customers for provenance certificates. The Archive knows which Crown pieces are fakes and doesn't say.
- **The Lift Guild:** quiet cars to the Core A transfer.
- **Seed savers:** the fixers are the savers' best market and greatest danger: heirloom strawberries at 120 a punnet need an R✓
  certificate the savers can't get, so the fixers buy them one by bribery (file 04), and every bribe is a paper trail to a
  saver's bed.
- **Insect ranchers, stinkers, shaft rats:** none, folk rain aside; rats run fixer parcels on the quiet.
- **Part two:** the Voided supply "vintage"; plasma buyers supply hair; Flats divers supply salt-line pieces and founding-era
  parts; Static's Ninety is how fixers learn what a household wants before it asks; the Amenity (new) sets the Assay the fixers
  pass; Fair Ashe the stamper sells them recovered goods.

### In the endings

- **Up by the story.** The player becomes a fixer: a Crown keycard on a household's account, a showroom on 89, and a client book.
  It is a real way up. The ending text is a story card for an object the player once pulled from their own chute.
- **Salt line.** A darker ending for a fixer player: the season moves on, and the Crown throws out the salt-line pieces, slashed
  and stamped VOID. They come down the North Chute. The Tide Folk pick them off the wrack line.

**Hook:** Adaeze Thorne has bought, for the Halden household, at least one of every founding-era part type the Archive's Index
lists, except one: a **Dark Floors access card**, Works pattern, 0000-series. She has a standing order for it at any price.

**Hook:** a story card Lucan Ferrabee swears he invented ("Oak dresser, Wellside, recovered from 14 Brine Lane, where the Merrow
family kept it above the flood") turns out to be true in every detail. Ada Merrow's recorded testimony (file 01) mentions the

## The insurance syndicate

> "Over ten thousand claims settled since founding."
> (the Syndicate's advertisement, on every licensed lane gate; true)

### Names first

File 04 gives the facts: the **Calder Mutual Assurance Syndicate**, office on floor 64, insures drones, cargo, shop stock and
machines; **Bay Cover** costs 4% of declared value on a licensed lane and 12% on a free shaft and pays 60% on a covered loss;
the exclusions run to four pages; it pays about one claim in nine. Its own adverts call it **the Mutual**. Below floor 30
almost nobody does: the Mills already had a Mutual (Local 9's strike fund, 06a), and nobody wants to share the word. In the
Mills it is **the Syndicate**, and on the Flats, where nobody can afford it, it is **One-in-Nine**, for the one claim in nine.

### Origin: the drones needed a reason to pay for lanes

The Syndicate was founded in **Y67**, the year after Halden's licensed drone lanes opened and the Lift Guild took its lane share
(06a). The lanes had a problem: they were slower and dearer than the free shafts, and a courier who flew a free shaft and lost
one bay in ten was still ahead. A group of Terraces investors, two Crown families and a Count House actuary put up the capital
for an insurer whose cover would make the lanes worth paying for. The cover was cheap on a lane and dear on a shaft, and it
excluded "acts of shaft". The Company gave the Syndicate a concession: Bay Cover is the only cargo insurance that may be sold on
a licensed lane gate.

The investors are called **Names**, after an old custom: each Name puts up capital, takes a share of the premiums, and (in theory)
is liable for losses without limit. In practice no Name has paid a loss from their own pocket since Y67, because the
exclusions are written by the same actuary who calculates the premiums.

### How a claim dies

A Bay Cover claim goes up the **Claim Ladder**. Each rung is a place the claim can stop, and most do.

1. **The cover note.** The policy slip must have been packed in the bay at launch (a 1x1 square of cargo space; file 04), with
   the bay **sealed** with a numbered seal.
2. **The incident number.** Within one day, an HPS incident number (file 05). No number, no incident; no incident, no claim.
   An HPS incident number for a free shaft costs a Basic subscriber "within the shift" to obtain, by which time the shift is over.
3. **Form C1**, with a manifest of the cargo as packed, signed.
4. **The bay seal.** The drone must come back with its bay seal intact. A looted drone's seal is broken, which means the loss
   cannot be proved, which voids the claim. A drone that does not come back at all cannot show its seal, which also voids it.
5. **The assessor's visit.** An assessor inspects the drone, the manifest and the packing record, and decides whether the
   cargo was packed to **the Standard** (below).
6. **The Determination of Cover.** A letter. Usually: "The Syndicate regrets that the loss falls within Exclusion..."
7. **Appeal.** To the Arbiter, 60 cr filing fee (file 04). The Arbiter has found for the Syndicate in every Bay Cover appeal on
   record but four.

Payment, when it comes, is 60% of declared value, less the premium already paid, less a **handling charge** of 5 cr, in
credits, within thirty days. "Within thirty days" means the thirtieth.

### The Standard

The **Approved Carriage Standard** (the Mills just says **the Standard**) is the Syndicate's packing rule. It is the most
useful thing the Syndicate ever wrote, because it is mostly true: bays packed this way do survive rough landings. It is also
the instrument that voids most claims, because almost nobody packs this way.

- **Liner.** A Syndicate-approved bay liner (12 cr at the store) in the bay.
- **Clearance.** Any item marked **fragile** (glass, eggs, founding-era boards, sealed medicine, live insects) must not touch
  the bay wall, and must not touch another fragile item. One clear square, or a square of **approved padding**, on every side.
- **Padding.** Approved padding is liner offcut, foam from the store, or (since Y78, after a long argument) **dried wrack**
  from the Flats (06a), which the assessor tests by squeezing.
- **Liquids.** Upright. A vessel lying on its side is "improperly stowed".
- **Manifest.** The manifest must list every item in the bay, including the policy slip itself. Most people forget the slip.
- **Weight.** No item more than a third of the bay's rated load.

**In play:** this is the Syndicate's real place in the game, and it is a packing puzzle. Buying Bay Cover adds visible
constraints to the bay: fragile items glow with a one-square clearance halo, and the launch screen shows a **Standard check**
(pass/fail per rule). A drone packed to the Standard, lost on a licensed lane, is paid. A drone packed to the Standard and
lost in a free shaft is excluded (acts of shaft), but at least the player knew. Insured bays carry less, because clearance
costs squares, which is exactly the trade the Syndicate's actuary priced. The player can always see why a claim was refused:
the assessor's letter names the rule and shows the square. No hidden roll.

### What the Syndicate also sells

- **Frame Cover**: the drone itself, 2 cr a day per frame. Excludes "wear", which the assessor finds on every frame older than
  a week.
- **Stock Cover**: goods on your squares against fire, flood and "sudden contamination". Excludes everything below floor 30 by
  **acts of tide** (the "historic waterline" is drawn at floor 30).
- **Certified Carriage**: full cover, 100% of declared value, on cargo carried by Lift Guild car with a freight chit (06a). The
  premium is 2%. It almost always pays, because a Guild car almost never loses anything. This is the Guild's best argument for
  chits, and the Syndicate's best advertisement.
- **Rent Protection**: pays your rent for up to four weeks if you are unable to work through illness. Excludes illness "arising
  from environmental conditions", which in the Stack's water, mould and dust is all illness.
- **Diver Cover**: life cover for Widows' permit divers. Excludes drowning.

### Recovery: the Syndicate as a buyer of stolen goods

When the Syndicate does pay a claim, the lost cargo becomes the Syndicate's property ("subrogation"). The Syndicate then hires
**recovery agents** to find it, which means buying it back from the Sump fences who bought it from the looters. The Syndicate
pays the fence a fraction, takes the goods, and sells them at auction on 64 as **recovered stock**. The looter, the fence and
the Syndicate all make money from the same lost bay. The courier who lost it got 60%, less handling, on the thirtieth.

### Structure

| Who | What | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **The Names** | investors: Terraces families, two Crown families, an endowment fund | 31 |
| **The Committee** | five Names who set premiums and exclusions | 5 |
| **The Chief Underwriter** | runs the Room on 64 | 1 |
| **Underwriters** | price unusual risks (a Gala cargo, a machine delivery) | 4 |
| **Claims clerks** | receive C1 forms | 6 |
| **Assessors** | inspect drones, bays, manifests | 3 |
| **Recovery agents** | buy back subrogated goods in the Sump | contracted |
| **Gate agents** | sell cover notes at the licensed lane gates | about 20 |

### People

- **Gideon Lasker, Chief Underwriter.** Fifty-five. A Count House actuary before Y67, one of the Syndicate's founders, and the
  author of the Standard and most of the exclusions. He believes, sincerely, that the Standard has saved more cargo than every
  claim the Syndicate ever refused, and keeps a framed chart on his wall that proves it. He has never flown a drone.
- **Nils Ferreira, Clock 2077, assessor.** Seventy-one (06a). A lift mechanic at the Works, then the Guild, now the Syndicate's
  senior assessor. Finds "pre-existing fault" in every claim, and is usually right, because everything in the tower has a
  pre-existing fault. The Canteen doesn't serve him. He eats alone on 64, and is better at his job than anyone in the building.
- **Priya Sandoval, claims clerk.** Thirty-four. Middle-born, rents on 33. Once a month she files one Mills claim under the wrong
  exclusion code, which sends it to a different assessor who passes it on the papers. She has paid eleven claims this way in
  three years. She keeps a list. **Hook:** Lasker knows. He lets her, because eleven claims in three years is cheaper than the
  advertising.
- **Imogen Rask, a Name.** Thirty-six. The youngest title holder in the Crown (she bought a title on 92 for 1.4 million cr,
  file 04). Put money into the Syndicate because her advisers told her to. Has started reading the Determination letters, and
  can't stop.
- **Corvin Daley, recovery agent.** Forty-two. Works the Hooks' sorting room on 6 and the fences of the Sump with a book of
  subrogated manifests. Recognises a lost bay by its contents. The Sump tolerates him because he pays.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** premiums; licensed lane traffic; packing to the Standard; no claims; the Co-op's Box and the shaft rats' Kitty gone.
- **Offer:** cover (Bay, Frame, Stock, Certified Carriage, Rent Protection, Diver); the Standard; recovered stock at auction.
- **Buy:** structural reports from the Archivists (drawings of shaft linings and load-bearing walls, to prove "inherent structural
  risk"); subrogated goods back from fences; information about Box and Kitty members.
- **Sell:** cover notes at the gates; recovered stock at auction on 64.

**In play:**
- **The cover note** (policy slip, 1x1) is the token and the product. Pack it, seal the bay, pay the premium at launch.
- **The Standard as a puzzle modifier** (above): the core use.
- **Claims as a small, honest event chain.** A loss on a covered launch produces a C1 slip to fill (one click), then an
  assessor's letter: paid, with the sum and date; or refused, with the rule and the square. A refused claim can be appealed (20
  cr; long odds) or taken to Priya Sandoval (a favour, at *Known*).
- **Recovered stock.** An auction slip: "Recovered stock, lot 14: bay contents, Kestrel, lost floor 22 shaft, unopened." A
  mystery lot, bought blind. It might be your own.
- **Recovery jobs.** The Syndicate hires the player to find specific subrogated goods: "a Vey sealed liner, lot 3311, last
  seen at a Sump fence. Buy it back at up to 60 cr. Fee: 25 cr." The player becomes the middleman between the insurer and the
  thieves, and can keep the goods instead, if they're good.
- **The Box.** The Co-op's own mutual (06a: pays half value, more often) is the alternative. Joining it requires Co-op standing;
  it pays on a member's word and a Walk captain's countersignature, in chits. The shaft rats' **Kitty** (file 04) is the third.
  Three insurers, three kinds of trust: a contract, a neighbour, a friend.

### Example slips

*Syndicate slips are printed, numbered, with a reference code on every line, and a blank where the claim number goes.*

1. **Assessment courier.** "Carry assessor's kit (spectro wand, seal reader, camera) to floor 18 for Mr. Ferreira's 14:00
   visit, and back." Pay: 15 cr. The visit is to a neighbour's claim. You will be in the room.
2. **Recovery.** (as above) Pay: 25 cr fee, 60 cr budget. The fence wants 80.
3. **Gate agent.** "Sell cover notes at the floor 30 lane gate, 08:00 to 12:00. Commission 10%." Pay: commission only. A day on

5. **Structural risk.** "Archive drawing, Shaft Schedule sheet for Core D, certified copy, to 64." Pay: 90 cr. The Syndicate

Correctly and coldly. The Syndicate treats everyone the same, which is its pride and the problem. At *Known* (a record of
Standard-packed launches) your premiums fall a point. At *Trusted* an underwriter will quote you for unusual cargo (a Gala run,

- **The Lift Guild:** allies. Certified Carriage is the Guild's argument; the Ordinance is the Syndicate's wish.

- **The Old Hands:** Nils Ferreira, whom the Canteen won't serve.

- **Rain Church, stinkers, seed savers, insect ranchers:** inherent vice excludes every living cargo; ranchers' live insects and

## The vent kids

> "Lid you lift, you put back. Lid you don't know, you don't lift."
> (the first rule of the Under)

### Origin: every lid opens

Marrow Teague's rule for the Stack was "every lid opens" (file 01). Every floor is laid in lift-out service tiles, eighty
centimetres a side, each a lid on the services underneath: water, power, data, air, drain. Under the tiles is the **plenum**,
about forty centimetres deep, running the whole width of every floor, crossed by pipe and cable and duct, and connected to the
air plant's trunks, the cable risers, the drip lines and the old Tube ducts. It is a second building, under the first, and it
is exactly as tall as a child on hands and knees.

After the Drowning, thousands of children were orphaned on the Gate Floor and the roofs of Low Calder, or came up from the
Lifeline barges with nobody. In the eleven days of dark, children were the only people who could move through the Mills
without the stairs: they went under the tiles. By the time the lights came back, they had learned the plenum, and the plenum
was theirs. The Mills calls it **the Under**. The children who live in it are **vent kids**, from the louvred vents in the
tiles where they come up.

They are not one gang. They are many small **crews**, each holding a stretch of the Under, and they have kept something going
for nearly thirty years that no adult has managed: the Under is better maintained than the floors above it. A crew that lifts a
lid puts it back. A crew that finds a leak reports it (to someone it trusts, not the Company). A crew that clears a chute jam
gets paid.

### The Under

- **The plenum.** Forty centimetres, under every Mills floor, dry where the drains work, wet where they don't. Warm near
  insect farms and the Dark Floors' bulkhead. Cold near the Gills' trunks.
- **The ducts.** The Lung's air trunks: big enough to crawl, loud, dusty. Kids who work the ducts are **Ducklings**.
- **The drip lines.** Places where condensate from the air trunks collects (file 03): vent kids know every one and sell the water
  by the cup. A crew's drip line is its income and its secret.
- **The chute collars.** The steel collars where the chute trunks pass each floor, with access hatches. Chute jams are cleared from
  here (file 02: **Chute Jam** events, "no fall today until a vent kid clears your trunk").
- **The car roofs.** The tops of Lift Guild cars, which kids ride between floors ("roofing", 06a).
- **The Tube.** The founding pneumatic post tubes, now licensed lanes. Kids go nowhere near them: a lane drone in a tube is a
  bad way to die. This is the one place the Under ends.

Kids map the Under in **chalk**, on the undersides of tiles and the walls of ducts, in a sign language every crew reads:
an arrow (way through), a circle (dry), a wavy line (wet), a cross (don't), a cup (water here), a crown (a lid under a rich
lease), a car with a line through it (a Guild operator watches this hatch), a "1" (one kid only: too tight for two).

### Growing out

The plenum is forty centimetres. At about thirteen or fourteen, a child's shoulders stop fitting. The Mills calls it
**shouldering**: "she's shouldered", meaning she's out. Shouldered kids lose the Under all at once, and what happens next is the
real story of the faction:
- many become **shaft rats** (a drone goes where you can't any more);
- some become **pickers** and **bale pickers** (they know the chutes);
- some become **Hooks** in the Sump, or walkers for Static, or carters for the plasma buyers;
- a few go to HPS, who value someone who knows the Under, and pay them to forget their crews. The kids call these **lid-lifters**,
  and a lid-lifter's old crew changes every chalk sign they ever knew.

### Structure

A **crew** is five to fifteen kids holding a stretch: one floor's plenum, a set of ducts, a chute collar. Each crew has a
**Lid** (the leader, usually the oldest who hasn't shouldered), a **Chalk** (the mapmaker, who keeps the signs current), and a
**Tin** (who keeps the crew's sweets, chits and cells). Crews are named for where they work: the **Seventeen Tilers**, the **East
Collar** (the East Chute's collar on 12 to 15), the **Ducklings of Twenty**, the **Bulkheads** (floor 21, against the Dark
Floors' wall, warm and strange).

Currency in the Under: **suckers** (Orrin sugar lumps, wrapped), chits, cells (a charged cell is a lamp), water by the cup.
Shouldered kids who still trade with crews are **grandads**, whatever their age or sex.

### People

- **Tansy Okoro, Lid of the Seventeen Tilers.** Twelve. Holds the plenum under the player's floor. Fierce, precise, honest in
  her own accounting, and terrified of shouldering (she is tall for twelve). Knows what's in every tenant's floor drain. Sells
  the player chute-jam clearing, parcels and information, and will hold a grudge over a single missing sucker for a year.
- **Fennel.** Nine. The smallest Tiler, the only kid who fits the "1" passages. Has seen things in the narrow places that he tells
  nobody but Tansy. Collects founding-era screws.
- **Bran Doyle.** Thirteen. Lid of the East Collar. Shouldering this season. Spends every spare credit on a broken drone frame,
  because he plans to be a rat by winter, and he will be.
- **Grandad Sami.** Fifteen, shouldered two years ago. Now the crews' fence: he takes what the kids find (screws, cells,
  dropped rings, chute finds caught in the collars) and sells it up. Takes a fifth. The kids trust him because he once spent a
  week in HPS custody and didn't give up a single chalk sign.
- **Wilhelmina "Owl" Achterberg.** Eleven. Dez Achterberg's great-niece (06a). Lid of the Bulkheads on 21. Says the Dark Floors' wall
  is warm in the middle of the night and cold in the morning, and has a chalk map of where the warm patches move.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** sweets; cells for lamps; water; to be paid in things they can eat; not to be counted (an occupancy count finds kids
  on racks and fines the lease); to never shoulder.
- **Offer:** messages and small parcels, fast, through the Under; chute-jam clearing; finding dropped things; lookout work
  (they see inspectors coming two floors away); information; drip-line water.
- **Buy:** sweets, cells, chalk, lamps, kneepads (the Under eats knees), gloves, cups.
- **Sell:** screws, washers, dropped coins and rings, small founding-era parts caught in the plenum, drip water, chalk maps.

**In play:**
- **The kid run.** A delivery route that isn't a drone: a 1x1 or 1x2 parcel goes through the Under to any lease within the
  band, in about an hour, for a sucker or two (0.5 to 1 cr). No lane, no shaft, no toll, no Syndicate. It doesn't cross a band
  gate (the Under is cut at floor 30, the transfer deck) and it can't carry anything bigger than a child can push. Perfect for
  small urgent goods (a dose of medicine, a spare part, a token) and a way to show the plenum as a living world.
- **Chute-jam clearing.** On a Chute Jam event, Tansy's slip appears: "Your trunk's jammed. Clear it first for 2 cr, or wait your
  turn." Pay to have your catch clearing first, or wait while your neighbours' bins fill.
- **Lookouts.** Paying a crew a few suckers a week gives a visible warning when an inspector, a stamper or a collector is on
  the floor below: an hour's notice before the event, enough to move one shelf. Not paying them gives the warning to someone else.
- **Finds.** Dropped things in the plenum come back to the crews. A lost token (06a's Rainmark, a dive tag, a door card) can be
  bought back from Grandad Sami. A crew can be sent to look for something specific in the Under: a "look for" slip.
- **Theft.** Crews that are not paid sometimes lift lids under unpaid leases. A theft event (a shelf missing a small item
  overnight) is often the Under. The Walk can't stop it; a crew can.
- **The map.** A crew's chalk map, copied onto paper (a 1x2 item, bought from a Chalk at *Trusted*), reveals the drip lines on
  your floor: a free water source of a cup or two an hour, if you're willing to crawl for it or pay a kid to.

### Example slips

*Vent kid slips are chalked on a scrap of tile underside or a sweet wrapper, pushed up through your floor vent.*

1. **Suckers.** (a chalked cup and a "20") "Twenty suckers to the Tilers' lid by the drain square. We'll bring it back." Pay:
   one found thing, unknown, small. A mystery-box contract for a few credits.
2. **Kneepads.** "Kneepads, six pairs. Old shoes cut up is fine." Pay: a chalk map of the drip lines under 17. Slashed shoes come
   down the chute by the bag.
3. **Lamps for the Bulkheads.** "Four cells, charged, to floor 21 vent by the bulkhead. Owl says hurry." Pay: Owl's chalk map of
   the warm patches on the Dark Floors' wall. Worthless, unless you're on the Dark Floors' trail (file 14).
4. **Hide it.** "Collector on 17 at 14:00. Give us anything small with a yellow tag and we'll keep it under till he's gone."
   Pay: 3 cr per item. A crime (frustration of recovery, file 05) that a vent kid will commit for a sucker.
5. **Dez's locker.** (only with Owl, at *Kin*) "There's a '1' passage from 21 into 22. Fennel fits. Do you want Dez's toolroll?"
   The drone-sized way in (06a) has a child-sized way in. Sending a nine-year-old into the Dark Floors is the player's choice to
   make or refuse. **Hook:** Fennel comes back. He won't say what he saw. He brings the toolroll, and a screw that is still warm.

### How they treat the player

As a lease above their heads. Pay the crew, keep your drain square clear, never lift a lid yourself, and they are the best
neighbours in the tower. At *Known* you get kid runs. At *Trusted* lookouts and the map. At *Kin*, Tansy's crew sleeps in the
plenum under your workshop, and nothing on your floor happens that you don't know about. Report a crew to a warden once, and
every chalk sign on your floor changes.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the Company pretends vent kids don't exist, because acknowledging them would mean acknowledging the plenum is
  open. HPS uses lid-lifters. Chute clearing by kids saves the Company a contract.
- **The Rain Church:** kids run Font messages and the Wet Servants' notes (06a), and are given a cup at any Font.
- **The Tide Folk:** kids deliver Tide Folk cord slips and say the words. Gull (06a) is Tide Folk and has never been under a
  tile in his life; he thinks the Under is "a drain with opinions".
- **The Mills Co-op:** lookouts for the Walk, paid in chits.
- **The Old Hands:** kids fetch things from crawlspaces, paid in sweets and stories. The Old Hands are the only adults who know
  the plenum as well as the kids do, because they laid half of it.
- **The Archivists:** buy kids' chalk maps, copied, at 5 cr a sheet: the Under has changed since Y0 and the drawings don't know.
- **The Lift Guild:** kids roof the cars. Operators sometimes bring a kid down by the ear and sometimes let them ride.
- **Shaft rats:** where shouldered kids go. Rats and crews are family by default.
- **Stinkers:** buy drip-line water to boil. Kids know every still in the Mills by smell.
- **Seed savers, insect ranchers:** kids carry seed packets (small, light, illegal); ranchers pay kids to find escaped breeding
  stock in the plenum.
- **Part two:** hot-bunk keepers let kids sleep on top tiers in return for running the knock; Static's chute report is built
  on kids' calls; the Foundation's Scholars exam is the one way up a kid knows about; plasma buyers' cuppers use kids as

- **The Lid.** A player who kept a crew all game ends with Tansy shouldered and the player's workshop as the crew's door: the

- **Scholar.** Tansy sits the Foundation exam (see the Foundation). If she wins, she goes up.

**Hook:** the chalk signs in the Under include one that no crew uses and every crew respects: a circle with a dot, found only on

plenum access on those floors at all.

## The Voided

> "They cut it so we couldn't have it. We sewed it so we could. Who's the criminal? Don't answer, I know what the bylaw says."
> (Marisol Eke, the Needle)

### Origin: the Retirement Ordinance and the word

Before **Y63**, the upper floors threw away whole things. A Terraces coat went down the chute as a coat, a Middle kettle as a
kettle, and the Mills wore and used them, and the Middle second-hand shops sold them back up. Then the brands noticed that every
coat that went down the chute whole was a coat they didn't sell twice. The **Ordinance on the Retirement of Branded Goods**
(Y63, file 05) required branded goods leaving a lease as waste to be "retired": slashed, punctured, snapped, clipped, bricked,
dyed, crushed, or stamped **VOID**. File 04 lists every method (the Stamp, the Cancel, the X, the Puncture, the Snap, the
Brick, the Clip, the Blue, the Crush, the Leg) and what it takes to undo each.

Within a year the chutes were full of ruined good things, and the Mills was full of people who knew how to un-ruin them. The
Middle had a word for people who wore mended VOID goods: **voided**, as in "she's voided", meaning she dressed out of the chute.
It was an insult. The menders took it. By Y70 they were **the Voided**, and some of them had the word stitched inside their
collars in red thread.

### The line they hold

The Ordinance makes it an offence to **sell** restored branded goods, not to own or use them (file 05). So the whole Mills
wears restored clothes legally; the Voided are the people who sell them. They hold one line hard, because their survival depends
on it: **restored is not counterfeit**. A restored coat is a real coat, honestly mended, sold as mended. A counterfeit is a fake
dressed as new (Vitabrik, Skywatter, Vey-pattern pills; file 05). Counterfeits bring the stampers down harder on everyone, and
the brands' **dilution damages** are a debt nobody survives. The Voided's rule, said at every apprenticeship:

> **Every seam shows.** We don't hide the mend. We hide the mender.

In practice: restored goods are sold as restored ("mended", "re-legged", "jigged"), with the mend visible if you know where
to look, and the **mender's mark** (a tiny private sign: a knot, a stitch pattern, a scratch inside a casing) hidden somewhere
only another mender would find it. Buyers in the Middle call restored goods **pre-loved**, which the Voided find hilarious.

### The trades

Each VOID method has its trade and its people.

| Trade | Undoes | Tools | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Needlers** | the X (garments, upholstery, bags) | needles, thread, patching cloth, an iron | the largest trade; invisible mending of Crown silk is the elite of it |
| **Leggers** | the Leg (furniture) | saw, glue, clamps, matching wood | real-wood chairs come back lower; matching four legs is the art |
| **Glaziers** | the Puncture (glass, bottles) | resin plug kits, polish | resealed bottles hold liquid, not pressure |
| **Jiggers** | the Clip (cells) | re-terminating jig, solder | a jigged cell works and is uncertified; grey only |
| **Splinters** | the Snap (devices, tools, toys, frames) | splints, solder, glue, luck | the ugliest and most useful trade |
| **Flashers** | the Brick (electronics) | a flash rig and a founding-era master key | rare; a master key costs 2,000+ cr |
| **Washers** | the Blue (Orrin-dyed food) | water, lots of it; basins; knives | the dark edge: feed is legal, food is not |
| **Strippers** | the Stamp | solvent, rags, UV lamp to check the shadow | quick, cheap, and the stamp shadow still shows under a stamper's torch |

Nobody undoes the Crush. Crushed medicine and seed are gone.

### How a voided coat travels

1. A Terraces resident's **disposal valet** cuts the X into a wool coat. A bad valet (paid by the Voided) cuts along the seams.
2. The coat falls down the chute, past the Reclaim, past the Middle catches. It lands in the Hooks' drop beside the Jaws on floor 8 (file 05)
   or in a Mills catch.
3. The **Hooks** pull it, sort it on 6, and price it. Voided buyers come down on sorting days.
4. A **needler** on 13 buys it for 4 cr, unpicks the X, patches inside, presses it. Puts her mark in the lining.
5. A **front** in the Middle (a pre-loved shop, or a Co-op stall under the counter) sells it for 40.
6. A Middle office worker wears it to an interview.
7. A **stamper** sees it on the Long Stair, checks it with a UV torch and a microdot reader, and "recovers" it (valued at the
   brand's list price, 300, his commission 60).
8. Fair Ashe (file 05) sells the recovered coat to a penthouse fixer as "vintage".
9. A Crown resident wears it for a season. Then her valet cuts the X into it again.

### Structure

The Voided are a loose guild of perhaps three hundred menders, with a council called **the Seam**: one seat per trade, eight in
all, meeting in the back of a laundry on 13 every new moon (an old Tide Folk custom; half the Voided's grandparents were Strand
people). The Seam keeps three things:

- **The Pattern.** A shared library of **brand pattern books** (original specs for branded goods, bought from the Archivists,
  06a) so that repairs match. A needler mending a Halden workwear jacket can check the seam allowance against the Y71 pattern.
- **The List.** Which stampers are walking which floors this week, and which take feeding. Compiled from vent kids and Static.
- **The Rule.** Every seam shows. A mender caught counterfeiting is **unpicked**: their mark is published to every Voided
  shop, and nobody will sell their work.

### People

- **Marisol Eke, "the Needle".** Fifty-seven. Chair of the Seam, needler, floor 13. The best invisible mender in the tower: she
  can close an X in Crown silk so that a stamper's UV torch finds the stamp shadow and nothing else. Her mark is a single
  backwards stitch at the base of the left inside seam. She has trained forty apprentices and refused nine. She wears VOID on
  the inside of her own collar, in red.
- **Rafe Kinsella, jigger.** Thirty. Re-terminates clipped cells at a bench on 12, forty a day. Sells to shaft rats (who don't
  care about certification) and to hot-bunk keepers. Has a cough from the solder fumes and the best hands in the Mills.
- **Odalys Brannock, flasher.** Forty-four. Owns the only founding-era **master key** in the Mills (a firmware chip, file 04)
  and a flash rig built by Halvard Pike of Static. She can un-brick a slate in an hour. She charges 40 cr and asks no questions,
  and the Bureau would give a stamper a year's commission for her key. Her brother Teo left the Voided for the Roll (new
  faction, below); they have not spoken in four years.
- **Hamish Toller, front.** Fifty. Owns **Toller's Pre-Loved** on floor 36, a Middle shop with a certified-refurbished licence
  for one brand (a fig leaf) and a back room full of the Voided's work. Pays the Seam on delivery. Pays Fair Ashe every week.
  Believes in nothing except paying people on time.
- **Old Rosel Quarry, legger.** Seventy-five. Re-legs Crown chairs. Her secret is that she doesn't cut the other three legs
  down: she sources a matching leg from another voided chair of the same maker, of which the North Chute brings two or three
  a season. A Quarry chair is full height. Fixers pay four times as much for one.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** a steady supply of voided goods; bad valets; stampers who can be fed; pattern books; master keys; the Ordinance
  amended to allow the sale of "honestly restored" goods. (They have been petitioning the Arbiter for that since Y71. Every
  petition is "noted".)
- **Offer:** restoration services (send a voided thing, get a mended thing back); restored goods at grey prices; tools (jigs,
  resin kits) and training.
- **Buy:** voided goods of every kind (in quantity, from the Hooks and from chute-sorting tenants like the player); thread,
  patching cloth, solder, resin, legs; pattern books; information on stampers.
- **Sell:** restored goods (to fronts, to Co-op stalls, to the Sump, to anyone below 30); repair services; jigs and kits.

**In play:**
- **The restoration chain** (file 04, section 9.6) is the Voided's trade, and the player's most natural way into it: chute → VOID
  item → tool or machine → restored item. The Voided are the supplier of the tools and the buyer of the result when you can't
  sell it yourself. Selling to a Voided buyer (with red thread in the bay) gets the full grey price and no risk on your shelves.
- **Send-out repair.** For methods the player can't undo (the Brick, needing a master key; Crown silk, needing the Needle), a
  Voided slip offers send-out: pack the item, it goes to the mender, it comes back restored after N hours, minus a fee. A
  hireling-like service without a hireling.
- **The mender's mark.** A restored item can carry a mark (visible after analysis or to an Old Hand). A Needle or a Quarry mark
  multiplies the grey price, and a fixer will pay more again for "a Quarry chair". A player who learns a trade at *Kin* gets their
  own mark.
- **The stamper's walk.** A stamper event on the player's floor. Restored goods on frontage shelves are at risk; goods in the back
  room or in a drone bay are not. **Feeding** (one item a week, stamper's choice) is a standing offer. The Voided's List (from
  Static or the crews) warns you which stamper is walking and whether they take food.
- **Bluewash.** Washing Orrin Blue off Crown food takes water (the expensive resource) and a basin. Sold as insect feed, legal.
  Sold as food, illegal, and very popular. A clean, ugly choice the player can make every morning.
- **Certified or not.** The Roll (new faction, below) offers the legal route: the Reclamation Licence (500 cr) plus 20% of list
  per item turns restored into **Certified Refurbished**, sellable anywhere. The Voided will not deal with a certified player
  again.

## The Flats divers and magnet fishers

> "Three things down there: what was dropped, what was drowned, and what was hidden. The first two you can sell."
> (Ama Fenwright)

### Two waters, three trades

The salvage crews of Calder Bay work two different waters, and the difference decides everything about them.

- **Old Calder**, the drowned town around the Stack's base: shallow at low tide, a few metres at high, streets that come up at
  the ebb. Worked by **street divers** and **mud-walkers** from the Flats, almost all Tide Folk, under the berths' rules (06a: the
  Bay's share, kin-houses, magnet rest).
- **The drowned plant**, floors 0 to 3 inside the Stack: the Well Hall, the pump galleries, the chemical vaults, the Gate
  Floor, flooded, dark, full of slurry and founding-era machinery. Worked by **plant divers** from the Sump, under the **Wet
  Widows'** permits (file 05: no dive alone, the line is sacred, a fifth to the house on 8, the Widows' mark on honest salvage).

And everywhere there is water and a ledge, **magnet fishers**: people with a strong magnet on a rope who pull iron out of the
bay from rafts, roofs, the Stump, and the Landing's rails.

### Origin

The first divers were wall gangers and fishermen on the Strand's rooftops in **Y57**, going down into their own flooded houses
for food, papers and the dead. Within a year, the tower's middlemen were paying for anything metal, and the Flats had a trade.
**Plant diving** began the same year from the Sump side, when Pumpmen went down into the flooded halls for pump parts. It killed
people fast: no light, no air, slurry that took your boots and then your legs, and a building full of doors that closed. The
**Wet Widows** organised in **Y61** to stop the dying, with permits, tethers and checked rebreathers. Magnet fishing came in
the **Y60s**, when the Flats learned that a founding-era motor magnet, stripped from a dead pump, would lift a spanner from four
metres of silt.

### Street divers and mud-walkers

- **Mud-walking.** At low tide, the shallow streets show. Walkers go out in waders with a **sieve** (a frame of mesh, 1x2, for
  sifting silt) and a **probe** (a steel rod) and work a house's ground floor: crockery, cutlery, coins, tools, jewellery,
  bottles, pipe fittings, door furniture. The tide gives them three to four hours; the **Great Ebb** gives the deep streets for
  four hours twice a month (06a).
- **Street diving.** At half tide, divers go into houses' upper floors through windows, with a lamp, a weighted belt and a
  partner holding a line. Shallow, short, cold. Breath-hold divers count their breaths aloud (**the Count**) so the partner
  knows they're thinking clearly. A diver who stops counting is hauled.
- **Rebreathers** are rare on the Flats: they need cells, sealant and checks, and the Widows hold most of them. Flats divers
  mostly breath-hold. Jory Malloch (06a) has lungs "that should have given out years ago".
- **What comes up:** Calder house goods (the fixers' "Calder antiques"); metal; cells from old Calder homes (dead, but the
  casings are worth something and some are founding-era); papers in tins (the Archivists' wet lots); and the Bay's share,
  thrown back.

### Plant divers

- **The permit.** A Widows' permit (from the house on 8): a numbered brass **dive tag**, a checked rebreather, a tether, a
  partner, and a dive plan logged on the Widows' board (which hall, which depth, which way out). No permit, no dive; a lone
  diver's haul is forfeit (file 05).
- **The halls.** Floor 3, the **Gate Floor** (the drowned loading quays and public halls; the stair doors, file 01). Floors 0 to
  2, the **Well Hall** and pump galleries (the wellheads that drew the Sweetwater Beds for fifty-seven years; founding-era pumps,
  bearings, valves; LM grease in the motor rooms, 06a). The **chemical vaults** (sealed drums; nobody opens a drum). The
  **spare-parts stores** (membrane casings, empty, file 03's hook: someone has been there first). The **Vault** on floor 2 (the
  Archivists' dream: a door rated for fire, found closed). The **process water hall** on floor 1 (warm water, under the Dark
  Floors, where good divers stopped coming back; file 05).
- **What comes up:** founding-era parts (the most valuable salvage in the game: pump bearings, valves, contactors, Sorrel meters,
  Kessin rail segments, Lung plates, Tube capsules, file 01), LM grease, cells by the thousand (most dead, some first-fit),
  cable, tooling, sealed stores, Works paper.
- **The Widows' mark.** A notch filed on the casing: honest salvage, through the permit system. It costs more and sells for
  more, because it means nobody was left on a cut line for it.
- **Tower divers.** Sump people who dive the plant without being Tide Folk. The berths treat tower divers who come out onto the
  streets of old Calder as poachers (06a). Tower divers think the berths are superstitious. Both are partly right.

### Magnet fishers

Magnets lift **iron and steel** only. Brass, copper, aluminium, lead and glass stay on the bottom, which is why magnet fishing
and diving coexist: fishers take the ferrous, divers take the rest. A magnet fisher's haul is spanners, wrenches, knives, keys,
hinges, nails by the kilo, bolts, chain, bicycle frames, steel cell casings, gun parts (rare, and the HPS buys them), cutlery,
and, once in a career, a safe.

- **The magnet.** The best are **motor magnets** from founding-era pumps and drives: small, flat, terrifyingly strong. Cheaper
  ones are store magnets bonded in a steel cup. A good magnet lifts 100 kg. The best are also the best tools for slowing a
  Sorrel meter (file 04's **slow stone**), which is why the Flats sell magnets up the tower for 25 to 35 cr and why HPS calls
  them "metering apparatus".
- **The pull.** A cast, a slow drag along the bottom, a pull. Fishers count casts and keep a tally on a stick. A magnet that
  comes up empty three times is "tired" and rests on a roof for a day (06a: it's fouled with silt; the sun cleans it).
- **The grapnel.** For anything the magnet finds but can't lift: a three-hook grapnel and a second person.
- **Rust grades.** Iron from the bay comes up in three states, and fishers sort by them: **bright** (in the water a year or
  less: tools that still work after a wire brush and oil), **brown** (years: scrap by weight, or a restoration job), **gone**
  (flaking through: scrap only). A fisher who can tell bright from brown by touch makes double.

### Structure

- **Crews.** Street divers work in berth crews of three to six (diver, line-holder, sorter, sometimes a boat). Plant divers work in
  Widows' pairs. Fishers work alone or in twos.
- **The middle.** Everything goes up through the **Landing** (Ruben Vass, who sets prices) and the **Ninefold**'s toll on 9 (one
  item in nine: salvage arrives at the Mills in loads of eight; file 05), or bypasses both by shaft-rat drone from the Landing's
  bottom hatches (nine items, no toll, the shaft's risks).
- **Specialist buyers** come down to the Landing to buy first: Simeon Pratt for the Archivists (wet paper by the kilo, a bonus
  by the legible page), Benedikt Rusk for Ottoline Wick (antiques, salt line), Augustin Moreau's runners for the Lift Guild (LM
  grease), the Pumpmen (pump parts), the Syndicate's Corvin Daley (sometimes a subrogated bay that fell into the bay).
- **Halden's Recovery 2**, the licensed salvage barge, dredges the deep streets with a crew paid by weight, under the **Calder
  Recovery Licence** (06a). It takes the big metal. The Flats take what's left.

### People

- **Ama Fenwright.** Thirty-four. Plant diver under Widows' permit, tag 41. Granddaughter of Tobiah Fenwright, a Wellside man
  who became one of the first Flats divers (his testimony is in file 01). Lives on 8 next to the Widows' house. The best plant
  diver working, and the only one who has been through the Gate Floor's south stair door (from the Gate Floor side, and back).
  Will not say what's on the other side of it.
- **Lio Paskett.** Fifty-two. Magnet fisher off the Stump. Holds the Flats' record pull: a pre-Drowning bank safe, raised
  over three Great Ebbs with a grapnel, a magnet and nine people, in Y79. It was empty. He keeps the door as a table.
- **Ruta Abernathy.** Sixty. Rebreather mender for the Widows. Rebuilds rebreathers from founding-era Works breathing sets
  (clean-room escape kits) and store parts. Every rebreather on the Widows' board has her mark, a filed "R" inside the scrubber
  housing. She checks every one before every dive and has never lost a diver to a set.
- **Cass Imbert.** Twenty-six. A tower diver: dives the plant from the Sump without a Widows' permit, alone, at night, and sells to
  the Hooks with no mark. Fast, brave, a cheat. The Widows have banished him twice. The Ninefold buy from him. **Hook:** Cass is
  the "someone" who has been in the spare-parts stores first (file 03). He's been selling the membranes to Vantage, through a
  poacher's boat that comes to the Sump's outer stair on moonless nights.
- **Old Moss Teague.** Eighty-one. Mud-walker, still, every ebb. Claims to be a great-nephew of the Teague of Marrow Teague, the firm that drew
  the Stack (file 01). Nobody believes him. He knows where every drain in Low Calder ran, which nobody else alive does.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** water above everything (the Flats are the thirstiest place there is); cells for lamps and rebreathers; sealant,
  rope, waders, gloves; medicine (Vey gut tabs, wound film, the itch salve); better prices than the Landing gives.
- **Offer:** salvage; dives to order; magnets; knowledge of what's down there.
- **Buy:** water, cells, rope, tarred line, sealant, rubber, waders, lamps, rebreather parts (scrubber granules, mouthpieces,
  hose), wound film, Vitabricks, salt.
- **Sell:** wet salvage lots, magnet pulls by rust grade, founding-era parts (with or without the Widows' mark), Calder
  antiques, LM grease, wet paper, magnets.

**In play:**
- **Wet salvage lots.** A 2x3 bundle, contents unknown, wet (06a): opened on the field into a small grid of items, all **wet**,
  some of which need a drying rack before they can be sold or analysed. A mystery box with honest odds, set by where it came
  from: a street lot (house goods, paper, small metal), a plant lot (founding-era parts, cells, grease), a magnet pull (ferrous
  only, in three rust grades).
- **Rust grades.** A new item state for ferrous salvage: bright (works after a wire brush and oil: the **derusting** step, a
  bench tool and a can of insect-fat oil), brown (restorable with hours on a wire wheel, or scrap by weight), gone (scrap). One
  spanner, three states, three prices, and a small chain that uses insect-fat oil from the ranchers.
- **Divers as hirelings with a plan.** Night expeditions and hunting are out of scope; diving doesn't need to be in. The player
  never dives. The player **hires a diver**: packs their kit (a back slot: rebreather or breath-hold; two hands: lamp, sieve, bag,
  grapnel, magnet), writes a plan (where: a street, a hall; how deep; when to turn back: "rebreather cell at half"; what to keep:
  value per square or a tag like "founding-era" or "paper"), and pays a day rate plus a share. The diver comes back with their
  kit packed and a **left-behind list** ("brass ship's clock: no room"), exactly the hireling-as-plan idea from `IDEAS.md`. The
  Widows' rules are the plan's constraints (a partner, a tether, a logged plan). A good plan and good kit bring good salvage.
- **Commissions.** "Look for" slips: the player can commission a dive for something specific (a Type 9 bearing for the Co-op's
  Pool pump, LM grease for the Guild, a master key for the Voided, a Works pattern book for the Archivists). The diver honours a
  **look-for note** packed with a dive tag.
- **Magnets.** The Flats sell the best magnets in the tower. A magnet is a tool (magnet fishing from the Landing, if the player
  ever goes down), a meter trick (the slow stone), and a buyer's item for the Old Hands.
- **The Widows' mark.** An honest-salvage property on founding-era parts. Marked parts cost more; unmarked parts may be from a
  cut line. Selling unmarked parts to the Old Hands or the Archivists costs standing; the fixers don't care.

### Example slips

*Diver slips are written on oilcloth in grease pencil and smell of the bay; magnet fishers' come as a knot in tarred line with a
tag on it.*

1. **Breath.** "Rebreather scrubber granules, two tins, and four charged cells, to the Widows' house (8) before the evening
   dive." Pay: 45 cr. Without them, two divers don't go down tonight.
2. **A Type 9 bearing.** "Dive commission: the Well Hall, pump gallery B, a Works Type 9 bearing, 32 mm. Diver: Fenwright, tag 41.
   Your kit, your plan, her share." Pay: the bearing (if found) and whatever else she brings back. Cost: her day rate (40 cr) and a
   fifth to the Widows.
3. **Bright iron.** "Magnet pull, 1 day, Lio Paskett, off the Stump. 20 cr. Whatever comes up." Pay: a magnet pull lot, sorted
   by rust grade.
4. **Water for the berth.** "Fifty litres, any drinkable grade, to the Landing on the ebb (hours 9 to 12), Malloch's crew."
   Pay: a wet salvage lot and a knot on the tide cord (06a).
5. **No mark.** "Founding-era contactor, Kessin pattern, no Widows' mark, 300 cr, at the Hooks' room on 6. Cass." *A sell
   slip, not a job.* A real part at half the marked price. The Widows will know who bought it.

### How they treat the player

The Flats crews treat you as a dry until you've delivered water on the ebb, on time, more than once. The Widows treat you as a
risk until you've supplied a dive without a delay. At *Known* you get first look at a Great Ebb's lots. At *Trusted* you get
commissions and a diver who will take your plans. At *Kin*, Ama Fenwright tells you what's on the other side of the south door,
and Ruta Abernathy fits a rebreather to your size, which is no use to you, and which is the greatest honour the Widows give.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the Calder Recovery Licence and Recovery 2 (06a); the Salvage Levy under Bylaw 95 (file 05), claimed when the
  Company wants the levy and denied when someone drowns. Most salvage pays nothing to anyone but the Ninefold, the Landing and
  the Widows.

## NEW: The Period Club (Company middle management)

> "Nobody here sets the rate. The card sets the rate. We just know what's on the card a little before you do."
> (Henrietta Spall)

### Origin: Card Night

Since **Y66**, the Count House has printed the **Floor Rate Card** every thirty days, a "Period" (the current card is Period
86/3; file 04). The same year the Company introduced a **yield bonus** for the managers of the Lease Office, the Tenancy Office
and Survey and Allocation: a share of the rise in "yield per square" on the floors each manager oversees. A manager whose floors
were reclassified, re-cut or uplifted earned more. A manager whose floors stood still did not.

The draft card circulates to managers in pencil a fortnight before it prints. In the first year of the bonus, a handful of
managers met for dinner at **Corvo's**, a restaurant on Lantern Row (floor 75), on the night before the card went to print, to
compare their floors' numbers. By the second year, they were coordinating: if your floor's reclassification would pull up my
floor's uplift, we time them together, and we both hit target. By the third year, they were selling the pencil draft to sublet
barons, who paid for a fortnight's notice of where the Chalk would move next. The night before printing is **Card Night**. The
diners are the **Period Club**. Nobody has ever called it that in their hearing.

They are not the Company. They are its managers, using the Company's machinery for themselves, and the Company would sack every
one of them if it found out, and keep every rate rise they made.

### What they do

- **The pencil.** The draft card, in pencil, two weeks early. Barons pay for it. So would anyone with a lease.
- **Pull timing.** Reclassifications are timed so that the 10–20% **pull** uplift on neighbouring floors (file 04) lands on
  floors members oversee.
- **Holes in the card.** A lease can be left out of an uplift for one Period, "pending survey". A hole costs half of what it
  saves, paid to the member whose floor it is, as a Foundation gift.
- **Quiet corrections.** A Standing mark can be "reviewed" off a tenant's record. Only at the Lease Office, only with a clerk who
  owes a member.
- **Buffers.** When a floor is "reclassified Transitional", its tenants are given notice. The Club decides which floors, and in
  what order.

Club rules, never written: nothing on paper but the pencil; nobody profits on the floor they live on; nobody talks to Static;
and every member brings one dish from their own floor to Card Night, because it reminds them where the money comes from. The
dishes have become a contest. Real food from the Mills is the winning dish, every time.

### Structure and people

About twenty managers; no officers; the most senior diner sits at the end with the pencil.

- **Henrietta Spall, deputy head of the Lease Office for the Mills (10–29).** Twenty-eight. One of the Foundation's first Scholars
  (the Y69 intake; born on 15). Knows the Mills from below, which is why she is so good at pricing it. Sends her mother a Hope
  Voucher every month. Holds the pencil.
- **Pell Dorrance, Tenancy Office.** Fifty-six. The man negotiating the Mills Charter with the Co-op for six years (06a). Every
  draft he gives Odile Brask is a little worse than the last because every Period the Club needs the Mills' yield a little more.
  He likes Odile. He would like the Charter signed, eventually, in a form the Club can live with.
- **Anselm Roke, Survey and Allocation.** Sixty. The chalkers' chief. Dorrit Vance (file 04) reports to him. Decides where the
  yellow lines go.
- **Yusuf Bellamy-Orr, Count House liaison.** Thirty-eight. Translates between the managers and the Ledger. Understands the
  Ledger better than anyone alive, which is to say, a little. Has noticed that the Ledger rounds a fraction of a credit in the
  Company's favour on every bill, and has started wondering where the fractions go.
- **Signe Okpara, the newest member.** Twenty-five. A Scholar, Y72 intake, born on 12. Brought bluewashed Crown bread to her first
  Card Night as her floor dish, as a joke. Nobody laughed. She's still deciding what she is.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** yield; their bonuses; secrecy; Static silent; the Charter delayed and then signed on their terms.
- **Offer (for a price):** the pencil; holes in the card; quiet corrections; early notice of re-cuts.
- **Buy:** real Mills food for Card Night; discretion; people to carry things that aren't theirs to carry.
- **Sell:** time. Two weeks' notice of the future is worth more than anything else in the Stack.

**In play:**
- **The pencil as an item.** A **pencil draft** (1x1, a folded carbon) bought through a baron or a member's go-between: shows
  next Period's card for the player's band fourteen days early, and any reclassification announced thirty days ahead (file 04:
  the Chalk moves roughly every 90 days). Knowing the Chalk is coming to floor 20 lets the player move, buy, or sell before
  everyone else.
- **A hole in the card.** At *Trusted*: pay half of next Period's uplift; your lease is skipped for one Period. A visible saving,
  a receipt from the Foundation, and a debt of favour.
- **Card Night catering.** A slip every thirty days: "One dish, Mills-grown, real, for twenty. Corvo's, 75, by 19:00. Bring it
  covered." Pay: 220 cr. A real-grade produce contract with no Grow Certificate needed, because nobody at Card Night will ever
  write anything down.
- **The menu card.** Corvo's prints a menu for every Card Night, with the diners' names on the back as hosts. One turns up in
  the East Chute every thirty-one days, VOID-stamped, because Corvo's disposal valet is careless. A player whose catch is on the
  East Chute can pull it. It is the only document that proves the Club exists. Static would read it out. The Co-op would take it to
  the Arbiter. Henrietta Spall would pay a great deal for it back.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the Club is inside the Company and against it at once. The Count House Review (an internal audit, every two years)
  is the only thing members fear more than Static.
- **Mills Co-op:** Pell Dorrance's Charter drafts. The Hall wing has guessed something like the Club exists.
- **Archivists:** Margit Halloran (06a) walked out of the Tenancy Office in Y79 with a box of lease files; some of them are Club
  holes. She hasn't worked that out yet.
- **Old Hands:** the Glass Line chalk marks on the lift lobby wall (file 04), closer every year, are the Club's work in the
  wall's own hand.
- **Lift Guild, Tide Folk, Rain Church, others:** little. Rudolf Kasza dines at Corvo's, not on Card Night.
- **Part two:** sublet barons buy the pencil; the Foundation's Scholars fill the Club; the Green Bands are told which floors need
  reports; Static is the danger; the Amenity doesn't know the Club exists and would be appalled to learn that its managers are
  enterprising.

### In the endings

- **Card Night.** The player, risen to the Terraces as a fixer, a Name or a lane operator, is invited to bring a dish. The ending
  text is the menu card.
- **The menu goes on air.** Every member is moved. The new managers inherit the yield bonus. The card prints on time.

**Hook:** some pencil drafts carry a margin note in a hand none of the members recognise, setting the reclassification order
for floors 20, 21 and 27 (the floors around the Dark Floors). It is initialled **D/0001**. Clock 0001 was the Works' first plant
director, dead forty years, who still clocks in at 02:00 (06a).

### Voices

- "I don't set the rate. Nobody sets the rate. It's a card."
- "Bring something from home. It keeps us honest."
- "A fortnight. That's all I'm selling. A fortnight is a long time on 17."

## NEW: The Amenity (the Crown's residents' committee)

> "The Crown is not a building. It is an agreement."
> (Philippa Strand-Esker, opening the Amenity's annual meeting)

### Origin: the Covenant

When the Crown floors (90–104) were sold as **title** after the Drowning, they were not leased like the rest of the tower: they
were owned. Owners needed rules among themselves that the Company's Charter didn't supply. In **Y58** the first title holders
signed the **Crown Covenant**, binding every title on floors 90 to 104 to the decisions of a residents' committee: the **Crown
Residents' Amenity Committee**, called **the Amenity**. Every title deed since carries the Covenant. A title holder who breaks it
is fined by the Amenity, and the fine is a charge on the title, and a charged title cannot be sold.

The Amenity governs nothing that matters to the Company and everything that matters to the Crown: what comes in, what goes
out, what can be heard, seen and smelled.

### What it decides

- **The Crown Assay.** The standard every delivery into the Crown must pass (file 02's Crown Assay; the fixers' Assay rate):
  grade by spectro wand (R✓ for produce, real for materials); origin (a provenance card for antiques); **no restored goods** (VOID
  shadows checked under UV); and **odour of origin** (goods must not carry "any odour characteristic of their place of
  production"). Goods that fail are voided and dropped down the North Chute.
- **The Approved List.** Suppliers allowed to deliver into the Crown direct, without a fixer: about sixty names, mostly
  Greenhold agents, Terraces purveyors and two fixers who sit on the list as suppliers.
- **The Quiet.** Quiet hours from 22:00 to 06:00 (the chutes held overnight, the drones grounded above 89); the **Quiet Glass Rule**
  (no drone within twenty metres of Crown glass; file 02); no amplified sound on terraces.
- **Staff passes.** Day passes for household staff from the staff floor on 89 and below. The Wet Servants (06a) carry them.
- **The Gutter.** The Founders' Gutter's overflow schedule on storm nights, agreed with Skywater "for the protection of terraces".
- **The Season.** Which "real" is in, agreed each autumn with the fixers.

### Structure and people

One seat per title: about forty seats. A **Chair**, an **Assay Chair** and a **Quiet Chair**, elected annually. Old Title
(families who were at the Glasshouse reception in Y57) and New Title (bought since) vote in blocs, and the Halden household
holds one seat and never attends.

- **Philippa Strand-Esker, Chair.** Sixty-seven. Esplanade money, Old Title, also chair of the Foundation's Gala. Believes the
  Amenity keeps the Crown civilised and the Crown keeps the tower civilised. Her terrace on 95 floods on storm nights unless the
  Gutter overflow is opened. She has never asked who opens it.
- **Desmond Faber-Quayle, Assay Chair.** Seventy-two. Collects real wood. Wrote the "odour of origin" clause after a Mills honey
  delivery smelled, he said, "of insects". Tests deliveries himself on his own floor with a spectro wand and his nose.
- **Imogen Rask.** Thirty-six. New Title (92, 1.4 million cr; file 04), a Syndicate Name. Has started reading the Determination
  letters (see the syndicate) and the Assay reject logs. Proposes amendments nobody seconds.
- **Mr. Lowther, steward to the Halden household.** Age unknown. Attends no meetings. Every resolution is sent to him before it is
  voted on. None has ever passed that he returned with a mark on it.

### What it wants, offers, buys, sells

- **Want:** the Crown quiet, dry, real and unchanged.
- **Offer:** a place on the Approved List; staff passes; the Assay's seal (an Assay-passed item sells in the Terraces for a
  premium: "Crown Assay grade").
- **Buy:** nothing as a body; every household buys through fixers, the List and the Foundation's Gala.
- **Sell:** nothing. It fines.

**In play:**
- **The Assay as packing rules.** Crown slips carry the Assay's conditions, and one of them is a bay rule: **odour of origin**.
  A Crown delivery fails if the same bay also carries anything tagged insect, frass, compost, bluewash or "Sump". (A bay is a
  container, so this is a rule inside a container, not a proximity rule on the field.) Ship Crown goods alone, or in a sealed
  liner. The Assay is visible on the slip; a failure comes back as a VOID-stamped item in your North Chute bin next morning.
- **The Approved List** is a late-game goal: deliver direct into the Crown at Crown prices, no fixer's cut. Requirements, all
  visible: a Standing threshold, Grow Certificates on every bed that supplies Crown goods, an Assay pass rate over twenty
  deliveries, a sponsoring household, and an annual fee (600 cr). The fixers will not thank you.
- **Staff passes.** The Wet Servants' passes are the Rain Church's whole back door (06a's Laundry Run). The Amenity's Quiet
  Chair is reviewing them.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the Company sells the Crown its titles and leaves it alone; the Amenity sends the Company its resolutions as a
  courtesy.
- **Rain Church:** the Gutter. The Amenity's minutes record the overflow opened "by staff" on storm nights; the Roofman (06a) is
  free drainage, and someone in the Amenity knows it.
- **Fixers:** the Season and the Assay. Ottoline Wick and Lucan Ferrabee are on the Approved List; Ji-ho Carrow isn't, yet.
- **The Foundation:** shared chairs, shared tables.
- **Syndicate:** Names on both.
- **Static:** Ninety reads the Amenity's minutes, somehow, verbatim.
- **Everyone below 60:** the Amenity has never discussed anyone below 60 by name, except the Rain Church, once a year.

### In the endings

- **Approved.** The player on the List: a Crown supplier, direct. The ending text is the Assay certificate for the player's
  first direct delivery, and a note in Faber-Quayle's hand: "No odour."
- **Rain.** If the Open Roof ending happens (06a), the Amenity holds an emergency meeting. The ending text is its minutes.

**Hook:** the Amenity's agenda has carried the same standing item since Y70: **"Riser cap 6: temperature."** It is never
discussed. It is minuted as "noted" (file 02: one riser cap on the roof is warm).

## NEW: The Green Bands (floor wardens)

> "Four a month. Not three, they'll think I'm asleep. Not five, they'll think I'm keen. Four."
> (Hester Moyle)

### Origin: the healthy irregularity rate

Every floor below 60 has a warden (file 05): a tenant appointed by the Lease Office, paid with a 30% rent rebate, a report fee and
a collection fee, marked by a **green armband**, a **Warden's Book** and a whistle. In **Y71** the Lease Office began paying a
bonus to wardens whose floors showed a "healthy irregularity rate", and quietly docking the rebate of wardens whose floors showed
too few. On 17 the healthy number is four reports a month.

That year the wardens of the Mills started meeting, because a warden who reports nothing loses the rebate and a warden who
reports everything finds their door glued shut. They needed each other to stay in the narrow middle. They met at a tea stall on
the Shelf (floor 30), **Merle's Urn**, on the eve of every rent day, and called themselves nothing at all. The Mills calls them
**the Green Bands**.

### What they do

- **The Exchange.** Wardens swap what they know. A warden can only report their own floor, but they can tell each other where
  things are going: "the baron on 17 moves his still to 12 the night before an occupancy count"; "the stamper takes feeding on
  14 but not on 15". Warden knowledge makes the quota easy to fill with the least harm.
- **Ghost reports.** A report against a nobody who has already left: a sleeper seen in a hot-bunk on a night HPS can't verify,
  a hawker on a half-square who's moved floors. The report counts; nobody is charged; the quota is met.
- **Soft reports.** Reports that only reach a **letter of concern** (no penalty; file 05). A warden short of quota may offer a
  tenant a soft report "as a favour".
- **Pinking.** Losing the pink carbon (the HPS copy) on the way to the post. The Lease Office gets the white; HPS never acts.
- **The Black Book.** Tenants who have harmed a warden. A name in the Black Book is reported first, every month, by every warden
  on whatever floor that tenant moves to.
- **Mutual defence.** A warden whose door is glued shut, whose pipes are cut, whose meter is slowed, gets the Bands' help: a
  fitter, a locksmith, a word with whoever did it.

### Structure and people

About fifty wardens in the Mills and the Middle who come to Merle's; the Sump's wardens answer to the Ninefold first and come
rarely. A **secretary** keeps the Exchange's notes, in a code of floor numbers and colours.

- **Hester Moyle, warden of 17.** Sixty-one (file 05). Files four a month, almost all against Gus Tanaka-Breen. Takes clocks for her
  brother Aldo. Comes to Merle's every rent-day eve, drinks one cup, says almost nothing, and leaves a sweet on her floor's drain
  tile on the way home for the Tilers.
- **Ludmila Dvorak, warden of 12, secretary.** Fifty. Keeps the Exchange's notes. Knows every irregularity in the Mills and reports
  almost none of them. Her floor is the hot-bunk floor: three hundred sleepers, most of them nobodies, and she has never filed an
  occupancy count against a keeper who keeps the cup.
- **Gaspard Mbeki, warden of 13.** Fifty (06a). Co-op delegate, descendant of the Nine, and paid to report Pool volumes and Walk
  rotas to the Tenancy Office. The Bands know. The Co-op doesn't. The Bands have decided it isn't their business, which is the
  most dangerous decision they have ever made.
- **Pim Okafor.** Twenty-three. Not a member. The keen new warden who files everything (file 05's alternative for 17, or a warden
  of 18 in this version). The Bands are trying to bring him round. He keeps reporting them.
- **Arno Pettibone, warden of 9.** Fifty-eight. Files his reports in the Ninefold's register before the Lease Office's. Has never
  had a door glued.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** the quota met, the rebate kept, the floor livable, their doors unglued.
- **Offer:** soft reports; warnings; lost paper; a word with the Lease Office.
- **Buy:** what wardens take in kind (Hester: clocks; Ludmila: tea; Pettibone: nothing, he's paid by the Ninefold), carbons,
  armbands, whistles, locks.
- **Sell:** quiet.

**In play:**
- **Warden's regard** (file 05): a visible pip by the lobby board. The Bands make it portable: good regard with your own warden
  passes to the warden of any floor you move to ("she's all right, she sent clocks").
- **Quota week.** Near month's end, the warden is short. A slip: "I need one more this month. A letter of concern for you
  (nothing comes of it), or I file on 17-K." 17-K is your client. Choose.
- **The armband.** If 17's warden post falls vacant (Hester dies, or retires; an event), the Lease Office offers it to a tenant
  in good Standing. Accepting: a **30% rent rebate**, a Warden's Book (which reveals every irregularity on the floor, a powerful
  information item), report fees, Co-op expulsion if the Co-op finds out, and a place at Merle's. The single biggest rent cut
  in the game, paid for in neighbours.
- **The Black Book.** Report a warden (to Static, the Co-op, anyone) and your name goes in. Every warden on every floor you live on
  reports you first, every month, for the rest of the game.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the wardens are the Lease Office's eyes, paid in rebates. The Bands are the eyes' agreement about what not to see.
- **Mills Co-op:** the Co-op hates wardens on principle; the Bands include Co-op members (Mbeki) and the Co-op doesn't know.
- **Barons:** pay wardens lamp oil; are reported anyway, to make quota.
- **Hot-bunk keepers:** occupancy counts are the wardens' biggest weapon; the Bands use it least.
- **Vent kids:** the kids sleep in wardens' crawlspaces; Hester leaves sweets.
- **Period Club:** Survey and Allocation tells wardens which floors to watch.
- **Static, Voided, stinkers, Rain Church:** wardens are told where the Fonts and stills are by the Exchange, and report the Fonts
  Skywater already knows about (06a: 50 cr per confirmed Font from Skywater). Never the stills.

### In the endings

- **The Armband.** The player became warden of 17 and held it to the end. The ending text is the last page of the Warden's Book:
  four reports a month, every month, and the names chosen.

**Hook:** Survey and Allocation has been asking the wardens of floors 20, 21 and 27 for occupancy counts at four times the usual
rate. Ludmila Dvorak has the numbers in her notes. Somebody wants those floors empty, and wants the wardens to do it (see the
Period Club's hook; file 04's Chalk hook).

### Voices

- "I've lost the pink. Shame. It'll turn up."

## NEW: The Stillcars (squatters in dead Core D)

> "We're not in the shaft. We're in the cars. The cars are parked. Parked is a place."
> (Barty Quell)

### Origin: the cars that stopped at 44

Core D was a passenger core, floor 0 to 104, until its drive failed in **Y70**. Its cars were parked at floor 44 and the track
below 30 went out (06a). The shaft has been the Stack's biggest free shaft ever since: four hundred metres of dark from the drowned
plant to the roof, fifty-odd hatches, a constant updraft (the shaft is a chimney: warm Mills air rises through it to the roof),
and four dead cars hanging on their locks at 44 to 47 like boxes on a shelf.

In **Y72** the Lease Office re-cut floors 44 to 46 into larger units and gave notice to about forty families. Some went down to
the Mills. Some went to hot-bunks. Nine families went through the motor-room hatch on 44, which nobody had locked, and into the
parked cars. A Kessin car is three metres by four, with a floor, walls, a ceiling hatch, and doors that open on to nothing. Nine
families made four cars into houses. There are about a hundred and twenty Stillcars now, in the four cars, on the motor-room
landings above and below them, and on platforms they have hung from the shaft's maintenance rails.

### What they do

- **The Pit.** The shaft is the longest free route in the tower, and drones flying it long distances run their cells down. At 44,
  the Stillcars hang a **pit basket** on a cable: a drone that hovers at the basket can be swapped a charged cell for a dead one
  in under a minute by someone leaning out of car D-2's door. Price: a cell for a cell plus 2 cr, or 5 cr straight. The Pit
  charges its cells off a socket in the 44 motor room that still draws from the Core D bus, which nobody bills, because Core D is
  dead.
- **The Net.** A cable mesh across the shaft between 43 and 48 with a drone-sized gap in the middle. It stops falling things
  (dead drones, spilled cargo, people) from falling through the Stillcars' homes. It also slows looters: a rat-hunter drone
  coming up from below has to thread the gap, past people watching.
- **Shaft fishing.** Magnet lines and hook lines dropped from the platforms catch what falls: cargo spilled from looted bays,
  drones that died mid-flight, tools dropped by lift greasers. The Stillcars buy their magnets from the Flats.
- **Wall gutters.** The shaft walls sweat (warm air rising, cool lattice). The Stillcars run tin gutters along the walls at
  every landing and catch a few litres a day of grey condensate.
- **The passage.** Rats flying through the Stillcars' stretch pay a **pass**: hang a small thing on the pass hook at 43 as you go
  by (a cell, a brick, a bottle). Rats who pay are watched over through the stretch. Rats who don't are not.

### Structure and people

Each car is a household of households with a **captain**; the four captains settle disputes in D-1, "the Parlour". The Pit is run
by whoever is best at it.

- **Bartholomew "Barty" Quell, captain of D-2.** Sixty. A Middle bookkeeper on 45 until Y72. Keeps the Stillcars' accounts (the
  passes, the Pit, the fishing) in the same neat columns he once kept for a dental practice. Believes the Stillcars are
  temporary. Has believed so for fourteen years.
- **Rosamund Fry, the Pit.** Twenty-five. Was eleven when her family went into the cars. Can swap a cell on a hovering Wren in
  forty seconds, leaning out of a car door over a forty-storey drop with one foot hooked on a strap. Every shaft rat knows her
  voice.
- **Jonah and Jessa Lark, "the Cable Twins".** Nineteen. Brought into D-3 as small children. Climb the rails, mend the Net, run the hook lines, and go
  further down the shaft than anyone else, as far as 31. They say there are hatches below 30 that nobody uses, and a smell.
- **Ezekiel Pram, teacher, D-3 ("the School").** Seventy. Teaches the Stillcars' children from three Middle schoolbooks he took
  with him in Y72. The children can do long division and nothing else.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** to stay. Water, food, cable, rope, magnets, cells, light, schoolbooks. A way to be somebody in the Company's paper.
- **Offer:** the Pit (range), the Net (safety through 43–48), the passage, fished goods, and a view of everything that flies the D
  shaft.
- **Buy:** water, Vitabricks, rope, cable, cable clamps, magnets, cells, lamps, blankets (the updraft is cold), books.
- **Sell:** cell swaps, passage, fished salvage (often recognisable as someone's lost bay).

**In play:**
- **The D route.** A free-shaft route option for long runs (the Mills to the Terraces). Flying D costs nothing at the hatch, takes
  the shaft's trouble roll as any free shaft does, and passes the Stillcars: pack a **pass item** (anything small) and the trouble
  roll for the 43–48 stretch is softened; at *Known*, the Pit swaps a cell and the drone's **range** extends for that flight, which
  lets a small frame make a run it otherwise couldn't. A free alternative to buying a better drone, paid in favours.
- **Lost bay, found.** If one of the player's drones is lost in the D shaft, a Stillcars slip may arrive a day later: "We fished
  your Wren. 25 cr and it's yours." Cheaper than the Syndicate, and it actually pays.
- **Supplies.** Small, steady, odd slips: rope, cable, chalk, blankets, schoolbooks, magnets.

### Relations

- **Halden:** the Stillcars are nobodies in a space nobody leases. HPS can't reach them without the Guild's cooperation or rope.

## NEW: The Roll (licensed refurbishers)

> "The difference between us and the Voided is five hundred credits and a seal. And honesty, but mostly the seal."
> (Cyprian Hale)

### Origin: if you can't stop it, tax it

By the early Y70s the brands understood that they couldn't stop restoration: the Mills wore it, the Middle bought it, the Bureau's
stampers were recovering a fraction. In **Y74** the Brand Integrity Bureau introduced the **Reclamation Licence** (500 cr) and a
per-item **reclamation fee** (20% of list price), which together turn a restored good into **Certified Refurbished**: a licensed
item, with a holographic **Bureau seal** and a thirty-day warranty, sellable anywhere below the Crown (file 05). The Bureau keeps
a register of licensees, the **Roll of Certified Refurbishers**, on floor 66. The licensees are **the Roll**.

Because the 20% fee is on list price, not on what the item is worth restored, the Roll can only make money on expensive goods:
Terraces electronics, real-wood furniture, good coats, appliances. For everything cheap, the Voided are cheaper. So the Roll's
trade depends on the Voided being stopped, and the Roll pays the Bureau's stampers to help.

### What they do

- **Certification.** Buy voided goods (from the same Hooks' room on 6), restore them, pay the fee, apply the Bureau seal, sell in
  the Middle and the Terraces openly, with a warranty.
- **Reversal codes.** The Bureau sells licensees an official **Retirement Reversal** code for bricked electronics: a legal un-brick,
  12 cr a device, so the Roll can do legally what Odalys Brannock does with a master key.
- **Information.** Roll members report Voided fronts to the Bureau; the Bureau pays a tenth of the stamper's recovery commission
  as a "market integrity" bounty.
- **The Roll fair.** Twice a year, on the Shelf: certified refurbished goods at a discount, with Bureau agents in attendance, who
  stamp anything uncertified they see on neighbouring stalls.

### Structure and people

About forty licensees, a **Roll secretary** who liaises with the Bureau, and the Bureau's patience.

- **Cyprian Hale, Hale & Daughters Certified Refurbishment, floor 41.** Sixty-four. By the Gap on the South Stair (file 02). A
  joiner who re-legs chairs and pays 20% of list on each, which on a Crown chair is more than Old Rosel Quarry charges for the whole
  job. Hates the Voided with the specific hatred of a man who once was one.
- **Wendeline Asante, Roll secretary.** Forty-two. Middle-born, efficient, sincere about warranties, which no Voided gives. Points out,
  correctly, that a certified cell won't burn your bunk down and a jigged one might.
- **Teo Brannock.** Forty. Odalys Brannock's brother (see the Voided). A flasher who went legit in Y82, bought the Reclamation
  Licence on a loan, and now un-bricks slates with reversal codes at 12 cr a time plus 20% of list, and is still paying the loan.
  Hasn't spoken to his sister in four years. Still has her old jig.
- **Benno Ruiz, floor 19.** Thirty-five. The only Roll member in the Mills: a one-bench repair shop that certified to sell to the
  Middle and has been in debt ever since. The Voided won't sell to him; the Roll fair won't give him a stall. He would trade
  standing with either for a good month.

**In play:**
- **The legal route.** Becoming Certified: the Reclamation Licence (500 cr) and 20% of list per item, a Bureau seal (a mark on
  the item), restored goods sellable openly on Middle and Terraces slips, no stamper risk. Thin margins except on expensive goods.
  The Voided unpick you.
- **Reversal service.** Send a bricked device to Teo; it comes back licensed, at 12 cr plus 20% of list. Expensive, safe, and it
  sells at full Middle price.
- **Bounties.** A slip with no pay but a percentage: "Report a Voided front in the Middle. 10% of recovery." Toller's Pre-Loved on 36
  is worth a lot. Marisol will know who.
- **Clients.** The Roll buys voided goods in bulk (the same goods the Voided want) and pays slightly more, in credits, on time, with
  paperwork.

### Relations

- **Brand Integrity Bureau:** the Roll's patron. The Roll exists at the Bureau's pleasure.
- **The Voided:** enemies, and family (Teo and Odalys).
- **Hooks:** sell to both, at the same price, and laugh.
- **Old Hands:** refuse to work with reversal codes. "It's not fixed. It's forgiven."
- **Archivists:** sell the Roll the same pattern books they sell the Voided.
- **The Amenity:** the Crown Assay rejects certified refurbished goods ("no restored goods"). The Roll's ceiling is floor 89.

### In the endings

- **Certified.** The player's business goes legal in the Middle and prospers on Terraces goods. The ending text is the Bureau's
  renewal notice.

**Hook:** Teo Brannock has noticed that a Bureau reversal code doesn't just un-brick a device: it re-licenses it with a fresh
founding-era signature, the same signature the Retirement Signal erased. The codes come from the Count House. Somebody in the Count
House has a master key, the one Odalys's key was copied from.

### Voices

- "Seal, warranty, receipt. Find me a needler who'll give you a receipt."
- "It's not about the coat. It's about the paper."
- (Teo, alone) "Twelve credits and twenty percent, and it does exactly what Odalys's key does."

## NEW: The Thursday People (debt labour and the Bastion returnees)

> "Four years. Then you come back on the same boat, and you've paid, and the Lease Office says you still owe it."
> (Corliss Mbatha)

### Origin: the boat

When a debtor has nothing left to seize, the debt becomes a **work-off contract**, and the worst of those are held under the
**Labour Transfer Agreement** between Halden and **Bastion**, the prison tower (file 05). The Agreement was signed in **Y64**. The
box for "elect transfer" is pre-ticked. Debtors who take the boat (Thursdays, from the floor-9 dock in the Sump) work Bastion's
lines, making cells and chips on the Works' old machines, at a day rate that clears a Mills rent debt in about four years.

The first returnees came back on a Thursday in **Y68**. They had paid. They had nothing: no lease, no Standing, and, they found, a
debt record. The Company's arrears register records debts; it never records them settled (the Ninefold's register does; file 05).
Every returnee who walked into a Lease Office was refused a Charter for arrears they had worked four years to clear.

The people who met them on the dock that day, and every Thursday since, are **the Thursday People**.

### What they do

- **The Meet.** Every Thursday at the floor-9 dock, when the boat comes in: families, returnees, and Thursday People with
  blankets, water, a brick, and a hot-bunk shift paid for a week.
- **The Wait.** A list of everyone on Bastion, with their transfer date and their release date, kept in chalk on a pillar at the
  dock and read on Static's Names when a date comes up.
- **Settling.** Taking returnees' Bastion discharge papers to the Ninefold to have the debt written **settled** in the Sump's
  register, and then to the Arbiter (60 cr filing fee, every time) to have the Company's arrears register corrected. The Arbiter
  has corrected eleven in eighteen years.
- **The Swap.** The Thursday People raise money (from members' wages, from Static notices, from the Co-op's Blood Fund when it has
  any) to **buy work-off contracts** from Brisk & Lowe's brokers before the boat, and turn them into Mills labour: the debtor works
  off the same debt in a Mills workshop instead of on Bastion. A Mills employer has to agree to take them.
- **Bastion craft.** Returnees learned cell assembly on Bastion's lines. They run reconditioning benches in the Sump, and they
  know things about cells the Old Hands' 4000s don't (newer tricks, older machines).

### Structure and people

No officers; a dock, a pillar and a Thursday. About two hundred returnees, and their families.

- **Corliss Mbatha.** Fifty. Two terms on Bastion (Y70–74, Y77–81): came back once, fell behind, took the boat again. Has
  organised the Meet since Y81. Speaks quietly. Has a list of every Mills employer who has ever taken a Swap debtor, and what they
  paid them.
- **Sunniva Holt.** Forty-six. One term (Y79–83). Worked a formation line on Bastion. Swears the machines were Halden Works
  machines, Works part numbers, Works brass plates, shipped to Bastion **after** the Drowning while the Company was telling everyone
  the plant was destroyed (file 05's hook). Also swears Bastion's formation cycle is forty minutes. Ruth Amadi (06a) says the Dark
  Floors' cycle is nineteen. "So whatever's behind those welds," Sunniva says, "it isn't making what we made."
- **Ned Farrow.** On Bastion now, release date Y88. Writes a letter every month that comes back on the boat; it is read at the
  Meet. His letters are funny. The last one wasn't.
- **Aga Wolny.** Fifty-five. Was a work-off broker for Brisk & Lowe for twenty years: the person who sold contracts to Bastion. Now
  brokers the Swap, because she knows the price of every contract and every broker's weakness. The returnees don't forgive her.
  They use her.

### What they want, offer, buy and sell

- **Want:** settled debts written down; returnees housed and working; fewer people on the boat; the pre-ticked box unticked.
- **Offer:** labour (returnees and Swap debtors, cheap and skilled); cell reconditioning; Bastion knowledge.
- **Buy:** work-off contracts (to Swap); blankets, water, bricks, hot-bunk shifts; filing fees.
- **Sell:** **Bastion seconds** (cells from Bastion's lines that failed inspection, smuggled back by returning workers: grey,
  uncertified, usually excellent); labour.

**In play:**
- **Swap hirelings.** A slip: "Debtor, work-off contract, 340 cr remaining. Will you take her in the Mills? 18 cr a day off the debt,
  board from your stock." The player gets a hireling-as-plan (`IDEAS.md`) who works for her debt, cheaply. Her remaining debt is
  shown on her, and shrinks each day she works. The player can pay it off at any time (a lump sum), and she becomes a free
  hireling or a client for life. Or the player can let the contract run, or end it, and she takes the boat. All visible.
- **Bastion seconds.** Cells (1x1) at a grey price, better than re-terminated chute cells, with a small chance of a hidden
  defect the analyser reveals. A supplier for drones and grow lamps.
- **The Meet.** A Thursday event at the dock: returnees, families, a slip or two (blankets, a week's shifts, water). The player can
  attend by drone (send supplies) or not.
- **Taking the boat.** The player's own worst ending (below), made concrete: if the arrears ladder runs to the end and nothing is
  left, the work-off contract arrives with the box ticked.

### Relations

- **Halden and the debt firms:** the Agreement; the pre-ticked box; Brisk & Lowe's brokers; the Varga Books on 6 uses its own
  work-off and doesn't ship anyone, which the Thursday People respect.
- **The Ninefold:** write settlements in the Sump's register; dock fees waived on Thursdays.
- **The Old Hands:** the Pension Book and the arrears register are the same fight. Ruth Amadi and Sunniva Holt meet at the
  Canteen and compare cycles.
- **The Mills Co-op:** takes Swap debtors in the Crib and the Pool; the Blood Fund lends to the Swap when it can.
- **Plasma buyers:** returnees sell plasma to fund the Swap. The Rain Church forgives them, which it does for nobody else.
- **Hot-bunk keepers:** a week's shifts for every returnee, on the Knockers' word.
- **Static:** the Wait read on the Names; Ned Farrow's letters on air.

### In the endings

- **The boat.** The bad ending. The player takes the boat. The ending text is four years later: a Thursday, the floor-9 dock,
  Corliss Mbatha with a blanket, and the player's name read on the Names as returned. The Lease Office still says they owe it.
- **The box.** A long chain (the Archivists' copy of the Agreement, the Arbiter, the Co-op's weight, Sunniva's testimony about the
  machines): the pre-ticked box becomes unticked. Fewer people "elect". The boat still goes on Thursdays.

**Hook:** Sunniva Holt kept one thing from Bastion: a brass maker's plate off a formation machine. It reads **HALDEN WORKS, LINE 9,
FLOOR 23**. Floor 23 is behind the welds.

### Voices

- "Thursday. Bring a blanket."
- "Settled. Write it down. Write it down where they can't lose it."
- "Four years on the line. I could make a cell in my sleep. I did, mostly."

## Days when everyone moves

These are world events that touch several factions at once, so one event plays out as many slips:

- **Rain day** (forecast a day ahead by the Sounding, read on Static): Rain Church slips flood the board; the Foundation's Clean
  Cup tables go up beside the Fonts; barons rent catch squares; the fixers order folk rain; the Amenity opens the Gutter overflow
  "by staff"; hot-bunk air-wells fall behind (damp air, more sleepers indoors) and keepers order water; the Syndicate's acts of
  tide exclusion is quoted more often.
- **Card Night** (every thirty days): the Period Club dines; a pencil draft surfaces through a baron; the next morning, a re-cut
  notice; the Green Bands get their floor instructions; Static reads the new card at 05:30 with the circled numbers.
- **The Great Ebb** (twice a month): the Exchange steps market; fixers' runners on the Landing; divers' biggest lots; magnet fishers'
  pulls; Pratt buying paper by the kilo; the blood boat stays away (no slack water).
- **Thursday:** the boat. The Meet. The Wait read on the Names. A Swap slip or two.
- **A sweep** (announced to the Ninefold the day before): Static goes quiet; the Stillcars pull in the Net; the Voided move stock
  out of fronts; stinkers' stills go behind hot-bunk walls; a rig is found (an old one).
- **Quota week** (month's end): the Green Bands' soft-report offers; barons' hour stalls cleared early; occupancy counts at
  unannounced hours; keepers pay stair money for the hour.
- **The Gala** (once a year): the best-paying produce contract in the game; fixers furnish; plasma buyers sponsor; the Syndicate
  insures the catering at a special rate; Static reads the menu on air beside the Hope Voucher budget.
- **Founding Day:** the Old Hands' "As it stands"; the Foundation's annual report, read by Static with commentary; the Amenity's
  annual meeting; Card Night falls in the same week every third year.
