# 07. Outside: the Flats, the bay and the other towers

Speculative. Plain text is the main version; **Alternative:** marks another version of the same thing; **Hook:** marks a
mystery or story seed; **In play:** marks where it surfaces in the game. Years are Halden Years (Y0 = the Works opened; the
present is Y86). Months are numbered the Company way (First Month to Twelfth Month). History of the flood is in 01, the
drowned floors as rooms of the building in 02, the bay's chemistry in 03, the Tide Folk and the Sump gangs in 06a and 05.
This file is everything outside the Stack's walls, and how it reaches a workshop on floor 17.

---

## 1. Calder Bay

### The shape of the bay

Calder Bay opens to the west. The coast runs roughly north to south, and the old Calder river came in from the east through
its delta, splitting into channels across the silt the town was built on. The Stack stands at the town's north-west corner,
on reclaimed land at the edge of the old harbour, so that its **Seaward** faces (west and south) look out over open water and
its **Landward** faces (north and east) look over what was the town and is now the Flats.

What a person sees from a cut window on the Landward face of floor 17, at low water on a clear morning, left to right:

- **North:** a pale grey plain with no birds on it. That is the bed of **Lagoon Six**, now called **the Grey Field**. Beyond it,
  the stub of the **Shared Bank** with a notch in it (**the Breach**, chainage 2+510), and beyond that, flat brown water over
  the drowned salt-pans of the **North Well Field**, which the Flats call **the Pans**.
- **North-east:** the chapel spire of **Wellside**, leaning, with a cormorant on it. Rows of roof ridges like the backs of
  animals lying down in the mud.
- **East:** **Low Calder**, the biggest stretch of Flats, cut by the old river channels, which still drain it at low tide in
  brown braids. In the middle distance, at a slant that makes visitors put a hand on the window frame, **the Hulk**.
- **South-east:** **Saltmarket**'s roofs and the stump of the clock tower (**the Stump**), and beyond them, rising out of the
  water as a long low island, **the Knuckle**, the gravel ridge that was the town's only high ground and is now the bay's only
  dry land. On its far end, the stacked coloured bands of **St. Ober's Spire**.

From a Seaward window (which most Mills tenants do not have, because the Seaward wall is salt-blasted and cheap glass does not
survive it), south to west:

- **South, close:** the bare steel skeleton of **the Frame**, two kilometres off along the line of the drowned **Esplanade**,
  humming. Beyond it the **Pleasure Pier** reaching out from the Knuckle's foot, white, with flags.
- **South-east, far:** the fourteen concrete cylinders of **Greenhold** in a row at the old port, and the windowless block of
  **Coldharbour** beside them.
- **South-west, across the bay mouth:** **Vantage**, white, on the south shoal, with its desalination plume a paler streak on
  the water to its west.
- **West:** **Bastion** on its island, low and walled, and on the island's sheltered eastern side the clutter of hulls that is
  **the Lee**.
- **West, very far, on a clear day only:** a dot on legs, **the Sounding**.

hours column above is its flight time on a crossing frame. The same table with wind (below) is what a player learns to read.

### Tides

The bay has two tides a day, a little more than twelve hours apart, and each day's tide comes about fifty minutes later than
the day before. Twice a month, at new and full moon, the range is biggest (**springs**); halfway between, smallest (**neaps**).
The spring range is about five and a half metres; the neap range is about two and a half. The two biggest springs of the year
are the **equinox springs**, in the Third Month and the Ninth Month. The Drowning was on the Third Month's.

The Tide Folk measure everything from **Wall Book zero**, the datum the wall gangs used, and talk about heights in **marks**
(the painted marks on the Stack's base, one every half metre, repainted by the berths every spring). Their words for the
fortnightly rhythm are old harbour words: the tides are **making** when the range is growing toward springs and **taking off**
when it shrinks toward neaps. A spring ebb is a **Great Ebb** (06a). A neap is a **slack week**, and nobody dives.

How the tide sets life in and around the Stack:

- **The Landing** (floor 4, Landward) floats at high water and hangs above the mud at low. Boats come to it on the flood; the
  mud people walk to it on the ebb. Its busy hours move fifty minutes a day.
- **The Race** (02) under floor 0 carries tidal turbines, so the Stack's base power surges at mid-flood and mid-ebb and sags at
  slack water. The Mills call the sag **slack hour**. The divers call the same hour **the window**, because the Race is
  still and the drowned floors are safe to enter. When the Mills go dark, the divers go down.
- **The boom.** Twice a day the flood reaches the drowned floors and pushes air out through the old Works vents, a slow
  thud you feel in the soles of your feet on 4 to 9. Sump children count booms instead of hours.
- **The Nine Dock** (floor 9, Seaward) is the deep-water side: the scour from the Race keeps a channel open in front of it, so
  boats can call at any tide. The Landing is for the Flats; the Dock is for the Company and for other towers.
- **The Great Ebb market** on the Exchange steps (06a) runs for the four hours around a spring low.

**In play:** a **tide strip** on the day's clock: two lows, two highs, sliding fifty minutes later each day, with spring and
neap marked across a fortnight. It drives at least four things already proposed elsewhere: slack hour (Mills sockets dead,
cell racks run on), Flats contract windows (06a), dive-supply contracts (divers want delivery before the window, not after),
and the Great Ebb market. A **tide table** is an item (a printed card, 1x1, sold by the Tide Folk and Static for 1 cr a month);
without one the strip shows only today, with one it shows the fortnight.

**Alternative:** the tide strip is free and always shows the fortnight, and the tide table item instead gives the exact hour of
the Great Ebb's lowest point, which is when the market's best goods appear.

### Wind

The prevailing wind is **south-westerly**, off the open bay, onto the Seaward faces. It is why the Crown's gardens face
Seaward behind glass walls and why Mills cut windows face Landward. In settled weather a second pattern runs inside it: in the
morning a **land breeze** draws off the drowned town toward the sea (the Flats call it **the draw**), and in the afternoon the
**sea breeze** pushes back in (**the push**). In the Blows (below) the south-westerlies come in gales, and the Seaward face of
the Stack takes them like a cliff.

**In play:** wind is a modifier on outside flight. A crossing flown with the wind takes the table's hours; into it, one or two
more hours and more charge. Morning flights out to sea and afternoon flights home ride the draw and the push; doing it the
other way round costs an hour each way. On days with a Sounding bulletin (below) the wind is shown in advance; without one the
player learns it from the slip ("the push is late today", says a Flats client) or from the flag on the Frame, visible from any
Seaward window.

### Seasons

Calder had four seasons on the old calendar. The bay has four of its own, and the Mills use these names.

**The Blows** (Tenth Month to Second Month). Gales from the south-west, rain in sheets, short grey days. Storm surges ride on
spring tides. Every winter the Sounding names between six and fourteen storms. Outside routes close for a day or three at a
time. Hanging Boxes are torn off the Plinth wall and land on the Flats, where the soil is picked out of the mud. The Rain
Church has its best season, and the Skywater Hall its fullest cisterns. After each storm the bay runs brown with silt for a week,
every filter in the tower clogs, and the Flats boom: storms dig.

**The Murk** (Third Month to Fifth Month). Sea fog, thick and cold, rolling in at dawn and sometimes not lifting for a week.
Fog nets (03) fill. Drones fly blind and slow, and poachers cannot see them either, so the Murk is smuggling season. The
Third Month holds the equinox springs and **Wall Night**, the anniversary of the Drowning on the 19th; Flats berths light
lamps on every roof that night, and from the Stack's Landward face old Calder looks lived in.

**The Still** (Sixth Month to Eighth Month). Calm, heat, and the smell. The bay lies flat and the Slick shows its colours. In
the hottest weeks **the Red** blooms and the water goes rust-coloured and the fog, when it comes, smells wrong. Eels die and
wash up on the wrack line; the gulls get fat. Wind gliders sit becalmed; solar is at its best. This is the Pleasure Pier's
season and the time the Crown sends out the most luxury contracts and the least work.

**The Turn** (Ninth Month). One month, its own season because of its tides and its wind: the autumn equinox springs give the
year's deepest Great Ebbs, the deep streets show for longer than at any other time, and steady north-westerlies blow clean and
dry for weeks. Best month of the year for the Lines and for salvage. Prices for salvage fall in the Stack in the Turn because
so much comes up, and rise again in the Blows.

**In play:** the season is a slow modifier on the contract board and on prices:

| Season | Board leans to | Prices | Routes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Blows | storm repairs, filters, cells, Rain Church slips | filters and sealant up; salvage up | outside routes close on storm days |
| The Murk | smuggling, fog-water, Lee contracts | fog water cheap; poacher risk down | slow flights; no visual landing at the Hulk |
| The Still | Crown luxury, Pier contracts, cold-bay work | ice and cold space up; water up | Drifters becalmed; Lines slow |
| The Turn | salvage, Flats contracts, inter-tower freight | salvage down; rope and magnets up | best month for Lines and crossings |

### The Sounding's bulletins

Nobody in the bay forecasts for free. The Sounding (section 6) sells weather by the hour of warning:

- **Public** (free, on the provincial band and repeated by Static): twelve hours, in soft words ("higher than usual tides").
- **Standard** (8 cr a week): twenty-four hours, wind, rain, fog, the Red.
- **Premium** (60 cr a week, or a Sounding contract reward): seventy-two hours, storm tracks, surge heights, storm names.

The Sounding names storms in alphabetical order through each season and sells the naming rights; most of the names in recent
years were chosen by Crown children at auction. The storms of Y85 included **Birdie**, **Clementine**, **Duchess** and
**Fenwick**, the last named after a pony.

**In play:** a weather subscription is a standing line on the weekly bill and a small paper item delivered each morning (a
**bulletin slip**). With Public, a storm appears on the day's clock twelve hours out; with Standard, a day out; with Premium,
three days out with a name and a strength. Knowing a storm early lets the player pull drones in, pack sealed bays, buy filters
before the price jumps, and sell sandbags, sealant and cells into the panic. The Rain Church pays well for rain forecasts it
cannot afford itself (06a).

### The colour and smell of the water

Water people read the bay by eye and nose before anything else.

- **Halden green.** The everyday colour around the Stack: a grey-green, opaque below a hand's depth, with the Slick's rainbow
  on it wherever it lies still. Every Mills child draws the sea this colour.
- **The Tongue.** Below the Sump's Outfall on the Seaward side, a brown plume that fans out on the ebb and lies back against
  the Plinth on the flood. Gulls sit on it in rafts. It smells of exactly what it is.
- **The Boil.** In front of the Race mouth at mid-flood and mid-ebb, the turbines push up a visible upwelling, darker and
  slightly warm, ringed with foam that does not pop. Eels stunned by the blades come up in it. The gulls know the tide table
  better than the reckoners.
- **The Grey Field.** Over Lagoon Six's bed the shallow water is pale grey and opaque, with a skin that holds the shape of
  anything dropped on it. Gulls will not land on it. It smells, on warm days, like wet cement and something sweet behind it.
- **Storm brown.** After a storm the whole bay goes ochre and milky with stirred silt for a week. Filters clog; stills foul;
  divers stay up; the Flats boom.
- **The Red.** In the Still, a rust-coloured bloom spreads from the Tongue outward. It smells like a cat's tray. It brings a
  rash to anyone who works the water, and a dead-eel stink to the wrack line.
- **The Blue Line.** Out past the Lee, roughly where the island of Bastion breaks the incoming swell, the water turns to an
  ordinary sea colour: blue-grey, clear enough to see your hand at arm's length. Inside the Blue Line is the bay; outside is
  the sea. Boat people say "past the blue" to mean "clean", and "inside" to mean "here".

Smells by place and hour: sulphur and rotten eggs from the mud at low water; the sweet hospital edge of **trike** over the Grey
Field on hot afternoons; the fishy sweetness of **pyrol** from any standing water in the sun; salt and diesel at the Nine Dock;
wet concrete everywhere after a storm, which old people still call **grey-wave smell** and will not stay in a room with.

**In play:** water drawn from different parts of the bay can carry a **source tag** on its vessel (Tongue, Grey Field, Boil,
Blue Line), which tells a stinker or a tester what is likely in it before any test: Grey Field water is metal-heavy, Tongue water
is germ-heavy, Blue Line water is only salt. Blue Line seawater is a real, if small, trade: Ma Senna Duku (06a) pays more for
it, because her white salt comes out whiter, and so do the Pleasure Pier's bathhouses, which advertise "open-sea bathing".

### Birds and other living things

No trees, no songbirds. What lives in and over the bay is what can eat what the tower and the town left.

- **Gulls.** Big grey-backed herring gulls by the thousand, nesting on the Hulk's open face, on the Stack's mechanical ledges and
  on every Flats roof that is not lived on. The Mills call them **tithers**, because they take a share of everything. They feed
  at the Tongue and the Boil, on the wrack line, on the Bale Hall's Spill Door bales, and on anything carried in an open drone
  bay. The biggest of them, black-backed, nest on the Crown's gardens behind the glass, where they are a protected nuisance:
  the **Crown gulls**. Crown gardeners are paid to take their eggs and are not allowed to harm them. Gulls will not land on the
  Grey Field. Nobody knows why, and everybody has a theory.
- **Cormorants.** Black, upright, wings hung out to dry, perched on the Wall stubs, the Stump and the Wellside spire. The Flats
  call them **the Board**.
- **Oystercatchers.** Black and white, red bills, loud; they run along the tide edge on the Flats exactly as it moves. Tide Folk
  call them **reckoner birds**, and say they are the drowned wall gangs still watching the water. Nobody harms one.
- **Terns.** White and quick, arriving each Sixth Month to dive at the Boil. Their arrival is the sign of the Still. Their
  leaving is the sign of the Turn.
- **Starlings.** In the Blows, a hundred thousand roost in the Hulk's broken floors, and at dusk the flock turns over the bay
  like smoke. Hulkers sell their droppings by the sack.
- **Bay eels.** Grey, thin, everywhere in the channels; they eat the dead. Sold to insect ranchers as bin feed and smoked on raft
  roofs as **Flats bacon**, which nobody should eat and plenty do.
- **Grey-backs.** The shore crabs of the Flats, shells grey from the slurry. Boiled on the berths, ground for bin feed, used as
  bait for eels.
- **Wall mussels.** Black, packed in mats on the Calder Wall's surviving stubs. They hold every metal in the bay. "One is a meal,
  a dozen is the shakes."
- **Barnacles.** The barnacle line on the Stack's base is the Flats' clock and calendar (06a).
- **Wrack.** Brown weed in thick mats on the Wall and the stilts. Dried, it is the tower's packing filler (06a).
- **Moon jellies** in the Still, by the thousand, clear discs with four rings; the Flats call them **ghosts**. They clog intake
  screens, including the Waterhouse's.
- **Lugworms** in the mud, dug for eel bait. Their casts on the mud surface are how a mudlark tells sand from slurry: worms do not
  live in slurry.
- **Raft rats.** Swim well. Eat everything. Ride drifting rafts from berth to berth.

**In play:** gulls are the one animal that touches the core loop. An **open-bay** drone (no lid, no seal) flying an outside route
risks the **gull tax**: one food item lost from the bay on landing, chosen from the top of the stack. A bay seal or a lidded
liner stops it. Gull feathers (down from the Hulk gullery, sold by the sack) are a packing and insulation material: lighter than
wrack, warmer, used to line cold boxes. Moon jellies blocking the Intake is a world event: Waterhouse output drops for a day,
tap hours shrink in the Mills, water prices jump.

**Hook:** gulls will not land on the Grey Field, but in the last year they have also stopped landing on one roof in Wellside,
a row house on what was Brine Lane, three doors up from the chapel. Nothing about the roof looks different.

## 2. Old Calder under the water

### How the town lies now

Old Calder did not drown evenly. The ground had been sinking unevenly for forty years, fastest near the Well Field and the
Lagoon, slowest on the Knuckle's slopes; then the grey wave laid a metre or two of slurry over the lowest districts; then
twenty-nine years of tides have scoured some streets bare and silted others to the windowsills. The result is a drowned town
with a **relief map** that every Flats child knows by heart and no tower person has ever seen:

| District | At low water | At high water | Character | Who works it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Grey Field** (Lagoon Six bed) | pale slurry plain, skinned, soft | shallow grey water | nobody lives or walks here; the slurry takes a leg to the knee | nobody, except the desperate |
| **The Pans** (North Well Field) | brown water over salt-pans; wellhead rigs stand out | brown water | Works pump gear; magnet fishing from boats | two berths and the *Recovery 2* |
| **The Strand** | roofs and upper floors; streets under knee-deep mud | roofs only | the wall gangs' old district; first to flood; most kin-houses | Tide Folk; nobody else may dive it |
| **Wellside** | rows of roofs; the chapel spire; streets silted to the sills | roof ridges | the best-named dead in Calder (01); quiet; well-licence plaques on the doors | Tide Folk, carefully |
| **Low Calder** | the main Flats: mud plain cut by braided channels, houses to the eaves | water to the gutters | the biggest salvage ground and the most picked over | everyone |
| **Saltmarket** | the market square shows on most lows; the Exchange steps on springs only | the clock tower stump only | old market and commercial streets; shops, banks, the Picturehouse | everyone; the Tide Table meets here |
| **Ferrybank** | the river bank; the Leaning Row lying on its side | channel water | strong ebb current in the channel | eel traps; little else |
| **The Esplanade** | a line of grand house fronts, upper floors dry; promenade under | upper windows | the rich town; private lots (01) | house divers on Crown contracts; Flats divers at night |
| **The Knuckle** | dry ground, always | dry ground, always | St. Ober's land; fenced | nobody from the Flats without a pass |

The **Works roads** (raised for the Company's trucks before the flood) make the Flats walkable. At low water they stand out
of the mud as grey causeways running from the Stack's base east to Saltmarket and south along the Esplanade toward the
Knuckle. The Flats call them **the Haul**, because everything is hauled along them. They are the only firm footing on the
Flats, and they flood first on the turn because the mud on either side is higher.

### Landmarks

Each landmark here is a place salvage comes from, a name that shows up on slips and in rumours, and somewhere a contract can
point to.

**The Stump.** The stone base of the Calder clock tower in Saltmarket, three storeys of it, the clock face gone (it is in a
Crown dining room on 97, working, set to Halden time). The Tide Table sits on top of the Stump at each Great Ebb (06a).
Reckoners chalk the next fortnight's tides on its west face; the chalk survives the water for exactly one tide, so the table
is rewritten twice a day by whoever reaches it first.

**The Exchange steps.** The broad granite steps of the old Corn Exchange, deep in Saltmarket's lowest corner. They show only on
spring ebbs, for about four hours, and that is when the Great Ebb market is held on them, the only market in the bay on dry
ground (06a). Stalls are planks laid across the steps. Every trader watches the bottom step. When it goes under, you have about
forty minutes.

**The Mission Chapel.** Wellside's chapel, its spire leaning ten degrees toward the Lagoon as if listening for it. The Tally
(01) began here: the first names were chalked on its inside walls by survivors who rowed back in Y57. The chalk was redone each
year until the Archivists photographed it in Y66. Divers do not take anything from inside the chapel. The bell is still in the
spire; nobody has rung it.

**The Teapot.** The cannery's water tower: a riveted steel tank on four legs, high enough that its belly clears the spring high
water by a man's height. The **Dorrance** family (stinkers, not Tide Folk) have lived in it since Y59. They catch rain off a
canvas skirt rigged around the tank's roof, filter it through charcoal and sand, and sell it at the Landing and to passing
boats. It is the largest unlicensed rain tank in the bay, about forty thousand litres when full, and Halden has never
touched it, because the Flats are outside the Structure and the Company's water licence, in law, covers "the Structure and
its appurtenances". The Dorrances painted HALDEN APPURTENANCE on its side in Y71 as a joke, and the joke has held for fifteen
years.

**In play:** the Teapot is a Flats supplier of **Teapot rain** (sold by the 5 L jug, 3 cr at the Landing, and cheaper in the
Blows), a real-ish water grade that is neither Halden Pure nor stinker water. Up the tower it is grey. A Dorrance contract asks
for charcoal, sand, mesh, canvas and sealant, and pays in water. **Hook:** Halden's Lease Office has a file open on whether a
water tower that was once the cannery's, which the Company has owned since before the Works opened (01), is "an appurtenance". The day it decides, the Teapot
gets a licence fee or a demolition barge.

**The Leaning Row.** The eleven Ferrybank houses that began to tilt in Y21 finished falling in Y57 and now lie on their backs
in the river mud, front walls up, so that at low water you can walk along the row on their brick faces and look down through
their front windows into their hallways. Eels nest in them. Children dare each other to lie on a window and look down.

**Mercer's.** The department store at the south end of the Esplanade: five storeys of stone and plate glass, the top two
above water at every tide. The store was evacuated, stock and all, on the 18th (the Mercer family were on the guest list), so the
sales floors hold nothing but fittings. What has kept Mercer's famous is **the Undercroft**: the basement strongroom where the store
kept its safe-deposit boxes for Esplanade families, behind a door divers have reached and none has opened. Mercer's is a
private lot (01): the Mercer family, now on 94, still own it, and hire divers to check the door every year and report that it is
closed. Flats divers say the family does not want it opened. They want to know that nobody else has.

**In play:** Mercer's is a late, rich contract site. Crown clients send oddly precise slips ("box 211, the green leather case;
deliver unopened"). The player never dives; the player supplies the dive (hose rig, cutting gear, sealed cells, a lamp) and
receives what comes up, or doesn't. **Hook:** box 211 is listed in the Mercer's ledger the Archivists hold under a name that is
not an Esplanade family's. It is Sefton Harrow's.

**The tram depot.** At the bottom of Low Calder, the deepest street the Flats walk: the depot yard and its sheds, nine tram
cars still on their rails, sunk to the window line in slurry. The Flats call them **the Sleepers**. On the year's deepest ebbs (the
Turn) the depot floor shows, and the copper and brass fittings of the cars are worked by whole berths at once, in a hurry. The
overhead wire went for scrap in the first year.

**The fire station.** On the edge of Saltmarket, its yard on a deep street, its tower above water. The bell is gone (a fixer
took it in Y74; it is in a garden on 99). The bell heard under the water on certain spring tides (06a) is, by elimination,
something else.

**The Hole.** The Saltmarket sinkhole of Y33, nine metres across, is now a pool that never drains, the deepest water inside
the Flats: eight metres at low tide. Things that fall into it stay. Eels gather in it in the Blows. Divers go into the Hole
on a line to recover whatever the Flats have dropped there that week, and charge for it.

**The Regal.** The Picturehouse on Saltmarket Street, its upper circle and projection room above high water. A berth of
fifteen lives in the circle; its reckoner sleeps in the projection room with the projector, which she oils. The screen still
hangs, brown with water stains to two-thirds of its height, a perfect tide gauge. On Wall Night the berth hangs lamps
behind it.

**The Strand post office.** Ma Senna Duku's brine pans on its flat roof (06a). The sorting room below still has its pigeonholes,
and in the pigeonholes, under the silt, letters: Y57 post, sorted for delivery on the 20th. The Tide Folk let no one take them.
The Archivists have asked for twenty years.

**The Esplanade steps.** The wide stone stair from which the Founding Reception's guests boarded the Company launches on the
night of the 19th (01). At low water the steps show to the bottom, where the iron mooring rings are still set in the stone. Flats
people call them **the Guest Steps** and spit when they pass.

**Number Three, Esplanade.** Dr Tamsin Pell's flat (01), a first-floor flat in a tall terrace at the Esplanade's north end, near
the Wall. Its windows are above water at low tide. Everyone who has been inside says it is empty: no papers, no furniture,
the shelves bare. It was emptied before the water, or after it, by someone neat.

**The Breach.** The notch in the Shared Bank at chainage 2+510, where the Lagoon went out and the sea came in. At low water you
can stand in it: a slumped gap forty metres wide with the bank's clay core showing in layers like a cut cake. Sefton Harrow
was standing near here at 21:12 on the 19th (01). On Wall Night, someone leaves a pair of men's boots in the Breach. They are
gone by the next tide.

**The Pans' wellheads.** Out in the drowned North Well Field, the steel housings of the Works' outlying wells stand above the
water like a field of grave markers, each with its number plate: NW-1 to NW-26. The pumps under them were founding-era. Most
have been pulled by the *Recovery 2*. Some have not, because they are under Halden Civil's own **Calder Recovery Licence** and
the Flats do not dive the Company's own wells. Not openly.

**The Guide Light.** The harbour light at the end of the old breakwater beyond the Knuckle's foot, still lit, by the Pier Trust,
because the Pier's guests arrive by water at night. It shows the deep channel. It shows the poachers the deep channel too.

**In play (all landmarks):** landmarks give salvage a **findspot**. A wet salvage lot from the Flats can carry its findspot as a
tag (Mercer's, the Sleepers, the Regal, the Pans), and the findspot changes who wants it: brass from the Sleepers sells to the
Frame (fittings); anything from the Esplanade with a name on it sells to a penthouse fixer; anything from the Strand or
Wellside may be a **kin-house** good, which the Tide Folk will not deal in and which ruins your standing with them if you are
caught passing it on. A **findspot card** (a slip of paper from the finder, with the place and the tide) raises the price to
fixers and Archivists and is a forger's favourite product.

## 3. The Flats

### Who lives there

About two thousand people live on the Flats. The Company census does not count them, because they are outside the Structure;
the Tide Folk count them by berth; the Ninefold book on 9 counts the ones who come up the outer stair.

- **The berths** (about a thousand people in some sixty berths): the Tide Folk (06a), mostly descended from the wall gangs, the
  Strand's fishing families and the Wellside households who would not sign on the water. They hold the best roofs, the dive
  rights to their own districts and the seats on the Tide Table.
- **Strays** (perhaps seven or eight hundred): everyone else. People who "went to the water" to get away from a debt (05);
  people the Pumpmen put out of the Sump for raising the Level; people who came back from Bastion and would not climb the
  tower again; Vantage residents released without a job who washed up here because there was nowhere else; drinkers; people
  who simply could not pay the Mills and would not go to the Sump. Strays live on rafts at the edge of the Grey Field and in
  the worst of Low Calder, take the leftover salvage grounds, and trade through whoever will have them.
- **The stinkers' rafts**: a dozen solar-still rafts moored in a ring off the Regal, run by stinker families who prefer no law
  to Halden law (03).
- **The *Recovery 2***: Halden Civil's licensed salvage barge, a converted dredger, with a crew of thirty paid by the tonne. It
  works the Pans, the deep streets of Low Calder and the Grey Field's edge with grabs and pumps. The crew live aboard and come
  ashore at the Nine Dock. On the Flats they are called **the Weighers**, and nobody sells them anything.
- **House divers**: hired by the Esplanade families and the Crown (on private lots), come out by launch from the Nine Dock,
  dive one house to order, go back. Well paid, not liked.
- **Visitors**: Hulkers who walk over on the Great Ebb along the Haul; plasma buyers on the blood boat; the Foundation's
  Outreach Launch (06a); Static's people; Archivist runners buying wet paper by the kilo; the Rain Church's Font-keeper on the
  Landing.

In the tower, Flats people are called **mudders**, **flatties** and, in the Middle, **the tidal community**. The Tide Folk call
themselves **wet** and everyone in the tower **dry**. Strays have no name for themselves.

### How they build

Everything on the Flats is built for a floor that rises and falls five metres twice a day.

- **Dry tops.** A house whose top floor or roof stays above the spring high water is a **dry top**: the best real estate in
  the bay, held by the berth that first lashed to it in Y57 or Y58. A dry top's roof gets a deck of doors and boards, a shelter
  of salvaged tin and canvas, a rain skirt, and a ring of lashing points for rafts. About a hundred and forty dry tops remain;
  every year a storm takes one or two.
- **Stilt houses.** Where a house has fallen but its walls stand, the berth builds on the walls: stilts of scaffold pole and
  rebar set into the brickwork, a platform above the high-water mark, a ladder down to the mud. Stilt houses sway. People who
  grew up in one cannot sleep in a room that does not.
- **Drum rafts.** The commonest home on the Flats. A platform on a bed of sealed drums, the blue two-hundred-litre **Halden
  drums** that floated out of the Works' chemical stores in the flood and have been the bay's flotation ever since. A raft
  rides up and down on **walking ropes** tied to a chimney or a stilt, each rope just long enough for the spring range. At low
  water it sits on the mud. A raft must be stiff and flat underneath; a raft that grounds across a hidden wall breaks its back
  (**hogged**), and a hogged raft goes down on the next flood with whatever is on it.
- **Door rafts.** The poor version: house doors lashed over rope-netted bottles and floats. Strays live on these.
- **The materials.** Mostly what the tower throws away. The Spill Door on floor 4 (02) puts unsold chute bales onto the mud at low
  tide, a Company clerk ticking them off "disposed to the foreshore"; the Flats are there before the clerk has finished
  ticking. Hanging Boxes torn off the Plinth in the Blows land in Low Calder. VOID-stamped goods from the Outfall come in on the
  wrack line. The Pier Trust's garbage lighter dumps on the Grey Field edge every Sunday. The Flats are built of the Stack's
  refuse, the Pier's, and the drowned town's own bones.

**Getting about:**

- **Splatchers**: flat boards strapped under the boots, a hand wide and a forearm long, for walking soft mud. Everyone owns a pair.
  A child's first pair is cut from a door and is a rite.
- **Mud-horses**: wooden sledges with an upright frame to lean on. You push one across the soft mud with your weight on the
  frame and your feet sliding behind: faster than walking, and it carries a load and a tired diver. Every berth has two or three.
- **Punts and skiffs** for the flood: flat-bottomed, poled more than rowed, because the streets are full of things to hit.
- **The Haul**, at low water.

**In play:** the Flats are a **client** before they are a place. Flats slips (06a) want what a raft needs: rope by the metre,
sealant, drum plugs, tarp, cells for lamps, needles, waders, splatchers' straps, nails. Payment is low in credits and high in wet
salvage. A **raft kit** (a contract asking for rope, four drum plugs, a tarp and sealant, all in one bay) is a good early downward
contract: cheap goods, odd shapes, a tide window.

### How they eat

- **Bought food.** Vitabricks and slab, bought at the Landing from Ruben Vass at the Landing price (about a third above the Mills
  price, because of the hoist and Vass). Most calories on the Flats come from the tower in brick form.
- **Eels**, from traps in the channels and the Hole, boiled or smoked on raft roofs over wrack fires. **Flats bacon** is the Flats'
  one food export: smoked eel, dark and oily, sold quietly up the tower as a treat in the Sump and as bin feed in the Mills.
- **Grey-backs** (shore crabs), boiled. **Wall mussels**, against advice.
- **Gull eggs**, traded from the Hulk in the Fourth and Fifth Months, eaten fresh or pickled in brine.
- **Raft greens.** Each dry top keeps a few boxes of soil (much of it picked out of fallen Hanging Boxes), watered from the rain skirt:
  chillies, beans, leaf greens. Rain is free on the Flats, so a Flats grow box costs nothing to water, which is the one thing
  a Flats family has that a Mills family does not.
- **Raft samphire.** The one crop that grows on brack: salt-marsh samphire grown in trays of rinsed sand, watered with sea
  water. It soaks up the bay's metals like everything else. Ma Senna's rule: "Adults twice a week. Children never. The Board
  (the cormorants) as often as they like."

**Alternative:** no samphire. Nothing edible grows on brack, and the Flats' green food is only what the rain boxes give, which
keeps the rule that fresh food is only grown with fresh water. Samphire then appears as a rumour, a Crown chef's request for
"coastal samphire" that nobody can fill.

### How they drink

The Flats have more rain and less water than anyone.

- **Rain skirts.** Every dry top and most rafts catch rain: canvas skirts and gutters leading into drums. In the Blows the Flats
  are better off than the Mills for water. In the Still they are worse off than anyone.
- **The Teapot** sells rain (above).
- **Solar stills** on the stinkers' rafts: black-painted trays under salvaged glass, slow, a litre or two a day each.
- **The Font** on the Landing, the Rain Church's free cup (06a).
- **Bought water**, at the Landing, in Halden SureSeal cans carried down from the Mills, at Vass's price (about 1.80 cr a litre in
  the Still, against 1.20 at a Mills tap).
- **Small beer**, brewed from raft greens' trimmings and whatever sugar the Landing has, mostly for the water in it.

On the Flats a **cup** (a quarter litre of clean water) is small money, and water debts (03) are counted in cups.

**In play:** water is the Flats' first want and the most reliable downward contract: "Fifteen litres to the Landing, on the
ebb" (06a). In the Still, Flats water slips multiply and pay in better salvage; in the Blows they nearly vanish.

### How they trade

- **The Landing.** Ruben Vass's steel platform at the floor 4 loading doors (06a): the only regular market between the Flats and
  the tower. Vass buys everything the Flats bring up, at his prices, and sells them bricks, water, cells and rope at his
  prices. He keeps a slate, not a ledger. The Tide Folk use him because nobody else will stand on the Landing every tide.
- **The Great Ebb market** on the Exchange steps, twice a month (06a). Tower middlemen come down on Vass's hoist to buy; Flats
  traders sell direct; barter is the rule.
- **Barter units.** A cup of water; a charge (one full cell); a brick; a metre of rope. A wet salvage lot is priced in bricks.
- **Tide cords** (06a): knotted cords, one knot per good trade, worn as a credit history.

What the Flats sell and buy is in 06a. What it comes to, in practice: the Flats sell weight (scrap, salvage) and buy calories,
water and light.

**In play:** the **Great Ebb market** as a barter screen (06a) is the place where the player can buy salvage at Flats prices
before the middlemen add their cuts (section 4). It costs a crate on Vass's hoist (Lift Guild toll) and must be over before
the bottom step goes under.

### How they worship and how they die

The Tide Folk faith is in 06a: the moon keeps the book; the Bay is owed; the first find of each ebb goes back. What the outsider
sees of it on the Flats:

- Tide slates on every berth, chalked fresh each morning.
- A bolt left at **Saint Pump** in the Moat (02) before a plant dive, and a bolt thrown into the Hole before a Saltmarket dive.
- The first find of every ebb dropped back into the water by the finder, without looking at it.
- **Ebb burial** (06a): the dead wrapped, weighted with scrap, walked out on the lowest ebb of the month to a place the
  reckoners know.

And the contrast, visible from every Flats roof: **the Knuckle**, the only dry ground in the bay, is a cemetery. St. Ober's
Trust sells burial plots on it (section 6) to the families of every tower who can pay. On a still day the Flats can hear the
Knuckle's funeral bell. Nobody from the Flats has ever been buried there. "Dry folk get dry graves."

### Health

The Flats live shorter lives than anyone in the bay. What kills them, in the berths' own words:

- **The hold**: the slurry grips the legs and the tide comes in (section 4).
- **Diver's grey**: a greyish skin, the shakes, blue gums (03), worse each year a diver works.
- **Mud fever**: a cut on a shell or a nail, worked in the mud, goes bad in three days. It is the commonest death on the Flats
  that is not drowning. Frass poultices (04) are the Flats' remedy, and they do something.
- **The Red rash** in the Still; **cold lung** in the Blows.

The blood boat (06a) comes alongside on slack days. A Flats donor gets 10 cr, less than a Mills donor, because "Flats plasma
needs more processing". St. Ober's buys it all.

**In play:** Flats medical contracts (wound film, antibiotics, gut tabs) pay double in salvage what they pay in cr, and the
**frass poultice** made from the player's own insect bin's frass is a downward product with a real customer.

### A Flats day, by the tide

A spring day in the Turn, at the Regal berth. Low water at 10:40 and 23:05.

- **04:30.** Dark. The flood is running; the berth's rafts are up on their walking ropes. The reckoner checks the barnacle line by
  lamp against her slate and calls the low at 10:40, "maybe 10:35, the wind's off the land".
- **06:00.** Small beer and a brick, cut in eight. Children to the Regal's circle for the tables. Divers sleep.
- **07:30.** High water. The Landing is afloat and crowded; Vass is buying. The berth sends one punt with yesterday's dried
  salvage: two sacks of iron, a copper coil, a box of cutlery. Vass weighs, slates, pays in bricks and a can of water.
- **08:45.** The ebb begins. Gulls come off the Stack in a cloud.
- **09:30.** The Haul shows. Mudlarks out on splatchers with riddles and probes. Magnet fishers on the Regal's roof cast into
  Saltmarket Street.
- **10:15.** The window. Divers on the mud-horses to the Sleepers; breath dives into the tram cars in pairs, a line on each.
- **10:40.** Low water. The first find of the ebb (a spoon) goes back into the water.
- **11:30.** The flood turns. Divers out of the deep streets first, mudlarks off the low ground next. The reckoner counts heads.
- **12:15.** Everyone on the roof. Finds washed in a drum of rain, laid out on the deck, sorted: iron, copper, brass, glass, cells,
  paper (for the Archivist runner), whatever the berth won't sell (kin-house goods go back to the water).
- **14:00 to 18:00.** Drying, mending, salt-boiling, eel-smoking, net-mending. The blood boat passes and does not stop; it is not
  a slack day.
- **19:30.** High water again; the Landing again. Lamps lit on the roofs.
- **23:05.** The night low. Nobody dives at night. A few strays work the Grey Field's edge with lamps, against all advice.

## 4. Working the mud: diving, magnet fishing and the rest

Salvage on the Flats is six crafts, not one. They differ by tide, by tool, by who does them and by what they bring up, and a
berth is strong when it has all six.

### The six crafts

**Riddling (mudlarking).** Walking the exposed mud at low water with a **riddle** (a round sieve of wire mesh on a wooden hoop)
and a **prodder** (a steel rod with a T-handle). You probe for hard things under the surface, dig them with your hands, riddle the
mud through the mesh in a tide pool, and pick what stays. Children and old people do this; so do strays, on the worst ground.
Finds are small and many: coins, buttons, cutlery, keys, rings, cell caps, bottle stoppers, teeth. A riddler's day is a pocketful.

**Magnet fishing.** Casting a magnet on a rope into flooded ground floors, channels and windows, and hauling back whatever steel
it takes. Done from roofs and punts at any tide; best on the ebb, when the water is moving less. Described in full below.

**Creeping.** Dragging a **creeper** (a four-pronged grapnel, weighted) behind a punt across the deep streets on the flood,
feeling for snags. A snag is hauled up hand over hand or marked with a float for divers. Creeping brings up chain, cable, rope,
bicycle frames, shopping trolleys from Mercer's, and once in a while a drowned car's door. Cable is the prize: copper sells by the
kilo, and most of old Calder's copper is still in the ground.

**Dipping (breath diving).** Short dives without air, into the ground floors of houses and shops, mostly at slack low water
when there is least to swim through. Divers go in pairs, one down and one on the line. A dip lasts a minute, two for a strong
diver. The rule is "two breaths and a third for the way back": you plan the dip on two-thirds of your air. Most Flats divers
are dippers. Most Flats divers who die are dippers.

**Piping (hose diving).** Diving on a hose fed from the surface by a **hand pump** (two people working a bellows on a raft) or a
**cell compressor** (a salvaged pump on a stack of cells, loud and precious). Deeper, longer, into basements and shops and the
Hole. The pump crew is the diver's life, and on the Flats a diver chooses a pump crew before a spouse. Piping is how Mercer's is
dived, and the Sleepers on the deep ebbs.

**Plant diving.** Diving the drowned Works floors from the Well on 7 (section 5), on hoses or Widows-checked rebreathers. These are
tower divers, mostly Sump families, under the Wet Widows' permits (05). The berths count plant divers as tower people, and a
plant diver who works the Flats' streets is a poacher.

### Magnet fishing, done properly

**The magnets.** The good ones are not made: they are salvaged from the Works. The cell lines ran on motors with founding-era
rotor magnets, small and heavy and stronger than anything made today, and the pumps of the Pans' wellheads had bigger ones. A
Flats magnet is one of these, ground flat on one face and set in a steel cup with an eye bolt. Magnets are rated by **pull**,
the kilos a flat face will hold on a clean steel plate; the Flats speak of a **hundred** (a hundred-pull), a **three-hundred**,
and in a few berths a **six-hundred**, which is heirloom-grade. A **two-face** has magnets on two faces of the cup and catches on
the drag. A good magnet is passed down in families.

**The rope.** Braided, twenty to thirty metres, tied to the eye with a **double fisher**, the knot every Flats child learns before
reading. Rope rots fast in brack; a magnet line is retired every season, and retired line becomes walking rope.

**The casts.**
- **The throw**: from a roof, out and dragged back along a street bed. Finds lie along the street's lowest line.
- **The drop**: straight down through a window or a stairwell, then lifted and dropped again across the floor. Finds lie along walls.
- **The drag**: from a moving punt, the magnet bounced along a channel bed. Finds lie in the bends.

**The finds,** in rough order of how often they come up: nails, bolts and rebar (sold by weight); cutlery; tools; bicycle frames;
cell casings, mostly dead; keys; steel cash tins and deed boxes (sealed, contents unknown); fallen drone parts (frames are mostly
alloy and do not take a magnet, but motors and cell cradles do); kitchen pans; and once a season, something nobody expected
(a safe door, a Works ID clip, a ship's bell clapper).

**The rules** (craft and faith together, 06a):
- **The tired magnet.** Empty three casts running: lay it on a roof for a day. (It is fouled with mud; the sun dries it.)
- **The married magnet.** A magnet that grabs a girder or a car will not come off with a straight pull. "My magnet's married."
  You **divorce** it by walking the rope sideways until the magnet slides off the edge of the steel. If that fails, you dive for it
  or cut the rope and the berth mourns it like a dog.
- **The bite.** Two strong magnets brought together catch whatever is between them. Every old magnet fisher has a short finger.
- **No magnets on the Landing.** A magnet near the Landing's steel will take a cell out of a buyer's hand and a filling out of a
  tooth. Vass fines it a brick.

**Where magnets go.** A Flats magnet is worth more in the tower than on the Flats. Mills tenants buy them for the **slow stone**
meter trick (03); the Frame buys them for generators (section 6). So magnets climb the tower too, and every magnet sold up is
one less on the Flats. Berths do not sell their heirloom magnets. Strays do, when they are hungry.

**In play:** the **Flats-fished magnet** (04: 25 to 35 cr) has a pull rating as a visible feature. Low pulls are meter tools.
High pulls are wanted by the Frame as generator parts and fetch three times as much there. A **married magnet** is an event a Flats
client brings to the counter: "My magnet's married under the Regal. I need a diver's rope and a lamp, and I'll give you what's
on the other end." The reward is a sealed steel box (contents unknown, analysable).

### Dangers

- **The hold.** The slurry under the Flats' skin is the Lagoon's: thick, heavy, and it does not let go of a leg. A diver or riddler
  who steps through the crust sinks to the knee and then to the thigh, and pulling one leg drives the other deeper. The flood comes
  in over the held. The rule: **lie down**. Spread your weight flat on the mud, work one leg out slowly, roll. Children practise
  lying down before they are allowed on the mud. Everyone has seen someone who stood up.
- **The cut-off.** The flood fills low ground first. The deep streets (the Exchange, the tram depot) are the last to show and the
  first to fill, and the Haul floods before the mud beside it. A diver in the Sleepers who misjudges by ten minutes walks back
  through water to the chest.
- **A house lets go.** Drowned houses fall in their own time. A dipper inside a ground floor when a floor above gives way is not
  found until the Turn.
- **Sweet pockets.** Basements and sealed rooms trap solvent fumes from the Lagoon's slurry. Opening a door releases them. The
  first sign is sweetness in the mouth; the second is the floor tilting. Pipers carry a **nose** (03) on big jobs; dippers hold
  their breath and hope.
- **Lines and wire.** Old cable, nets, fallen wiring. The dive knife is for this.
- **Silt-out.** One kick near the floor of a drowned room and the water goes brown and stays brown. Divers follow their own line
  out by feel.
- **Cold**, in the Blows: the water is cold enough to stop a diver's hands in ten minutes.
- **Mud fever**, from any cut (section 3).
- **Live cells.** A salvaged cell that still holds charge can swell, short in wet hands and burn. Divers carry cells in wet
  bags, one per bag, and never stack them.

**In play:** dangers are not things the player suffers (the player does not dive). They are why Flats clients want the things they
want, and why salvage has the states it has. A **live cell** is a salvage item with a hidden feature (holding charge or not);
packed with other cells in a bay, an unsealed live cell can short and damage its neighbours on a rough landing. A wet bag is the
fix: one cell per bag.

### States of a find

Salvage changes state on its way up, and each state is worth something different. This is "one item in several states"
in the purest form.

1. **Mudded.** As it comes out of the ground: caked, unidentifiable, heavy. Sold by the sack.
2. **Washed.** Rinsed in rain on the berth. Now you can see what it is. Still wet.
3. **Wet.** Washed but holding water: paper swollen, cells unsafe, metal still rusting. Cannot be analysed (06a). Sells at a
   discount.
4. **Dry.** After a drying rack (a machine: slots and hours) or a day in the sun on a roof. Analysable.
5. **Greened / rusted.** A tag that lowers value until cleaned (a wire brush, vinegar, time).
6. **Sealed.** A tin, a box, a case, a drum: contents unknown until opened, and opening may ruin them.
7. **Sound.** Tested and working (analysed). Full price.

Two legal tags ride on top of these (01, 05): **drowned** (Company property in law, from the Calder seabed) and **findspot**
(where it came from, if anyone wrote it down). A part from the drowned floors may also carry a **Widows' mark** (05).

Two pieces of salvage, followed from the mud to the top.

not a kin-house to anyone in the Regal berth.

Vantage wants a Widows' mark; the Pier wants a story. Where to stand in the chain is a strategy, and each strategy is a different

**Alternative:** the player can **sponsor** a berth: send a regular supply contract (water, bricks, cells) and in exchange receive
the berth's first pick each Great Ebb, before Vass. It is a standing arrangement that pays in salvage and costs in goods and
standing with Vass (who notices).

## 5. The drowned plant as a dive site

File 02 describes the drowned floors as rooms of the building. This is what they are like to dive, and what that means to a
shop that supplies the dives.

### Getting in

- **The Well** (floor 7, Dive Hall): the founding-era freight shaft, flooded below 4. Divers go down a fixed rope to 3 and below,
  hoses paid out from the benches above, family members on the pumps (02). This is the Widows' entrance; their checker sits at
  the top with the board of who is down.
- **The Mouths.** The Gate Floor's public doors and loading doors on the **Landward** face open toward the Flats. The slurry bank
  that has built against the Plinth for twenty-nine years covers them to the lintels; at the deepest equinox ebbs the tops of
  two doors show as dark slots in the mud. Flats divers have dug a way through one of them (**the Slot**) and dive the Gate Floor
  from outside, without a Widows' permit.
- **The scour pool.** On the **Seaward** side, the Race keeps the water deep in front of the old quays. A boat can sit over the
  quay doors and divers can go straight in. This is how the *Recovery 2* tried to work the Gate Floor in Y62, and why it stopped:
  the Race's surge at mid-flood took a diver out through a door and up into the Boil.

**The Doors dispute.** The Widows hold that floors 0 to 3 are theirs to permit (05). The berths hold that the Mouths are on the
Flats side and the Flats are nobody's. Divers from the Slot do not pay the Widows' fifth; the Widows say a part without their mark
is a line-cutter's part. Both sides are partly right, and a part without a mark may be honest Flats salvage or something taken off
a dead diver, and nobody can tell which from the part.

**In play:** a founding-era part arriving with no Widows' mark is cheaper (by about a third) and comes with a choice: buy it and
risk being the shop that deals in line-cutters' goods (standing with the Widows and the Sump falls if it comes out), or refuse it.
A **Slot part** (findspot: the Mouths) is the honest kind of unmarked; the player can only know by asking the right Flats client.

### The dive calendar

Dives in the plant are timed by four clocks:

- **The window.** At slack water the Race is still and the plant's water stops moving. That is the hour to go in and the hour to
  come out. It is also slack hour in the Mills: the same tide that cuts the Mills' sockets opens the plant.
- **The Intake.** The Waterhouse draws the bay through the old Intake on floor 3 (02). When its pumps run hard (morning and the
  Peak Window), the water near the Intake grilles pulls. Divers call it **the Pull**, and stay forty metres away. During
  **Membrane Week** (03), when the trains are shut for cleaning, the Pull stops and the Gate Floor's Seaward half can be worked.
  Membrane Week is the plant divers' busiest week of the year.
- **Storms.** After a storm the drowned floors silt out for a week or more. No dives. Then the best week: storms move the silt and
  uncover things.
- **The Red.** In the Still, the Red reaches the Well's water and brings the rash. Divers go down greased with insect fat.

**In play:** **Membrane Week** is a world event already proposed (03): the tap goes short in the Mills. In this file it is also the
week plant salvage floods the market: founding-era prices dip, and dive-supply contracts (lamps, sealed cells, rope, grease, wet
bags) spike in the days before. A player who reads the calendar stocks up on dive kit before Membrane Week and buys founding-era
parts during it.

### Room by room, as a diver sees it

**The Gate Floor (floor 3).** Three to five metres of water depending on the tide; the best light, coming in through the upper
windows of the Seaward quays; silt to the knee everywhere. The lobby with Ansel Halden's portrait (02), which divers touch for
luck. The Express car pits on the east side, one car sitting at the bottom of its pit with its doors open. The quays with the
trucks still backed up to them, their cargo gone (01: Operation Keel cleared them). The stair doors north and south, shut; the
pry bar in the south door's hinge gap; the scratches on the far side (01). The old Union hall by the quays, empty to the walls.
The Intake grilles on the Seaward wall and the Pull in front of them.

The Gate Floor is also a grave. Somewhere between six hundred and two thousand people died here (01). Their bones are in the
silt. The Widows' rule and the Hooks' rule are the same: **bones stay**. A Gate Floor diver who brings up anything personal (a
ring, a watch, a shoe) brings it to the Tide Folk or the Archivists, for the Tally, and does not sell it. Penthouse fixers have
offered a great deal for "Gate Floor provenance". This is the one thing in the Sump that is not for sale, and the fixers who asked
know it now.

**In play:** a Gate Floor personal item is a **Tally contract** (01): no cr, standing with the Archivists and the Tide Folk, and
sometimes a name. Selling one instead is possible, very profitable, and the worst thing a player can do in the Sump's eyes:
the Widows, the Hooks and the Tide Folk all stop dealing.

**The Polishing Hall (floors 1 and 2).** Eight to fifteen metres down; dark, lit only by lamps; the water strangely clear (02).
The membrane trains in rows like organ pipes, every cartridge casing empty (03). The ion beds. The **spares stores**: steel racks
of sealed crates, most opened and emptied by divers over twenty-nine years, a few still sealed at the back where the racks have
fallen across the aisles. A diver who clears a fallen rack finds crates nobody has touched since Y57.

**The Drum Store (floors 0 to 2, north side).** The chemical vaults: where the pyrol and trike were kept, and where the blue
Halden drums of the Flats came from. Hundreds of drums still stand in the dark, some leaking. The water here has a skin on its
underside, against the ceiling. Sweet pockets in every side room. Divers do not go in. Those who have describe a sweetness that
gets into the mask.

**The Vault (floor 2).** The Archivists' interest (06a): a strongroom with a door rated against fire, not water. Divers have
reached it. Its contents are paper, which is to say pulp, which is to say worth more to the Archivists than anything else on the
floor, if it can be lifted in blocks and dried slowly.

**The process water hall (floor 1).** Under the Dark Floors in the plant's plan. The Widows' list of divers who did not come
back has too many names from here (05). The last diver who came back said the water was warm.

**The Well Hall (floor 0).** The forty wellheads that pumped the Sweetwater Beds, capped, in a hall twenty metres down. Below it,
the Race, where nobody goes.

## 6. The other towers

Calder Bay has more than one tower, and every one of them is what the flood and the money left. Each entry gives what the place
is, who runs it, how it lives, what it wants from the Stack, what it offers, how you get there, and how its contract slips look,
since a slip's look is how the player meets a tower first (06a: slips look like where they come from).

### Vantage

**What it is.** The Corvane Group's tower on the south shoal, across the bay mouth: sixty-six floors of white composite and
tinted glass, built Y49 to Y54 on piles twice as deep as the Stack's, its ground floor raised four metres on a plinth, its own
desalination plant in the base (01). It took the Drowning with wet ankles. From the Stack it looks like a bottle of pills.
Corvane makes batteries, and makes them better than Halden does now.

**Who runs it.** The Corvane board, in the province; in the tower, the **Residency Office**, headed by a Residency Director
(currently **Paulus Wen**, who signs every slip that leaves the tower, by stamp). There are no police. There is **Compliance**,
which does the same things in better clothes. Disputes are settled by **performance review**.

**How it lives.** Vantage is a company town that never stopped being one. Residency is tied to employment, the way the Stack's
tenancies were before Y41: **No Idle Residency**. A resident who loses a job has thirty days to find another inside Corvane; after
that they are **released**, with a single bag, to the Nine Dock of whichever tower will take them, and the Stack always takes them,
because Halden charges rent. Released Vantage people in the Mills are called **drops**; they are good with machines, bad with
the Stack's damp, and ashamed.

Inside, Vantage is clean, quiet, well-lit and metered to the decimal. Its desalinated water, **Corvane Clear**, is cheap inside
the tower and banned from export. Its cells, the **Corvane C-cell**, hold half again what an H-cell holds and have no cycle counter.
Instead they have **CellCare**: each cell must receive a monthly activation signal, by subscription, or it locks itself. Vantage
is also dry: the **Sobriety Standard** forbids alcohol in the tower "in support of resident wellbeing and productivity".

**What it wants from the Stack.**
- **Proof.** Anything that shows Halden's negligence drowned Calder: the Wall Book, the Pell Report, the Harrow Memo, Seawall
  Section papers (01, 06a). Corvane would use them in inter-company arbitration to take Halden's Calder Recovery Licence, and
  with it the Flats and the seabed.
- **Founding-era parts,** with clean provenance. Corvane cannot make them either, and wants to learn how, and needs a legal
  chain of ownership to patent what it learns. A part with a Widows' mark and a salvage receipt is worth twice as much to Vantage as
  to anyone in the Stack.
- **Old Hands.** Corvane recruiters come on the Nine Dock boat twice a year and offer residency to anyone with a founding-era
  clock number and steady hands. Few go. Those who go do not come back.
- **What Vantage forbids.** Its residents buy, quietly, what the Sobriety Standard and Compliance do not allow: stinker spirits,
  Mills gin, pirate radio sets, mended goods with character, real tobacco-substitute, gambling tokens. A bottle of Mills gin worth 6
  cr on floor 17 sells for 60 on Vantage's twentieth floor.

**What it offers.** Magnetic-bearing pumps (which Halden buys at ruinous cost, 03), smart meters (04), C-cells, precision tools,
the **Corvane Tern** crossing frame (section 7), membranes (rumoured, 03), and the best pay in the bay for the right goods.

**Getting there.** Four hours on a crossing frame, across the bay mouth, in open water, into the south-westerly. Vantage's
**Receiving Deck** on floor 20 scans every drone. Goods must carry Corvane-compatible marks or a declaration; anything marked
Recovered (Rc) is refused at the deck unless it is the founding-era part they asked for. Vantage enforces a **Corvane Safety
Zone** of five hundred metres around the tower, patrolled by Compliance cutters, which "inspect" drones that enter it without a
filed route and keep what fails inspection.

**Slips.** White synthetic sheet, unfoldable, untearable, with a printed **compliance code** in the corner and a deadline to
the minute ("Receipt by 14:20, Receiving Deck 20B"). A penalty clause for lateness. The pay is printed, not written.

**In play:**
- **Vantage contracts** are the highest-paying regular contracts in the game and the least forgiving: exact goods, exact
  deadlines, no Recovered goods unless named, a penalty for lateness taken from the pay.
- **Smuggling to Vantage** is a different contract under the same roof: a homesick resident's slip, handwritten on Vantage
  paper, asking for two bottles of gin "packed so the deck won't see them". The packing puzzle becomes concealment: goods
  wrapped inside other goods, a gin bottle inside a hollowed protein block crate, wrack around it. Get caught at the deck and the
  drone is held by Compliance (a buy-back fee and a mark on your standing with Vantage).
- **Drops** at the counter: released Vantage residents as clients and as hireable hands (if hirelings ever come back), with skills
  and no money.
- **CellCare** as a mechanic: a C-cell is a better cell that stops working on the first of the month unless the player pays
  CellCare or has it cracked (a grey service, Sump only).

**Hook:** Corvane has been buying **Lifeline Loans** (01) from Brisk & Lowe for years, at four cr per hundred owed. It now holds the
debts of several thousand Mills families. It has never collected a single one. **Hook:** the Reader in Grey (06a) comes on the
first of each month; the Nine Dock's log shows a Corvane launch arriving on the last day of each month and leaving on the second.

**Alternative:** Vantage is not stricter than the Stack, only newer: the same rent, the same meters, the same Company, with
better paint. Its residents envy the Stack's black market and the Stack's residents envy Vantage's water. The two towers are
mirrors, and the player's trade between them is mostly in the things each one has made illegal.

### St. Ober's Spire

**What it is.** Calder's old hospital, a thirty-floor block on the Knuckle's southern end, which lost its lower wards in Y57
and has been built upward ever since in a series of extensions, each clad in a different colour as the money came in: grey,
then cream, then a band of green, then blue, then white glass at the top. Fifty-eight floors now. The Flats call it **the
Layer Cake**. Its emblem is a key and a cup.

**Who runs it.** The **St. Ober's Charitable Trust**, a charity, with trustees in the province and a **Bursar** in the tower who
decides who is treated, in what order, at what price. The current Bursar is **Canon Aurelia Stent**. The Spire's motto is
carved over the old entrance, which is now under the bay: *No One Turned Away*. The new entrance, on floor 9, has a cashier.

**How it lives.** "A charity that charges like a bank." The Spire treats anyone, on credit:
- **Care Bonds.** A patient who cannot pay signs a bond for the cost of treatment at the Trust's rate of interest. The Trust sells
  bonds on to collectors (05: St. Ober's referral debts).
- **Care Credit.** Part of a Spire worker's wage, and every Spire refund, is paid in Care Credit, which spends only on Spire
  services. It is the bay's other scrip.
- **Rest in Ground.** The Trust owns the Knuckle, the only dry ground in the bay, and sells burial plots on it to the families of
  every tower: a twenty-year lease of a grave for 4,000 cr. When the lease ends the remains are **reinterred** in the Spire's
  ossuary and the plot is sold again. Crown families buy plots in perpetuity. The Flats watch the funerals.
- **The Kidney Programme** (03), buying stone-belly patients' samples through the plasma buyers. **The Donor Scheme**: 150 cr to a
  family for a body, "for the advancement of care". **Blood**, bought through the plasma buyers at 15 and billed at 400 (04).

**What it wants from the Stack.**
- **Plasma**, by cold chain (the blood car and the blood boat, 06a).
- **Sterile larvae**: fly maggots raised clean, for cleaning wounds. The Spire's wound wards use them by the jar, and the Stack's
  insect ranchers are the only people in the bay who know how to raise fly larvae in bulk. They must arrive in a sealed, cold bay.
- **Sharps, recovered.** Steel instruments from the South Chute's medical waste (02), cleaned and boiled by Mills hands, sold back to
  the Spire's charity wards, which use them on the poor. The private wards upstairs use new.
- **Real food** for the private wards: eggs, greens, herbs, broth bones.
- **Cress twists** from the seed savers, which the Spire's kitchens grow as garnish.
- **Samples**: whatever the Kidney Programme is collecting.

**What it offers.** **Spire generics**: medicines made in the Spire's own pharmacy, as good as Vey's at a third of the price, and
illegal to sell in the Stack under Vey's licence, so grey in every band. Care Credit (to the right client). Treatment, on bond. And
the Spire's own discards: expired drugs it dumps at sea from a launch on the ebb, which wash up on the Flats' wrack line in sealed
blister packs.

**Getting there.** Two hours by crossing frame along the shore. The Spire's **sterile receiving** pad on floor 30 refuses any drone
whose bay is not sealed. Cold goods must arrive below a temperature stamped on the slip.

**Slips.** Cream card with the key-and-cup seal in red, a ward number, and "In Charity" printed above the pay.

**In play:**
- **Cold-chain contracts** (plasma, larvae, samples): the late-game use of the cold bay liner and the sealed liner (04). The insect
  bin gets a medical output: **sterile larvae** need a clean bin (a cleaned bin, fed on clean feed, a different machine setting),
  which pays far more than bricks.
- **Recovered sharps**: a chute-to-Spire chain. South Chute medical waste, cleaned and boiled (a pot over heat: the vessel material
  idea), packed in a sealed bay. The Spire pays in credits; the charity wards that use them pay in nothing.
- **Spire generics** as a grey upward-and-sideways good: bought at the Spire, sold in the Mills under Vey's price.
- **Care Credit** as a reward: the Spire pays some contracts in it. It buys a treatment for a client, a debt cleared for a
  neighbour, or nothing at all for a player who never needs a doctor.

**Hook:** the Archivists believe the Spire holds the Works' old toxicology files (03). The Spire's Kidney Programme has been running
for nineteen years, and in those years nobody from the Spire has published a word. **Hook:** a Rest in Ground plot on the Knuckle's
west end, in perpetuity, was bought in Y58 in the name of **Brede Lomax** (01). It is tended. Nobody knows by whom.

### Greenhold

**What it is.** The fourteen concrete silos of the old **Grain Terminal** at the port, fifty metres tall, in a row, linked along the
top by a steel gallery, fitted out after the flood as a growing tower by **Ostrander Seed**. Each silo is a vertical farm: racks
of grow trays climbing a central spiral under grow light, fed by drip lines. The silo tops are rain catchments. The triangular
hollows between the cylinders, which grain men called the **stars**, are where the workers live: windowless three-cornered shafts
with bunks bolted up the walls.

**Who runs it.** Ostrander Seed's board, in the province; in the tower, **the Agronomist**, who is a title and not a person (the
current one has held it for four years and nobody below the gallery knows his name). Enforcement is **Field Security** and,
outside the tower, the **Trait Auditors**.

**How it lives.** Greenhold grows real food for the Crowns and the Terraces of every tower, and seed for everyone else.
- **Growers** sign seven-year **Cultivation Agreements**: bunk in the stars, meals, a wage held in escrow until the seventh year.
  Most growers leave before then and forfeit it. The growers do not eat what they grow. All of it is export. They eat Vitabricks,
  bought from Orrin.
- **Brushers**: young workers on stilts and ladders who pollinate the fruiting crops by hand with soft brushes, since there are no
  bees in the bay. A brusher's wrist is a Greenhold signature.
- **Seed** is sold under licence (05: Greenhold certified seed). It does not come true from saved seed (03), and every packet carries
  the **Greenhold Licence Agreement**: saving, sharing or replanting the seed is infringement.
- **Seconds**: misshapen and blemished produce that cannot go to the Crowns. Greenhold will not let it undercut the real market, so it
  is sprayed with **Ostrander Violet**, a harmless food dye that marks it "not for sale as Real", and sold cheap as feed.
- **Water** is Greenhold's weakness. The silo tops catch rain; the rest comes from Vantage by **water lighter**, at Corvane's price.

**What it wants from the Stack.**
- **Frass**: the dry droppings of insect bins, the best fertiliser in the bay. The Stack's insect ranchers produce it by the tonne and
  most of it goes down the drains. Greenhold pays for it by the sack.
- **Insect protein** for its growers' canteens (cheaper than Orrin's bricks if bought in bulk as paste or slab).
- **Heirloom seeds.** Greenhold buys seed savers' heirlooms through third parties, sequences them, patents the useful traits, and
  then sends the Trait Auditors after the savers. The seed savers know. Some sell anyway, because rent is due.
- **Grow lamps**, repaired or expired (04: Brightline lamps), for the stars, where Ostrander does not spend money on light.
- **Drums, trays, mesh, hose**: everything a farm wears out.

**What it offers.** Certified seed; real-grade produce wholesale (which competes with the player's own beds on Crown contracts);
violet seconds (cheap bin feed and, washed carefully, something else); and the **Trait Audit**, which it offers to nobody and
applies to everybody.

**Getting there.** Three hours by crossing frame. Greenhold's **biosecurity deck** quarantines every arriving bay for an hour: no
live seed, no soil, no insects in, unless the slip asks for them. Frass is accepted only heat-treated and sealed.

**Slips.** Green-edged forms with a licence number and a **Trait Declaration** box: "I declare that no goods in this consignment
contain Ostrander genetics except under licence." Signing it falsely is a breach of the Agreement, which the Auditors enforce.

**In play:**
- **Frass as a product.** The insect bin already produces insects; give it a second output, **frass**, which piles up in the bin's
  slot and must be emptied. Frass has three uses: grow-bed fertiliser (more yield), frass poultice for Flats clients, and
  Greenhold sacks (heat-treated in a drying rack first). A waste product with three buyers is exactly an interweaving.
- **Violet seconds** as cheap feed for the insect bin, bought from Greenhold by the crate, or washed (a vessel of water and hours)
  to fade the dye and sold as Real by a player willing to commit grade fraud (04).
- **The Trait Audit** as an event: an Auditor comes to the counter with a swab. If the player grows from saved Greenhold seed, the
  Auditor files a royalty claim: a fine, or a contract to sell the next harvest to Greenhold at Greenhold's price.
- **Heirloom deals**: a Greenhold buyer offers 300 cr for a packet of seed savers' heirloom seed. The seed savers will know.

**Hook:** silo eleven has been dark for two years. Field Security says it is fallow. Growers from the stars next to it say they
hear pumps.

### The Hulk

**What it is.** The **Calder Civic Tower**, the council's offices and flats, thirty-four floors built in Y30 on cheaper piles over
Low Calder. It tilted six degrees in the storm and lost its northern third in Y58, when that side slid off its piles into the
mud. What stands is a leaning wedge with one face torn open, every floor showing its rooms to the north like a doll's house.
That face is **the Face**. The rest of the bay calls the whole building the Hulk.

**Who runs it.** Nobody, by law: on paper it belongs to the Calder Recovery Authority, the town council that dissolved in Y59, and Halden Civil
holds a demolition option it will not pay to use. In practice about three thousand **Hulkers** live in it in **crews**, one or two
floors to a crew, and the nearest thing to authority is the **Plumb**: a lead weight on a long wire hung down the central
stairwell from the top floor, with a chalked target on the floor of the bottom dry floor. The **Plumb-keeper** (currently **Mattias
Drury**, the son of the council's last surveyor) reads it every morning. The lean is now six degrees and a little more than a third.
When the bob crosses the chalk line marked **GO**, everyone leaves. Nobody knows what happens then; nobody has had to find out.

**How it lives.**
- **Eating the Hulk.** Hulkers strip the building for steel, copper, glass and fittings, carefully, from parts the Plumb-keeper says
  carry no load. Sometimes he is wrong.
- **The gullery.** Thousands of gulls nest on the Face. In the Fourth and Fifth Months, Hulk children rope down the Face to take
  eggs. **Hulk eggs** go to the Flats, to the Mills, and (washed, boxed in straw, labelled "coastal wild eggs") to the Crown and
  the Pier. **Guano** and **starling droppings** go to Greenhold by the sack. **Gull down** is sold for packing and insulation.
- **The Registry.** On floor 12, in the old Town Clerk's office, the **Registrars**, a crew of former council clerks' children, keep
  the **Calder Registry**: the land register, the births, marriages and deaths, the rates books, the planning files. It survived
  because floor 12 was above the water. Every Esplanade family's claim to a drowned lot runs through it; so does every name the
  Tally adds. The Registrars sell certified copies, stamped with the old borough seal.
- **The Lamplighters.** Some Hulkers are wreckers: they set **false lights**, spoofed landing beacons on the Hulk's roof, that draw
  lost or cheap drones down into nets strung across the Face. A netted drone is unpacked on the spot.
- **The Rail.** A long balcony rail on floor 20 where netted and fished-up drones are hung for sale. The Lamplighters sell
  their catch here, openly, including to the owners: this is the **buy-back**.
- **The Hulk Anchor.** The eastern end of the Lines (section 7) is made fast to the Hulk's core, and the Hulkers take the toll.

**What it wants.** Water, food, cells, rope, medicine; and above all **screw jacks**, shoring props and steel plate, to brace floors
that are starting to sag, and anyone who can read a structure.

**What it offers.** Hulk eggs, guano, gull down, stripped metal, certified Registry copies, a place to buy back your lost drone,
and the cheapest landing in the bay (a flat roof with no clearance and no questions).

**Getting there.** One hour by crossing frame, the first outside route a player is likely to fly. No customs. The risk is the
Lamplighters: a drone that flies to the Hulk without a Hulk crew's **lantern code** (a light pattern the drone's controller is set to
answer) may be guided to the wrong roof.

**Slips.** On **council letterhead**: CALDER BOROUGH COUNCIL, OFFICE OF THE TOWN CLERK. The Registry found thirty years of blank
headed paper in a store room, and the Hulk writes everything on it: slips, receipts, love letters.

**In play:**
- The Hulk is the **first outside destination**: one hour, low pay, low requirements, the place where a player learns that outside
  routes have their own risks.
- **Hulk egg season** (Fourth and Fifth Months) as an event: eggs cheap and plentiful, a Crown contract for "coastal wild eggs"
  appears, and the player who can get them up the tower fast (they spoil) makes good money.
- **Buy-back**: when a drone is lost on an outside route, it may appear on the Rail a day later, with some of its cargo. The player
  can buy it back (a fee) or let it go. A lost drone is not always gone.
- **Registry copies** as a document item: the Tally wants death entries; penthouse fixers want lot deeds; the Archivists want the
  rates books. A **certified copy** is a 1x1 paper item with a borough seal, and forgeries exist.
- **Jacks** as a contract: a Hulk crew pays in eggs and down for screw jacks and steel plate, and the slip comes with a note about
  how much the floor has dropped since last month.

**Hook:** the Plumb has moved faster this year than in the last five together. Drury says it is the Turn's tides scouring the
north piles. Some Hulkers say Halden Civil has started to exercise its demolition option, one pile at a time, from underneath.

### Bastion

**What it is.** The provincial prison on the bay island, nine kilometres west: a granite fort from the age of sail, with a
concrete cell block of eight floors built inside its walls in the province's last prison-building year. Cut off for nineteen days
in Y57; nobody came (01). Since Y60 it has been run under contract by **Calder Correctional Services** (CCS), a private operator, and
since Y64 it has held a **Labour Transfer Agreement** with Halden (05). Inside the old fort's magazine and drill hall, **the Line**
makes cells and chips: the things Halden Works used to make.

**Who runs it.** A **Governor** appointed by CCS (currently **Governor Ruth Abernathy-Oake**), and the officers, who live with their
families in the old fort's **Gatehouse** and are, after the inmates, the poorest-paid people on the island.

**How it lives.** About five thousand inmates, and about fifteen hundred **transferees**: work-off debtors from the Stack and other
towers who "elected" transfer (05). In law, transferees are not prisoners but "residents on a labour contract". They sleep in the
same cells and work the same Line. Everyone on the island is paid in **Bastion marks**, which spend only at the **Tuck** (the
commissary). The Tuck is supplied by Orrin under contract, at prices that would embarrass the Stack's company store.

The Line runs three shifts. Its output leaves on the Thursday boat (the Company calls it ***Halden Lighter 6***; everyone calls it the
boat) to the Nine Dock, in crates marked only with lot numbers. Nobody official says that H-cells are made on Bastion (04). Every
returnee says so.

**What it wants from the Stack.**
- **Tuck goods** (officially through Orrin): protein blocks, kof, soap, sweet rations, writing paper.
- **Scrap**: cobalt, nickel and copper for the Line. Flats salvage, through the Landing, through Halden, to Bastion: the drowned
  plant's metal goes back into cells.
- **Care parcels** from families: a box per inmate per month, brown paper, an inmate number, a list of allowed contents.
- **Contraband**, via the Lee: radios, real tobacco-substitute, gin, cracked cells, pills, letters that skip the censor.

**What it offers.** **Bastion seconds**: cells that failed the Line's QA, sold off the island by officers at a quarter of store
price, half of them good. Sewn workwear. Hand-assembled drone controllers ("Bastion-built", cheap, honest, unremarkable).
Labour. And returnees, who bring Bastion tricks for making and mending cells (05).

**Getting there.** Five hours by crossing frame, out past the Blue Line. Landing only at the **Gatehouse pad**, on a CCS
procurement slip or a family parcel slip. Everything is searched. A drone that lands without a slip is impounded.

**Slips.** CCS Procurement forms in triplicate, blue, yellow and white, with a lot number. Family slips are brown paper with an
inmate number and a mother's handwriting.

**In play:**
- **Care parcels** are the human contract of the game: a client at the counter (a mother, a sister) asks you to pack a parcel for
  someone on Bastion. The allowed list is printed on the slip. The pay is small. The reward is a letter back. The choice is what
  else goes in the box, and whether to say yes when the client asks for it to.
- **Bastion seconds** as a cell source: cheap cells, each with a hidden **good / dud** feature (the analyser shows which).
- **The boat** every Thursday from the Nine Dock (05): debtors leave on it. A player whose arrears ladder runs out can be
  offered the transfer box on a work-off contract. In the endings (13) this is the worst way out, and a real one.

**Hook:** the Line's machines (05). One returnee, **Cosmo Fairweather**, fifty-two, now sweeping on floor 13, worked the Line for
four years and swears that the machine he ran had a brass plate on its side reading **HALDEN WORKS, LINE TWO, Y3**, and that the
plate had been painted over and the paint had chipped.

### The Pleasure Pier

**What it is.** Calder's old ferry pier at the Knuckle's foot, closed in Y47, its end lost in the Drowning (01), rebuilt between
Y64 and Y70 on new piles and extended a full kilometre into the bay by **the Pier Trust**: a consortium of Halden, Corvane, the
St. Ober's trustees and a provincial leisure firm, **Brightwater Leisure**, which runs it. A white deck of pavilions, baths,
restaurants and lights, strung out over the water on iron legs, with flags. The rich of every tower come here: it is the one
place in the bay where Halden and Corvane directors drink at the same bar.

**Who runs it.** The **Steward of the Pier**, Brightwater's manager, and the **Pier Stewards**, security in white jackets. The Pier
keeps one rule above all, **Pier Neutrality**: no company's law applies on its deck, no recordings are made except the Trust's own,
and disputes are settled by the Steward. Debts run up at the Pier's tables are passed to Brisk & Lowe (05), who collect them in the
guest's own tower.

**What is on it,** from the land end out:
- **The Sweet Baths.** Bathhouses of fresh water: desalinated, heated, scented. Bathing in fresh water is the bay's most
  expensive pleasure. The bath water is changed once a day and the old water is sold, by the drum, to the Pier's staff.
- **The Old Calder Room.** A bar decorated entirely with salvage from the drowned town: street signs, a Saltmarket fish stall's
  enamel price board, tide-stained photographs of streets that are under the Flats, a Wellside well licence framed above the till.
  Cocktails are named after drowned streets. The Tide Folk know about it.
- **The Lens.** A theatre that shows edited HPS body lens footage (05) from every band of the Stack, set to music, to
  audiences who laugh. Its most popular programme is called *Slack Hour*.
- **The Course.** A racing loop for drones around buoys off the Pier's end: **the Drone Derby**, each week of the Still. Crown
  families own racing frames; Stack shaft rats fly them for a fee and a share; spectators bet.
- **The Underdeck.** A barge moored under the deck where the Pier's staff (mostly from the Stack's Middle and Mills) sleep in
  shifts, in the dark, under the music.

**What it wants from the Stack.** Real luxury goods: real-grade produce, eggs, herbs, flowers; real wood and natural fibre; ice;
Calder antiques with **pre-Drowning provenance** (the Old Calder Room); "coastal wild eggs" from the Hulk; **Flats bacon**, which is on
the menu as *smoked bay eel, foraged*; Teapot rain for its **rain tasting** flight; performers; racing drones and the people to fly
them. And staff.

**What it offers.** The highest prices for luxury in the bay, a **Pier Supplier** listing (which raises standing with the Crown),
and its waste: the Pier's garbage lighter dumps on the Grey Field's edge every Sunday, and the Flats are there first. Half a meal
left on a Pier plate is a good meal on a raft.

**Getting there.** Two hours by crossing frame; or the Saturday **Pier Excursion** boat from the Nine Dock for Middle tenants
with a ticket (25 cr return, standing room); the Crown comes by its own craft from the Crown Pad (02).

**Slips.** Heavy cream card, gold edge, no names: "A guest requests..." Deadlines are given as times of service: "by sundown
service", "for the late table". Goods must arrive in **unmarked packaging**: no brand, no grade stamp, no shop mark.

**In play:**
- **Pier contracts** pay the most for real-grade luxury and demand the most presentation: no marks on the packaging is a packing
  rule (labels and stamps must be removed or covered: a cloth wrap item).
- **The Drone Derby** as a weekly event in the Still: enter a drone (a light, fast frame), pay a fee, and earn a purse by placing.
  A Derby win raises Crown standing. A Derby crash is a lost drone (on the Rail at the Hulk, by Monday).
- **The Old Calder Room** as a buyer of salvage with a story, and a moral: kin-house goods sell here best of all.
- **Pier waste** reaching the Flats is a Flats supplier event (cheap food, Pier linen, broken glassware).

**Hook:** the Old Calder Room's framed well licence above the till is real, Y48, and bears a Wellside address and a name that is
on the Tally. A Tide Folk berth wants it back. The Steward says it was bought in good faith.

### The Sounding

**What it is.** The old Calder Bay Meteorological Station, fifteen kilometres out in the bay, on twelve steel stilts: a platform
with a radar dome (**the Egg**), a mast, a tide gauge, living quarters, and a crew of about forty. Public until Y50, sold to a
private operator, **Meridian Atmospherics**, which renamed it the Sounding (01). It recorded the highest water level ever measured
in the bay on the night of the Drowning, and sold the figure.

**Who runs it.** Meridian's **Station Chief** (currently **Lowell Ibsen**) and the **Soundmen**, forecasters and technicians on
three-month rotations, who are paid well and go slowly strange.

**How it lives.**
- **Forecasts** by subscription (section 1).
- **Storm naming rights**, auctioned each season.
- **The Wire**: the Sounding's mast relays radio and data between the towers and the province. Every message between towers that
  does not go by drone or boat goes through the Egg, at so much a word.
- **Certified readings**: the Sounding's water levels, wind speeds and rain totals, stamped and signed, used in insurance claims
  and arbitration. A **Sounding Certificate** for a storm can decide whether a flood was "an unprecedented weather event" or not.
  Meridian sells certificates to whichever side asks first.
- **Radar logs.** The Egg sees every drone in the bay. Meridian sells the logs, by the week, to anyone who pays.

**What it wants.** Fresh food above everything (they are fifteen kilometres from a grow bed), real coffee, cells, founding-era
electronics for the Egg (it was built at the same time as the Stack and has the same problem), books and screens, and people who
can repair a magnetron.

**What it offers.** Forecasts as payment; certificates; the Wire; and the logs.

**Getting there.** Seven hours by crossing frame, out past the Blue Line in open sea: a long flight that usually means an overnight
turn. One pad. They are always glad to see a drone.

**Slips.** A strip of teleprinter tape glued to a card, stamped with the time to the second.

**In play:**
- **Forecast subscriptions** as a weekly cost and as a contract reward (a month of Premium for a crate of fresh greens).
- **Certificates** as documents: a Sounding Certificate for a storm can, once, make the insurance syndicate (06b) pay a claim, which
  is the most surprising thing in the game.
- **Radar logs** as a hook into poaching (below): whoever buys the logs knows where your drones fly.

**Hook:** the Rain Church buys rain forecasts and the Skywater Hall seems to know the Church's plans (03). The Sounding's Wire carries
both their messages. **Hook:** the Sounding's certified reading for the 19th of the Third Month, Y57, exists in two versions with
different water levels and the same signature.

### The Frame

**What it is.** **Esplanade Heights**, a luxury tower begun in Y53 by a provincial developer, **Lysander Property**, on the
bay front two kilometres south of the Stack. It was topped out at thirty-eight floors of bare steel and concrete cores when the
province's insurers withdrew cover for Calder in Y56 and the money stopped. It was never clad. It stands in the water as a skeleton:
floor slabs, columns, two concrete lift cores, and wind going through all of it. Its owners are bondholders in administration,
and have been for thirty years.

**Who runs it.** The **wind crews**, who call themselves **Framers**: about four hundred people who came out in the years after
the flood with salvaged turbines and sail and made the skeleton into a wind farm. They elect a **Foreman** each Turn (currently
**Bettany Roe**, a former Works electrician's granddaughter, who has been re-elected six times).

**How it lives.**
- **Wild charge.** Turbines hang in the open floors, dozens of them, small and large, salvaged, rebuilt, made from Works motors
  and Flats magnets. In a gale **the Frame sings**: a chord you can hear on the Stack's Seaward face. The crews charge cells, any
  cells, including End of Service cells that licensed chargers refuse (04), at a little over half the Stack's exchange price.
- **The Lines.** The Frame is the hub of the kite tethers (section 7). Its crews string the cables, run the travellers and take
  the toll at the Frame anchor.
- **Drifters.** The crews build the bay's wind-and-solar gliders (section 7) from sailcloth, solar film and salvaged spars.
- **The Open Deck.** A pad on the eighteenth floor where any drone may land without clearance, paperwork or toll, for a cell's
  worth of charge. The only free pad in the bay.
- **Dry lockers.** The concrete cores have rooms that stay dry; the crews rent them as storage, by the square, by the week.

**What it wants.** **Magnets** (high-pull Flats magnets for generators), **bearings**, copper wire, cable, rope, grease, sail, solar
film, cells to fill, and food and water, which the Frame cannot make at all.

**What it offers.** Charge, at a price Halden cannot match; a place to break a long crossing; the Lines; Drifters; dry storage
outside Halden's meters.

**Getting there.** One hour by crossing frame; a tether pod on the Lines (section 7); a punt from the Flats on the flood.

**Slips.** A tag of sailcloth, written in marker, tied with a turbine-blade washer. Payment is often in charge: "eight cells
filled".

**In play:**
- **Cell runs**: send a bay full of empty cells to the Frame and get them back charged. Cheaper than the socket, no meter, works
  on End of Service cells. Halden's answer is a bylaw: cells charged outside the Structure are **unmetered imports**, and a
  Company charger detects them (a "wild charge" flag) and bills the difference with a fine.
- **Magnets to the Frame**: the Flats-fished three-hundred sells for 200 here. A Flats-to-Frame magnet trade is a small, steady
  sideways chain.
- **Dry lockers** as an outside storage rental: squares the player leases outside the Stack, cheap and unmetered, a drone flight
  away. Space is the constraint; here is more of it, at the price of distance.

**Hook:** Lysander's bondholders have been quiet for thirty years. This year a Halden Civil surveyor was seen on the Frame's
fourth floor with a tape and a camera. The Framers think the Company is buying the bonds, to cut the Frame down and take the
wind.

### Coldharbour

**What it is.** The port's cold store, on the old fish quay beside Greenhold: a windowless six-storey block of insulated panels
on a concrete base, built ten years before the Works opened to freeze Calder's fish catch. It has an ammonia refrigeration plant
in its basement engine hall, provincial-era, rebuilt and rebuilt. Since Y59 it has been owned by **Orrin Provisions** under Halden
licence.

**Who runs it.** Orrin's **Chief Engineer** at Coldharbour, **Silas Brand**, and three shifts of **freezer hands**: about three
hundred people who work in minus twenty-five in padded suits and sleep in the **Warm Rooms** above the engine hall, the only warm
rooms in the building, warmed by the compressors' waste heat.

**How it lives.**
- **Cold space**, rented by the pallet and the day. A cold square at Coldharbour costs more than a square in the Middle.
- **Ice**, made in blocks and sold to the towers (03: ice from above; much of it comes from here).
- **The Cold Exchange.** A board in the loading hall where Greenhold produce, province meat and fish, plasma in transit to the
  Spire and every tower's cold goods are listed and bought. The prices chalked on it each morning are the bay's real food prices.
- **Province meat and fish**, brought by sea from outside the bay and stored for the Crowns.

**What it wants.** Compressor parts, bearings, seals, insulation, ammonia, gull down (for the suits and the panel gaps), labour,
and cheap protein for the Warm Rooms.

**What it offers.** Cold storage, ice, province goods, and the price board.

**Getting there.** Three hours by crossing frame; Coldharbour's **chilled bay** takes cold-lined drones only.

**Slips.** Orrin's standard slips, printed in blue, with a pallet number and a temperature.

**In play:**
- **Cold lockers**: rent cold squares at Coldharbour to hold produce until prices rise (a speculative play: buy in the Turn, hold,
  sell in the Blows), at a daily cost per square.
- **The Cold Exchange board** as a table in the world (IDEAS: a price board): a slip from Coldharbour each morning (with a
  subscription) shows the bay's prices for real produce, which tells the player which contracts to chase.
- **Ice** as a packing material for cold goods without a cold liner: ice blocks fill gaps, keep a bay cool for a few hours,
  and melt into water (which wets everything else in the bay). An item that changes state on a timer.

**Hook:** **Bay 9**, on the top floor, is listed on the Cold Exchange board as "reserved, long term" and has been since Y58. Its
door has a Halden lock, not an Orrin one. The compressor that serves it never cycles off.

### The Lee

**What it is.** Moorings on the sheltered eastern side of Bastion's island, out of the south-westerlies: about two hundred hulls
(barges, old trawlers, the upper deck of a sunken car ferry, pontoons, rafts) lashed side to side and end to end in a floating
town of about twelve hundred people. Every hull is tied to its neighbours with a **painter**, a mooring rope, and the Lee's one law
is about it.

**Who runs it.** **The Muster**: a meeting of every hull's master, called by a bell. Its law is **the painter**: cut a painter and you
are cast off, hull and all, to drift. Everything else is negotiable.

**How it lives.** Smuggling, mostly: between the towers (around every customs deck in the bay), into Bastion (through the
officers), out of Bastion (Bastion seconds and Line output that the lot numbers do not count). The **Lost and Found**, run by
**Mother Kell** on the ferry deck, buys poached drones and cargo from every poacher in the bay and sells them back to their owners,
or to anyone. Hull repair, for every boat in the bay. **Outer fish**: in the Turn, the Lee's boats go out past the Blue Line for the
mackerel run, the only fish in the bay worth eating, and sell it to the Pier and the Crown at the price of meat.

**What it wants.** Fuel cells, rope, sealant, food, water, and goods to move. The Lee does not make; it moves.

**What it offers.** Passage: a way to get anything anywhere in the bay without a customs deck, for a fee and a risk. And buy-back.

**Getting there.** Four hours by crossing frame, or by Lee skiff from the Flats at night (not something the player does; something
the player's goods can do).

**Slips.** None. A Lee contract arrives by word: a Flats client or a Sump fixer says it at the counter, and the player writes it
down or doesn't.

**In play:** the Lee is the **smuggling route**. At launch, a third route option for inter-tower contracts beside the licensed
crossing and the plain crossing: **through the Lee**. No Egress Duty, no customs at the far end, a Lee fee (a fifth of the pay),
two extra hours, and a roll: delivered, delayed, or "lost" (and for sale at the Lost and Found next week). Contraband goes this way
or not at all.

**Hook:** the Lee's Muster bell has WELLSIDE MISSION cast into its lip, and a Lee master swears his grandfather cut it down from
the chapel spire in the first winter after the flood. The chapel's spire, seen from the Stack, still has a bell hanging in it. The
Mission had one bell.

### Ferrybridge and the Dry Line

Not a tower: the answer to "why not leave". **Ferrybridge**, a day's barge up the Calder river, was the provincial reception camp
in Y57 (01). It is still there: rows of prefab cabins on a gravel flat by the river locks, a provincial labour exchange, a clinic
two days a week, about eleven thousand people. The province calls it a **Resettlement Area**. Nobody in it has a job that pays
rent anywhere else.

Beyond Ferrybridge is **the Dry Line**: the provincial residency checkpoint at the upper locks. Inland residency needs an
employer's sponsorship and a **water allocation**, and inland water allocations are held by farm cooperatives that do not take new
members. A **Bay-registered** person (anyone whose last address is a Calder tower or the Flats) can cross the Dry Line for three days
on a visitor's pass and must come back. Leaving the bay is possible. Arriving anywhere is not.

The **Dry Barge** goes up the river once a week from the Nine Dock and comes back two days later. People who take it come back on it
more often than they don't.

**In play:** a **Ferrybridge ticket** (12 cr) is an item the player can buy and never needs. A client who saves for one is a story
across several visits to the counter. An ending (13) can be the Dry Barge, and the ending text is the Dry Line.

## 7. Crossing the bay

Inside the Stack a drone flies in a shaft, out of the wind, between walls the Company owns. Outside it flies over water nobody can
drink, in wind off the open sea, past people who make a living catching it. A frame that does well in the shafts fails outside.
A cell that lasts a day in the Mills is flat halfway to Vantage. And the Company charges for the door.

### The door: leaving the Stack legally

All licensed traffic between the Stack and the other towers goes through **Halden Bay Freight** (HBF), a Company subsidiary, at the
**Shelf Port** on floor 30: a fenced corner of the Shelf (02) with eight pads, a scale, a scanner, a customs bench and a waiting
room with a view the Middle pays to sit in.

- **Crossing Permit**: 120 cr a month; needs Standing 700 (04) and a registered crossing frame.
- **Egress Duty**: 8% of the declared value of goods leaving the Stack, paid at the bench.
- **Landing Duty**: 12% of the assessed value of goods arriving. This includes the **reward bay** a drone brings home from another
  tower. The Company taxes what you were paid.
- **Skin clearance** (02) is included in the Crossing Permit for flights from the Shelf Port, and only from there.
- The Shelf Port closes when the Sounding's wind reading passes force eight (**Port closed**, chalked on the board, which in the
  Blows is up for days at a time).

Illegal ways out:
- **Window launch**: flying out of a cut window on the Landward face, unlicensed. Bylaw-wise it is "unregistered egress", a
  standards charge if caught (05), and the drone has no skin clearance, so HPS's Crown Detail treats it as a threat if it strays
  near the Quiet Glass (02). Most Mills crossers do it anyway, at dawn, from the Landward face, where nobody important looks.
- **The Bodge** (below): the Lines, from a Mills window.
- **The Landing**: hand goods to a boat at floor 4 (Flats punt, Lee skiff, the blood boat for a fee).

**In play:** inter-tower contracts can be flown **licensed** (permit, duties, safe paperwork, no customs trouble at the far end)
or **unlicensed** (window launch: no duties, a small chance of a fine, and the far tower's customs may refuse goods without HBF
seals). The duties are the price of sleeping well. The difference is large enough to matter and small enough that both are
reasonable.

### Cell-and-frame crossers

A crossing frame differs from a shaft frame in three ways: a **sealed hull** (salt spray kills open electronics in one crossing),
a **stable airframe** (shaft frames are built to hover in still air; outside they are thrown about), and a **cell cradle**, a
separate slot in the frame that holds the cells it flies on. Range is the number of cells in the cradle.

Charge, roughly, per hour of crossing flight in still air:
- **H-cell** (04): one cell per hour.
- **Corvane C-cell**: one cell per hour and a half.
- **Founding-era cell**: one cell per three hours.
- Into the wind: half again. In the cold (the Blows): half again. A heavy bay: half again.

| Frame | Bay | Cradle | Range (H-cells, still air) | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Strap-on** (shaft-rat frame with a cell harness and a taped hull) | odd, about 3x2 | 2x1 outside the bay | 2 h | 220 + 40 harness | flies to the Hulk or the Frame and, usually, back; leaks |
| **Halden Shearwater** | 4x3, sealed | 2x3 | 6 h | 3,600; Standing 700 | the Company's crossing frame; HBF-registered; reliable and dull |
| **Corvane Tern** | 3x3, sealed | 2x2, C-cells only | 6 h on C-cells | 2,800 at Vantage; grey in the Stack | light, fast (one hour less on every route); needs a **guest transponder** (5 cr a day) at the Shelf Port, and CellCare |
| **Bastion-built** | 3x3, sealed | 2x2 | 4 h | 1,100 via the Lee | honest, ugly, heavy; a Line hand-me-down |
| **Wafer runner** (founding-era Works courier) | 2x2, sealed and armoured | built in, founding-era | all day | not sold | below |
| **Frame Drifter** | 5x3, open (lidded) | none | wind and sun | 900 at the Frame | section below |

**The wafer runner.** Halden Works flew finished chip wafers from the fab halls to the port warehouse in small founding-era
courier frames: armoured, sealed, with a built-in founding-era cell and a controller that could hold a course in a gale. A few
went down with the Gate Floor's quay stores in Y57. Plant divers have brought up three in twenty-nine years. Each was dead; each
was woken by the Old Hands, for a price and a favour; each is now worth more than a Crown lease's first year. A wafer runner's
bay is tiny (2x2). It crosses the bay in any weather a frame can fly in, on its own cell, without a cradle. It is the drone the
whole bay wants and nobody can make.

**In play:**
- **The cradle is a second packing puzzle.** Cells take cradle squares; a long crossing needs more cells than the cradle holds,
  so extra cells ride in the bay, taking cargo squares. Range versus cargo, decided at the launch pad.
- **Charge at the far end.** Many tower slips include "**return charge**" (the destination charges your cells for the trip home)
  as part of the pay. Those that do not leave you to pay for charge there (Frame cheap, Vantage dear) or to carry it.
- **The Frame as a waystation.** Any crossing can be split at the Frame's Open Deck for a cell's worth of charge: one extra hour,
  full cradle.
- **The wafer runner** as a late-game reward: a sealed 2x2 item in a dive client's reward bay, dead, analysable. Waking it is an
  Old Hands contract chain. It changes what the player can do.

### Drifters: wind and sun

The slow way. A Drifter is a glider the Framers build from sailcloth, solar film (**sun-skin**) and salvaged spars: a long thin
wing over a big, light, lidded bay. It has a small motor for take-off and landing and a sun-skin that charges a single cell, and
for the rest it rides the wind, tacking like a boat. It costs nothing to fly and takes forever.

- **Bay**: 5x3, lidded, unsealed. Bulk, not fragile.
- **Time**: one to three days for a crossing, by wind. With the draw in the morning and the push in the afternoon, a Drifter can
  reach the Frame and back in a day; Vantage takes two days out and one back in the south-westerlies.
- **Weather**: becalmed in the Still (it sits on the water and waits); destroyed in a storm; best in the Turn.
- **Risk**: slow is visible. Drifters are the poachers' favourite. A Drifter's cargo is usually bulk nobody wants to poach (frass,
  scrap, bricks), which is the point.

**In play:** the Drifter is the "painfully slow" alternative the canon asks for. It unlocks inter-tower trade without strong
cells: bulk contracts with long deadlines (frass to Greenhold, scrap to Bastion, bricks to the Lee), flown when the forecast
allows. A Sounding subscription matters most to a Drifter player.

### The Lines: kite tethers and their tolls

After Y60, the Framers began stringing cables between the towers along the shore: steel-cored fibre lines hung in long curves
from anchor to anchor, high enough to clear masts. Cargo rides them in **pods** (sealed canisters, a 3x2 bay) clipped to a
**traveller**, a pulley carriage. Above each traveller a **kite** (a parafoil on its own short line) lifts and pulls it along the
cable when the wind is on the right side. In a calm, pods hang where they are (**hung**) until it blows. **Line walkers** go out
hand over hand to free stuck travellers and splice frayed cable.

Each anchor is held by whoever holds the building it is tied to, and each takes a toll per pod passing, **the pull**:

| Line | Anchors | Length | Pod time | Tolls per pod |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Shore Line** | Hulk Anchor, the Frame, the Pier's mast, St. Ober's | about 6 km | 4 to 8 h | Hulk 2 cr; Frame 3 cr or a cell; Pier 5 cr; Spire 4 Care Credit |
| **The Cold Line** | the Frame, Greenhold, Coldharbour | about 4 km | 3 to 6 h | Frame 3 cr; Greenhold 3 cr and a biosecurity seal; Coldharbour 3 cr |
| **The Bodge** | a cut window on the Landward face of floor 21 to the Hulk Anchor | 1.5 km | 1 to 2 h | 1 cr to the skinners who run it |
| **The Drowned Line** | (was) the Frame to the Lee | — | — | snapped in storm Fenwick, Y85; lies on the seabed; magnet-fished for cable |

Halden refuses to let the Lines touch the Stack: an anchor on the facade would be an "unregistered attachment to the Structure".
So the licensed Lines stop at the Hulk and the Frame, and goods for the Stack go the last kilometre by drone or by boat. The
**Bodge** is the answer: an unlicensed line from a cut window on floor 21 to the Hulk, run by skinners (02), cut by Halden riggers
every few months, restrung within the week.

**In play:**
- **Pods** are a third way to cross: cheap, slow, wind-dependent, with fixed routes and stacking tolls. A pod is a container item
  (3x2 sealed) packed like a bay and sent along a Line; it does not come back by itself (send it back with return goods or buy
  another).
- **The Bodge** lets a Mills player reach the Lines without a Crossing Permit or a crossing frame, early. Every few weeks an event
  cuts it (Halden riggers), and pods in transit hang at the cut end until it is restrung.
- **Hung pods** are a weather event: a calm leaves pods hanging over the water for hours or a day, and poachers know.

Not everything flies. The bay's scheduled boats carry freight by the crate, at any size a crate can be, which no drone bay can.

| **The blood boat** | the Flats' berths | slack days | plasma buyers | plasma, cold |
| **The Outreach Launch** | the Flats | monthly | the Foundation | vouchers (06a) |
| **Compliance cutters** | the Corvane Safety Zone | always | Compliance | what they confiscate |
| **The Sounding tender** | Nine Dock to the Sounding | fortnightly | relieving Soundmen | food, cells |
| **Lee skiffs** | anywhere | nights, the Murk | whoever pays | whatever pays |

**In play:** **crate freight** on a scheduled boat is the bulk option: book a crate on the green boat or the boat, pack it (a big
container, 6x4), and it goes on the boat's day. No range, no poachers to speak of, HBF duties apply, and the schedule decides.
A contract with a long deadline and a large order (forty kilos of frass to Greenhold) is a crate job, not a drone job.

### Smugglers

Every customs deck in the bay has a way around it, and the people who know the ways are the Lee, the shaft rats who fly outside,
and the Flats. The techniques:

- **Inside honest cargo.** A gin bottle inside a protein-block crate; pills in a cell casing; letters in the lining of a care
  parcel. The commonest, and the one the packing grid already models.
- **Ghost frames.** A frame with no transponder, flown low over the water at dawn or in the Murk, below the Egg's best angle.
- **Wrack rafts.** Goods sealed in drums, wrapped in wrack, and floated out from the Flats on the ebb to be picked up by a Lee
  skiff in the channel. Slow, cheap, invisible, and sometimes found by the wrong punt.
- **Bought decks.** A Receiving Deck officer at Vantage or a Gatehouse officer at Bastion paid to look elsewhere. Expensive and
  reliable until it is not.
- **The Murk.** Fog season is smuggling season. Poachers and Compliance alike fly blind.

What moves: gin and stinker spirits to Vantage; cracked cells, radios and pills to Bastion; Bastion seconds and Line output out of
Bastion; Spire generics into the Stack; Teapot rain everywhere; violet seconds washed into Real; founding-era parts without marks to
Vantage's back door; documents (the Wall Book, the Pell Report) to whoever will pay.

### Poachers

The bay's poachers do not fight drones. They catch them.

- **Lamplighters** (the Hulk): false landing beacons that draw drones without a lantern code onto the Hulk's nets.
- **Netters**: fine nets flown on kites across the flight lines near the Frame and over the Haul, where drones come low. A netted
  drone drops into the mud and is picked up at low water.
- **Dunkers**: strays with a jammer in a punt. The jammer makes a cheap controller lose its course; the drone ditches in the water;
  the dunker magnet-fishes it up. Drones with founding-era controllers ignore jammers.
- **Line-hookers**: a grapnel thrown from a boat over a hung pod on the Lines. They take the pod.
- **Compliance** (Vantage): "inspection" in the Safety Zone. Legal. What fails inspection is kept.
- **Egress assessment** (Halden): at the Shelf Port, goods with doubtful paperwork are "detained for assessment" and returned weeks
  later, short.

Where poached goods go: the Rail at the Hulk, the Lost and Found on the Lee, the Landing (via Vass, if it came up from the mud). A
lost drone is usually for sale somewhere within a week.

**In play:** each outside route has a **trouble roll** on landing, like the free shafts' (IDEAS: routes), with its own table:

| Route | Common outcome | Rare outcome | What lowers the risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| To the Hulk | one item taken (Lamplighters) | drone netted, on the Rail tomorrow | a lantern code (bought from a Hulk crew) |
| Over the Haul / near the Frame | none | drone netted into the mud, sold at the Landing | flying high (more charge) |
| Along the Lines (pods) | hung for hours | pod hooked | not sending in a calm |
| Open water (Vantage, Lee, Bastion) | delay | dunked; on the Lost and Found | founding-era controller; the Murk; a convoy of two |
| Vantage's Safety Zone | inspection, one Recovered item kept | drone held for a fee | a filed route; HBF seals |
| Out of the Shelf Port | none | goods detained for assessment | clean paperwork |

Two more layers sit on top:
- **The radar logs**: the Sounding sells them. A player whose drones fly the same route on the same days is easier to catch. Meridian
  will, for 30 cr a week, leave a frame out of the logs it sells (a **log gap**). Nobody knows if they keep that promise.
- **The insurance syndicate** (06b) excludes "acts of the bay": weather, wildlife, unlicensed interference and "the conduct of
  third parties in unregulated waters". That is everything that happens outside.

### Storms and the crossing

On a storm day every outside route closes: HBF's Shelf Port posts Port closed, the Lines' crews bring the kites in, and the boats
stay at their docks. A drone already out when a storm arrives without warning (no Sounding subscription, or a storm the Sounding
got wrong) rolls to **ditch**: it comes down in the water and is lost. Ditched drones and their cargo wash up over the next days on
the Flats' wrack line, where the Flats find them, and Vass sells them at the Landing.

**In play:** a storm is the single biggest outside event. Before it: prices for filters, sealant and cells rise, Flats and Hulk
clients send panic contracts. During it: no crossings, Hanging Boxes stripped, the Landing awash. After it: the bay goes brown,
filters clog, plant dives stop for a week, and then the Flats boom: storm salvage floods the Landing and the Great Ebb market,
and lost drones come home through Vass.

### Gulls and the crossing

- **The gull tax** (section 1): an open bay loses a food item outside.
- **Mobbing**: in the Fourth and Fifth Months, gulls nesting on the Hulk's Face attack anything that flies near the nests. A drone
  flying to the Hulk in egg season loses an hour or, now and then, a rotor.
- **Crown gulls** sit on the Crown Pad and the Mast. The Crown's own craft carry a recorded hawk cry. It does not work.

## 8. Trade between towers: the late-game unlock

### What it takes

Inter-tower trade is not one switch. It is four keys, and a player can turn them in any order:

1. **A frame that survives the crossing**: a strap-on (barely), a Shearwater, a Tern, a Bastion-built, a Drifter, or the luck of a
   wafer runner. Or no frame at all: pods on the Lines, crates on the boats.
2. **Charge enough for the distance**: cells in the cradle and the bay; founding-era cells, which change everything; the Frame's
   waystation; return charge on the slip.
3. **A way out**: the Crossing Permit at the Shelf Port (Standing 700 and 120 cr a month), or a window launch, or the Bodge, or the
   Landing and a boat, or the Lee.
4. **A way in**: each tower lets in only what it has asked for. A tower's contracts appear on the board only after a first
   contact (a slip from that tower in a reward bay, a client from that tower at the counter, a standing threshold with a faction
   that deals with it: the Tide Folk for the Lee, the Old Hands for Vantage, the plasma buyers for the Spire).

A natural ladder, in the order a Mills player would meet it:

| Stage | Reach | How | What it pays in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | the Landing and the Flats | shaft drones down; crates on Vass's hoist | wet salvage |
| 1 | the Hulk and the Frame | strap-on frames; window launch; the Bodge | eggs, down, charge, buy-back |
| 2 | the shore towers (Pier, Spire, Greenhold, Coldharbour) | pods on the Lines; Drifters; crates on the green boat | credits, Care Credit, seed, cold space |
| 3 | the shore towers by drone | a crossing frame and a permit (or a habit of window launches) | credits; standing up the Stack |
| 4 | open water: Vantage, the Lee, Bastion | a sealed frame, a full cradle, a route through or around customs | the best pay in the game; contraband |
| 5 | the Sounding | a long flight, an overnight turn | forecasts, certificates, logs |
