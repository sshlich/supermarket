# 13. The player and the endings

*Speculative. Rocks and soil. Plain text is the main version; **Alternative:** is another version of the same thing;
**Hook:** is a story seed; **In play:** is how it surfaces in the game. Names, numbers, floors and dates follow
`00-canon-brief.md`. Money numbers follow `04-economy.md` (a Mills labourer earns about 30 cr a day; a Vitabrick is 3 cr;
a square on 17 is 5 cr a week; the starting lease is 24 squares). Faction standing uses the five steps from `06a`
(Hostile, Wary, Known, Trusted, Kin).*

This file is about you: who you were before the stores cage on 17, what you were handed on the first morning, how you
climb (or don't), what the tower asks you to do along the way, and the ways the story can stop. It leans on every other
file and tries not to repeat them; where a faction's own ending is already written in `06a` or `06b`, this file says how
the player gets there and what it costs, and points back.

---

## Part one: who you were

### How a past works

Nobody arrives on 17 from nowhere. Halden's Lease Office will not sign a Proprietor Pathway with a stranger; the form has
a box headed **PRIOR REGISTRATION** and the box must be filled. What is written in it is your past.

A past gives you five things, always the same five, so the choice reads cleanly:

1. **A skill.** One rule of the game bent slightly in your favour. Never a stat bonus in the abstract: always a thing you
   can do with an item or a machine that other people cannot, or can only do with a tool.
2. **Contacts.** One or two people or groups who start at *Known* instead of *Wary*, and who send **bay slips** (addressed
   contracts that never touch the board, file 11) from the first week.
3. **A debt.** A second line on your books besides the Pathway. Every past has one. Some are money, some are goods, some are
   a promise, one is a percentage. Debt in the Stack follows people (file 05: "the debt remains"), so it came with you.
4. **An enemy.** Not someone who fights you. Someone who costs you: a toll that is higher for you, a client who will not
   take your slips, an inspector who looks at your lease first, a faction that starts at *Wary* or *Hostile*.
5. **A starting thing.** One item or machine in your **move-in crate**, beyond what the Pathway issues.

And a sixth, quieter number: your **starting Standing**. New tenants start at 300, tower-born at 250, Flats-born at 150
(file 04). A past that came from somewhere sets which.

**The move-in crate.** The Lift Guild carries "one crate per registered occupant, free of toll, on the day of first
registration" (a founding-era rule from when the Works moved six thousand workers in a month; the Guild keeps it because
the Company pays the Guild for it). The crate is a 4x3 container that arrives on Old Two at 08:00 on day one, sealed with
the Guild's paper band. What is in it is your past, physically: the starting thing, a few personal items that are also
goods, and the paper that proves who you were. Unpacking it onto the field is the first thing you do, and the first
packing problem you meet is where to put a life in a 24-square cage that already has three machines in it.

**In play:** the past is chosen on a single screen styled as the PRIOR REGISTRATION box of the Pathway form, with the
fourteen pasts below as tick-boxes in a clerk's typeface. Ticking one fills the crate. Nothing about the past is hidden from
the player; some of it is hidden from the floor (see "Who knows" at the end of this part).

**Alternative: the sealed past.** The player does not choose. The crate arrives and the PRIOR REGISTRATION box is a "?",
the same "?" the analyser idea (IDEAS) puts on unexamined relics. The past is revealed one feature at a time over the first
two weeks, as the contents of the crate are used and as people on 17 recognise you ("You're Cas Lenk's girl." / "I know
that tag gun."). The debt is the last thing revealed, on the first rent day. This is crueller and more atmospheric; it
costs the player the chance to plan around their past.

**Alternative: the past from the first week.** No past at the start; a plain crate. During the first seven days the
choices you make (who you take slips from, what you sell, whom you help) fill the PRIOR REGISTRATION box retroactively:
the Lease Office "completes your file" on the first rent day, and a past is assigned that fits what you did, with its
debt attached. Funny and bleak: the Company decides who you were from how you behaved.

**Alternative: no past.** The box says "TRANSFER, INTERNAL" and nothing else. The game starts plain; pasts come back as
unlockable starts for later runs.

### The fourteen pasts

#### 1. The Heir

**Who you were.** You grew up partly in this cage. Your uncle, **Casimir "Cas" Lenk**, held the Line Four stores cage for
twenty-two years as a repairer of small electronics, and you were registered on his lease as an occupant from the age of
nine to fifteen, sleeping on the mezzanine over the old issuing counter. You left for work on another floor; you came back
when the Lease Office wrote to say the cage was "vacated without notice".

Cas left one night a month before the game starts. His tally was found in the catch hatch at the back of the cage the next
morning, which is the Mills way of saying *don't look for me*. His Pathway was 61% paid. Halden does not transfer equity to
relatives: under the Pathway's lapse terms everything Cas paid was "applied to rent and services", and the cage went back on
the market. You signed a fresh Pathway on the same 24 squares. The brochure congratulated you on "continuing a family
tradition of enterprise".

**Skill: the Book.** Cas's ledger, a 1x2 cloth-bound book wedged behind the issuing counter, lists his clients by floor and
by what they sent him. Read it and six of them become bay-slip clients from week one: a lamp shop on 33 that sends dead
drivers, a Terraces dentist on 64 whose suction pump fails every six weeks, Mattias Orme's overflow, two hot-bunk keepers who
want fans rewound, and one entry that is only a floor number, **21**, and the words *call plate*.

**Contacts.** Mattias Orme (Cas's oldest friend on Litho Run) starts at *Trusted*, not *Known*, and sends repair work from
day two. Hester Moyle knew Cas and starts your warden's regard one pip up.

**Debt.** Cas owed Brisk & Lowe 340 cr on a parts account. You were a Registered Occupant on his lease, and under Clause 77
(file 05) the debt reached you. A Brisk & Lowe pair visits on day 9 with cream cards and polite faces.

**Enemy.** A man in a clean coat who comes to the lift lobby on the first of every month and asks the warden whether Cas
Lenk has come back. Hester calls him **Mr Quarrie** because that is the name on the card he leaves, and the card has no
floor on it. He does not threaten anyone. Each visit, one of your bay-slip clients from the Book stops sending.

**Starting thing.** Cas's **bench loupe** (a first-fit Works magnifier, 1x1, grade F), and, under the mezzanine boards, a
roll of Works requisition stock with three blank forms on it.

**Starting Standing.** 300.

**In play:** the Heir is the story start. The **requisitions** (file 02, "The requisitions") begin in week one instead of
week four, because the Clean Core's procurement never stopped writing to STORES 17-C and Cas had been answering. The Book is
a ready-made client list, which makes the early game gentler, and Mr Quarrie slowly takes it away again, which makes the
middle harder.

**Hook:** Cas's last entry in the Book, dated the night he left: *STORES 17-C. 1 EA delivered. Paid in Y40 scrip. Don't.*
The word *Don't* is underlined twice. Under the cage's floor is the sealed hatch of the old stores hoist (file 02, floor 16).
Cas's bench is screwed down over it, which Clause 11 forbids, which means he wanted it held shut more than he wanted his
bench.

#### 2. Whistle-born

**Who you were.** Mills-born, on the Winding Floor (15), in a hot-bunk your parents rented by the shift. Your grandmother,
**Ilse Varro**, Clock 0264, worked Line Four from Y19 to the Last Shift, and her glove is on the Glove Wall on Glove Run, third
row, left hand, grey. You grew up on her stories of the line and the plant, and on her Lifeline lease, and you have never
lived above floor 19.

**Skill: bench hands.** Any repair you do on a **Works Bench** (the founding-era ESD benches of Four Hall) takes one hour
less than it should. Your cage has one: the old issuing counter's back bench. A whistle-born repairer also hears the Works
Whistle as a clock, so machines started on a Whistle (06:00, 14:00, 22:00) are logged in your ledger slip to the minute,
which matters to the Old Hands and to anyone logging the Dark Floors (02).

**Contacts.** The Old Hands start at *Known* (you carry Ilse's clock card, and Old Fen at the Crib knew her). The Co-op starts
at *Known* because every Varro since Y60 has paid dues.

**Debt.** Ilse's **Lifeline Loan** from Y57 (file 01), the emergency loan Halden gave flood families to rent their first
dry squares, at a rate typed in after the signature. It is 1,100 cr now. It sits on the arrears register in the family
name, and Brisk & Lowe sold it on years ago to a buyer who has never collected (file 07: Corvane buys Lifeline debts and sits
on them). It costs nothing week to week. It caps your Standing: nobody with an open Lifeline entry can pass 599 until it is
settled or bought back.

**Enemy.** **Dacre Mott**, leaseholder of the Glove Wall squares on Glove Run, who charges Old Hand families 2 cr to stand in
front of the wall and has started taking gloves down to sell to penthouse fixers (the Crown pays well for "authentic
industrial heritage"). Ilse's glove is on his list. He knows you know.

**Starting thing.** Ilse's **CCU card** (her Works union card, 1x1, with clock number), a Line Four **wafer wand** (a
first-fit vacuum pick that lifts chips and tiny parts without a touch; F grade), and a jar of Canteen Mint seed from the
Co-op's shelf, a birth gift.

**Starting Standing.** 250 (tower-born).

**In play:** the warm start. Cheap help from people who remember your family, a Standing ceiling that the player feels in
the second half of the game, and a small sharp errand early: buy, steal or negotiate Ilse's glove off Dacre Mott's wall before
it goes up to the Crown. The glove is worth 2 cr to anyone but an Old Hand family and 400 cr to a Crown buyer; Mott wants 150.

**Hook:** the CCU card's magnetic strip still reads at the Works' clock-in machine on floor 10 (06a). If you swipe Ilse's
card, the machine prints a stub with her clock number and a line: *SHIFT OWED: 1*. She was laid off mid-shift.

#### 3. Brownglass

**Who you were.** Middle. For nine years you and a partner ran **Grade & Go**, a licensed cell-grading counter on floor 38:
tenants brought H-cells in, you load-tested them, printed a grade card, took a percentage when they sold. You wore a pressed
shirt and paid 18 cr a square. Then the Glass Line came down to 60 (file 04), floor 38 took its proximity uplift and a Rate
Review in the same month, the counter's rent rose by a third, and you missed two rent days. Your partner, **Leopold Marr**,
had the licence in his name. He kept the counter. You kept the debt.

**Skill: the grader's eye.** You read a cell's hidden features (cycle count, true capacity, whether it is first-fit, Company-new
or grey) on sight, without an analyser. Every cell that comes down your chute shows its numbers. For anyone else a cell is a
"?" until scanned. In a tower that runs on cells (file 09), this is the most commercially useful skill of the fourteen.

**Contacts.** **Ambler & Daughters**, Grocers, floor 44 (the Middle broker of file 11), who bought cells from Grade & Go, now
send you brokered slips at a better cut than they give the Mills. A Middle client of yours, a dentist's receptionist on 41,
still writes.

**Debt.** Brisk & Lowe, 860 cr, the counter's arrears plus recovery charge. They take 20 cr every rent day "by arrangement"
and remind you, on cream card, that the arrangement can be reviewed.

**Enemy.** Leopold Marr. He still runs Grade & Go on 38, he grades the same cells you would, and when a cell contract
appears on the Middle row of the board he has usually claimed it first. Worse: Marr reports grey cells to the Brand
Integrity Bureau, because grey cells undercut his trade, and he knows exactly how good your eye is at spotting them.

**Starting thing.** A licensed **load bank** (a cell tester, 2x1, Company-new, with a calibration seal) and a framed **Authorised
Reseller licence**, lapsed. Reinstating a lapsed licence is not an application, so it costs no inquiry penalty (file 04): pay the
month and it is live.

**Starting Standing.** 300, with one arrears register entry, which blocks Middle leases until it is cleared.

**In play:** the clean start for a player who wants to play the Ledger game: legal, licensed, cell-heavy, with an enemy who
plays the same game one band up. The pressed shirt in the crate (a 2x2 garment) is worth 18 cr at a hock, and nothing on 17,
where it marks you; it is also the only thing that lets you through the Shelf's doorman without a Standing check.

**Hook:** Grade & Go's grading records for the last nine years are in Marr's back room. They show which cells came through
the Middle with plant code HW-9 (file 02) before anyone noticed the code. You graded the first one. You remember who brought
it in.

#### 4. The Drop

**Who you were.** For six years you worked the C-cell formation line on Vantage's floor 31, for the Corvane Group, under No
Idle Residency (file 07). Then a "line rebalancing" took your post, you had thirty days to find another inside Corvane, you
did not, and you were **released**: one bag, one 2-litre bottle of Corvane Clear, a transit seat on the boat to the Nine Dock,
and an invoice. The Stack took you, because Halden charges rent. The Mills call people like you **drops**.

**Skill: tolerance.** You pack to the minute and to the millimetre. Your drones' landing checks are never failed on
"condition" (a bay packed by you never shifts in a hard landing, as if it had dividers), and Vantage slips show on your board
from the start as ghosts, readable, with Corvane's compliance code, so you know what the richest regular client in the bay
wants long before you can supply it. You also know how a C-cell works inside.

**Contacts.** **Imre Sallis**, a former line-mate still on Vantage's floor 20, homesick, who writes on Corvane paper asking
for things Vantage forbids (Mills gin, a Static receiver, a mended shirt "with some character"). Other drops in the Mills, who
start at *Known* and work cheap.

**Debt.** Corvane's **Release Kit** invoice: bag, bottle, transit, "outplacement counselling" (a leaflet), 380 cr, payable in
twelve monthly instalments to a Corvane account that the Halden Ledger can pay into at a 6% "inter-company handling fee". If it
lapses, Vantage's Receiving Deck refuses your drones.

**Enemy.** The Mills. Drops are good with machines and bad with damp, and the floor knows it. The Co-op and the insect ranchers
start at *Wary*. Mother Ruus will not sell you water for a month.

**Starting thing.** A **Corvane C-cell** (holds half again an H-cell) locked by **CellCare**: it stops on the first of the month
unless you pay the 8 cr subscription or have it cracked in the Sump. And a Corvane **torque driver** (1x1), precision, worth more
than your drone's rotors.

**Starting Standing.** 300, with a "foreign origin" line that adds −5 to every inquiry.

**In play:** the outsider's start. The player begins as a stranger to everyone and an insider to the late-game client. It sets
up two story lines at once: Imre's smuggling slips (the concealment packing of file 07), and the question of whether you would go
back if Vantage offered (see "Leave for another tower").

**Hook:** your release letter cites "line rebalancing". The line was not rebalanced. Imre writes in his third letter that
Corvane laid off the whole C-cell formation crew in one week, and replaced it with a contract supplier. The supplier's cells
arrive by sea at night. The code on the crates is HW-9.

#### 5. Mudfoot

**Who you were.** Flats-born, on a raft off the Strand, a magnet fisher from the age of eight. You know the tide table the way
other people know their name. You came up the tower because your mother's lungs went and the Flats have no medicine but Vey's,
which costs what the Flats do not have.

You could not sign a Pathway on your own: Flats people are registered at Standing 150, and the Pathway needs 250. So someone
**sponsored** you (file 04: a Standing sponsor copies a fraction of their Standing across for a year). The sponsor was **Ruben
Vass**, who runs the Landing, the steel platform where Flats salvage comes up into the tower. Ruben does not do things for
nothing.

**Skill: reading wet.** You know the Great Ebb days a week ahead without buying the Sounding's forecast, and you buy salvage from
Flats people at Flats prices: no Landing cut, no Exchange broker. Wet salvage that comes into your field (anything from the
water, from the Flats, from the plant below) shows its condition without being dried and scanned.

**Contacts.** The Tide Folk start at *Trusted*; **Hesper Quill** knew your grandmother. A diver, **Ama Fenwright**, sells you
first pick of what she brings up.

**Debt.** To Ruben Vass: for a year, **a tenth of every salvage-related sale**, and first refusal on anything founding-era you
find. Ruben keeps the account in his head and is never wrong.

**Enemy.** **Ticket Joe** (Joachim Lindqvist), toll clerk at the Landing hoist, whose thumb on the scale you skip. Joe reports
your crates to the Lift Guild as "untolled freight". And the tower itself: Mills clients pay a **mudfoot discount**, about a
tenth less, to anyone registered from the Flats, until your Standing passes 400.

**Starting thing.** A **magnet line** (a 1x3 coil with a pull magnet), a hand-drawn **tide card** for the year, and a 2x2 **wet
trunk** of unexamined mud finds, each a "?".

**Starting Standing.** 150, raised to 250 by the sponsorship for one year. The sponsorship ends on day 365. If your own Standing
has not caught up by then, you drop back by 100 overnight.

**In play:** the hard start with the best supply line. A clock (the sponsorship year) that matters more than the Pathway in the
first year, and an early decision about Ruben: pay the tenth honestly, hide sales from him (he finds out), or buy him out.

**Hook:** Ruben sponsored eleven Flats people into the Mills in the last five years. All of them signed Pathways on squares near
a chute trunk. None of them knows the others.

#### 6. Off the boat

**Who you were.** When you were nineteen your parents' rent debt on floor 13 reached termination, the Varga Books bought it, and the
work-off contract had the Bastion box pre-ticked (file 05). You "elected transfer". You **took the boat** from the floor 9 dock
on a Thursday and spent four years and two months on a cell line in Bastion, the prison tower, making the things Halden Works
used to make, alongside people who had actually been convicted of something. The debt was cleared. You came back on a Thursday.

**Skill: the line.** You can build. Not machines (nobody builds machines), but cells: given a clipped or dead cell and a crimper,
you re-terminate and re-form it to a grade nobody else in the Mills reaches, because Bastion taught you on Works machines.
Re-terminated cells from your bench grade one step better than a jigger's. You also recognise Bastion's plant code (BX) and
its tricks, which means you can tell a Bastion cell sold as "Halden Certified Renewed" from a real one.

**Contacts.** **The Thursday People**: other returnees, a few dozen across the Sump and the Mills, who meet at a noodle stall on 14
on the first Thursday of the month and help each other without discussing it. They start at *Trusted*.

**Debt.** None. Your discharge paper says CLEARED in red. It is the only past with a clean debt line, and it costs you something
else: discharged persons are registered at Standing 200, and the **discharge mark** on your file means HPS may stop and search
your drones on a lane without cause.

**Enemy.** The Varga family (the Varga Books) who sold your parents' debt to the boat. They do not hate you; they are embarrassed to
see you, and embarrassment in the Sump costs money: your drones pay a higher toll at the Knot on R7.

**Starting thing.** A **Bastion crimper** (a 1x2 terminating tool, grey, made on the line from scrap), and four clipped cells
somebody gave you on the boat home, "for a start".

**Starting Standing.** 200.

**In play:** the cell-chain start, with a lane penalty (random HPS stops on licensed lanes, which cost an hour and sometimes a
grey item) that pushes the player toward the free shafts, and a social network that is small, poor and completely reliable.

**Hook:** on the Bastion line you worked a forming rack with a brass plate: HALDEN WORKS / LINE FOUR / RACK 11. The Company says
the Works' machines were lost in the Drowning. Rack 11 was on 17. Its bolt holes are in your cage's floor.

#### 7. The Scholar who came back

**Who you were.** At twelve you sat the Foundation exam (06b) and won. You went up to the Foundation's school in the Terraces,
wore a blazer, learned to say "one" instead of "you", ate real fruit on the founder's birthday. At seventeen you were
**deselected** "for fit", which is what the Foundation calls it when a scholar asks why the Mills pay for the Scholars' water.
You were sent back down on Old Two with a bag, a blazer and a **Scholar Bond**.

**Skill: manners.** You read the upper floors. Brokered slips (file 11) show their original pay to you without holding them to
a lamp, because you know how a steward writes. Terrace and Crown clients' slips cost you no **etiquette penalty** (the small
deductions upper clients make for "presentation": wrong paper in the bay, goods packed "like a Mills crate"). And you can talk
to a Terrace receptionist in their own register, which unlocks one Terraces bay-slip client per season.

**Contacts.** **Tamsin Greer**, the Foundation's assessor for floors 14 to 19 (06b), herself a Scholar, who feels guilty. **Felix
Strand-Esker**, a classmate from the school, now a junior in his family's office on 77, who sends you slips for "something
authentic from downstairs" and pays well and late.

**Debt.** The **Scholar Bond**: the Foundation charges deselected scholars for "education received", 2,000 cr, interest-free,
"payable from commercial income": a tenth of every contract's pay is skimmed at the pay post until it is cleared. The Bond is
printed on the same cream card as the Foundation's gala invitations.

**Enemy.** The Mills. **Glass-struck** is the word: a child who went up and came back thinking she was above. The Co-op and the
vent kids start at *Wary*. Your own family on 14 has not spoken to you since the day you were deselected; your mother still
keeps your exam certificate on the wall.

**Starting thing.** A **Foundation blazer** (2x2, crested; 30 cr at a hock, and a pass through the Shelf doors), and your
**Scholar's slate**, **bricked** the day you were deselected by the Retirement Signal (file 04). Unbricking it (an Old Hand or a
flasher like Odalys Brannock) gives back your contacts list: forty Terrace children, now Terrace adults.

**Starting Standing.** 300.

**In play:** the upward start. Fast access to upper clients, a tithe on everything until the Bond is paid, and a lower floor
that does not trust you. Its natural pull is toward the Ascent endings, and its natural irony is that every climb you make is
the Foundation's success story; the Foundation will put your picture in its annual report if you reach the Terraces, and send
you the invoice for the photograph.

**Hook:** your slate, unbricked, has one message you never read: a note from the school's bursar, sent the morning you were
deselected, to someone at the Foundation whose address is only an account number. *She asked about the water again. Proceed.*

#### 8. The Valet

**Who you were.** For seven years you were a **disposal valet** to the **Ashgrove** household on floor 96 (file 04: valets do
the BIDS voiding for the Crown). You put the X through silk, punctured wine, sent the Retirement Signal to slates that had
nothing wrong with them. And you did it badly, on purpose, for the Voided's cut: an X that missed the seams, a puncture at the
edge, a Signal never sent. Then the Ashgroves' new house manager audited the chute logs, and you were dismissed without a
reference, fined for "breach of the Disposal Standard", and shown the service lift.

**Skill: the valet's eye.** Every VOID item in your catch shows its method and whether it was voided properly or **badly**,
without an analyser. You know which bad-valet coats are near-whole under the cuts. You can also void, which turns out to be a
skill, because the **Disposal Rebate** (0.40 cr a kilo of certified-voided goods, file 04) pays anyone who logs voided goods at a
Reclaim Station with a valet's code. Yours was never cancelled.

**Contacts.** The Voided start at *Trusted*; **Marisol Eke** sewed for your cuts for seven years. **Roz Achebe**, cook at the
Pantry on 90 (file 08), takes your slips at the Crown's back door.

**Debt.** The BIDS fine, 600 cr, owed to the Ashgrove household and collected by **Nettlefold Settlement** (file 05), who, since
you have no wages, write to your clients instead: on any contract from the Terraces or the Crown, Nettlefold takes a quarter of
the pay at the pay post until the fine is cleared.

**Enemy.** The **Brand Integrity Bureau** has your name. On a **brand sweep** event, your lease is the first one the agents walk.

**Starting thing.** A **canceller** (a hand press that punches VOID through card and tin, 2x2, Company-licensed, which you were
supposed to hand back) and your **valet's code** on a 1x1 card.

**Starting Standing.** 300.

**In play:** the grey start. The valet is the past that makes the VOID chain (file 04) the centre of the game from day one, and
puts two crimes in the crate. The canceller lets you claim the Disposal Rebate on goods you void yourself, which is a small,
steady, perfectly legal-looking income built on destroying usable things. Restoring voided goods is the opposite crime. A valet
can do both on the same afternoon, to the same coat.

**Hook:** the Ashgroves' new house manager is **Pim Okafor**'s older sister (file 05's alternative warden). She audited the chute
because somebody on 98 told her to. Somebody on 98 is sending Marisol the same silk shirt over and over (06b).

#### 9. The Grounded Rat

**Who you were.** A shaft rat for eight years: R1 and R3, the Chimney and the Three, Mills to the Middle combs and back, three
frames at the peak and a name at the Drop. Then a **Turn draught** at the 30 comb took your best frame and its bay with it
(a Kitty claim the Kitty could not pay that week), and you were grounded: no frame worth flying, a debt to the pot, and a
landlady who wanted rent in credits.

**Skill: chalk.** You read rat-chalk without paying for it. Free-shaft trouble rolls are one step softer for your drones on R1 and
R3, and you know which comb holes are open each week (a free version of the rat-chalk token in 06a). You also fly by ear: a drone
that comes back with an odd sound shows what failed before you open it.

**Contacts.** **Yusuf Haddad**, the Drop warden, and **Ama Duarte, "Kestrel"**, who taught you, start at *Trusted*. The Kitty takes
you back at once if you pay what you owe.

**Debt.** To the **Kitty**: three bays' worth, about 180 cr in goods, paid one item a week into the pot. Skip it and the Drop
stops warning you about closed holes.

**Enemy.** The **Lift Guild**. The Guild's Ride Book has your frames in it. Your Lane Permit costs a third more ("risk
assessment") and the Guild files a report each time one of your drones uses the Three Grate.

**Starting thing.** **The Ell**: a rat-built riser frame, a Kestrel with a broken quarter cut away and a second frame bolted on
sideways (file 09), with an L-shaped 4x3 bay with a 2x2 notch. Unlicensed. Two drones on day one, if you can fly them both.

**Starting Standing.** 300.

**In play:** the route start. Twice the launches from the first morning, the free shafts as the main road, the Guild as the
standing enemy, and every packing job in the Ell a puzzle (nothing square fits the notch).

**Hook:** the Turn draught that took your frame happened at 22:01 on a hum night. Drones caught in the R6 updraft come back four
minutes fast. Yours did not come back. Somebody on 27 says they heard it go past their bricked window, upward.

#### 10. The Tagger

**Who you were.** Six years with **Brisk & Lowe Recoveries**, in a pair: the talker talked, you tagged. You walked leases
choosing goods by **value per square** (file 05: collectors take small, dense, valuable things first), wrote the yellow tags,
came back with HPS forty-eight hours later. You quit the week your pair sent you to a lease on Wet Run and you tagged your own
cousin's grow lights.

**Skill: value per square.** You see it. Every item on your field shows its value per square as a small number, the way a
collector reads a room. Packing for profit becomes a sum you can do at a glance, and you know which of your own goods a collector
would take first.

**Contacts.** Brisk & Lowe still like you. They offer **debt-buying** slips: small Mills debts bundled at 30 to 40 cr per 100 owed,
yours to collect or sit on. **The Varga Books** on floor 6 knows you as a professional and starts at *Known*.

**Debt.** None in money. You owe your cousin **Danuta Ostrowski** on Wet Run three grow lights, which you will have to buy at
full price because the Recovery Store sold hers.

**Enemy.** The floor. Families you lemoned live on 14, 15 and 17. The Co-op starts at *Hostile* (you tagged a Co-op bench in
Y84), the vent kids at *Wary*, and Hester's warden's regard starts a pip down, because Hester knows exactly who you are and
wants to see what you do.

**Starting thing.** A **pocket scale** and a **tag gun** with a roll of 200 yellow tags. Tagging your own stock with fake yellow

## Part two: the first day, the first week, the first month

### The paper

The Pathway is signed the evening before day one, at the pay post by the lift lobby on 17, with a **Pathway Adviser** who comes
down on Old Two for the purpose: **Ottavia Marsh**, thirty, Middle, from the Proprietor Pathway desk on 44, who has signed
eleven hundred Pathways in six years and says "your future" about forty times an evening. She brings the form, the brochure, a
stud wand to show you your squares light up green on its little screen, and a **Welcome Pack**.

The numbers are file 04's: principal **5,200 cr** (lease premium 1,440, title price 2,400 fixed "at the Period of signing", starter
machines 620, the Wren and its pad 540, licences 80, arrangement fee 120), a daily service charge of 0.15% on what is outstanding
(7.80 cr on day one), and a **minimum payment of 15 cr taken at 20:00 every day**. Rent continues while the Pathway is open. Ottavia
explains this as "building equity while you trade" and has a diagram.

**The Kasks.** Benno (forty) and Sarai (thirty-eight) Kask run a cell-charging rack on twelve squares next to the cage, four of them
frontage. They have a son, **Mikko**, seven, who sits on their counter and counts the Fall. They are two weeks short on rent when the
game starts and pretending not to be. On day 9 a Brisk & Lowe pair yellow-tags their charging rack. On day 14 the Arbiter's lease
adjustment order takes two rows. On day 21 the lease ends and the squares go back to Halden, which lists them for re-letting at the
current card: twelve squares adjacent to yours, four of them frontage.

**In play:** the Kasks are the first moral pressure and the first expansion opportunity, on purpose, in the same family. Things the
player can do in the first three weeks, each with a cost:
- Lend Benno the cell (lose a cell; he pays it back on day 8, or he does not).
- Buy their rent debt when Brisk & Lowe offer it on a slip (30 cr per 100 owed): then you own the debt and can sit on it, which
  saves the lease, and the Ledger's association penalty (file 04) costs you Standing for every trade you do with them.
- Buy the tagged rack at the Recovery Store after it is seized, for a third of its worth.
- Apply for a **lease extension** on their twelve squares the morning they fall vacant (−5 Standing for the inquiry), which is the
  cheapest expansion you will ever see, with frontage, and Mikko watches you sign.
- Hire Sarai (she is good with cells) and pay her in hours of your rack.
- Do nothing. The squares go to Pell Ostrander, who will then have twice your frontage across the aisle.

**Pell Ostrander.** Thirty-three, a refurbisher with eighteen squares opposite you on Stores Run, all frontage, Middle-born and
furious about it. He sells the same things you do at a credit less, has been on 17 three years, and is Pathway-paid to 70%. He is
not cruel. He is the version of you that is a year ahead and has stopped asking questions. He is also the tenant most likely to
report you to Hester when the quota week comes, because you are the one he is competing with.

**Hook:** Pell paid a chalker in Y85 to "survey favourably" before a re-cut, and gained two frontage squares from the lease next to
his. That lease was Tamar Lisowski's. She lives in the offcut now.

### The first week

**Rent day** is the night of day 7. On floor 17, 24 squares: 19 at 5 cr, 4 frontage at 7.50, 1 pillar at 2.50, 127.50, rounded by
the Ledger to **128 cr**. Atmosphere Levy 6 cr. Machine service contracts 15 cr. The Pathway minimums over the week, 105 cr. About
**280 cr a week** before a litre is drawn. Draw adds 60 to 90.

The night before is **Thumb Night** (file 08): debts settle between neighbours, slips are posted on the Fringe by everyone selling
whatever they can, the hock on 11 does its best trade. Benno Kask comes round again.

If the player is short on day 7, the arrears ladder starts (05): a red Arrears Notice and the tap on a trickle from day 1, the mesh lockout on day 8,
forfeiture on day 15. Every machine that draws water slows to a tenth. The player feels arrears as production stalling.

**In play:** the first rent day should be survivable with a little care and impossible with none. The suggested balance: a player
who clears the catch every morning, keeps Gus's breakfasts going and takes two Mills slips a day makes rent with about 40 cr spare.
A player who sits on the board waiting for a good slip does not.

### The first month

- **Day 9.** The Kasks are tagged.
- **Day 10.** Store restock: the Hatch on 18 has new drone parts, scrip rises to 66.
- **Day 12 or so.** Yusra's offer, again, in person, with a basket of her greens as a sample (real, R, they smell of the soil).
- **Day 15.** Halden payday: the street fills with scrip; the rate falls to 52.
- **Day 20 to 28.** **The first requisition** (02). A slip on the board nobody pinned, on Works requisition stock, dot-matrix, addressed
  STORES 17-C. "GLOVE, CLEANROOM, NITRILE, LINE 4 ISSUE, 12 PR." The delivery point is a brass call plate on floor 21. (The Heir gets it
  in week one.)
- **Day 21.** The Kasks' squares fall vacant.
- **Day 25 to 30.** **Quota week.** Hester is short of reports. She comes to the counter in the morning with the Book under her arm
  and a choice for you (see Part four).
- **Day 30.** **Rate Review.** The new Floor Rate Card arrives; 17's base rate goes from 5 to 5.10. The Ledger rounds your rent up to
  131. A **Pathway statement** reads "You are 3% of the way to owning your future!"

**In play:** the first month introduces each long system once: supply (the catch, the Kitchen), money (the statement), rent (the
first rent day), law (Hester's registration, the Kasks' tags), Standing (the ghost slips), the neighbourhood (the Kasks, Yusra,
Pell), the mystery (the requisition), the long clock (the Rate Review). After it the player has met every pressure the game has.

## Part three: climbing

Progression in the Stack is never a level. It is always something the world sells or allows: a square, a frame, a permit, a
licence, a number on a tally, a lease on another floor, a seat on a boat. Each step costs one of the five real resources (squares,
hours, Standing, credits, risk, file 04) and each changes what the player's day looks like.

### Two ladders

There are two ways up and the game should let a player climb either, or both, and be rich in different currencies.

| | The Ledger ladder | The other ladder |
|---|---|---|
| Kept by | the Count House | the Co-op's pencil ledger, the Kitty, the Ride Book, Ruben's head, the Drop's chalk |
| Measured in | Standing (0 to 1,000), balance, title | faction standing (Hostile to Kin), turns owed, jugs, chits, cells under the bunk |
| Opens | the board's upper rows, lanes, licences, Middle and Terraces leases, the Crown by invitation | bay slips, cheap water, free-shaft safety, founding-era parts, the Under, the Fringe |
| Costs | fees, inquiries, association penalties, rent | favours, risk, time, and Standing whenever the two touch |
| Looks like | tally colour: amber, white, blue, silver | chalk signs on your drain tile, a Co-op pin, a Kitty token, a Font cup |
| Ends in | Held Title, Glass, the Crown, Partner, Bought Out | the Charter, the Bench, the Library, the Lid, Free Shafts, Going wet |

The two ladders touch at points that hurt: the association penalty (−3 Standing per Ledger trade with a Delinquent account) makes
it expensive to be good to poor clients through the pay post; the Lift Guild's reports make it expensive to fly free while
holding a Lane Permit; a warden's report hits the Ledger for something the Co-op would call neighbourliness.

### Squares

The first and most physical axis. Every square is space on the field and a line on the rent.

- **Offcuts.** Squares left over after a re-cut (file 04), around pillars and pipe runs, let at x0.7. Awkward shapes: an L around a
  pillar, a 1-wide strip along a pipe run (holds only flat items). The cheap way to grow, and every offcut is a packing puzzle.
- **Lease extension.** When an adjacent lease falls vacant (the Kasks, or anyone who is lemoned), sign a lease extension slip at the
  pay post: the squares appear on your field, the rent rises next rent day. −5 Standing for the inquiry, approved or not.
- **Frontage strips.** Sublet barons hold frontage in long strips and rent single squares by the day to hawkers. Renting one gives you
  a shelf square on another stretch of corridor: a remote shelf, restocked by hand or by a one-floor drone hop, that sells to
  whoever walks there (a frontage square on Glove Run sells to Gus's sleepers; one at the lift lobby sells to everyone).
- **Buying into title.** Squares added after the Pathway are rented at the current card; they can be bought into held title at the
  current title price (100 cr a square on 17 today, up about 10% each time the Chalk moves a floor closer).
- **The re-cut.** Survey and Allocation will "rationalise" your sector every so often; your lease can lose a frontage square or gain a
  pillar offcut overnight. A chalker paid 30 to 60 cr "surveys favourably". A player with a big lease is a target for re-cuts; a
  player with a friendly chalker is not.

What sizes feel like on 17:

| Squares | What it is | What the floor calls it |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | a locker lease; a needler's offcut | "a stall" |
| 24 | the cage; one person and three machines | "a shop" |
| 36 to 48 | the cage and the Kasks'; two people, a hand, six machines | "a business" |
| 80 to 120 | a third of Stores Run's frontage | "a name" |
| 400+ | a whole Run (only barons hold this) | "an aisle" |

**In play:** squares are bought one decision at a time, never in bulk, and always with a shape the world chose. The player's field
literally grows into the space the neighbours lost.

### Bays

The second axis, and the one IDEAS made central: bigger bays, odd-shaped bays, cold or sealed bays, more range.

| Step | What | Cost | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Halden **Wren**, 3x2 | in the Pathway | one crate and a slip per launch |
| Cheap | **document sleeve** | 5 cr | paper rides free; the Crown's three-paper slips become possible |
| Cheap | **Mutual liner** | 12 cr | insurable on lanes |
| Early | a second Wren, or a rat frame from the Drop | 300 to 540 cr | two launches at once; the day changes shape |
| Middle | Halden **Kestrel**, 4x3, two cell mounts | 820 cr | Middle contracts in one launch |
| Middle | **cold liner**, **sealed liner**, **live box** | 90, 120, 25 cr | Crown greens, Vey medicine, live insects |
| Late | Halden **Heron**, 5x4, cold liner fitted | Std. 600 to buy | long range, slow to spin up |
| Grey | a **capsule frame** (a stolen Tube capsule as a bay) | 600+ cr, from a rat | the best sealed bay in the Stack; Halden buys them back for scrip, no questions |
| Late | a **crossing frame** with batteries for the bay | see "Other towers" | other towers |

Rat frames (the Ell, the Tray-back, the Strut-through, the Bucket, file 09) are the sideways step: cheaper, odder, riser-only, each
one a different packing puzzle.

### Lanes and permits

How high a drone may go, and how safely.

- **Single-launch lane ticket.** 3 cr + 0.2 per floor. For occasional lane use before a permit.
- **Lane Permit, Middle (to floor 59).** 25 cr a week. Std. 400. Packed in the bay or on the sleeve.
- **Lane Permit, Terraces (to 89).** 90 cr a week. Std. 650.
- **Crown Lane clearance.** Std. 850 **and a sponsor**: a Crown or Terrace account in good order that signs for you. Through the Ninety
  Gate and the Postern on 93. The sponsor is a person, and a person wants something.
- **The free shafts.** No permit. The risers to the 30 comb and the 60 comb (rat holes, changing weekly), never past the Crown Comb at
  89. Trouble rolls by riser. The Kitty for cover.

**In play:** permits are paper you pack, and a week's permit is a weekly bill. A player who flies only free never pays a permit and
never reaches the Terraces by drone; a player who flies only lanes pays every week and never loses a bay to the Knot.

### Licences

Licences are the decision to formalise. Each costs a fixed sum and unlocks a class of contracts or a grade (file 04, table in §8):
the **food handling licence** (Orrin supply contracts), the **Grow Certificate** per bed (the R✓ grade the Crown wants), the
**Authorised Reseller licence** (new branded goods, openly), **launch pad registration** (no fine), and the **water treatment
licence** (600 cr up front and 150 a month, Std. 700), which turns a stinker into a certified safe provider.

Every licence is also an inspection: the office that sells it now has a reason to visit.

### Standing as a feeling

| Band | Tally | What changes on 17 |
|---|---|---|
| Probationary (200–399) | amber | prepay meters run dry at night; half the board is grey; Hester watches |
| Standard (400–599) | white | **postpay**: the machines stop stopping. Store account. Middle lanes. "Flat." |
| Preferred (600–799) | blue | Terrace slips go live; Middle leases open; neighbours ask you to vouch for them |
| Trusted (800–1,000) | silver | Crown slips; sponsor-ready; the Lease Office sends you a card on Founding Day |

At Preferred the player starts to be asked for things: to vouch, to sponsor, to lend their tally. A silver tally in the Mills is
visible from across a hall, and people's voices change.

### Moving floors

Moving is legal, costly and slow: give notice on rent day at a pay post, broom-clean inspection (15 cr, and the warden looks at
everything), a new lease premium on the new floor, the move itself (Lift Guild freight at 0.5 cr per crate per floor, or your own
drones, many trips). Title squares stay where they are: a player who moves before the Pathway closes owns squares on a floor they
have left, and can only sublet them (illegal, which is how barons start).

Where a player might go, and why:

| Floor | Rent (cr/sq/wk) | Gain | Lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12** (the Chirp, Two Hall) | 3 | cheap squares; the insect trade next door; the Co-op hall on 13 | the chirp all night; worse catch (lower on the trunks) |
| **16** (the Ovens) | 5.5 | open flame tolerated: stills, smoking, baking, kilns | the catch after 17's; heat; oven-sleepers' noise |
| **21** (Underhum) | 8 + thermal line | **warm floor**: bins run the night block without heaters | the dawn slack; the hum; the Dark Floors through the ceiling |
| **27** (Overhum) | 8 | warm floor; the Hatch | the bricked window; the mushroom grower's questions |
| **28** (the Exchange) | 9 | the best market in the Mills; brokers; founding-era part dealers | rent; competitors who used to be Middle |
| **29** (the Chalk) | 9, rising | Middle clients at Mills prices, for a while | the Creep arrives here first; title revaluation risk |
| **33 to 44** (the Middle) | 12 to 28 | Middle row as home row; a Supply counter; clean water pressure | everything metered, wardens who file everything, Std. 500 to lease |
| **6** (the Sump) | 2 | Crown sacks from the Spill; the Kettle on Six; chits | wet floors at spring tides; gang tolls; no Grid slips ("service ends at Ten") |
| **60+** (the Terraces) | 60 to 190 | glass, daylight through cut windows, Terrace clients as neighbours | Std. 750; a 24-square shop costs 1,440 cr a week at the bottom of the band |

**In play:** a move is a large, deliberate choice that changes the catch table (better floors throw better rubbish; file 04), the
board's mix (more slips from your new band), the routes (shorter up, longer down), the neighbours, and the floor features (warm,
wet, flame-tolerated). It should feel like a new chapter, with a new set of neighbours and a new warden. Moving down is a real
strategy, never only a failure: 6 with a Spill contact can be richer than 17.

### A second lease

Instead of moving, a player can hold two leases: the cage on 17 and a frontage strip on 28, or a warm square block on 21 for bins.
Two leases means two rent lines, two wardens, two catches, and drone hops between them. It is the beginning of a business that
exists in more than one place, which is the beginning of both "the Fleet" and "the Landlord".

### Hands

Nobody works alone for long. Hands are paid people, run as plans (IDEAS: "hirelings are just plans"):

- **A picker.** A Mills kid or an old person who sorts your catch in the first hour for a share or 2 cr a morning, by a plan (what
  to keep), and leaves a **left-behind** pile you can check.
- **A bed-watcher.** Sits up overnight with the grow beds and bins (file 08), so the night block runs tended: a tended night is a
  better grade.
- **A counter hand.** Serves the frontage while you work the bench. Sarai Kask, if she is still around.
- **A courier.** A shaft rat flying on your slips, for a cut of the pay. Two or three of them and you are the Fleet.

Hands eat from your stock, want wages on Seventh, and have lives: a counter hand who is a Registered Occupant on your lease is
someone the arrears register reaches if you fall.

### Other towers

The last axis, late. Trade across the bay needs a **crossing frame** (the **Corvane Tern** if you can buy one from Vantage, or a rat
build on a Heron's bones), **batteries** strong enough for four hours into a south-westerly, and a frame stable enough to come back.
Poachers wait on the crossing (file 07). The slow alternative is wind and sun: a **kite rig** or a solar sheet on a slow frame that
takes two days to cross and is mostly ignored by poachers because nothing that slow carries anything worth stopping.

Other towers are where the highest pay is (Vantage), the strangest clients are (the Pier), the debt is made (St. Ober's), the seeds
are patented (Greenhold), the salvage is free (the Hulk), and the work is done that the Works used to do (Bastion). They are also
where some of the endings go.

### What climbing looks like

IDEAS: looks follow the data. The field's backdrop is your floor (Four Hall's amber safelights and grey gloves; the Ovens' warm
steel; the Middle's brownglass; the Terraces' daylight through a cut window), the light gets better as you rise, contract slips
change paper as your clients change band, and your own tally changes colour. A player who has climbed should be able to tell
without reading a number.

## Part four: what the tower asks of you

### No conscience meter

There is no morality bar. Nothing in the game adds up "good" and "bad". Instead, everyone keeps a ledger, as file 06a proposes:
the Count House keeps Standing, the Co-op keeps a pencil book, Hester keeps the Warden's Book, the vent kids keep chalk, the Tide
Folk keep knots, the Drop keeps a wall. A choice moves one or more of those, visibly, and the people whose ledger moved behave
accordingly. The player is never told they did wrong. They are told that the tap queue no longer lets them in early, that the
Fringe slips stopped carrying their board mark, that Tansy put the drain tile back and did not come up again.

Below are the choices the tower puts in front of a workshop on 17, again and again, in many forms. Each is a recurring kind of
event, not a single scene.

### Reporting neighbours

**The situation.** **Quota week** (file 05): near the end of each month, the warden is short of reports. The Lease Office pays
wardens whose floors show "a healthy irregularity rate", and on 17 that number is four a month.

**The offer.** Hester comes to your counter at 08:00 with the Book under her arm. "I need one more. I can write you up for the
mezzanine, a letter of concern, nothing comes of it. Or there's Ostrander's extension cable through his mesh, that's an inspection.
Or the Kasks have someone sleeping on their rack. You tell me."

| Choice | Pays | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Take the letter yourself | Hester's regard +2 pips; nothing from the Lease Office | a letter of concern on your file (−5 Standing; one more and the next is an inspection) |
| Name Pell Ostrander | Pell's lease inspected; he loses a shelf for a week; your shelf sells more | Pell knows within a day; he reports you next month |
| Name the Kasks | nothing for you; the Kasks get an occupancy count | the Kasks' cousin is the sleeper; Mikko saw you talking to Hester |
| Refuse | nothing | Hester picks; her regard −1; she will not ask you again, which means she will not warn you either |

**Alternative: Pim.** If the warden is **Pim Okafor** (05's alternative), there is no quota-week favour. Pim offers a trade: a report
against you, or a report from you about someone else. Every month. A Pim floor is a floor where everyone reports everyone.

**Beyond the warden.** Other people want reports: the **Water Wellbeing Office** pays a bounty for information "leading to the
sealing of unlicensed apparatus" (a stinker's still: 40 cr, file 03's informant economy); the **Brand Integrity Bureau** pays a share
of the fine for a seller of VOID-restored goods; Orrin's field agent pays in "supplier preference" for the name of anyone pressing
grey brick from Mills Brown. Each bounty is a slip. Each slip has a name on it, and the name is someone the player has traded with.

**In play:** reporting is a slip you can pack and send, like any contract, with a pay line and a reward. The cost is never shown on
the slip. It arrives later, as a closed door.

### Voided goods

**The situation.** Every morning the catch drops goods the floors above killed to protect prices (file 04): silk with the X, wine
with a puncture, slates bricked by the Retirement Signal, cells with their terminals clipped, bread sprayed Orrin Blue, chairs with
their legs sawn at different heights.

**The offer.** Undo it. A sewing kit, a resin plug, a flash rig, a re-terminating jig, a wash basin and a lot of water. The undone
item sells for 30% to 70% of new, to grey clients only, and carries **brand risk**: on a brand sweep, any restored good on a shelf
or a frontage square is a fine and −50 Standing.

The ladder of the choice, from mild to dark:
1. **Restore and use.** Mend a voided coat and wear it, re-terminate a clipped cell and run your own grinder on it. Illegal in
   theory, invisible in practice. Nobody ever has been fined for wearing a crossed jacket (file 08: in the Mills it is a style).
2. **Restore and sell down.** A mended coat to a Mills client by drone, a re-terminated cell to a shaft rat. Grey, low risk, low pay.
3. **Restore and sell up.** A re-legged real-wood chair to a Middle client who pays as if it were new. High pay; the client might
   check; if the client is a brand agent's cousin, you are done.
4. **Restore and sell as new.** Re-seal a punctured bottle of real wine, re-box it in a counterfeit sleeve, and sell it to the Middle
   as Skywater-grade. Counterfeiting (below).
5. **Blue as food.** Wash Orrin Blue off bread and sell it as bread. Legal as insect feed (full value); illegal as food (high value;
   Orrin Blue is mildly toxic). If you are caught selling dyed food to people, your Standing is gone. If you are not caught, someone
   on 15 eats it.

**The valet's inversion.** With a canceller and a valet's code (the Valet's past, or bought from a dismissed valet), a player can
**void goods themselves** and log them at a Reclaim Station for the **Disposal Rebate**, 0.40 cr a kilo. Ruining usable things for
a Company rebate is perfectly legal. A player who restores voided goods in the morning and voids good ones in the afternoon for the
rebate has understood the economy completely.

**In play:** VOID is an item state with a method; each method has its tool; each restored item is "mended" (a visible state) and
sells to a narrower set of clients. The moral line here is drawn by who you sell to, and the game draws it with client slips, not
with a meter.

### Counterfeiting

The Stack is full of marks: brand seals, grade marks, licence stamps, chits' foil, Works part numbers, plant codes. Every mark can be
faked, and every fake is a business.

- **Sleeving.** Put slab (unbranded insect block, 2 cr) in a Vitabrick sleeve (from the chute, uncancelled) and sell it at 3. The
  sleeves come down the chute cancelled most days; an uncancelled sleeve is worth half a credit on its own. Orrin's agent hunts
  sleevers, and the Mills mostly do not care.
- **Real-washing.** Synthetic paste in a tomato shape, waxed. Printed fibre with a cotton slub. Stained board as wood. A crime only if
  someone checks; penthouse fixers carry spectro wands (04).
- **Pooling.** The Co-op lends a Grow Certificate stamp between members (it is a Co-op practice, openly: "pooling"). R becomes R✓.
  The Crown's Assay sometimes checks.
- **Foil.** Chits and brand seals both use foil, and the Voided are very good at foil. A counterfeit 20 cr chit costs about 4 cr to
  make. A pay post refuses it. A Sump trader takes it at 80%. Passing chits is the crime the Company actually prosecutes, because it is
  the Company's money.
- **Plant codes.** A cell marked **HW-9** (Line Nine, the Dark Floors) is worth ten times a normal cell to the Archivists. A cell can be
  re-marked. The Archivists can tell. The Bureau cannot.
- **Provenance.** A founding-era part with a Widows' mark and a salvage receipt is worth twice as much to Vantage (07). Receipts are
  paper. Paper can be written.

**In play:** counterfeiting is a set of small tools and states: a sleeve item, a wax pot, a borrowed stamp, a foil press, a code
punch, a receipt book. Each fakes one mark. The risk is always the same shape: a check on delivery (fail the contract, −25), or an
inspection event (a fine and a Standing hit), or a client who finds out later and stops sending slips. Counterfeiting pays best at
the boundaries between bands, where buyers want the mark and cannot check it.

### Serving up or serving down

**The situation.** One grow bed, one harvest, two slips. File 04's grades: the Crown wants R✓ and pays for it; the Mills want food
and pay in bricks.

**The example.** Twelve strawberries come ripe in your bed the same morning two slips are on the board:
- **A household on 97:** 12 strawberries, R✓, cold liner, 260 cr. (You need Std. 850, a Grow Certificate, the cold liner and a
  Crown Lane clearance. By mid-game you might have all four.)
- **Mother Ivy Okonkwo, the Long Shift, floor 12:** "fresh anything, for the children on the 14:00 rail, Tally Day treat", 12 items of
  real produce, any grade, 14 cr and a Long Shift token.

Both are honest slips. One pays nineteen times the other. Neither client is a villain; the household on 97 is buying strawberries.
The game never says which to take.

**The pattern.** The same choice comes in every chain: a working founding-era cell to an Old Hand who needs it to keep a lift
running (pays a turn) or to a Crown fixer who wants it for a display case (pays 400 cr); water in a shortage to the Rain Church's
queue (pays nothing) or to a Middle client at triple (pays rent); a mended chair to a Mills family who needs a seat (pays 6) or to a
Middle client who wants "real wood" (pays 80).

**In play:** serving up raises Standing faster (upper clients are licensed and report through pay posts: +4 per delivery) and
raises money faster; serving down raises the other ledgers (Co-op, Church, Tide Folk, vent kids, Long Shift) and opens the endings
that run through them. A player who only serves up reaches the Crown with nobody on 17 who would carry them down the stairs. A
player who only serves down never pays off the Pathway. Most players will do both, and the game should make them feel each trade.

### Water

**The situation.** Licensed water is 1.20 cr a litre at the Mills tap, during tap hours, on a key-meter that over-reads (file 04).
A grow bed drinks every hour. A still under a tile makes sweet water from the salt line at a third of the price.

**The offers.**
- **Buy from a stinker.** Cheap, unlicensed, sometimes bad (solvent, test it). Mother Ruus, Pell & Daughters, the Kettle on Six.
- **Run your own.** A pot and coil, a ledge still, an air-well (self-supply, Clause 9: needs a licence), fog nets. Takes squares, which
  cost rent. Takes a hidden tile, if you want it unseen.
- **Sell it.** Your own still's spare cuts to the tap queue at 0.40 a litre, in your jerrycans. Now you are a stinker.
- **Report it.** 40 cr from the Water Wellbeing Office for a still's location. The informant economy.
- **Go Certified.** 600 cr up front and 150 a month, Std. 700: the same still, the same rusty kit, now a "certified safe provider" with a label
  and a monthly inspection.
- **Steal it.** The Rain Church steals rain from the Crown's gutters and gives it away (06a); a player who carries Font water down the
  free shafts warms the Church and cools the Lift Guild.

**In play:** water is where the lore's sharpest joke (the licence turns a criminal into a provider without changing the water) becomes
a decision with numbers: compare a month of 150 cr licence fees against the inspection risk, the bounty risk, and the price you can
charge openly.

### Debt, both ways

You will owe. You can also be owed, which in the Stack is a business.

- **Buying debt.** Brisk & Lowe sell small Mills debts on slips at 30 to 40 cr per 100. Buy a neighbour's debt and you own it plus
  the recovery charge. You can collect it (tag their goods, by the Charter), sit on it (which saves them, and costs you the association
  penalty on every Ledger trade with them), or sell it on to the Varga Books at 8 per 100. The Tagger's past starts with this on the board.
- **Lending.** Benno Kask's cell until Seventh. A neighbour's rent. The Mills lend in turns, jugs, cells and bricks; the Ledger does not
  see it, so the Ledger does not enforce it.
- **The hock.** 40% of value as a loan, 10% a week, four weeks. Raise cash before rent day without selling a thing you need back.
  Hock tickets trade; buying a desperate owner's ticket for a drone part with two weeks left is buying their drone part.
- **Renting your tally.** A Delinquent tenant can trade through your account for a cut. You earn; your Standing pays when their trades
  go bad (association, 04).
- **Sponsoring.** At Preferred (650), you can sponsor a new tenant: copy part of your Standing to them for a year, for a fee (about
  1,500 cr for +150 on the street). Ruben Vass did it for the Mudfoot. You can do it for a Flats family, or for whoever pays.
- **Bleeding for rent.** Plasma at the Red Door on 13, 18 cr a bag, once a week, if your grade is good enough (06b). The Stack's last
  resort before the hock.
- **The Pathway lapse.** Miss three minimums in a rent week and the Pathway becomes due at once. If you cannot pay, Halden
  "restructures" (new fee, higher charge) or ends it, and everything paid becomes rent. A player can deliberately let it lapse to walk
  away from a floor they hate, losing every credit of equity.

**In play:** debts are slips: a debt slip in your hand is an asset (you own someone's debt), a debt slip on your books is a line on
the statement. The player who buys the Kasks' debt to save them and the player who buys it to take their squares both hold the same
slip. The difference is what they do on day 14.

### Squares over people

The cleanest way to grow on 17 is to take squares other people lost. The game should make this unavoidable and make it feel like
what it is.

- **The Kasks' twelve squares** (Part two).
- **Letting the floor.** At night, while you sleep on the mezzanine, your cage's floor is empty. Gus Tanaka-Breen offers to rent it:
  six sleepers on bedrolls between your machines, 10 pm to 6 am, 2 cr each a night, half to you. That is 42 cr a week for nothing, on
  squares you already pay for. It is also a sublet, which the paper forbids, and which every baron in the tower started with. Machines
  that run in the night block share the floor with six strangers; someone will touch the still.
- **The re-cut bribe.** Pay a chalker to "survey favourably" before a re-cut, and the frontage square you gain is Tamar Lisowski's.
- **The strike.** In a Co-op rent strike (06a), the Company offers strike-breakers a **rent holiday**: four weeks free on any lease that
  pays on time during the strike. For a player in the middle of a Pathway, four weeks of rent is 500 cr. The Co-op keeps the names.

### Answering the requisitions

**The situation.** A slip addressed to STORES 17-C, on Works requisition stock, asks for something small: twelve pairs of Line Four
cleanroom gloves; a first-fit sync clock; an electrostatic Lung plate. Deliver to the call plate on 21. Payment arrives in your bay
the next time it lands: fresh HW-9 cells, a founding-era part, once a roll of Y40 Works scrip.

**The choice.** Answer, and you are supplying whatever runs in the Dark Floors. The pay is the best in the game for the goods, and
the goods are hard to find: Line Four gloves come off the **Glove Wall** (an Old Hand family's memorial), a sync clock off Aldo
Moyle's shelf. Refuse, and the slips keep coming, and then one day they are addressed to someone else.

**In play:** the requisitions are the strangest client in the game and the path to the strangest ending (Stores). They ask for things
the neighbourhood holds dear, so answering them is a neighbourhood choice. They pay in things that make the Company very interested
in you.

## Part five: arcs

An arc is a run of slips and events with one group, from first contact to the point where it turns into an ending. Every arc has
the same bones: **how it starts** (usually a trade), **what deepens it** (a token, a favour, a risk taken), **the fork** (a moment
where the group asks for something that costs you elsewhere), and **where it leads**. Factions' own detail is in 06a and 06b; this is
the player's road through each.

### Halden (the Company arc)

The Company is a faction too, and the one every player is in from the first night.

1. **Tenant.** The Pathway, the board, the statement. Halden is a set of numbers.
2. **Supplier.** At Standard, Orrin and Vey supply contracts open (food handling licence; sealed liner). Pay partly in "supply credit".
3. **Preferred Supplier.** At Preferred, with a licence in good order and fifty on-time deliveries, a cream envelope: the **Preferred
   Supplier Programme**. Lower lane fees, store account terms, a plaque for the frontage ("A Halden Preferred Workshop"). In return:
   Orrin's sleeve on your brick, Vey's price on your medicine, an exclusivity clause, and a quarterly **compliance visit**.
4. **The fork.** A Company officer asks you to stop supplying one client (the Co-op's Box, the Rain Church, a stinker) "to protect the
   Programme's integrity". Or the Programme's renewal requires you to install a Company meter on your still.
5. **Leads to:** Partner, Bought Out, Glass.

### The Mills Co-op

1. **Starts:** Yusra's greens, or a Co-op tool from the Crib on 13, or water from the Pool when your tap is on the trickle.
2. **Deepens:** dues (in produce or hours), a Co-op pin, a seat at a Hall meeting, a share of the pooled certificate stamp.
3. **The fork:** the rent strike. Hold (meters capped, lifts closed, your Pathway frozen in arrears) or take the Company's rent holiday.
4. **Leads to:** the Mills Charter, the Co-op floor, the Strike. Costs with Halden: Standing for every strike day; the Preferred
   Supplier Programme will not have you.

### The Old Hands

1. **Starts:** Mattias Orme's overflow. A first-fit part from the catch, taken to the hall on 19.

   (06a: one a season, scripted). Whose bench do you take?
4. **Leads to:** the Bench (the Last Apprentice), the Pension, Written Down. Costs: time. An apprentice's hours are not paid.

### The Rain Church

1. **Starts:** a Font cup when your tap is red-flagged. Wen Liao's laundry on 12.

## Part six: the endings

### How an ending works

The game is open-ended. An ending is not a final level; it is a **state the player can reach and then choose to close**. When the
conditions of an ending are met, the next 20:00 statement carries an extra slip, printed in the style of whoever is offering it
(the Lease Office's form, the Co-op's pencil, a Corvane white sheet, a requisition): an offer to **close the books**. Accept, and the
ending plays. Decline, and the game goes on; the offer stays open while the conditions hold, and some endings expire if they stop
holding (a title revaluation, a sponsor withdrawn, a vote lost).

Some endings are not offered. They happen: the **falling** endings (the Rail, the Boat) arrive when the arrears ladder reaches
termination and there is nothing left to tag.

Every ending is told the same way, in three in-world pieces and nothing else (no narrator, no score screen):

1. **The document.** One page of the world's paper: a certificate, a statement, a letter, minutes, a chalk mark, a receipt.
2. **The morning after.** The lobby board on 17 at 07:00 the next day, as Noor pins it: which slips are there, whose board mark is
   on the Fringe, what is missing. The board is the tower's face, and it shows what the ending changed (often: nothing).
3. **What became of them.** A short list of the neighbours (Part seven) and what happened to each, set by what the player did.

Endings can **stack**. A player can hold title on 17 and wear the armband; run the Fleet and sell the Proof; reach Glass and be
Bought Out on the way. When several are open at once, the player chooses which one to close on, and the others appear as lines in
"What became of them" ("You kept the armband until Y91.").

The endings below are grouped by what they are about. Each lists **what leads there** (in game conditions), **what it costs**
(what the player gives up getting there, and what the ending itself takes), **the document**, variants, and how it is used.

---

### I. Staying

#### Held Title ("own your squares")

**What it is.** The Pathway closes. The 24 squares named in it convert from leased to **held title** (file 04). Rent stops on them.
Nobody can evict you from them. You are, in the language of the paper, a **Proprietor**.

**What leads there.**
- Pathway principal at zero. At the minimum 15 cr a day, about 430 days; at 40 a day, about 150; a strong player under 100.
- No Pathway Lapse in the last rent week.
- The Chalk has not reached 17 (if it has, the title price was revalued at the Middle rate, the remaining principal jumped, and
  the ending moved away; see "The Chalk arrives").

**What it costs.**
- Everything the Pathway took: a year or more of 15+ cr a day, the 3% on lumps, rent paid twice the whole time.
- Rent stops; the **Title Maintenance Charge** starts: 0.5 cr a square a week, "subject to Rate Review". The meters run as before.
  The Creep moves to the TMC.
- The squares are yours, the floor is not. The Chalk still moves. When it reaches 17, your neighbours are priced out one by one and
  replaced by Middle families, and you are a Proprietor of 24 squares in a Middle (Transitional) hall, the last Fourer on Stores Run.
- Squares added after signing are still rented.

**The document.** The **Certificate of Held Title**: founding-era stock, a Works gear-and-tower watermark the Company kept, your
stud coordinates in a column, and the Count House seal. Clipped to it, the first weekly bill after closure:

```
HALDEN LEDGER : WEEKLY STATEMENT                 F17/C : PROPRIETOR
RENT (HELD TITLE, 24 SQ)                                     0.00
TITLE MAINTENANCE CHARGE (24 @ 0.50)                        12.00
ATMOSPHERE LEVY                                              6.00
METERED SERVICES                                            71.40   ROUNDED 72
CONGRATULATIONS. YOU OWN YOUR FUTURE.
TMC IS SUBJECT TO RATE REVIEW (PERIOD 88/1).
```

**The morning after.** The board is the board. On the Fringe, Benno Kask's board mark (or whoever's) on a slip offering "squares to
let by the night, see Gus".

**Variants.**
- **The Chalk arrives.** Close the books after the Chalk has reached 18 but not 17: the ending's morning after shows a Middle slip on
  row 5 for the first time, a neat CO-3 asking for "authentic Mills repair, discreet".
- **Owning the cage only.** The player closes on Held Title and lets every added square go. The cage, 24 squares, rent-free. The
  smallest secure life in the tower.

**In play:** Held Title is both an ending and a midpoint. It is offered the night the Pathway closes; most players will decline and
keep going, because the weekly maths just changed in their favour by 128 cr. The ending exists so a player who wanted exactly this
can stop here, satisfied, and see it framed.

#### The Landlord

**What it is.** You became a sublet baron. Your squares (titled, leased, and other people's leases you manage) are let by the hour,
by the shift and by the night, under licences to occupy. Your income is other people's sleep.

**What leads there.**
- 100+ squares under your control, by title, lease or management agreement, of which at least 40 are let to others.
- Letting the floor (Part four) for 60 nights or more, or a hot-bunk house of your own (06b: "A house of your own").
- Gus Tanaka-Breen bought out, partnered, or outlasted; or Mezzanine Living's franchise accepted.

**What it costs.**
- Everyone you let to is someone who used to be your client; the Long Shift and the Co-op go to *Hostile*.
- The licence-to-occupy argument holds only while the Arbiter accepts it (06b's hook: A/61/0007). If the Co-op overturns it, you pay
  15% to the Company for a Sublet Licence on every square, the same week.
- The work is collecting. You stop making things. Your machines are sold or let to tenants by the hour.

**The document.** A **tick sheet** for one square on Stores Run, 24 hourly boxes, each ticked with a sleeper's initials, full at noon
(06b).

**Variants.**
- **Turned** (06b): you sold to Gus and stayed on as sitter. The document is a licensee's receipt for one night on squares you used
  to hold, with the hour stamped.
- **The Kind Landlord.** You let at cost, to people you know, and lose money slowly. The document is the Bunk Book's last page.

#### A car of your own

The Lift Guild ending of 06a: you buy Nell Pradesh's car right (C-7) when her aunt's debts come due. The only property in the Stack
that is not a Halden lease, and it only works as long as the car runs. **Leads there:** Guild at *Trusted*, Nell's debt event, about
9,000 cr, and an Old Hand who can keep a Kessin drive turning. **Costs:** the rats (*Hostile*: you are Guild now) and a lifetime of
parts hunting. **Document:** the Ride Book's first page in your hand.

### II. Climbing

#### Glass

**What it is.** A Terraces lease. A licensed workshop with a glass frontage on a floor in the sixties, between a private clinic and a
lawyer's office. Daylight through a cut window. You are "glass" (file 08: "Her daughter went glass").

**What leads there.**
- Standing 750 (Terraces lease gate), held for 30 days.
- A licensed trade in good order (Reseller, Grow Certificate, food handling, or the Preferred Supplier Programme).
- Enough money to carry Terraces rent for eight weeks: a 24-square workshop at the bottom of the band is 1,440 cr a week, so about
  11,500 cr in hand, or a sponsor.
- A Terrace client willing to write a **character reference** (the Terraces lease form has a box for one; the Scholar's past
  starts with a classmate who might).

**What it costs.**
- The rent. Terraces squares cost twelve to forty times Mills squares. A Terraces workshop must clear 250 cr a day to stand still. One
  bad month and you are "two missed rents and a cough" from the Middle.
- The catch: Terraces catches are rich, and they are licensed: on 60+, the diverter bin belongs to Reclaim, and tenants may not keep
  what falls. Your supply chain from the chute is gone.
- The neighbours: the Terraces want things quiet. No bins, no stills, no presses, nothing that "changes state" (the Mutual's word). You
  sell what you make elsewhere, or you stop making.
- 17: a chalk mark on the lift lobby wall with the year and the floor you went to, under the Glass Line marks the Old Hands keep.

**The document.** The Terraces **Floor Rate Card** for your period, with your floor circled in a Lease Office officer's green ink, and
your new tally, silver.

**The morning after.** The board on 17. Row 2 (TERRACE 60–89) has a slip from your new workshop: you are buying, from the Mills, the
things you used to make, at Terraces prices minus a broker's cut.

**Variants.** **Glass, returned**: the player reached Glass, could not carry it, and came back down. The ending is offered on the night
you move back into the cage: Hester's handwriting in the Book, "Returned. Same squares."

#### The Crown

**What it is.** Rain on the lid. A title above 90. Gardens. Real food. Quiet. File 04: Crown squares are not leased, they are **held as
title**, and title is "not a Standing question; by invitation".

**What leads there.** There are three doors into the Crown, and none of them is the front.

1. **The money door.** The smallest title the **Amenity** (the Crown's residents' committee, 06b) will approve is a **garden title**:
   nine squares on 91 with a three-square strip of roof garden, a gardener's flat from the founding years. Price: **240,000 cr**. Plus
   Standing 800, a Crown sponsor's signature, and the Amenity's **Assay** of your household ("no odour"). The money is the point:
   240,000 cr is about twenty years of a Mills labourer's wages. A player can get there with a Fleet, a Crossing house and a Bought
   Out lump sum together, and almost no other way.
2. **The service door.** A household on 9x needs you near: you are the only person left who can keep its founding-era rain plant, or
   its sync clock, or its grandmother's dialysis unit running (the Bench ending's skills). The household holds a **retainer title** on
   squares in its own name, and you live in them while in service. Rain, daylight, real food. You own nothing. You serve.
3. **The story door.** The fixer's road (06b, "Up by the Story"): a Crown keycard on a household's account, a showroom on 89, a client
   book. It puts you one floor short, under the Crown Comb, with a key that opens the Postern. Most fixers die on 89.

**What it costs.**
- Everything below. The Amenity's rules: no bins, no stills, no presses, no grow beds that are not ornamental, nothing that smells.
  Your machines are sold.
- Your chute. Your rubbish now goes down.
- Your neighbours: the Mills ledgers are frozen where they were. Nobody on 17 sends you slips. Some of them read your name on a Crown
  slip, ghosted, Std. 850, and know it is you.

**The document.** The Amenity's **minute of approval**, three lines, with Faber-Quayle's note in the margin: *No odour.* (06b) Under it,
in the same envelope, a **Waste Schedule** for your household: the BIDS methods your valet must use, and your **Disposal Rebate** code.

**The morning after.** Not the board. The catch. At 04:00 your household's first bag goes down the **North Chute**, the Crown's. At 06:00
it lands in a North catch on floor 17, two leases from the old Line Four stores cage, where whoever took the cage after you sorts it at 08:00:
a punctured bottle, a silk shirt with the X, the Welcome Pack mug, chipped.

**Variants.**
- **Service, ended.** The household no longer needs you (the plant was replaced; the grandmother died). The retainer title is
  withdrawn on a Thursday. The ending text is a reference letter, very kind, and a lift pass valid to floor 60.
- **The Pantry.** A title on **floor 90**, the Crown's kitchen floor, the "Crown's back door" (file 08), bought for 60,000 cr: technically
  the Crown, with no daylight and no rain, above the Comb and below everyone. The document is your new address card, which says
  "The Crown" and is accepted by nobody above 91.

#### Up by the story

The fixer ending (06b). **Leads there:** fixers at *Kin*, ten story cards accepted by the Assay, a Crown household's keycard. **Costs:**
whatever you sold to get there, and the story cards you wrote about objects from your own chute. **Document:** a story card for an
object you once pulled from the Kitchen.

### III. Together

#### The Mills Charter

**What it is.** Halden signs a **collective lease** on the Mills: every lease from 10 to 29 under one Charter held by the Co-op. Rent is
frozen. The ending text reads the Charter's clauses, and the last one is a buyout option the Company can exercise in twenty years
(06a). Everyone signs anyway.

**What leads there.**
- The Co-op at *Kin*.
- Standing 600 or more (the Company will not negotiate with a delinquent's signature on the Co-op's side of the table: the player's
  Standing is literally weight in the room).
- The **Settlement** document from the Archive (the original signed text of the Settlement of Y41, the severance deal the Co-op grew from, with a clause that says "until the reopening
  of the Works").
- One of: the Strike held for seven days on at least twelve Mills floors; or proof that the Works never formally closed (the Dark
  Floors: a HW-9 cell with a date code, the requisitions, Aldo's sync map).

**What it costs.**
- The strike, if it comes to that: seven days of capped meters, lifts closed to Mills floors, HPS on the stairwells. Your Pathway in
  arrears for a week (−60 Standing, then −10 a day). Bins that die on the trickle.
- The Preferred Supplier Programme, which will never have you.
- Your own Held Title, if you had it: Charter leases replace titles in the Mills (the Co-op's terms; it will not have proprietors inside
  a collective lease). You trade ownership for a frozen rent shared with everyone.

**The document.** The **Mills Charter**, nineteen clauses, read in full. Clause 19: *Halden may, at any date after Y106, purchase the
Collective Lease at a price determined by the Office of the Arbiter.*

**The morning after.** The board. The Floor Rate Card pinned beside it has a line through floor 17's rate in red pencil and FROZEN
written beside it. Above it, on the lift lobby wall, the Old Hands' Glass Line chalk marks, and a new one, the first that is not about
the Glass Line: *Y87. Mills. Ours.*

**Variants.** **The Strike, lost**: evictions and barons. A falling ending for the whole floor, offered to a player who still has
something (a document: the list of evicted leases, with your board mark beside the ones you took). **The Strike, won small**: a rent
cut of 10% and nothing else. The document is the new rate card.

#### The Co-op floor

**What it is.** The ending from `LORE.md`: your workshop becomes a **Co-op floor**. You own nothing alone, you share everything, you are
never evicted, and you never get rich.

**What leads there.**
- Co-op at *Trusted* or better.
- Held Title on your squares (you need to own them to give them).
- A Hall vote on 13, carried.

**What it costs.** You transfer your titled squares into the Co-op's **Pool**. Your machines go into the **Crib**: you can draw them
as any member can, by the hour, in the book. Your income becomes your share-hours and produce chits. You will never reach Glass or the
Crown from here; the Co-op's Standing is not yours.

**The document.** Ben Books's pencil ledger, one page: your name, your squares, your machines, a line across them, and under it
"POOL, F17/C, 24 SQ."

**The morning after.** The cage's frontage has a hand-painted sign: CO-OP 17-C, OPEN TO MEMBERS, TAP HOURS SHARED. Mikko Kask is on the
counter, counting the Fall.

#### Free Shafts

**What it is.** The free shafts stay free, and safer. The rats' map is open. (06a: "The Open Map".)

**What leads there.**
- The full **Shaft Schedule** (the Archive's founding-era map of every riser, duct and hole), bought or stolen.
- Chalked onto the Drop board on 20, with Yusuf Haddad's leave.
- The **Shaft Safety Ordinance** vote lost by the Company (your Standing with the Company, the Guild and the rats tips it).

**What it costs.** The Archive will never sell you anything again. The Lift Guild loses its best argument, and three cars stop within
a year for lack of parts money: the slow world event of the Last Car. The free shafts get safer for everyone, including the looters, who
read the map too.

**The document.** The Drop wall, photographed in chalk: every riser, every hole, every comb, and in the corner your board mark.

**The morning after.** Row 6 (SERVICE) on the board is empty: Halden has withdrawn the chute-clearance contracts, because the rats now
clear the chutes for free in exchange for passage.

### IV. Business

#### Bought Out ("a business the Company has to buy")

**What it is.** You got big enough at something that a brand would rather own you than compete with you. The offer is a lump sum, a
lease upstairs and a **non-compete**. Taking it is the ending from `LORE.md`.

**What leads there.** One of three markets, each with its own buyer:

| Buyer | What you did | Condition (working numbers) |
|---|---|---|
| **Orrin Provisions** | Mills Brown bins and grey brick (06a) | you supply a third of the protein slips on the Mills rows for 60 days, and Orrin's Mills depot intake falls by a fifth |
| **Halden** (via Halden Civil Works) | restored founding-era parts, or water (a certified still that outsells the meter) | 40 founding-era parts delivered to Old Hands, the Guild or Vantage in a season; or 30% of Wet Run buying your water instead of the tap |
| **Vey** | a grey medicine line (re-sealed doses, cracked DoseLocks refilled) | you are Vey's biggest unlicensed competitor below 30 for a quarter |

The offer arrives as a heavy envelope in your bay, delivered by a Halden Sparrow that lands on your pad without a slip.

**The offer (Orrin's, as an example).**
- **Lump sum:** 14,000 cr, paid in two halves, the second "on completion of transition".
- **A lease:** 24 squares on floor 44, two years' rent paid, frontage, a Supply counter downstairs.
- **A position:** "Heritage Strains Consultant", 40 cr a day, for two years.
- **The non-compete:** for five years you may not keep, breed, sell or press insects anywhere in the Stack or "any structure in Calder
  Bay under Halden licence". Your bins, colonies and press become Orrin's on signing.

**What it costs.** Your trade. Your colonies. Your name on the thing you made: within a month **Mills Brown** is relabelled **Orrin
Heritage Strain** and sold back to the Mills at 3 cr a brick in a sleeve. The ranchers you supplied with Brown stock go to *Hostile*
the morning the sleeve appears. The lump sum is large, and the Pathway's prepayment adjustment takes 3% of whatever you use to close it.

**The document.** The first **Orrin Heritage Strain** label, printed in brown on cream: *A taste of the Mills, since Y86.*

**The morning after.** On the Mills rows of the board, an Orrin slip offering supply contracts for Heritage Strain at a rate a tenth
lower than you used to pay your own suppliers.

**Variants.**
- **Refused.** You turn the offer down. Orrin cuts the Vitabrick price across the Mills for a season (the Brown Brick price war, 06a).
  Not an ending: a world event, and a test of whether you can last.
- **Vantage buys you.** For a founding-era parts trade, Corvane bids against Halden. Vantage's offer includes **residency**, which turns
  this ending into "Another tower" (below).
- **Halden buys your water.** The Certified variant (06a: "Certified"): the Company does not buy the still, it buys the licence. Your
  still becomes a Halden Pure "community supplement point". You run it on a salary.

#### Partner

**What it is.** You become a **Halden Approved Workshop**: a franchise of the Company in the Mills. The Preferred Supplier Programme,
taken all the way.

**What leads there.**
- Standing 700 or more.
- Two licences in good order for 180 days.
- The Preferred Supplier Programme accepted (the Company arc, Part five), and its fork passed in the Company's favour.

**What it costs.**
- 12% of turnover to the Company, taken at the pay post.
- The **Schedule of Permitted Goods**: you may sell what is on it and nothing else. No restored goods, no grey water, no Mills Brown.
- Your prices are set by the Company's regional pricing desk.
- In return: your rent is fixed for five years ("Partner Rate Protection"), the Chalk cannot revalue your title, HPS answers your
  calls at Assured speed, and brand agents never walk your frontage.

**The document.** The **Schedule of Permitted Goods**, eleven pages, with the last line: *Items not listed are deemed not permitted.*

**The morning after.** A plaque on your frontage: A HALDEN APPROVED WORKSHOP. Tansy's chalk sign under your drain tile has been
scrubbed: someone has drawn, in its place, the sign the vent kids use for *don't*.

#### The Fleet

**What it is.** You became the biggest courier in the Mills (06a, "A fleet"). Rats fly on your slips. You are not a rat any more.

**What leads there.**
- Five drones or more, at least two of them riser-class.
- At least three hands flying as couriers on your slips for 30 days.
- The Drop at *Trusted*; your board mark on the Drop board as a **boss mark** (a mark with a ring around it).

**What it costs.** The Kitty will not cover a boss. Your couriers lose bays and you pay or you don't. The packing, which was the game,
is now something your hands do; you are reading **left-behind lists** at 20:00 instead of filling bays at 08:00. The Lift Guild
counts your launches and the Company is drafting an ordinance with your name in the margin.

**The document.** Your board mark on the Drop board, with the ring. Beside it, a rat's chalk word nobody has ever written next to a
mark before: **BOSS**.

**The morning after.** Eleven slips on the board on 17 are already claimed when Noor pins them. Your mark is on the stub of each.

#### The Crossing

**What it is.** You trade between towers. A **bay house**: a lease near the Nine Dock (or a pad on the Landing) where crossing frames
come and go to Vantage, Greenhold, St. Ober's, the Pier, the Hulk.

**What leads there.**
- Two crossing frames (Corvane Terns, or rat builds on Heron bones), batteries rated for the crossing, and a kite rig or solar sheet
  for the slow route.
- Contracts delivered to at least four other towers.
- A standing with the Sounding (the forecasts) and a way past the **poachers** (an arrangement, an escort, or a slow frame nobody
  bothers to stop).

**What it costs.** Batteries (the most expensive thing you will ever buy), frames lost to poachers and weather, Vantage's Compliance,
Greenhold's line inspectors, the Hulk's false lights. And 17: you are rarely there.

**The document.** A **bill of lading** for one crossing, stamped at both ends: Halden's Nine Dock stamp and a stamp from the receiving
tower, each in its own style (a Corvane compliance code; the Hulk's chalked plumb-line mark; St. Ober's red cross with an invoice
number).

### V. Leaving

#### Another tower

**What it is.** You leave the Stack. Not for nowhere (there is nothing to go to on the water): for another tower that will have you.

The variants are separate endings:

- **Vantage.** Residency. Corvane recruiters come on the Nine Dock boat twice a year and offer residency to anyone with steady hands and
  something Corvane wants (a founding-era clock number, a cell skill, a parts trade). **Leads there:** a Vantage standing of *Trusted*
  (built through clean, compliant Vantage contracts), a recruiter's offer (the Drop, Off the boat and Brownglass pasts get one sooner).
  **Costs:** you cannot take your machines (non-compliant kit); everything on your squares is sold to the Recovery Store at a third
  of value or given away. Your residency is tied to employment under **No Idle Residency**. The Sobriety Standard. **Document:** a
  Corvane employment contract, white synthetic sheet, with clause 14 highlighted by someone in Compliance: *Release in accordance with
  Residency Policy 3 (thirty days).* For the Drop's past, the ending text adds one line: *Re-hire. Seniority not restored.*
- **Greenhold.** A contract grower's berth in the licensed agricultural tower. **Leads there:** Grow Certificates on six or more beds
  for a season, R✓ produce delivered to Greenhold, a line inspector's recommendation. **Costs:** your seed. Greenhold grows only
  Greenhold seed; every saved variety you carry is forfeit at the door, and the seed savers (06a) mark you. **Document:** a
  Greenhold Grower's Licence with your patent obligations listed: forty-one varieties, none yours.
- **The Hulk.** A place in a crew on the leaning tower. **Leads there:** salvage trade with the Hulk, a crew's invitation, the
  Plumb-keeper's word. **Costs:** you own nothing, and nobody can charge you rent. The Plumb reads six and a third degrees and rising.
  **Document:** the Plumb-keeper's morning reading, chalked, with your crew's floor beside it.
- **St. Ober's Spire.** You went for treatment (yours, or someone's: Mother Ruus's lungs, Sarai Kask's child) and stayed for the debt.
  A falling ending wearing a clean gown. **Document:** an invoice, itemised, with the **Saint's** terms: repayable in service. You
  work on the Spire's supply floor for ten years. "Gone to the Saint."
- **The Pleasure Pier.** You go to sell, or to play, or to serve the rich of every tower at once. **Leads there:** Pier contracts and a
  manager's offer. **Costs:** the Pier's season ends every year and so does your berth. **Document:** a season pass, valid to the
  equinox.

**The morning after (for all).** Your cage is let to someone new within a week. The Welcome Pack mug is on the counter. Your board mark
is still chalked on the Fringe and nobody scrubs it for a month, which on 17 is a sign of respect.

#### Going wet

**What it is.** You give up the lease and move down past the Sump: a raft on the Flats, a stall on the Exchange steps on Great Ebb
days, life by the tide table. No rent, no meter, no water rights. Neither failure nor escape (06a).

**What leads there.** Tide Folk at *Trusted*; a raft (bought, built from salvage, or Hesper Quill's gift); the lease surrendered with a
broom-clean inspection; your debts settled or abandoned (if abandoned, the Vargas follow; the Vargas always follow).

**What it costs.** The Ledger: a Flats registration resets you to Standing 150. Water: the Flats drink what they can catch, ferment and
buy. Your lungs, slowly, if you dive. And it is a strange freedom: nobody can raise your rent.

**The document.** Hesper Quill's **knot cord** with a new knot on it, and the tide card for next year, hand-drawn.

**The morning after.** Not the board. The Landing at low tide. Your raft is the fourth from the hoist. Ticket Joe is weighing a crate
with his thumb.

### VI. Becoming

These are the endings in which you become one of the things the tower used on you. Each is a real success by the world's measure.

#### The Armband

**What it is.** You are the warden of 17. Green armband, the Warden's Book, a key to the lobby board, a whistle.

**What leads there.**
- Hester Moyle has died or retired (a scripted late-game event: Old Hands die one a season, and Hester is a former line inspector:
  she goes in the winter of the second year, or earlier if a Pim floor took her job).
- Standing 500; Hester's regard at its top pip, or the Lease Office's approval, whichever comes first (the Lease Office does not ask
  Hester).
- You applied (−5 Standing for the inquiry).

**What it costs.** Four reports a month. Every month. The Lease Office's healthy irregularity rate is not a suggestion. You choose
whom. You get 30% off your rent, a fee per report that leads to a charge, a collection fee for registration and chute-clearance
charges, and the floor's regard, which goes one of two ways: the warden people talk about like weather, or the warden whose door is
glued shut.

**The document.** The last page of the **Warden's Book** (06b: "The Armband"): four reports a month, every month, and the names chosen.
The player's names are the names the player chose, printed in Hester's old column format. If the player kept Hester's practice, most
of them are Gus Tanaka-Breen.

**The morning after.** You pin the Fringe yourself at 07:05, after Noor. A watch crystal is waiting on your counter. Aldo left it.

#### Recoveries

**What it is.** You buy debts. A small recovery firm on 17, licensed to buy Mills debt from Brisk & Lowe and collect it "however the
Charter lets it". The Tagger's past made whole.

**What leads there.** Twenty debt slips bought and collected; a **recovery agent's licence** (500 cr, Std. 500); a tag gun.

**What it costs.** Your clients are now your debtors. The vent kids stop coming. Tamar Lisowski moves her offcut to 14. The Co-op
writes your name in the margin of the dues book, where it writes the names of HPS Attendants.

**The document.** Your **card**, printed, cream with a navy border (Brisk & Lowe's style, because you bought their stock): *We are
here to help you resolve this.*

**The morning after.** A yellow tag on a charging rack two leases from your cage. Your handwriting.

#### Become what you hated (the general case)

The Landlord, the Armband, Recoveries, Partner, and the Crown by the service door all share a shape: the player escapes the pressure
by becoming the pressure. The game should not punish any of them, and should not reward them in a way that hides what they are. The
"What became of them" list (Part seven) carries the weight: the endings that look like victory are the ones where the neighbours'
lines are shortest.

### VII. Keeping

Endings in which you are never rich and something is still alive because of you.

#### The Bench

**What it is.** The Last Apprentice (06a). The Old Hands die over the game, one a season. If you have been apprenticed, the bench passes
to you: when the last one goes, you are where people bring machines. The founding-era tech that nobody can make, only repair, is now
repaired by you.

**What leads there.**
- Old Hands at *Kin*.
- Apprenticed to at least two Old Hands (each teaches one technique a season: Kessin drives, Sorrel meters, Lung plates, sync clocks,
  membranes, cell formation).
- The last scripted death has happened (late; the third year or so).

**What it costs.** Hours: an apprentice's time is not paid, and every season of apprenticeship is a season of fewer contracts. And
the weight: everyone who needs a founding-era repair now needs you, from the Lift Guild to the Crown's rain plant, and you cannot do
all of it. Some machines will stop because you chose others.

**The document.** A list of **clock numbers** (06a), the Old Hands' Works numbers in the order they died, and yours at the end. You
were never at the Works. The Old Hands gave you a number anyway: the next one after the last Works clock ever issued.

**The morning after.** Three machines on your frontage with paper tags, from three bands: a Mills grinder, a Middle meter, a Crown
sync clock with a household's crest. A queue at 08:00.

**Variant: Written Down** (06a): the player brokers peace between the Old Hands and the Archivists, and the knowledge gets written.
Founding-era repairs stop being a mystery and the price of founding-era parts drops for everyone, including the player's own stock.
The Bench ending, given away.

#### The Lid, and the Library

Two small keeping endings from 06b and 06a, reached through the player's own squares:

- **The Lid.** Vent kids at *Kin*, Tansy shouldered (grown out of the crawlspaces), your workshop the crew's door for twenty years. The
  document is a chalk sign on the underside of your drain tile: a crown (a lid under a rich lease) and a cup (water here). **Costs:**
  a square of your floor permanently given to a hatch, food left out every night, and the warden's occupancy counts.
- **The Library.** Seed savers at *Kin*. Your workshop becomes the Library's main bed, with an isolation room and the envelope with the
  locations. **Costs:** your grow beds grow nothing that sells; they grow thirty families' seed, once a year, in isolation.

### VIII. Truth

#### The Proof

**What it is.** Everyone in the Stack knows the people at the top drowned Calder and blamed the storm. The player assembles the proof:
the Archive's seawall correspondence, the Tide Folk's **Wall Book**, the **Pell Report** and the **Harrow Memo** (01), maybe the Vault's
dry contents from floor 2. Then the player decides who gets it (06a: "The Proof").

**What leads there.** At least three of the four documents; the Archivists and the Tide Folk at *Trusted* or better; a sealed bay to
carry paper that cannot get wet.

**What it costs.** The Company notices. Your Standing drops by 200 the week a copy leaves your squares, for "conduct" (the inputs are
never shown). HPS stops your lane drones. Everyone who helped is named somewhere.

**The ends, by buyer.**
- **To Vantage.** Corvane takes it to inter-company arbitration and wins Halden's **Calder Recovery Licence**. The Flats and the
  seabed get a new owner with stricter rules. Nothing else changes. You are paid very well. **Document:** a Corvane remittance slip,
  white, with a compliance code.
- **To Static.** Read aloud over three nights. Everyone hears what they already knew. **Document:** the morning after: the meters still
  run (06a).
- **To the Strongroom.** Catalogued, numbered, shelved. Imelda Vos tells you it's the safest place for it, and it is. **Document:** the
  catalogue card.
- **To the Arbiter.** The player files it (60 cr filing fee). The Office of the Arbiter determines that the matter is "outside Halden
  jurisdiction, as it concerns events prior to the Emergency Stewardship Order", and returns the documents with a receipt for the
  fee. **Document:** the receipt.
- **To the Company.** Halden's records office buys it back to "preserve corporate heritage". The price is a title. Twelve squares on
  77, clear, with a view (x2 on the Terraces). **Document:** the title certificate, and the Count House's acknowledgement of
  documents received, numbered for destruction.

**The morning after.** The board, unchanged. The Floor Rate Card for the next Period, printed on time.

#### Stores

**What it is.** The strangest ending: the Works employs you.

The player's cage is the Line Four stores cage, and the Clean Core's automated procurement has been sending its requisitions to STORES
17-C for forty-five years (02). If the player answers the requisitions, logs the hum, follows Aldo's sync map, and finally opens the
plate under the Works Bench (**HOIST 17-C**: the old stores hoist that ran between the lines and the Works' central stores), the Works'
system does something it has not done since Y41. It registers a new employee.

**What leads there.**
- Twelve requisitions delivered to the call plate on 21.
- The Copyist's sheet 17-C/4, or the Archive's equivalent, showing the hoist.
- Aldo's sync-node map, or Benedek Sarr's Common Load notebook (02), or a logged month of the hum.
- A **Works clock number**: Ilse Varro's CCU card, a dead Old Hand's card given to you, or Cas Lenk's (the Heir's past).
- The hoist plate opened (a founding-era key, an Old Hand, or a cutter and a night's noise that Hester will hear).

There is no expedition. The hoist is a dumbwaiter, sixty centimetres square. You put something in it, and something comes back.

**What it costs.** Nobody knows what you work for. The requisitions change: they stop asking for gloves and clocks and start asking for
things only a person can provide: **OPERATOR, LINE 9, 1 EA**; **READINGS, HUMIDITY, F21, HOURLY**; **CLOCK CARD, ISSUE, 1**. Halden notices:
Adaeze Thorne (06b) has a standing order for a Dark Floors access card at any price, and someone in the Count House sees your squares
change status overnight. The Mills notice too: Big Lou's bins on 21 depend on the warm ceiling; if anything you do ever stops the Dark
Floors running, forty bins die in a day.

**The document.** A **Works pay packet**: a brown envelope with a cellophane window, Works stock, dot-matrix, stamped with a date that
is today and a clock number that is yours: **0000**. Inside, a pay slip:

```
HALDEN WORKS (CALDER) : LINE 9 : STORES
CLOCK 0000          GRADE: STORES CLERK          SHIFT: NIGHT
HOURS               --
RATE                AS PER SETTLEMENT Y41
DEDUCTIONS          NONE
NET                 1 EA CELL, H, HW-9
NOTE: LEASE F17/C RECLASSIFIED WORKS PROPERTY (STORES). NOT LEASED.
```

**The morning after.** The lobby board on 17. Your 20:00 statement from the Ledger the night before, pinned by the pay post because it
printed wrong: every line where your rent should be reads **WORKS, NOT LEASED, 0.00**. Halden cannot bill squares that the Works owns.
The studs under your cage answer to the Works, not to the Company. Hester reads the statement on the board for a long time and then
writes something in the Book and does not show anyone.

**Variants.**
- **The person.** The requisitions were from a person all along (02's alternative): someone from Line Nine who never left, or someone
  put in there since. The hoist brings back a handwritten note on requisition stock. The ending's document is the note.
- **The Company's engineer.** The requisitions were from an engineer in the Lantern running the Clean Core remotely (02's alternative).
  The ending is a job offer on Company paper: Clean Core Logistics, floor 71, Standing 800 by fiat. Accepting is "Glass" by a side door.
- **Nothing.** The hoist plate opens on an empty shaft full of dust and a single glove. The requisitions stop. The ending is the glove.
- **The Settlement.** If the Works never closed, the Settlement of Y41 is still in force (06a's hook). With the pay packet in hand, the
  Co-op's Charter (above) and the Old Hands' Pension become possible at once. Stores is the key that turns the other locks.

**In play:** Stores is the ending that rewards curiosity and gives the player the one form of security nobody else in the tower has:
squares the Company cannot charge for, because they belong to something older than the Company. It should cost the player the
neighbourhood's trust in proportion to how much they took from it (the glove from the Glove Wall, Aldo's clock).

#### The Open Roof (and Water for Nothing)

The Rain Church's ending (06a), reached through the player: Drawing W-104 from the Archive, Hollis Brandt's downpipes, the Roofman's
overflow on a storm night, cutting gear on seven landings in one day, your drones carrying it. For three days the rain taps run on every
tenth floor. Then the welders come. **Costs:** everything you have with Skywater and Halden's water office; the stinkers lose three days
of trade and spend them giving water away too. **Document:** the three days, as a list of landings and the number of pans on each.

### IX. Falling

These endings are not offered; they happen. Each is the end of the arrears ladder (05) with nothing left to tag. They should be written
gently and without punishment music: they are the commonest endings in the Stack, and the people in them are not losers in the world's
eyes, only in the Ledger's.

#### The Rail

**What it is.** You lost everything and ended on a rail: a hot-bunk box, the 14:00 shift, with two rail-mates who sleep in the same box
on the other shifts and leave notes (06b).

**What leads there.** Termination of the lease (day 15 on the ladder), the Recovery Store takes the contents, the debt remains, and the
player has no other squares. Lapsed Pathway, no title.

**What it costs.** Everything, and the debt is still there.

**The document.** Three notes left in the box by rail-mates, in three hands. *Took your blanket, mine's wet, back by 6.* / *You talk in
your sleep about a cage.* / *Pick tomorrow at the Turnstile, they want lifters, I put your name.*

**The morning after.** The board on 17. A slip on the Fringe in your handwriting: "Repairs, cheap, ask at the Long Shift, 14:00."

#### The Boat

**What it is.** Work-off, Bastion box pre-ticked. You "elect transfer" and take the Thursday boat from the floor 9 dock.

**What leads there.** Termination with a debt larger than about 2,000 cr and no one who will buy it or sit on it.

**The document.** The work-off contract with the box ticked, and a line in the boat's manifest. For the Off-the-boat past, the ending
adds: *Second transfer.*

**The morning after.** Families stand on the outer stair on 9 and watch the boat go. One of them is Mikko Kask.

#### Lapsed

**What it is.** The Pathway lapsed and Halden "restructured", then ended it. Everything paid was applied to rent and services. You are a
plain tenant of the cage, or of nothing, and you work for someone else: a hand on Yusra Dimitriou's beds, or on Pell Ostrander's bench,
or at Gus's counter.

**What leads there.** A Pathway Lapse not paid out, and no lockout (you kept up the rent, just not the Pathway). Or deliberately.

**The document.** A wage slip from your new employer. If it is Pell, the pay is partly in scrip.

### X. Not ending

#### The Whistle

**What it is.** No ending. The game goes on. The Whistle blows at 06:00, 14:00 and 22:00. The Chalk moves a floor every ninety days and
the Glass Line one a year. Hester gets older. The requisitions come or they don't. A player who does not want to close the books never
has to, and can ask for a **Final Statement** at any rent day to see where they stand.

**What it costs.** Nothing more than it already costs.

**The document.** The Final Statement, on demand: your Ledger account summarised from day one (every contract, every rent day, every
fine, every Standing change), printed on the long roll the pay post uses for the Count House's own audits. Under it, the line the Ledger
prints on every closing statement: *THE LEDGER REMEMBERS KINDNESS.*

**In play:** this is the default state of the game and should feel like one: a living floor that keeps changing whether the player
pushes or not, so that "no ending" is a real choice and not a lack of one. The long clocks (Creep, Chalk, Glass Line, the Old Hands'
deaths, the Last Car, the Dark Floors' requisitions) give a long game its seasons.

## Part seven: how endings are put together

### What became of them

Every ending closes with a short list of the neighbours from Part two and what happened to each. The lines are not random; each is
set by one or two things the player did. A sketch of the rules:

| Neighbour | If the player... | ...the line reads |
|---|---|---|
| **Hester Moyle** | kept her regard high, sent clocks | "Hester kept the Book until Y89. She never reported a still." |
| | named neighbours in quota week, often | "Hester retired in Y88. She asked for her Book to be burned. It was filed." |
| | became warden after her | "Hester's armband is in your drawer." |
| **Aldo Moyle** | gave him clock backs, followed his map | "Aldo finished his map. There is one node he never drew." |
| | took Aldo's clock for a requisition | "Aldo stopped coming to your counter. He does not say why. He does not say anything." |
| **Mattias Orme** | apprenticed, sent back his overflow | "Mattias left you the microscope. The Crown asked for it twice." |
| | sold a first-fit part he told you to keep | "Mattias died in his chair on Litho Run. The Carry passed your frontage without stopping." |
| **Yusra Dimitriou** | took her greens up yourself | "Yusra's beds supply Lantern Row direct. Her broker on 44 sends you a card every Founding Day, unsigned." |
| | undercut her broker but kept her cut | "Yusra has twelve beds now and says your name at Hall meetings." |
| | never answered her slip | "Yusra still sells through the broker at forty per cent." |
| **Mother Ruus** | bought her water, never reported | "Mother Ruus's pot still runs. Nobody knows what feeds the drip." |
| | took the informant's bounty | "Mother Ruus's lease was sealed in Y87. The pot was not found. Neither was she." |
| **The Kasks** | lent the cell, held their debt | "The Kasks still charge cells next door. Mikko counts the Fall." |
| | took their squares | "Benno works the Pick at the Turnstile. Sarai is on the Long Shift, 22:00. Mikko sat the Foundation exam." |
| | hired Sarai | "Sarai Kask runs your counter. She has never once been short." |
| **Pell Ostrander** | competed fairly | "Pell's Pathway closed in Y87. He bought the offcut next to his. It was Tamar's." |
| | reported him first | "Pell moved down to 12. He undercuts someone else now." |
| **Tamar Lisowski** | sent her VOID garments | "Tamar mends for the Seam. She sewed a red thread into your cuff without asking." |
| | took her offcut in a re-cut | "Tamar moved to 14. Her new offcut is around a pipe run." |
| **Gus Tanaka-Breen** | kept his breakfasts going | "Gus's sleepers still buy from your frontage at every Whistle." |
| | let him manage your squares | "Gus manages you now." |
| **Wren and Tansy** | paid for the tide table, left food at the tile | "Wren chalks your pillar for free. Tansy shouldered in Y88. Her crew still uses your tile." |
| | became Recoveries, the Armband or Partner | "The drain tile has not lifted since Y87." |
| **Noor Halvorsen** | tucked a chit under the frame | "Noor leaves your slips up a second day without asking." |
| **Cas Lenk** (the Heir) | answered the requisitions | "Cas's tally was found in the catch a second time, in Y88. It had been charged." |
| **Mr Quarrie** | (any) | "A man in a clean coat still asks for Cas on the first of the month. He has started asking for you." |

**In play:** this list is the game's real verdict, delivered without judgement. It reads from the same flags the arcs already set
(regard pips, faction steps, bounty slips taken, squares taken from named leases) so it costs little to build and pays for every
small choice the player made in the first month.

### The Final Statement, for every ending

Whatever the ending, it ends with the same last object: the **Final Statement**, a long roll from the pay post, which is the Ledger's
summary of the player's life on its books. The ending's own document comes first; the Final Statement comes after, and it is the same
for everyone in shape, which is the joke. The Ledger's summary of a Crown title holder and of a sleeper on the Rail fit on the same
roll, in the same typeface, with the same last line.

```
HALDEN LEDGER : ACCOUNT SUMMARY                      CLOSED AT TENANT REQUEST
DAYS ON LEDGER           412
CONTRACTS DELIVERED      318   (LATE 22) (FAILED 9)
RENT DAYS                 58   (MISSED 1)
PATHWAY                  CLOSED DAY 301
FINES                      4
STANDING (FINAL)         712   PREFERRED
LIFETIME PAID TO HALDEN  41,906.00
LIFETIME ROUNDING        +603.18   (IN HALDEN'S FAVOUR)
THE LEDGER REMEMBERS KINDNESS.
```

**In play:** the "lifetime rounding" line is the Ledger's thumb added up over a life, and it should always be there, always in Halden's
favour, always a few hundred credits.

### Questions this file leaves open

- Should the past be chosen, sealed (revealed piece by piece), or assigned by the first week's behaviour? The three versions in
  Part one play very differently.
- Is "close the books" an end screen, or should accepted endings continue as a new state of play (Held Title and the Armband in
  particular could be modes, not stops)?
- How many endings should the first build carry? A suggested first set: Held Title, Glass, the Charter, Bought Out, Going wet, the Rail.
  The rest wait for the systems they need (factions, other towers, the requisitions).
- Should failure endings be gentle (the Rail as written) or should a failed run end harder?
- Does the player have a name, a face, a gender? This file avoided all three on purpose; each past works for anyone.
- Gus Tanaka-Breen's house is the Eight Squares (06b and 11 corrected); the sitter on 28 in 06b is Odile Prentice, not Odile Fenn.
