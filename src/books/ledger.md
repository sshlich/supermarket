# Spring

Opened again today. Swept the counters, both of them. The outside one for what they bring, the inside one for what I give. My mother kept them that way and I see no reason to stop.

A man brought three apples, so red they looked painted. Synthetic, of course. He swore they were from a tree. I paid him for synthetic and he left angry, but he left the apples.

Bought a blister of painkillers with two gone. Sold the four that were left, one at a time, for more than I paid for the six. I do not feel good about this. I do not feel bad enough to stop.

# Summer

Nobody wants bread in the heat. Everybody wants water. I have started keeping the clear bottles on the inside counter where they can be seen, and the dark ones under it where they cannot.

A girl came in wanting medicine and had nothing to pay with but a locket. I looked inside it. Then I closed it and gave her the bandage anyway and told her to come back for the locket when she could. She has not come back. I keep it in the cabinet.

Wine sells to the same four people, every week, and they all pretend they are buying it for someone else.

# Autumn

Rain. The roof over the outside counter leaks onto the left side, so I tell people to put their things down on the right. Some listen.

A trader brought lamp oil and wanted only books. I gave him two. He read the first lines of each standing at the counter, nodded, and went. I think about that more than I should.

Counted the money: enough. Counted it again: enough, but less.

# Winter

The line is long in the mornings now. They stand in the cold and do not talk to one another. When I open the door they come in one by one and set down what they have and tell me what they need, and I try to make the two meet.

That is the whole trade, really. What people have and what people need, and a counter in between.

Last page. I will need a new book.
