# The First Rule

Water that looks clean is not clean. Water that smells clean is not clean. Water that a neighbour swears by, that a child drank yesterday and is still well, that comes from a pipe with a tap on it: none of it is clean until you have made it so.

This is the first rule, and it is the only one that has never been wrong. Everything else in this book is a way of keeping it.

Most of what makes people sick in water cannot be seen. Mud is honest; you can see mud. What you cannot see is what lives in it, and that is what this book is about.

# Settling

Begin by letting the water rest. Fill a tall vessel and leave it, covered, for half a day. The heavy dirt sinks. Pour off the top two thirds slowly, and throw the rest away, or keep it for washing floors.

Settling does not make water safe. It makes the next steps work better, and it saves your cloth and your fuel. A patient person settles everything.

# Boiling

Fire is the oldest cure and still the surest. Bring the water to a rolling boil, where the bubbles break the surface hard and do not stop when you stir, and keep it there for as long as it takes to say this paragraph aloud three times. Higher up a mountain, keep it longer.

Boiled water tastes flat. Pour it from one clean vessel to another a few times and the taste comes back. Do not add anything to it to make it taste better; whatever you add was not boiled.

Count your fuel. A household that boils all its water needs a great deal of it. Lamp oil, wood and coal will run short before water does, and a sensible person knows which of the three they can trade for.

# Cloth and Charcoal

When there is no fuel, there is still cloth. Fold a clean cloth four times and pour the water through it into a clean pot. This takes out what you can see. It does not take out what you cannot.

A filter of sand, gravel and crushed charcoal, in that order from the bottom up in a pierced bucket, does better. The charcoal must be from wood, not from coal or from anything painted. Change the charcoal when the water through it starts to taste of anything at all.

Filtered water is better water. It is not clean water. Boil it if you can, and if you cannot, drink it in the knowledge that you have made a choice.

# Storing

Keep clean water in a clean, closed vessel, out of the sun. Clear glass shows you what is in it, which is a comfort, but light breeds green things. Dark glass keeps longer.

Never dip a cup into stored water. Pour from the vessel. A hand in the water undoes all your work.

Mark the day you filled it. After a week, use it for washing, and make more.

# A Last Word

People will come to you for water when you have it and they do not. Some will offer more than it is worth, and some will offer nothing. The first rule does not change for either of them.
