# The Stack (worldbuilding, seeded 2026-10-07)

The world the game is set in, settled bit by bit. `IDEAS.md` holds the mechanics; this file holds why the world is the way
it is, so items, contracts and clients can be invented from it instead of named at random.

Each point is marked **decided** (the user said so), **proposed** (to confirm or change) or **open** (a question).

## Premise

- **Decided:** a vertical slum, gritty and bad, not an arcology (no utopian ecology). It should show dystopian capitalism,
  inequality and the divide between the top and the bottom.
- **Decided:** no forests and no foraging. Fresh food is only grown, with precious water. Protein blocks are ground insects
  and food waste from the upper floors.
- **Decided:** orders are contracts, fulfilled by packing and launching drones.
- **Decided:** the Stack was a **factory town**: one tower, about a hundred floors, built by a company to house the workers of a
  plant on its lower floors. The plant closed long ago; the Mills are its old halls.
- **Decided:** **the city outside drowned.** The Stack stands in the water. Leaving is possible, but **there is nothing to go
  to**: ruins, no work, no water rights. Surrounded by water nobody can drink.
- **Proposed:** the flood took the plant's lowest floors, so the Sump is the flooded remains of the factory: wet, rusted,
  half underwater.

## The floors

- **Proposed bands,** from the top:
  - **The Crown (90+):** the owners. Rain, daylight, real food, quiet.
  - **The Terraces (60–89):** managers and professionals. Offices, private clinics, gardens behind glass.
  - **The Middle (30–59):** skilled workers and licensed shops. Clean enough, metered everything.
  - **The Mills (10–29):** workshops, grow co-ops, insect farms, dormitories. The player starts here.
  - **The Sump (0–9):** the bottom, the flooded factory floors. Grey water, the chute ends, fixers, whatever the floors above
    stopped wanting.
- **Proposed:** what flows down is trash, grey water, used goods and rumours. What flows up is goods, labour and rent.

## Who runs it

- **Proposed:** the Halden Company owns the building: the leases, the meters (water, power, maybe air), the company store and
  its scrip, and the licensed drone lanes.
- **Proposed:** other brands trade under Halden licence: Orrin Provisions (food), Vey (medicine), Skywater (rain from the roof).
- **Open:** is there law beyond company security? Gangs, fixers, a union?

## Money

- **Proposed:** credits (a plain number), and scrip that only spends at the company store.
- **Decided:** a **square** is a square of the board, the same cell items sit on (a cell is 1x1, a charger rack 3x3). Your lease
  is a block of squares, drawn as the field (25 x 20 now). It is a game unit, not a size in metres or
  feet: nothing in the world is counted in squares except the player's board.
- **Decided:** rent is **a fixed number that increases each month**: one figure for the lease (125 cr a week on floor 17 to start,
  then 128, 131, 134...), not a sum worked out per square.
- **Proposed:** metered utilities, a credit rating that decides which contracts you are offered, and
  your own shop held on a company loan.

## The player

- **Proposed:** you run a small workshop-shop in the Mills, leased square by square. You grow, make, sort, refurbish and
  deliver, mostly by drone.
- **Decided:** **open-ended, with several endings.** Moving up the floors and owning your lease outright are both valid paths;
  growing the business without a fixed goal is too.
- **Proposed endings:** own your squares (debt paid, nobody can evict you); reach the Crown (a lease up top, rain and
  daylight); others to find (a co-op of the Mills, a business the Company has to buy out...).
- **Open:** who you were before.

## Tech

- **Decided:** everyday tech is **near-future** and believable: cells, drones, grow lights, filters, cheap screens.
- **Decided:** the tower itself was built with **more advanced tech that is now breaking down**. Nobody can make it anymore,
  only repair it: the meters, the lifts, the air plant, the old factory machines. A broken part from the founding era is
  worth more than anything new.

## Drones

- **Proposed:** drones are everyday kit, not a specialist trade: cheap short-hop **hoppers** are as common as bicycles, and
  a courier frame with a real bay is the first big purchase. A drone flies itself on a route loaded before launch (no signal
  reaches through the steel); only the licensed lanes can be talked to.
- **Proposed:** the lifts and stairs are slow, owned and tolled, so goods move by drone through the shafts. Licensed lanes
  (Halden transponders, permits) are safe and paid. Free shafts are free and dangerous.

## Rules for inventing things

- **Practical first.** Most items are something someone needs, or a step in a chain. Variety comes from use, not from junk
  with different names. Flavour goes in the name and one line of description.
- Every item should say something about where it sits in the divide: who makes it, who can afford it, where it ends up.
- Tone: dark humour by understatement. No utopia, no heroes, no lectures.
